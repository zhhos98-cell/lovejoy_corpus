# Archive-side Batch 113 — Goodnow writer-file logic, presidential-record fallback, and the Stanford 1901 letterpress-manifestation test

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: follow Batch112's two highest-yield unresolved retrieval problems without manufacturing new components: (1) translate the Hopkins–Russell BRACERS packet from a historical repository label into current filing-regime tests; (2) determine what Stanford's documented early copying and custody practices imply for canonical `GLA0056`, Lovejoy to Orrin L. Elliott, 15 April 1901.

## Core result

Batch113 adds **no new GLA component by design**. It converts two broad leads into deterministic archival tests.

At Johns Hopkins, the official Frank Johnson Goodnow papers finding aid supplies a decisive arrangement rule:

> correspondence is filed alphabetically by the writer, with copies of Goodnow's replies attached.

This matters because BRACERS `121121` independently identifies a Woods-to-Goodnow communication about inviting Bertrand Russell to Johns Hopkins. If that historical object survives in Goodnow's personal papers, the current search target is therefore not a generic `Russell` folder and not a Goodnow recipient file: it is the **Woods writer file**, with a possible attached Goodnow reply.

But the rule cannot yet be transferred to BRACERS `121122`, canonical `GLA0030`. That object is a Lovejoy / Department of Philosophy document concerning the proposed Russell lecture, but the public BRACERS note still does not expose its literal recipient. The adjacency of `121121` and `121122` in a correspondence catalogue does not prove they share a recipient. Thus `121122` may only be filed under Lovejoy in MS-0003 if independent evidence first establishes Goodnow as recipient.

A second current JHU provenance domain is now added as `GAL0045`: **Office of the President records, RG-02-001**. JHU's official description identifies this as a large institutional presidential record group, principally transferred by the Office of the President; Goodnow's authority record links him to it. Given Batch112's correction that `Ferdinand Hamburger Jr. Archives` was a historical name for JHU University Archives rather than Hamburger's personal fonds, RG-02-001 is a justified institutional fallback for the 1916-17 Russell invitation packet. It is not a positive Lovejoy component and must remain separate from Goodnow's personal MS-0003.

At Stanford, the official David Starr Jordan papers finding aid produces an equally strong archival-process result. It states that early President's Office, General Letters and University Letters were intermixed because David Starr Jordan and Orrin Leslie Elliott used adjacent offices and later shared a secretary. More importantly, the guide states that **all incoming letters and loose carbons/drafts of outgoing letters were copied into letterpress books**. It also records that, after the Stanford Archives was established, **59 volumes of Jordan letter books, chronological files**, were transferred from the Registrar's Office.

This does not prove a second surviving copy of `GLA0056`. It does, however, turn the 15 April 1901 Lovejoy-to-Elliott event into a highly specific manifestation test: identify the chronological letterpress volume covering 15 April 1901 and inspect it for a Lovejoy entry; in parallel inspect the intermixed loose correspondence. Only a literal textual/object match can support creation of a second manifestation record.

## 1. MS-0003 gives a filing algorithm, not a new Lovejoy hit

The current JHU description of the **Frank Johnson Goodnow papers, MS-0003** says the collection contains about 12,000 items spanning 1880-1940, with correspondence forming the majority. Its arrangement note is unusually operational:

`writer -> alphabetical correspondence file -> copies of Goodnow replies attached`.

Official control: https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/11

For BRACERS `121121`, the known documentary relation is Woods -> Frank J. Goodnow. The archival consequence is therefore testable:

`121121 historical event`  
`-> search MS-0003 under Woods`  
`-> inspect for literal date/text and any attached Goodnow reply`.

The public digitized MS-0003 surface currently exposes only four digital records: a Chinese scroll, two colonial-administration manuscript witnesses, and James Bryce letters. None concerns Woods, Russell or Lovejoy. This is only a digitization ceiling, not a collection-content result.

Official digitized control: https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/11/digitized

The new governing rule is:

> **A filing rule becomes a locator only after the document's filing key is independently established.**

For `121121`, writer Woods is known. For `121122`, sender Lovejoy is known but recipient is not. Since MS-0003 is Goodnow's papers, `Lovejoy as writer` only predicts a Lovejoy file there if the object is independently shown to have been sent to Goodnow.

## 2. Catalogue adjacency does not transfer recipient identity

BRACERS `121121` and `121122` form a compelling subject sequence:

- Woods asks Goodnow whether Russell might lecture at Hopkins about *The Principles of Social Reconstruction* and criticism of war;
- Lovejoy, Department of Philosophy, wants Russell to lecture on technical philosophy but rejects a war lecture because of Russell's ultra-pacifist position.

They share institution, actor network and invitation problem. They may well belong to one administrative episode.

But they remain two documents.

The public note for `121122` does not expose the recipient. Therefore the following inference remains prohibited:

`121121 recipient = Goodnow`  
`+ adjacent 121122`  
`=> 121122 recipient = Goodnow`.

Batch113 records `recipient = unresolved` for canonical `GLA0030` and refuses to assign MS-0003 as current custody until the recipient/current record group is recovered.

This also preserves the possibility that the historical University Archives held the Lovejoy document in a university administrative file rather than in Goodnow's personal papers.

## 3. RG-02-001 is the institutional-provenance fallback

The official JHU **Office of the President records, RG-02-001** span 1878-1997 (plus the later website), are arranged into 19 series, and were principally transferred by the Office of the President. Goodnow's official authority page links him to this record group.

Official controls:

- https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/834
- https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/agents/people/1263

This becomes `GAL0045`.

The lead is justified by provenance, not by a Lovejoy name hit. Its target is narrowly bounded:

`1916-17 | Woods | Russell | Lovejoy | Department of Philosophy | lecture invitation / appointment planning`.

If a BRACERS-matching object appears here, it must be compared against any MS-0003 witness before deciding whether the two descriptions refer to the same physical document, an office copy, an attached copy, or separate communications.

The historical label `Ferdinand Hamburger Jr. Archives` is therefore now decomposed into three layers:

`historical repository name`  
`-> current JHU repository`  
`-> current fonds/record group still to be established for each object`.

## 4. Stanford's letterpress system creates a specific second-manifestation opportunity

Canonical `GLA0056` is the historical letter event:

`Arthur O. Lovejoy -> Orrin L. Elliott, 15 April 1901`.

Secondary archival scholarship cites it to `Elliott MSS, Stanford University Archives`, and current catalogue data confirms the Orrin Leslie Elliott papers. The physical Stanford witness remains unresolved because the Elliott collection includes copies of documents assembled for Elliott's later Stanford history.

The official **David Starr Jordan papers, SC0058** now adds a separate institutional-process layer. Its custodial history says:

1. Jordan and Elliott used adjacent offices and later shared secretary George A. Clark.
2. Their correspondence files became intermixed; the categories `President's Office`, `General Letters`, and `University Letters` proved effectively meaningless.
3. **All incoming letters and loose carbons/drafts of outgoing letters were copied into letterpress books.**
4. A 1934 archival assistant found segregation so difficult that alphabetical rearrangement was necessary and segregated several thousand letters useful to Elliott's university history.
5. After Stanford Archives was founded in 1965, staff recovered additional Jordan letters, including those segregated for Elliott.
6. Following approval of a microfilm edition, **59 volumes of Jordan letter books (chronological files)** were transferred from the Registrar's Office to the Archives.

Official control: https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/tf3f59n6bn

This creates at least three potential manifestation domains around the same historical communication:

`Elliott-papers archival witness (form unresolved)`

`intermixed loose institutional correspondence (unverified for Lovejoy)`

`contemporaneous letterpress copy (unverified for Lovejoy)`.

A fourth, later derivative domain may exist in microfilm, but the public guide does not establish that every letter/book or GLA0056 specifically was microfilmed.

None of these potential domains is counted as a new component until object-level evidence appears.

## 5. The 15 April 1901 retrieval test is now date-mechanical

The best Stanford action is no longer a surname search.

Because the historical letter date is exact and the letter books are described as chronological, the primary test is:

`identify letterpress volume spanning 15 April 1901`  
`-> inspect date run around 15 April`  
`-> search for Lovejoy / Elliott / Ross context`  
`-> if present, record volume/page and collate wording with the Elliott-papers witness`.

In parallel, inspect the early intermixed President's Office / General Letters / University Letters correspondence and the Elliott materials segregated for his Stanford history.

The comparison order should be:

`literal text`  
`-> sender/recipient/date`  
`-> physical form`  
`-> annotations/routing`  
`-> custody/series`  
`-> manifestation relationship`.

A matching date alone is not sufficient for duplicate identity.

It does not claim:

- `121121` and `121122` were originally filed together;

- the 15 April 1901 letter definitely survives in a letterpress book;
- the Elliott-papers witness is Lovejoy's autograph original;
- Stanford's microfilm edition contains GLA0056.

All are now finite retrieval questions.

## Evidence rules added

> **A filing rule becomes a locator only after the filing key is independently established.**

> **Adjacent catalogue records do not inherit each other's sender, recipient, date, or custody.**

> **A repository-level historical label must be resolved through current fonds/record-group provenance object by object.**

> **A documented copying system creates a manifestation opportunity, not proof that a particular document was copied or survives.**

> **Exact date plus chronological filing can create a deterministic retrieval test without creating an archival-presence claim.**
