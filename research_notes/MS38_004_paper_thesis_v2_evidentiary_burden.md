# MS38_004 paper thesis v2 — evidentiary burden, not proto-unit-ideas

Date: 2026-08-18; synchronized 2026-08-26
Status: **CURRENT 004 PAPER-THESIS MEMO**

## Thesis in one sentence

The early Lovejoy archive does not reveal a hidden vocabulary of `unit-ideas`; it reveals a practice of **separating historical derivation from present explanatory function**, thereby assigning different evidentiary burdens to genealogy, semantics, causation and configuration.

## What is genuinely new at the notebook level

The broad proposition that Lovejoy's early work in religion/history of philosophy anticipates his later history of ideas is already present in Daniel J. Wilson. The contribution of notebook 004 must therefore be narrower and documentary.

The notebook allows us to reconstruct, at page level, how named source propositions are transformed before publication:

- Rhys Davids supplies part of the Sāṁkhya/Buddhism chronology and classification problem;
- Warren supplies the false-presupposition/practical-aim alternatives and a patchwork hypothesis for dependent origination;
- Senart supplies a highly developed programme of term filiation, stratification and composite-source analysis;
- Hardy supplies the causal architecture later rendered as `fact` versus `manner` of renewed existence;
- Oldenberg supplies much of the semantic and canonical case for ethical `upādisesa` and, in the true 1897 third edition, a broad set of inference controls that sharply limit claims of Lovejoy-specific novelty.

Lovejoy's notebook does not show these propositions being invented. It shows them being **reweighted, tested against different kinds of evidence and recomposed into a different explanatory allocation**.

## The key historiographical correction: Senart is not a straw man

Senart 1896 p.295 is the control that prevents an inflated Lovejoy claim. Senart explicitly advocates studying Buddhist material by the **analysis and filiation of terms** and reconstructing **successive stratifications**. He warns that inherited nomenclature may give a false appearance of deliberate philosophical system.

Accordingly, the contrast cannot be:

`Senart = historical debris` versus `Lovejoy = analytical system`.

Both perform historical decomposition and lexical comparison. The sharper disagreement concerns what those operations are allowed to prove.

Senart often uses term-filiation and layered composition to limit confidence in apparent systematic unity. Lovejoy can grant the layered composition while withholding the further conclusion that the later arrangement is therefore unintelligible or accidental.

## Principal manuscript hinge: 004 p.49

Notebook p.49 is the cleanest manuscript instance. Lovejoy grants that:

- the formula's terms are not original to Buddhism;
- they are drawn from existing sources;
- objective and subjective elements are mixed;
- the sequence is not homogeneous in strict temporal relation.

He nevertheless denies that these facts prove the formula unintelligible or merely accidental. The surviving corrected wording says, with medium confidence, that a **discoverable technical system** gives the arrangement.

The historical point is not the claim of coherence alone. Oldenberg and others also sought more coherent interpretation. The distinctive operation is:

> evidence of **composite provenance** is not sufficient evidence of **absence of present systematic relation**.

This is a division of evidentiary burden.

## First microgenesis: Hardy → 004 → Lovejoy 1898

Hardy p.394 says that `upādāna` produces renewed existence while karma controls its manner. 004 turns that observation into an analytic question: may `upādāna` be the cause of the **fact** of continued existence and karma the cause of its **manner**?

The 1898 article stabilizes the distinction:

- karma = cause of the **particular condition** of rebirth;
- `upādāna` = cause of the **fact of rebirth in itself**.

Lovejoy then adds a historical explanation. Karma is inherited moral-causal vocabulary that presupposes rebirth; the Buddhist possibility of terminating rebirth creates a separate explanatory problem. The old and new categories therefore coexist but perform different causal jobs.

The source transformation is:

`source observation → analytic distinction → textual/semantic testing → historical-functional reconstruction`.

## Second microgenesis: Senart → 004 → Lovejoy 1898

Senart's alleged repetition of the skandhas becomes historically probative only if `upādāna` is semantically identical with `upādānakkhandha` / the five skandhas.

Lovejoy first tests that semantic premise against Sutta usage. He distinguishes:

- skandhas as such;
- skandhas subject to attachment;
- attachment itself.

Only after identity has been tested can recurrence count as evidence of repetition or compositional duplication.

The rule is:

> **formal recurrence cannot carry genealogical weight until semantic/functional identity has been independently established.**

This is a specific evidentiary restriction, not a generic preference for coherence.

### Oldenberg priority control

The recovered true third edition of Oldenberg's *Buddha* (1897) independently makes essentially the same semantic objection to `upādāna = upādānakkhandha`, and more broadly insists on comparison by characteristic doctrine, conceptual function, source chronology and textual usage rather than generic resemblance or speculative etymology.

Therefore this inference rule is firmly a field-level control by 1897. The notebook's value is Lovejoy's local application and recombination, not priority.

## Third microgenesis: Oldenberg/Childers/Dahlmann/Müller → Lovejoy 1898

The `upādisesa` half produces the cleanest published formulation.

Oldenberg already supplies the main ethical-residue thesis and much canonical evidence. Childers/Rhys Davids and Müller/Dahlmann represent competing derivational routes for `upādi/upadhi`.

Lovejoy explicitly concludes that both derivations may be etymologically possible; consequently **meaning must be settled without reference to etymology**. He turns to usage and keeps contradictory textual witnesses open.

Here the evidentiary division is explicit:

`etymology/filiation evidence ≠ sufficient semantic evidence`.

The recovered Jacobi 1897 review and Oldenberg 1897 third edition also show that diagnostic discrimination and usage-over-generic-affinity were already live Indological proof practices. This narrows the paper's novelty claim toward source transformation and evidentiary allocation.

## 1902 scale expansion

`Religion and the Time-Process` is the strongest later bridge because Lovejoy explicitly refers readers back to his 1898 Buddhist technical paper.

At a larger comparative scale he again refuses to let one relation settle another. The resemblance between Aristotle's changeless/self-sufficient deity and Vedānta does not establish an eastern source when the Greek conception can be reconstructed internally. The same caution applies to Neoplatonic otherworldliness.

The parallel should be phrased operationally:

- **1898:** derivation/filiation does not by itself determine semantic function;
- **1902:** structural resemblance does not by itself determine historical transmission.

Both separate questions require their own evidence.

## 1909 reception control: Oltramare

The complete Paul Oltramare, *La formule bouddhique des douze causes* (1909), is now in the primary corpus. It discusses Western interpretation, including Warren, Oldenberg, Senart and Deussen, and treats the formula through compositional history, doctrinal recombination and later theological interpretation.

A full-object OCR pass produced **no `Lovejoy` hit**.

The safe reception statement is:

> **Lovejoy does not surface in this controlled 1909 specialist-synthesis OCR, while several adjacent interpreters do.**

This is evidence that the 1898 paper was at least non-canonical in this particular near-contemporary synthesis. It is not proof that Oltramare never read Lovejoy.

## What the article should NOT claim

Do not claim that:

1. Lovejoy invented decomposition or recombination;
2. Lovejoy first discovered the composite character of dependent origination;
3. Lovejoy first objected to `upādāna = upādānakkhandha`;
4. Lovejoy invented the fact/condition causal distinction;
5. Lovejoy alone restored coherence to the nidāna formula;
6. Senart simply declared the formula meaningless without a historical method;
7. 004 already contains mature `unit-ideas`;
8. similarity between an 1898 operation and later history-of-ideas terminology proves a direct developmental line;
9. Lovejoy's 1898 paper was a canonical intervention in Buddhist studies merely because its method is reconstructible in retrospect;
10. the JHU finding-aid descriptions alone identify Marillier or any other specific teacher as the lecturer behind every Paris notebook.

## Strong paper formulation

> Notebook 004 places Lovejoy inside a late-nineteenth-century Indological field already committed to decomposition, lexical filiation and historical stratification. Its distinctive evidentiary value is therefore not that it reveals those operations before the later history of ideas. It shows Lovejoy learning to limit what each operation may prove. Composite provenance need not imply present unintelligibility; formal recurrence need not imply semantic identity; etymological derivation need not determine contextual meaning. In the 1898 article these controls become publishable semantic and causal arguments, while in 1902 an analogous division of evidentiary burdens governs the distinction between conceptual affinity and historical transmission. The recovered Oldenberg/Jacobi controls relocate the constituent inference rules firmly inside contemporary Indological practice; the Paris finding aid tightens the material setting; and Oltramare's 1909 synthesis supplies a bounded negative reception control. The continuity is not a proto-vocabulary of unit-ideas but a documentary microhistory of source transformation and evidentiary allocation.
