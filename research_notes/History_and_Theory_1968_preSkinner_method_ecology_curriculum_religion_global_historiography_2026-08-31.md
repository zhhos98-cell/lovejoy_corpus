# *History and Theory* 1968: pre-Skinner method ecology across curriculum, religion, intellectual history, and global historiography

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT OFFICIAL VOLUME CONTENT / JSTOR ISSUE METADATA / WHOLE-VOLUME ECOLOGY CONTROL / NO EXHAUSTIVE FIELD-GENERALIZATION**

## Core result

Volume 7 of *History and Theory* (1968), the year immediately before Skinner's `Meaning and Understanding`, is unusually valuable as a control on retrospective rupture narratives.

Within one annual volume, the journal carries:

- Louis Mink on Collingwood and historical understanding;
- Rudolph Weingartner on the logical form and selected objects of cultural/intellectual history;
- methodological work on explanation, reasons and narrative;
- David Hollinger on Perry Miller and philosophical history;
- Romila Thapar on historiographic interpretations of ancient India and the rejection of static/homogeneous periodizations;
- Jürgen Herbst's survey of theoretical work in American university history curricula;
- review essays crossing intellectual history, social science, Momigliano, Hempel, Walzer and others;
- Beiheft 8, `On Method in the History of Religions`, with explicit debate over evidence, object categories, phenomenology/history and anthropological explanation.

Thus immediately before Skinner 1969:

`METHOD / THEORY OF HISTORY`
was already distributed across
`JOURNAL ARTICLES + REVIEWS + CURRICULUM + GLOBAL HISTORIOGRAPHY + RELIGION METHOD + THEMATIC SUPPLEMENT`.

The safest conclusion is:

> **Skinner entered a methodologically crowded and institutionally plural field, not an empty pre-method landscape.**

This does not diminish his argument's distinctiveness.

`PRE-EXISTING METHOD ECOLOGY`
!= `SKINNER'S ARGUMENT ALREADY EXISTED`.

## I. Mink: historical understanding as question-specific and presupposition-sensitive

Louis O. Mink, `Collingwood's Dialectic of History`, H&T 7.1 (1968), 3–37.

The official H&T summary emphasizes:

- history as knowledge of mind;
- the logic of question and answer;
- `thought` as including feelings, desires, perceptions and imagination;
- historical answers as responses to specific questions reflecting sets of presuppositions;
- history as re-enactment rather than simple representation.

This is important as a pre-Skinner control because it shows historically specific understanding and question-structure already being discussed at high methodological level.

Do not equate Collingwood/Mink with Skinnerian speech-act contextualism.

`RELATED PROBLEM FIELD`
!= `IDENTICAL METHOD`.

---

## II. Weingartner: intellectual history already raises UNIT and composition problems

Rudolph H. Weingartner, `Some Philosophic Comments on Cultural History`, H&T 7.1 (1968), 38–59.

The official summary directly treats the subject matter of typical intellectual history as **selected features of products of human activities**, rather than simply individuals/actions.

It also describes historians tracing relations among components that compose later products even where causal knowledge remains obscure.

This is striking for the repo's UNIT×RELATION architecture:

`INTELLECTUAL-HISTORICAL UNIT`
can be
`SELECTED FEATURES / PRODUCTS / COMPONENT RELATIONS`,
not only
`INDIVIDUAL AUTHOR UTTERANCE`.

And:

`COMPOSITION RELATION`
!= `CAUSAL RELATION`.

This is another pre-1969 route into relation differentiation.

Do not infer Weingartner caused or anticipated Skinner.

---

## III. Dietl and Arthur: explanation remains plural and contested

Paul J. Dietl, `Deduction and Historical Explanation`, H&T 7.2 (1968), 167–188, rejects strict deduction/high expectability as necessary conditions for historical explanation and emphasizes reasons, emotions and motives.

C. J. Arthur, `On the Historical Understanding`, 203–216, critiques overly direct narrative understanding by stressing the generalized expectations required to judge contingencies.

These articles show that:

`WHAT COUNTS AS HISTORICAL EXPLANATION`
was an active, unresolved problem before Skinner.

The field already contained competing models involving:

- deduction;
- reasons;
- motives;
- narrative;
- generalization;
- moral evaluation.

This makes `contextualism` one later intervention in a larger explanatory ecology rather than the moment explanation first became theoretically self-conscious.

---

## IV. Hollinger: intellectual history as interaction, not pure internalism

David A. Hollinger, `Perry Miller and Philosophical History`, H&T 7.2 (1968), 189–202.

The official summary reconstructs Miller's history as an interaction between inherited cultural forms and immediate environmental circumstances.

Culture is neither simply produced by environment nor wholly autonomous.

This provides another direct pre-1969 control:

`INTERNAL INTELLECTUAL FORM`
<-> `ENVIRONMENTAL / HISTORICAL CIRCUMSTANCE`.

The relation is interactive rather than one-way reduction.

Again:

`PRE-SKINNER CONTEXTUAL PRESSURE`
!= `SKINNERIAN METHOD`.

---

## V. Thapar: global historiography and inherited periodization are method objects

Romila Thapar, `Interpretations of Ancient Indian History`, H&T 7.3 (1968), 318–335.

The official H&T summary says Thapar reconstructs and criticizes:

- Orientalist depictions of ancient India as other-worldly and unchanging;
- reliance on Sanskritic tradition;
- James Mill's Hindu / Muslim / British periodization;
- nationalist historiography's acceptance/glorification of Sanskritic and political-dynastic frameworks.

She emphasizes newer concerns with:

- social history;
- economic organization;
- local history;
- precise social relations;
- political power;
- rejection of static/homogeneous India and inherited periodization.

This makes **the historiographic construction of a non-Western historical object** part of H&T's ordinary method ecology before Skinner.

Thus:

`GLOBAL / NON-WESTERN HISTORIOGRAPHY`
was not simply a later addition to an otherwise Western methodological field.

But one article does not establish symmetrical global representation in H&T.

## VI. Herbst 1968: theoretical work already had broad teaching infrastructure

Jürgen Herbst, `Theoretical Work in History in American University Curricula`, H&T 7.3 (1968), 336–354.

JSTOR directly controls the article metadata; the official H&T summary reports a survey finding that:

- theoretical courses existed in nearly 90 percent of universities and colleges in the survey;
- among schools offering such courses, about 90 percent located them in history departments;
- about 40 percent also had such courses in philosophy departments;
- theoretical work was concentrated especially in graduate departments at larger public universities;
- more than half of the courses offered mixtures of historiography, philosophy and methodology;
- eclectic courses were the most favored;
- history-department support was high, though some complained about vagueness of subject matter;
- theoretical theses remained uncommon.

The critical institutional implication is:

> **By 1968, theoretical reflection on history was not only a journal specialty; it had broad curricular presence inside history departments, often in deliberately mixed or eclectic form.**

Therefore:

`METHOD JURISDICTION`
pre-dates Skinner 1969 at
`TEACHING / CURRICULAR INFRASTRUCTURE` level as well as journal level.

Do not turn the survey into a claim about uniform quality, content or student uptake.

## VII. Beiheft 8: history of religions makes category construction explicit

The same annual volume includes `On Method in the History of Religions`.

Companion dossier:

`research_notes/History_and_Theory_1968_history_of_religions_method_categories_origin_object_bridge_to_Lovejoy_2026-08-31.md`.

Direct official summaries show:

- Morton Smith separating individual historical reconstruction from claims about general origin/nature;
- Baird attacking inherited named-religion categories as pre-imposed interpretive units;
- Neusner treating Judaism as a composite multi-field/area-study object;
- Penner differentiating anthropological functional explanation from history-of-religions understanding/symbolic relations.

Thus Volume 7 ends with an explicit thematic method forum about constructing alien/religious historical objects.

This is directly relevant to the Lovejoy corpus because it shows category/unit/relation problems becoming formal history-of-religions method decades later, while blocking any originality/genealogy claim.

## VIII. Same-year ecology matters more than a single precursor list

The strongest historical observation is not that one 1968 author `anticipated` Skinner.

It is that one venue/year simultaneously contained:

- Collingwoodian question/answer and presuppositions;
- component/product relations in intellectual history;
- reasons/motives versus deductive explanation;
- narrative/generalization debate;
- cultural-form/environment interaction;
- non-Western historiographic category critique;
- empirical curricular mapping of theory/method instruction;
- history-of-religions category and evidence disputes.

This is an **ecology**, not a precursor chain.

Compact rule:

`MANY PRIOR RELATED PROBLEMS`
!= `ONE LINEAR GENEALOGY TO SKINNER`.

But it also means:

`SKINNER 1969`
should not be narrated as
`METHOD SELF-CONSCIOUSNESS ENTERS HISTORY FOR THE FIRST TIME`.

## IX. Relation to the four-venue model

The four-venue ecology can now be widened from publication form to educational infrastructure.

### APSR

Broad disciplinary carrier with uneven political-philosophy article visibility and internal theory participation.

### JHI

Ordinary research/review forms carrying diverse intellectual-history and methodological relations.

### H&T

Dedicated metahistorical carrier plus evidence that method/theory is broadly taught across history curricula; method infrastructure exists simultaneously in journals and teaching.

### Political Theory

Specialist political-philosophy carrier deliberately engineering multi-role controversy and additional publication capacity.

Thus differentiation occurs across:

`JOURNALS`
+
`THEMATIC ISSUES`
+
`REVIEWS`
+
`CURRICULA`
+
`PROFESSIONAL SOCIETIES`
+
`SPECIALIST VENUE FORMATION`.

The broader formula becomes:

> **METHOD CHANGE = ARGUMENT + CARRIER + CURRICULUM + NETWORK + CANON, with none reducible to the others.**
