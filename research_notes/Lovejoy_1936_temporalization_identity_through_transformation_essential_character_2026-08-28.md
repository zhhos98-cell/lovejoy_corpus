# Lovejoy 1936 — temporalization: identity through transformation and the `essential character` test

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **DIRECT MATURE PRACTICE / GATE-A TRANSFORMATION CONTROL / IDENTITY-DESTROYING THRESHOLD STILL OPEN**

Primary: Arthur O. Lovejoy, *The Great Chain of Being* (1936), especially Lecture IX (`The Temporalizing of the Chain of Being`) and Lecture X (`Romanticism and the Principle of Plenitude`) / final outcome discussion.

## Core result

Lovejoy's own canonical history contains a direct case in which a unit/idea-complex undergoes a profound transformation while he explicitly judges that its **essential character remains**.

The change is not minor:

`static / permanently complete plenum`
→ `temporal program / becoming`.

Yet Lovejoy continues to recognize the transformed structure as the Chain / Scale / plenitude tradition.

His own historical language therefore supplies an actor-level transformation test:

> **historical identity can survive a reversal in temporal mode and genetic direction when Lovejoy judges that a thinner generative/fullness structure remains invariant.**

This makes `invariant thickness` a direct issue in his mature practice rather than a purely analyst-imposed concern.

---

## I. Inventory → program

Lovejoy's famous formulation of the eighteenth-century transformation is that the `plenum formarum` came to be conceived by some thinkers:

- not as the **inventory** of nature;
- but as its **program**;
- a program carried out gradually and exceedingly slowly in cosmic history.

The transformed version still says that all possibilities demand realization, but they need not all receive actuality simultaneously.

Instead:

- some possibilities have been realized in the past and disappeared;
- some are realized now;
- others are destined for future realization.

Thus plenitude changes from:

`∀ genuine possibilities: actual now / static totality`

toward something like:

`∀ genuine possibilities: actual at some point in total cosmic history`.

The temporal quantifier changes profoundly while the maximal-realization demand remains.

---

## II. What remains invariant?

Lovejoy's mature discussion of Schelling is unusually explicit.

The traditional Chain is inverted:

- the complete immutable hierarchy becomes Becoming;
- realization proceeds `grade after grade` through time;
- God himself can be placed in / identified with the process;
- the World of Ideas becomes a realm of unrealized possibility awaiting actualization;
- the direction changes from a metaphysical `way down` from perfection to imperfection into a `way up` from lower to fuller being.

Despite this inversion Lovejoy says that converting the Scale of Being into an abstract ideal schema **does not alter its essential character**.

He then identifies the surviving elements:

- insatiable generativeness;
- tendency to produce diversity;
- necessity of realization of the greatest possible fullness of being.

What changes is the status of fullness:

`permanent character of reality`
→ `flying goal of the world-process`.

This is direct evidence of Lovejoy deciding which features are **identity-bearing** and which may vary.

---

## III. The practical identity criterion is thinner than temporal mode

Lovejoy's judgment implies that at least in this case the following are **not** identity-destroying:

- static vs dynamic ontology;
- simultaneous vs diachronic realization;
- actual completeness vs goal-directed incompleteness;
- descending emanation vs ascending development;
- even the placement/status of God relative to becoming.

The invariant is thinner:

> **a generative tendency toward maximal diversity/fullness through realization of possibilities.**

This is a powerful mature example of `different at thick doctrinal scale / same at thin structural scale`.

---

## IV. Consequence: proposition + corollary profile is not a sufficient universal account of 1936 identity

Earlier branch work reconstructed one practical 1936 identity technology as:

`specific proposition/principle + associated corollary/inferential profile`.

The temporalization case shows that this must be qualified.

A unit can remain historically identifiable even when:

- temporal consequences change;
- metaphysical rationale changes;
- direction of derivation changes;
- associated theology changes;
- some inferential consequences are reversed or newly developed.

Therefore:

> **Lovejoy does not require a stable full inferential profile for cross-context identity.**

At most, a selected germinal principle/core plus some characteristic family of consequences may survive while other corollaries and rationales change.

This increases the importance of the historian's choice of abstraction level.

---

## V. `Essential character` is an actor-level Gate-A judgment

The phrase matters because it is not simply our reconstruction.

Lovejoy himself distinguishes:

- a transformed historical configuration;
- the features changed by the transformation;
- the features whose persistence lets him assert essential continuity.

The Gate-A problem can therefore be phrased in actor-near language:

> **What does Lovejoy count as belonging to the `essential character` of an idea/unit, and what does he relegate to its historically variable form, rationale, direction, accompaniments, or applications?**

This may be more precise than asking whether he believed ideas were `fixed` or `changeable`.

---

## VI. The temporalization case creates a direct Skinner-facing pressure point

The strongest contextualist objection is no longer that Lovejoy fails to notice change. He makes radical change central to the narrative.

The pressure point is instead the classification of change:

`which changed features are constitutive of historical meaning?`

Lovejoy treats static/dynamic status and simultaneous/diachronic realization as compatible with persistence of an essential plenitude structure.

A contextualist may argue that these differences alter:

- what claim an actor is making;
- what problem the principle addresses;
- what consequences are available;
- its relationship to theology, nature, progress and agency;
- the practical/illocutionary work performed by the doctrine.

Thus the dispute becomes an argument about **invariant thickness**, not about the existence of contextual transformation.

---

## VII. Identity can survive changed logical relations

The final lecture intensifies the problem.

Lovejoy retrospectively says that continuity had been treated as implied by plenitude; yet when the implications are fully drawn, plenitude and qualitative continuity prove to be at variance.

Thus the historical `same` principles persist through a history in which their perceived logical relationship changes:

`B thought to follow from A`
→ later analysis
→ `A and B conflict`.

Therefore:

> **stable logical relations among a unit's corollaries are not required for Lovejoy to continue identifying the historical principle.**

The object being tracked can survive discovery that its historical articulation was internally unstable.

This is a crucial correction to any formal-logical reading of unit identity.

---

## VIII. A provisional transformation matrix

| Feature | Static Chain | Temporalized Chain | Lovejoy identity judgment |
|---|---|---|---|
| realization of possibilities | simultaneous / complete | distributed through cosmic time | variable |
| fullness | permanent actuality | future/flying goal | variable |
| temporal status | static | becoming/process | variable |
| genetic direction | top-down emanation | bottom-up ascent | variable |
| divine status | perfect source outside/above process | can itself enter becoming | variable |
| generativeness | yes | yes | **invariant** |
| production of diversity | yes | yes | **invariant** |
| maximal fullness demand | yes | yes, as program/goal | **invariant** |
| label / historical complex | Chain / Scale / plenitude family | temporalized Chain / Scale | persistence claimed |

This is an analyst matrix based on Lovejoy's actor-level judgment.

---

## IX. What would destroy identity for Lovejoy?

The temporalization case tells us what he permits, but not yet the negative threshold.

Potential destruction candidates include:

- introduction of a selective principle that rejects maximal realization;
- abandonment of generativeness/diversity as value or necessity;
- treating gaps/limitations as positive and fundamental rather than defects;
- rejection of the possibility→realization pressure altogether.

Whitehead's `principle of limitation` at the end of the book may be a useful negative control because Lovejoy presents it as a reversal of the traditional principle of fecundity/fullness.

This should be tested directly rather than assumed.

---

## X. Revised Gate-A model

The temporalization case suggests:

`historical occurrence`
→ identify thick doctrinal configuration
→ strip away selected historical variables
→ isolate thin `essential character`
→ recognize recurrence/persistence
→ separately trace changes in rationale, temporalization, application and consequences.

The critical hinge is the strip-down step.

The historian must justify why removed features are nonessential to the identity relation being asserted.

---

## Current shorthand

> **Lovejoy's own Great Chain narrative provides a direct test of identity through transformation: the Chain can be inverted from static inventory into temporal program without, in his words, altering its `essential character`, because generativeness, diversity-production and the drive toward maximal fullness survive. The core problem is therefore which features Lovejoy permits the historian to thin away.**
