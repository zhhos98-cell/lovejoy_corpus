# Batch 86 — Jung 1928: from proof-bearing appropriation to epistemic re-audit of Lovejoy's `primitive philosophy`

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced with edition-state caution
Scope: continue Batches 82 and 85 from Jung's 1917 revision-layer reception into the essay `Über die Energetik der Seele` / `On Psychical Energy`, first published in 1928. The question is whether Jung continues merely to use Lovejoy as authority or begins to decompose Lovejoy's propositions and evidence.

## Core result

Jung's later use of Lovejoy is substantially more discriminating than the 1917 revision-layer citation.

In the 1917 enlarged `Psychology of the Unconscious Processes`, Lovejoy functions principally as a **historical warrant**. Jung proposes that the modern idea of energy corresponds to a primordial image, explicitly says this stronger psychological-genealogical proposition requires historical proof, and then inserts the comparative field of `dynamistic religions` and Lovejoy's `primitive energetics` as proof-bearing evidence.

In `On Psychical Energy`, first published in 1928, Jung returns to the ethnographic source field itself. The preserved original-version text does three separable things with Lovejoy:

1. **uses Lovejoy as a source-routing apparatus** for McGee and the North-American `wakonda` packet;
2. **accepts Lovejoy's substantive semantic/causal characterization** of mana-family terms as efficacy, power and productivity, including the claim that the force is conceived as regular enough to be manipulated;
3. **rejects Lovejoy's epistemic representation of the native datum as an abstract `concept of primitive philosophy`.** Jung says the `concept` is the modern scholar's abstraction; for the people being described, mana is a representation/psychic phenomenon perceived as inseparable from the object.

The reception relation therefore changes form:

> **1917: Lovejoy as synthesized historical warrant.**
>
> **1928: Lovejoy as decomposable evidentiary source — accepted for one proposition, corrected for another.**

This is a highly consequential result for the project's broader argument because Jung now performs on Lovejoy the same kind of **proposition-relative source grading** that Lovejoy himself performs on ethnographic authorities in 1906.

The strongest concise formulation is:

> **Jung accepts Lovejoy's evidence for efficacy while rejecting Lovejoy's abstraction of that evidence into an indigenous concept.**

Or:

> **reception becomes source criticism.**

## 2. Jung now enters Lovejoy's source packet below the level of the theory

The 1928 essay's section `The Primitive Conception of Libido` begins by arguing that early religious symbol formation is intimately connected with magical potency understood both as objective force and subjective intensity.

Jung then works through specific ethnographic terms and sources:

- McGee on Dakota `wakonda`;
- Iroquois `oki`;
- Algonquin `manitu`;
- Hetherwick on Yao `mulungu`;
- Gold Coast `wong`;
- Australian `churinga`, Torres Strait `zogo`, and related power terms;
- Melanesian `mana` through Codrington;
- further regional parallels and Söderblom's survey.

This matters because the reception function is no longer merely `Lovejoy supplies a label`. Jung is now rebuilding the evidentiary field around the label.

One footnote explicitly couples McGee's *Siouan Indians* p.182 with Lovejoy, `Fundamental Concept`, p.363. Lovejoy therefore functions partly as a **bibliographic and semantic relay back to the primary ethnographic authority**.

A second Lovejoy citation follows the summary of `wakonda` as diffused, pervasive, manipulable and transferable life-energy.

The source relation has become granular:

`Lovejoy article`
→ `specific ethnographic authority / page`
→ `specific semantic proposition`.

That is a different reception form from 1917.

---

## 3. Jung explicitly accepts Lovejoy's substantive causal diagnosis

After assembling the comparative packet, Jung states that he is in full agreement with Lovejoy's distinction between two competing interpretations.

The power terms are not primarily names for:

- the merely unusual;
- the `supernormal`;
- that which evokes awe/respect.

Their center is instead:

- efficacy;
- power;
- productivity;
- a diffused force/energy correlated with exceptional capacity and fertility.

Jung also reproduces Lovejoy's stronger causal characterization: the pre-animistic principle concerns a force conceived as sufficiently regular and intelligible to be studied and controlled. He records Lovejoy's proposed name `primitive energetics`.

### Proposition code

- existence of non-personal efficacy/power field: **ACCEPT**;
- semantic center = efficacious/powerful/productive: **ACCEPT**;
- regular/manipulable energetic relation: **ACCEPT / REUSE**;
- `primitive energetics` as useful comparative designation: **REUSE**.

This is considerably stronger uptake than Jung's 1917 naming citation because Jung now walks through the supporting evidence and expressly aligns himself with the causal-semantic claim.

---

## 4. The decisive turn: Jung says Lovejoy's `concept` is our abstraction, not theirs

Immediately after accepting Lovejoy's substantive characterization, Jung changes level.

He objects that, strictly speaking, it is incorrect to call the native datum a **concept**. He identifies Lovejoy's phrase/title — `a concept of primitive philosophy` — as an abstraction produced by **our own mentality**.

Jung's alternative is epistemological and psychological:

- for the analyst, `mana` can be abstracted into a psychological energy-concept;
- for the people whose usage is being described, it is not an abstract conceptual object;
- it is a representation / psychic phenomenon experienced as inseparable from the object or phenomenal relationship.

He connects this to Lévy-Bruhl's `participation mystique`.

This distinction is not a rejection of Lovejoy's ethnographic evidence. It is a rejection of what Lovejoy claims the evidence exists **as** in native cognition.

Formula:

`native lexical/use pattern`
→ Lovejoy: supports an abstract causal **concept/category**

but

`native lexical/use pattern`
→ Jung: supports a **representation / phenomenal relation** that the modern analyst can abstract into a concept.

The disagreement is therefore at the level of **epistemic status**, not at the level of the empirical power pattern.

---

## 5. This directly mirrors Lovejoy's own proposition-relative source criticism

Batches 77–78 reconstructed Lovejoy 1906 as refusing to assign global reliability to an ethnographic source.

Examples included:

- an old observer can report external custom while misclassifying its ontology;
- a missionary can preserve lexical application while mistranslating the native term;
- McGee can be clear and trustworthy for distributional usage while failing to separate semantic core from connotation;
- grammar/native explanation can support a stronger proposition than observer gloss.

Jung's treatment of Lovejoy has the same formal architecture:

### Lovejoy as source for empirical/semantic field

**ADMISSIBLE.**

The efficacy/power/productivity pattern and its cross-regional evidence remain useful.

### Lovejoy as interpreter of native cognitive form

**CORRECTED.**

The move from recurring native use to `concept of primitive philosophy` overstates the abstract/conceptual status of the native representation.

The source is therefore not simply accepted or rejected.

> **Lovejoy becomes proposition-relative evidence for Jung.**

This is not evidence that Jung consciously borrowed Lovejoy's source-critical method. It is a structural reception finding.

---

## 6. The reception trajectory is now three-stage rather than one-stage

### 1906 — Lovejoy produces the object

Ethnographic words/grammar/customs are reorganized into a comparative causal category: `primitive energetics`.

### 1917 — Jung uses the synthesized object as historical proof

`Primitive energetics` becomes evidence that a universal magical-energy image existed historically, supporting Jung's primordial-image / collective-unconscious genealogy.

### 1928 — Jung reopens the object

He goes back into the ethnographic packet, uses specific Lovejoy citations and source relays, accepts Lovejoy's efficacy diagnosis, but disputes the epistemic step by which the modern analyst's abstraction is redescribed as a native `concept`.

Thus:

> **appropriation → evidentiary unpacking → epistemic re-audit.**

The Lovejoy object does not simply become more entrenched in Jung. It becomes more differentiated.

---

## 7. A striking reversal of the 1906 `vera causa` operation

Lovejoy 1906 strengthened his causal theory by moving from ambiguous customs to native vocabulary and grammar:

`custom inference`
→ `native linguistic existence-control`
→ `causal category established as vera causa`.

Jung accepts the linguistic/customary cluster but raises a new question:

> does this evidence establish that the indigenous actor possessed **the abstract concept** that the comparative theorist has formulated?

His answer is effectively no.

Therefore a piece of evidence that Lovejoy had used to strengthen ontological/categorical confidence becomes, in Jung, the basis for distinguishing:

- **empirical representation**;
- **analyst's abstraction**.

This is a second-order critique of the inferential step from evidence to category.

It does not invalidate Lovejoy's `vera causa` argument wholesale. It narrows what the independent native evidence is permitted to prove.

The project's four-level language can describe the result:

`native use attested`
→ YES

`efficacy relation recoverable`
→ YES

`modern comparative abstraction useful`
→ YES

`abstract concept exists for native actor in the same form`
→ **NO / overlicensed**.

This is one of the cleanest external stress-tests yet found for Lovejoy's own evidentiary architecture.

---

## 8. Relation to Leuba: two readers attack different higher propositions while retaining the lower one

Leuba 1912 and Jung 1928 now make a very useful paired reception control.

### Leuba

Accepts the existence of non-personal power but contests Lovejoy's stronger claim of mechanical/regular causality.

### Jung

Accepts efficacy/power and even Lovejoy's regular/manipulable characterization, but contests the stronger epistemic claim that the comparative abstraction corresponds to a native abstract `concept`.

So two readers preserve different parts of Lovejoy while blocking different upward inferences:

`power exists`
→ Leuba blocks `therefore mechanically regular`.

`power-pattern exists`
→ Jung blocks `therefore native abstract concept/category`.

This is exactly why reception cannot be coded as simple assent/dissent.

## 9. Historiographic payoff

A strong article sentence is now possible:

> **Jung's reception of `primitive energetics` moved from appropriation to audit. In the enlarged 1917 essay Lovejoy supplied historical warrant for a primordial energy-image; in `On Psychical Energy` Jung returned to the underlying ethnographic packet, accepted Lovejoy's description of mana-like terms as efficacious, powerful and productive, but rejected the title's epistemic implication that this amounted, for indigenous actors themselves, to an abstract `concept of primitive philosophy`. Lovejoy's evidence survived; the license assigned to it was narrowed.**

A compressed formulation:

> **Jung did to Lovejoy what Lovejoy had done to his ethnographers: he retained the datum and revised the interpretation.**

Use the compressed sentence only with the explicit caution that this is a structural analogy, not a demonstrated line of methodological borrowing.
