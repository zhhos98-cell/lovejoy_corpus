## Core result

The December 1910 first installment of `Kant and Evolution` provides an unusually explicit endpoint for the classificatory operations reconstructed from notebooks 004/005.

Lovejoy attacks the retrospective habit of classifying Kant wholesale as an `evolutionist` or `precursor of Darwin`. His corrective procedure has four parts:

1. examine the relevant writings in **approximate chronological order**;
2. restore disputed expressions to their **contexts**;
3. respect the **meanings Kant gives particular terms**;
4. decompose the umbrella label `evolution` into distinct propositions and relations before assigning any historical identity.

The resulting verdict is structurally striking. The same Kant can be:

- a **cosmic evolutionist**;
- disposed toward **anthropological evolutionism**;
- yet not a **general biological evolutionist / transformist**;
- and consistently resistant to a mechanistic account of organic generation.

Lovejoy states the decisive relation explicitly: there is **no necessary logical connection** between mechanistic physiology and transformism, just as vitalism does not logically entail fixity of species. One classificatory dimension therefore cannot be used as a proxy for another.

At the end of the 1910 installment he sharpens the point into a clean historical predicate split:

> Kant could remain what Lovejoy calls an `anthropological evolutionist` while deliberately refusing the transition to `general biological evolutionism`.

This is strong evidence for a project-level claim already emerging from 004/005:

> **a historical actor, text or system does not possess one synchronized intellectual `age`; predicates such as `evolutionary`, `primitive`, `original`, `derived`, `moral`, or `mechanistic` must be assigned relation by relation.**

The 1910 text supplies an explicit published instance of the same anti-synchronizing logic.

## 1. Lovejoy begins by attacking a retrospective category

The article opens by calling Kant-as-evolutionary-pioneer one of the generally accepted **`legends of the history of science`**.

Lovejoy reconstructs the claim from several historians who treat scattered Kantian formulations as evidence that Kant had already anticipated Darwinian descent, struggle, selection, or a modern evolutionary worldview.

His response is not simply that Kant was `not an evolutionist`.

Instead he immediately partitions the question:

- mutability/descent of species;
- spontaneous generation / organic origin;
- mechanistic explanation of life;
- cosmic evolution;
- later anthropological change.

The historical error lies in turning resemblance on one dimension into a global actor label.

### Relation code

`GLOBAL HISTORICAL LABEL → DECOMPOSE INTO PROPOSITION-SPECIFIC PREDICATES`.

---

## 2. Approximate chronological order is itself part of the proof procedure

At the end of his opening verdict Lovejoy says that, because contrary beliefs are so widespread, the matter can be substantiated only by examining all the more important relevant writings of Kant in their **`approximate chronological order`**.

That phrase matters methodologically.

Chronology is not a final ladder onto which Kant is placed. It is a control for reconstructing **changes and non-changes among specific propositions**.

Lovejoy asks, separately:

- when does Kant accept/reject a mechanism?
- when does he distinguish one theory from another?
- whether apparently similar statements perform the same conceptual job at different dates;
- whether later interpreters have collapsed temporally dispersed utterances into one modern category.

Thus chronology functions as a **diagnostic coordinate**, not as an actor-level stage label.

This closely matches the repository's `chronological non-isomorphism` result from notebooks 004/005: multiple relations can carry different temporal positions inside one object.

---

## 3. Context and term-meaning block retrospective synchronization

Lovejoy then gives an explicit diagnosis of how the Kant legend arose.

He says the misunderstandings are understandable results of:

- taking certain expressions **apart from their contexts**;
- disregarding the **meanings Kant was accustomed to give certain terms**.

This is a very strong published methodological statement because it identifies two separate controls:

### A. contextual position

A proposition cannot be extracted from the argumentative relation in which it occurs and reassigned a later category merely by verbal resemblance.

### B. semantic position

A recurring word cannot be treated as carrying a stable modern meaning across authors or periods.

Together:

> `same expression / same word` ≠ `same proposition / same historical category`.

This is directly continuous with the operations already documented in 004 and 005, where lexical or formal similarity is repeatedly subordinated to relation, mechanism, provenance and logical role.

## 4. `Evolution` fractures into different historical predicates

The article's most important substantive operation is the decomposition of `evolution` itself.

### 4.1 Cosmic evolution

Kant's 1755 `Universal Natural History` does propose a natural history of cosmic formation. Lovejoy is perfectly willing to call this **cosmic evolutionism**.

But he then refuses the familiar identification of Kant's cosmogony with Laplace's nebular hypothesis. Structural similarity is insufficient; Lovejoy reconstructs the actual mechanism and historical antecedents, placing Kant closer in important respects to other models.

### 4.2 Mechanism in the organic realm

Lovejoy next asks whether cosmic mechanical evolution licenses a mechanistic explanation of organisms.

His answer is no. Kant accepts mechanical explanation for cosmic formation while withholding it from the generation of organic beings.

Thus:

> `mechanistic cosmic evolution` ≠ `mechanistic biology`.

### 4.3 Transformism / mutability of species

Lovejoy then separates biological mechanism from descent theory.

He explicitly says there is **no necessary logical connection**, though there may be a natural affinity, between mechanistic physiology and transformism; equally, vitalistic physiology does not logically entail fixity of species.

Therefore:

> `mechanism/vitalism axis` ≠ `transformism/fixity axis`.

This is exactly the kind of axis non-equivalence that notebook 004 already makes explicit when something can be temporally antecedent while logically a subdivision.

## 5. Lovejoy reconstructs Kant as internally asynchronous

The Moscati review gives the strongest 1910 endpoint.

Lovejoy quotes Kant accepting a historical anthropology in which humanity's earlier posture is quadrupedal / animal, while the development of reason and sociality produces a later upright condition suited to society.

Lovejoy says this certainly shows a **predisposition to evolutionary ways of thinking** and could tempt a reader to infer general species mutability.

He then blocks the inference.

The doctrine of general transformism is neither expressed nor directly implied in the Moscati review, and later evidence shows Kant would not have accepted the transition.

Lovejoy's final sentence of the 1910 installment is exceptionally useful:

> from the Moscati review onward Kant remained what may be called an **`anthropological evolutionist`**, while he **deliberately refused** the transition to **general biological evolutionism**.

This means one actor can occupy different `evolutionary` statuses simultaneously depending on the proposition tested.

### Analytical representation

`Kant`

- cosmic-history axis → evolutionary;
- human/social-development axis → evolutionary;
- transformism axis → not securely evolutionary;
- organic-mechanism axis → anti-mechanistic / vitalist.

There is no single scalar coordinate `Kant = stage X of evolutionism` that preserves these relations.

## 6. The most important methodological fact: Lovejoy publicly corrects his own 1909 classification

Footnote 4 contains a rare direct self-correction.

Lovejoy says that **he himself**, before coming `to close quarters with the subject`, had fallen into the error of classifying Kant among the early evolutionists in a November 1909 *Popular Science Monthly* article.

This is highly diagnostic for the research project.

The sequence is:

> `1909 broad historical category assignment`  
> → closer source-level investigation  
> → category decomposition  
> → `1910 relation-specific reassignment`.

This is not merely a generic statement that scholars can change their minds. It documents Lovejoy altering a historical classification because closer primary-source control showed that the umbrella category had fused distinct propositions.

It gives a published analogue for what the notebooks repeatedly do at smaller scale:

- provisional classification;
- encounter with contradictory / borderline evidence;
- split the category;
- redistribute the case across more precise relations.

### Strong formulation

> **Lovejoy's own 1909→1910 correction shows classification as a revisable output of source decomposition rather than a stable input imposed on the archive.**

## 9. Importance for the larger `primitive = stratification` thesis

This 1910 endpoint clarifies why the project's larger chronology thesis should not be reduced to a new linear evolutionary ladder.

Once a category such as `evolutionary` can be assigned separately to:

- cosmic history;
- social/human development;
- organic descent;
- causal mechanism;

then a thinker/tradition does not occupy one homogeneous historical position.

The same architecture is visible with `primitive` in 005/1906:

- a relation can be primitive;
- one intratraditional layer can be primitive;
- one mechanism can survive beneath later structures;
- equivalent primitive relations can then be compared transversely across otherwise heterogeneous traditions.

Thus the key historical operation is not simply ranking whole objects. It is **predicate stratification**:

> historical predicates attach to selected relations/components rather than necessarily to the whole actor, society, religion or system.

`Predicate stratification` is a project analytical term, not Lovejoy's vocabulary.

This may be the most compact technical bridge from 004/005 to the 1910 cutoff.

## 12. Publication-level formulation

A strong, source-safe formulation is:

> **By December 1910 Lovejoy was explicitly treating broad historical classifications as proposition-relative. In `Kant and Evolution` he argued that the familiar picture of Kant as a Darwinian precursor resulted from removing expressions from their contexts and ignoring the meanings Kant assigned to key terms. He then separated cosmic evolution, mechanistic biology, transformism and anthropological development into distinct axes, concluding that Kant could be an `anthropological evolutionist` while refusing general biological evolutionism. The methodological point was personal as well as polemical: Lovejoy acknowledged that he himself had classified Kant among the early evolutionists in 1909 before examining the problem more closely. The episode turns historical classification into a revisable consequence of relation-specific source analysis.**

For the larger paper:

> **The notebooks' layered chronology reaches a clear public analogue by the 1910 cutoff: historical predicates no longer need to synchronize at the level of the whole actor or system.**
