# Lovejoy 1909→1910 — Kant as a direct `category fission` self-revision (Batch 218)

Date: 2026-08-26  
Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / BEFORE-AFTER CONTROL**  
Scope: Lovejoy, `The Argument for Organic Evolution Before the Origin of Species`, Part I, *Popular Science Monthly* 75 (November 1909), esp. p.513; and `Kant and Evolution`, Part I, *Popular Science Monthly* 77 (December 1910), 538–553. No 1911 evidence used.

## Core result

Batch217 established that Lovejoy's December 1910 `Kant and Evolution` decomposes the umbrella predicate `evolutionist` into distinct relations and publicly acknowledges that Lovejoy himself had previously misclassified Kant before coming `to close quarters with the subject`.

Batch218 retrieves the exact 1909 statement Lovejoy was correcting.

In the November 1909 article, while reconstructing pre-Darwinian arguments from vertebrate homology, Lovejoy writes that these facts had before the end of the eighteenth century **made evolutionists of Diderot, of Kant**, and nearly of Buffon.

One year later he explicitly retracts the global classification and replaces it with a relation-specific verdict:

- Kant can be a cosmic evolutionist;
- Kant can remain an `anthropological evolutionist`;
- neither fact licenses the claim that he accepted general biological transformism;
- mechanistic explanation of cosmic formation does not license mechanistic biology.

This is therefore a directly observable **category-fission event** in Lovejoy's own scholarship:

> `1909 GLOBAL LABEL: Kant = evolutionist`  
> → `closer source investigation`  
> → `1910 CATEGORY FISSION`  
> → `anthropological evolutionism ≠ biological transformism ≠ organic mechanism ≠ cosmic evolution`.

`Category fission` is a project analytical term.

---

## 1. The exact 1909 classification

Lovejoy's 1909 paper asks when the evidence for organic evolution became logically sufficient before Darwin and treats the problem as an object lesson in the logic of scientific reasoning.

In the section on vertebrate homologies he argues that the relevant anatomical facts were old. He then states that these facts had already, before the eighteenth century ended, **made evolutionists of Diderot, Kant, and effectively Buffon**.

The sentence is not a passing bibliographic listing. Kant is used as evidence for Lovejoy's larger 1909 contention that the evidentiary basis for organic evolution existed well before Darwin.

Thus the 1909 proposition is genuinely:

> `KANT → EARLY EVOLUTIONIST`.

There is not yet an explicit separation in that sentence between anthropological evolution, cosmic evolution and species transformism.

---

## 2. The 1910 footnote identifies this exact statement as Lovejoy's own error

In `Kant and Evolution` Part I, Lovejoy surveys historians who have over-read Kant as a Darwinian precursor.

Footnote 4 then says, in substance, that **he himself**, before coming to close quarters with the subject, had fallen into the error of classifying Kant among the early evolutionists, citing:

> *Popular Science Monthly*, November 1909, p.513.

That reference points precisely to the 1909 homology passage recovered above.

This is unusually clean evidence because the correction edge is author-supplied:

`Lovejoy 1910`  
→ explicitly identifies  
`Lovejoy 1909 p.513`  
→ as his earlier classificatory error.

No retrospective biographer or project inference is needed to establish the self-revision.

---

## 3. The error is corrected by splitting the predicate, not merely reversing it

Lovejoy does **not** move simply from:

`Kant = evolutionist`

to:

`Kant = not evolutionist`.

That would preserve the same binary ontology.

Instead the 1910 article asks what precise kind of historical proposition each Kantian text licenses.

The result is a decomposition:

| Relation / domain | 1910 verdict |
|---|---|
| cosmic formation/history | evolutionary in an important sense |
| anthropology / human social-developmental history | `anthropological evolutionist` |
| general mutability / transformation of biological species | not licensed; Kant refuses the transition |
| spontaneous generation of organic life | rejected |
| mechanistic explanation of organic generation | rejected / vitalist position |

The correction therefore operates through **predicate multiplication**.

The historical actor is no longer assigned one value on one evolution/non-evolution axis.

---

## 4. Why this is more than ordinary scholarly self-correction

Scholars often correct facts. Here Lovejoy changes the **ontology of the classification problem**.

### 1909 working ontology

`person → evolutionist / non-evolutionist`.

### 1910 revised ontology

`person × proposition/domain/relation → specific historical predicate`.

The unit of classification has become smaller.

This parallels other repository findings:

- Batch77: `source × proposition type → evidentiary competence`;
- 005: apparent reward/punishment must be classified by actual selector;
- 004: temporal relation cannot be inferred from logical relation;
- Batch217: `evolution` itself must be decomposed into non-isomorphic axes.

The 1909→1910 pair now provides a direct developmental instance of this granularity increase inside Lovejoy's own published work.

## 5. The trigger is closer source engagement

Lovejoy's own phrase is diagnostic: his earlier classification was made **before coming to close quarters with the subject**.

The 1910 replacement then demands:

- the important writings in approximate chronological order;
- contextual reconstruction;
- attention to historical term meanings;
- discrimination among logically independent doctrines.

The sequence can therefore be stated with unusually high confidence:

> **closer source engagement altered the category architecture.**

This does not prove which physical archive, notebook or teaching practice caused the change. It does prove that Lovejoy himself retrospectively linked closer study to abandoning his broader 1909 category.

## 8. Importance for `primitive` as an operational category

The episode cautions against reading Lovejoy's `primitive` as if it necessarily supplied a single whole-population stage.

By 1910, Lovejoy can explicitly demonstrate that another apparently global historical category, `evolutionist`, breaks apart once one asks which domain and which proposition are actually at stake.

Notebook 005 and the 1906 `primitive philosophy` paper show both sides of the analogous operation:

1. `primitive` can attach to a relation/layer/mechanism inside a heterogeneous system;
2. those relation-specific primitive features can subsequently be reaggregated across populations into `primitive philosophy`.

The Kant self-correction shows why the first operation matters epistemically: without predicate decomposition, verbal or formal resemblance produces false historical identities.

## 9. A sharper model of Lovejoy's classificatory development

Current evidence supports a tentative but strong micro-development:

### 1909

Lovejoy is already highly sophisticated about proof structure in evolution debates, separating:

- fact of evolution;
- causal explanation of evolution;
- sufficient evidence;
- psychology of conviction;
- source prestige versus logical force.

Yet within that same paper he still makes the broad historical assignment:

> `Kant = early evolutionist`.

### 1910

The Kant-specific source investigation forces a further refinement:

> **proof decomposition expands into actor-category decomposition.**

It is therefore wrong to model Lovejoy's method as simply arriving fully formed in Paris and then remaining static.

A narrower longitudinal possibility is:

> notebook-era relation discrimination  
> + later repeated source criticism  
> → increasingly explicit proposition-relative historical classification.

Causal continuity remains inferential; the published change from 1909 to 1910 is direct.

## 11. Publication-ready formulation

> **Lovejoy's own writings preserve a rare before-and-after view of historical category revision. In November 1909 he could still say that comparative-anatomical evidence had made an `evolutionist` of Kant. Revisiting Kant at close range a year later, he explicitly cited that sentence as his own earlier error. The correction was not a simple reversal. Lovejoy broke `evolutionism` into non-equivalent predicates: Kant could be a cosmic and anthropological evolutionist while refusing biological transformism and mechanistic organic generation. Closer source work, in other words, did not merely change the answer; it changed the unit by which historical identity was assigned.**

Project shorthand:

> **1909 global label → 1910 category fission.**
