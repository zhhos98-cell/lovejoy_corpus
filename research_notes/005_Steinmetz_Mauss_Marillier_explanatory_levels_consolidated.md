Consolidated: 2026-08-26

This dossier consolidates the evidentiary and argumentative work formerly distributed across Batches 33, 34, 35, 41, 147, 148 and 149, with the decisive Marillier primary verdict from Batch159 incorporated. Git history retains the batch sequence.

## Core result

The Steinmetz line should not be narrated as a progression from crude monism to increasingly sophisticated pluralism. Rudolf Steinmetz's 1896 `Endokannibalismus` is itself methodologically plural at the case level: he attacks `Simplomanie`, distinguishes motives, tests negative conditions, attends to source reliability, and allows animistic, magical, ceremonial and communion-like refunctionalizations. His stronger commitment lies one explanatory level higher: a universal historical hierarchy in which ordinary nutritional corpse-eating is reconstructed as primary and later religious meanings are secondary additions.

Marillier's 1896 review already attacks the crucial inferential move. He says the reported reasons for cannibal practice are so varied and disparate that only **external resemblance** permits them to be grouped together, and that they are **not susceptible to one interpretation**. Physiological/economic causes may explain some cases, but Steinmetz has not established a unique origin; animistic or magical conceptions can independently account for others.

Mauss then redivides the surviving practice at a relational/ritual level: `endocannibalisme` becomes more precisely `l’anthropophagie rituelle des parents`, with kin solidarity, funerary setting, common meal and transmission of the dead person's virtues doing classificatory work.

Lovejoy's 005 notebook pushes the burden of proof one step further. Kin relation, ceremoniality, ingestion and historical derivation are repeatedly treated as non-diagnostic by themselves. Cases are redistributed according to the operative causal bearer or relation: hunger, revenge, affection/kin relation, propitiation, sympathetic/transferable efficacy, sacramental union, funerary provision, social incorporation, and related mechanisms.

The strongest sequence is therefore:

> **local motive plurality under an origin hierarchy → anti-morphological plural interpretation → relational/ritual redivision → mechanism-level decomposition.**

This is an explanatory-level history, not a priority story. The anti-morphological rule itself is securely pre-Lovejoy in Marillier.

## 1. The exact bibliographic object in Lovejoy 005

Lovejoy 005 PDF p.62 / manuscript p.125, under `Sacrifice / Bibliography`, reads:

> **`Endokannibalismus — Steinmetz.`**

The earlier cautious OCR/transcription reading was corrected by direct manuscript-image inspection and propagated into the clean transcription.

The work is:

Rudolf S. Steinmetz, `Endokannibalismus`, Vienna, 1896, printed in / issued from the *Mitteilungen der Anthropologischen Gesellschaft in Wien*, vol. XXVI, pp.1–60.

The same exact work was:

- reviewed by Léon Marillier in *Revue de l'histoire des religions* 34 (1896), pp.113–115;
- used repeatedly by Marcel Mauss in his 1896–97 criticism of Steinmetz;
- entered by exact title into Lovejoy's own sacrifice bibliography.

This is stronger than a generic shared-authority overlap. It connects Lovejoy's working notebook to a recent specialist object at the center of an active Marillier/Mauss controversy. It does **not** establish that Marillier personally handed the work to Lovejoy or that Lovejoy read Mauss.

## 2. Steinmetz primary text — what the original actually does

The complete 1896 text has been recovered in the bound Vienna Anthropological Society volume, printed pp.1–60.

### 2.1 Steinmetz explicitly rejects one-motive ethnology

Before the central requested packet, Steinmetz says scientific explanation requires motives to be distinguished rather than selecting one plausible motive and treating it as sufficient. He attacks this tendency as `Simplomanie`.

His reconstruction tests a set of enabling/negative conditions for ordinary corpse-eating, including:

- demand for meat / omnivory;
- absence of aesthetic disgust toward corpses;
- absence of fear that the dead person's spirit will retaliate;
- absence of sympathy/inhibition toward the corpse;
- absence of a sense that consumption desecrates the person or memory.

This is a comparative and partly counterfactual programme, not a crude one-factor table.

### 2.2 §§16–22 / pp.36–47 — hunger is not sufficient

Steinmetz tests omnivory, taste/disgust, spirit fear, sympathy/solidarity and hunger. On p.46 he explicitly separates episodic hunger-cannibalism from habitual endocannibalism and rejects hunger as a sufficient explanation of the continuing custom.

Thus the historiographic contrast cannot be:

`Steinmetz = hunger only`.

The relevant structure is:

`plural local conditions/motives`
+
`strong reconstructed historical priority`.

### 2.3 p.45 — corpse manipulation, danger and transfer

The p.44–45 run contains corpse manipulation, magical danger, acquisition of strength and anti-ghost prophylaxis. It includes cases where corpse matter is used as magical material or rough treatment disables a dangerous dead person.

This should not be flattened into a communion reading. It is a mixed evidence cluster in which transfer, danger, mutilation and magical material remain separable.

### 2.4 p.52 §26 — animism and vital-force transfer are secondary in Steinmetz's genealogy

Section 26 asks whether animism could have caused endocannibalism. Steinmetz's answer is negative at the origin level. Animism can:

- add new motives;
- preserve an existing practice;
- reinterpret an older custom;
- support the idea that eating the dead appropriates soul/vital force;
- generate ceremonial/reduced forms.

Therefore **transfer of soul/vital force is already present in Steinmetz**, but he ranks it as a later animistic motive rather than the original cause.

### 2.5 pp.59–60 — universal origin hierarchy remains architectonic

At the conclusion Steinmetz allows later religious interpretation, alliance/communion and ceremonial preservation, but retains the thesis that nutritional corpse-eating is historically primary. Primitive humans are reconstructed as omnivorous and lacking later inhibitions; religious/magical motives are secondary refunctionalizations.

The defining tension is:

> **anti-simplification at the local explanatory level + strong unilinear priority at the global origin level.**

## 3. Marillier 1896 review — primary text recovered

Léon Marillier's review of Steinmetz, *RHR* 34 (1896), **pp.113–115**, is now direct-primary controlled. The old pp.113–118 retrieval range was wrong; p.116 begins the independent Diekamp item.

### p.113

Marillier accurately presents Steinmetz's view that observed religious motives may be superadded and that the original cause should be sought elsewhere. He also acknowledges the strong religious significance of many surviving practices.

### p.114 — decisive anti-morphological formulation

Marillier stresses the **variety and disparate character of the reasons** given for cannibal practice. His conclusion is that the grouped cases are phenomena which **only external resemblances allow one to bring together** and which **do not admit one interpretation**.

This is the crucial pre-Lovejoy control:

> `same visible practice / external resemblance`  
> `≠ one explanatory class or one origin`.

### p.115

Marillier grants that physiological and economic causes can be decisive in some cases, but rejects Steinmetz's proof of a unique origin. Animistic beliefs or magical conceptions can independently explain other cases; therefore one need not assume the chronological priority of endocannibalism over exocannibalism.

He closes by praising the information while criticizing Steinmetz's `théories exclusives et parfois hasardées`.

### Attribution consequence

The rule formerly treated as a possible Lovejoy intensification is directly available in Marillier before Lovejoy's documented Paris participation:

> **external morphology does not license explanatory unification; plural causal histories remain possible.**

Lovejoy's delta must therefore be sought in arrangement, repeated application, cross-domain portability and later abstraction, not invention of this rule.

## 4. Mauss — reclassification by relation and ritual setting

Mauss's 1896–97 critical discussion does not merely repeat Steinmetz. He narrows `endocannibalisme` to:

> `l’anthropophagie rituelle des parents`.

This changes the classificatory level. Instead of naming the act by an inside/outside group relation, Mauss specifies:

- the victim as a relative;
- the funerary/ritual setting;
- family/clan solidarity;
- transmission of the dead person's virtues;
- the common meal / communion relation.

Mauss also explicitly cites Marillier's `Tabou mélanésien`, pp.53–56, as an assembled body of Melanesian texts in the same controversy.

The key operation is therefore:

> **a broad behavioural class is redivided by social relation and ritual mechanism.**

This is a contemporary controversy control, not proof of direct Mauss → Lovejoy reading.

## 5. Lovejoy 005 — mechanism-level redistribution

Lovejoy records Steinmetz's exact title and later decomposes anthropophagic and sacrificial cases at still finer resolution.

### pp.106–107 — ceremoniality is non-diagnostic

A practice can be repeated, public, solemn and rule-governed without thereby becoming sacramental. Famine, revenge, hatred, affection, acquisition of qualities and other motives remain live.

### p.108 — transfer of qualities is not automatically sacrifice or communion

Ingestion can work through sympathetic/transferable efficacy. The causal mechanism must be separated from offering to a deity or mystical union.

### pp.109–111 — propitiation is not communion

A victim's dangerous spirit, hostile water power and friendly/protective deity generate different relations. Similar visible acts can therefore belong to different causal classes.

### p.117 — synthetic distribution

Alimentary and expiatory/propitiatory sacrifices are treated as common; sacramental union is comparatively exceptional and must be demonstrated rather than presumed.

### p.119 — selected body parts

The clearest mechanism allocation is:

- head/brain → intelligence;
- heart → courage/breath/life;
- sexual organs → sexual power.

Lovejoy treats these primarily through transferable/sympathetic efficacy. He can still allow historical derivation from whole-body eating, but historical derivation and present causal mechanism remain analytically separable.

### Strong notebook operation

The unit of comparison becomes progressively narrower:

`cannibalism / sacrifice / ritual form`

→ `who relates to whom, under what ritual setting?`

→ `what causal bearer/mechanism produces the effect in this case?`

This is the strongest Lovejoy-specific documentary behavior in this line.

## 6. Final explanatory-level matrix

| Problem | Steinmetz 1896 | Marillier 1896 | Mauss 1896–97 | Lovejoy 005 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multiple local motives? | yes; explicitly anti-`Simplomanie` | yes; stresses disparate reasons | yes | yes; extensive case redistribution |
| Governing classification | historical origin hierarchy | external resemblance insufficient; plural interpretation | social/ritual relation | operative causal bearer/mechanism |
| Nutritional cause | primary/global origin | valid for some cases, not unique | not governing surviving ritual class | one mechanism among several |
| Animism / magic | later secondary refunctionalization | can independently explain cases | integrated into religious/ritual redivision | separated by local causal role |
| Transfer of qualities | real later animistic motive | compatible with plural causal reading | central in ritual kin anthropophagy | often sympathetic/transferable efficacy, not sufficient for communion |
| Kin relation | compatible with later practice | not a universal genus | classificatorily central | non-diagnostic by itself |
| Ceremoniality | can be later/reduced form | external form insufficient | helps specify ritual setting | insufficient by itself |
| Communion | later religious interpretation | one possible explanation among others | strong class for ritual kin practice | possible but must be demonstrated case-by-case |
| Genealogy vs present function | genealogy architectonic | unique genealogy rejected | present relation reorganizes class | derivation and mechanism repeatedly separated |

The historical shift is therefore not a march from ignorance to plurality. It is a reallocation of **which explanatory level is allowed to govern comparison**.

## 7. Relation to the broader 005 / 1906 argument

This dossier strengthens the distinction between institutional genealogy and causal abstraction.

Lovejoy 005 repeatedly extracts mechanism from forms with different possible origins. A recurrent relation such as direct/intrinsic/transferable efficacy can appear in blood, body parts, charms, foundation rites, agriculture and anthropophagy without implying that those institutions descend from one primordial rite.

Thus:

> **recurring causal mechanism ≠ single institutional origin.**

This is the crucial bridge to the 1906 comparative category. The later abstraction can range transversely across historically heterogeneous practices.

But the ingredients are not uniquely Lovejoyan. Marillier had already published the anti-morphological rule; Steinmetz had already distinguished motives and later refunctionalizations; Mauss had already redivided a broad class relationally. Lovejoy's stronger delta is the notebook's dense local redistribution and the later cross-domain reaggregation of one causal architecture.

## 11. Public-facing formulation

> **The appearance of Steinmetz's _Endokannibalismus_ in Lovejoy's notebook is valuable because it locates the manuscript inside a dispute over what comparison was entitled to unify. Steinmetz himself was no crude single-cause theorist: he distinguished motives and attacked explanatory simplification, but he retained a historical hierarchy in which nutritional corpse-eating preceded later religious meanings. Marillier's 1896 review challenged the governing inference more directly, arguing that practices grouped by external resemblance could have disparate causes and need not share one origin. Mauss then reclassified the surviving kin practice through ritual and social relation. Lovejoy's notebook redistributed the field again, treating kinship, ceremoniality and ingestion as insufficient on their own and asking which causal bearer — hunger, revenge, propitiation, transferable efficacy, communion — actually explained each case. The shift is therefore best understood as a tightening of explanatory level, not the invention of plurality.**
