# Skinner 1975 post-Mew claim state: broader convention/context formula and `context is no magic wand`

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **JSTOR ISSUE/CARRIER DIRECT / 1975 PAGE-SPECIFIC QUOTATION CONTROLS / FULL ARTICLE NOT DIRECTLY EXPOSED / POST-MEW VERSION CONSISTENCY LOCKED, COMPLETE ABSENCE CLAIM HOLD**

## Core result

Skinner's 1975 `Hermeneutics and the Role of History` provides a useful post-Mew version-state control, but the present source state requires a deliberately asymmetric conclusion.

What can be controlled from page-specific quotations and contemporary/later scholarly reproductions is that the 1975 argument is expressed in a **broader context/convention architecture**:

- a text is interpreted within a `field of assumptions and conventions` to which it contributes and from which it derives its distinctive meaning;
- historians need an appropriate context of assumptions and conventions in order to recover authorial intentions/meaning;
- context is explicitly **not a magic wand** that mechanically settles interpretive disputes;
- even esoteric/oblique utterance remains interpretable because it operates against tacit conventions enabling distinctions between what is said and what is meant.

This is fully compatible with Skinner's 1974 statement that Mew's counterexamples had defeated his attempt to require, **invariably**, the invocation of both social and linguistic conventions.

Safe:

> **The directly controlled 1975 formulations are consistent with a post-Mew architecture in which historical interpretation depends on assumptions/conventions and contextual relations without restating the same universal `social + linguistic conventions must invariably be invoked` formula.**

Unsafe:

`THE 1970 UNIVERSAL FORMULA IS PROVEN ABSENT FROM EVERY LINE OF THE 1975 ARTICLE`.

The full 209–232 article is not presently machine-readable in the available JSTOR carrier, so absence cannot be proven from controlled excerpts.

## II. p.210: text/context relation expressed as a field, not a two-convention universal

Page-specific scholarly quotation of Skinner 1975 p.210 gives the formulation that a text/action is to be interpreted within a **field of assumptions and conventions** to which it contributes and from which it derives its distinctively meaningful character.

This matters because the object is relationally constituted:

`TEXT / ACTION`
<-> `FIELD OF ASSUMPTIONS + CONVENTIONS`.

The formulation does not, in the controlled excerpt, repeat the 1970 stronger requirement that **both social and linguistic conventions must invariably be invoked** for the intended force of every relevant utterance.

But source discipline requires:

`DIFFERENT CONTROLLED WORDING`
!= `PROOF THE EARLIER WORDING NEVER OCCURS ELSEWHERE IN THE ARTICLE`.

---

## III. p.216: the method is stated as a sequence of intention + context + understanding

Page-specific reproductions of Skinner 1975 p.216 present a three-stage architecture:

1. recover an author's intentions in writing in order to understand what the text means historically;
2. surround the text with an appropriate context of assumptions and conventions from which intended meaning can be decoded;
3. conclude that knowledge of those assumptions/conventions is essential to historical understanding.

This retains a strong contextual proof burden.

It does **not** amount to abandonment of conventions after Mew.

Rather, the safe relation is:

`MEW DEFEATS ONE UNIVERSAL FORM`
while
`CONVENTION / CONTEXT REMAINS CENTRAL IN A BROADER FORM`.

Therefore:

`SCOPE REVISION`
!= `PROGRAMME ABANDONMENT`.

This directly reinforces the Round-10 distinction between a counterexample to a universal formulation and refutation of every narrower member of the claim family.

---

## IV. p.227: context is not a `magic wand`

Later scholarly discussion page-specifically attributes to Skinner 1975 p.227 the warning that **context is no magic wand**.

The point is important because it blocks a mechanical contextualism:

`ADD CONTEXT`
!= `MEANING AUTOMATICALLY FALLS OUT`.

A reconstructed context may constrain interpretation, supply possible conventions and establish a range of intelligible actions, but it does not by itself select one interpretation without further evidence about what the agent was doing.

This is particularly congruent with Skinner 1974's post-Mew retreat from an invariant conventional formula.

It also parallels Haddock 1976:

`THEORY / CONTEXT CAN CONSTRAIN POSSIBILITIES`
!= `THEORY / CONTEXT PROVES ACTUAL HISTORICAL MEANING`.

Keep this as structural convergence, not causal genealogy.

---

## V. p.228: tacit convention remains relevant even for oblique/esoteric speech

Page-specific later discussion cites Skinner 1975 p.228 to argue that even allegedly esoteric communication occurs within a field of tacit conventions that makes it possible to distinguish what is said from what is meant.

This shows that the 1975 article still assigns conventions a positive interpretive role.

Thus the post-Mew trajectory is not:

`CONVENTIONALISM -> NO CONVENTIONS`.

It is better represented as:

`OVER-STRONG UNIVERSAL CONVENTION REQUIREMENT`
-> `BROADER / RELATIONAL CONVENTION-CONTEXT ARCHITECTURE WITH NON-MECHANICAL CAUTION`.

Again, exact logical formulation of the whole 1975 article remains HOLD until full text is directly controlled.
