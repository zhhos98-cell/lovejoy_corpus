# Arthur F. Wright 1960 JHI — Sinology, history, social science, and object jurisdiction

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **PAGE-SPECIFIC PUBLIC TEXT CONTROL + CONTEMPORARY 1964 RECEPTION / NO LINEAR DISCIPLINARY VICTORY STORY**

## Core result

Arthur F. Wright's `The Study of Chinese Civilization`, *Journal of the History of Ideas* 21.2 (1960), 233–255, can now be used more precisely in the global-JHI map.

The important object is not simply `JHI became more interested in China`.

Wright's article explicitly reconstructs a disciplinary/jurisdictional problem:

- older Sinology was shaped by Chinese literati self-image;
- European/Japanese sinological traditions inherited elite textual problems and categories;
- `Orientalism` and the mass/complexity of documentation constrained the field;
- the sinologue was slow to engage the historical and social sciences that had developed in nineteenth-century Europe;
- the study of Chinese civilization therefore needed a different relation among philological source competence, historical reconstruction and social-scientific questions.

This is a direct JHI case of **regional object rescaling and disciplinary recombination**.

## I. Direct public page control

Full public article witness:
`Arthur F. Wright, The Study of Chinese Civilization`, JHI 21.2 (1960), 233–255.

A page-specific public text witness for printed p.245 directly says that the earlier coherence of a Chinese self-image had fragmented but survived in multiple modern forms. Wright then opens `The Changing Image of China in XXth-Century Scholarship` and explains that, because of:

- the authority of the literati self-image;
- the `incubus of Orientalism`;
- the mass and complexity of documentation,

the sinologue was slow to respond to the growth of historical and social sciences in nineteenth-century Europe.

Public witness:
https://www.kurumbiwone.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/The-Study-of-Chinese-Civilization.pdf

## II. Contemporary 1964 interpretation

Maurice Freedman, `What Social Science Can Do for Chinese Studies`, *Journal of Asian Studies* 23.4 (1964), directly cites Wright p.245ff and summarizes the disciplinary move sharply:

Sinology, in entering modern scholarship, had to free itself from the `incubus of Orientalism` by learning to write history; by the same token it needed to engage social-scientific ideas.

Cambridge Core direct source:
https://doi.org/10.2307/2050233

This contemporaneous reception confirms that Wright was read as addressing **disciplinary method and object architecture**, not simply writing a substantive China survey.

## III. But 1964 immediately prevents a simple replacement narrative

The 1964 `Chinese Studies and the Disciplines` symposium did not produce consensus that Sinology should disappear into generic disciplines.

Participants included:

- Joseph Levenson — humanistic disciplines / Sinology;
- Mary C. Wright — Chinese history / historical vocation;
- G. William Skinner — what China study can do for social science;
- Maurice Freedman — what social science can do for Chinese studies;
- Frederick W. Mote — case for the integrity of Sinology;
- Rhoads Murphey;
- Benjamin Schwartz — `The Fetish of the "Disciplines"`;
- later comments by Denis Twitchett and Kung-Chuan Hsiao.

Current programme/reference witness:
https://chinaheritage.net/journal/new-sinology-in-1964-and-2022/

Thus:

`ORIENTALIST / PHILOLOGICAL JURISDICTION CRITIQUED`
!=
`REGIONAL SPECIALIST JURISDICTION ABOLISHED`.

The live question becomes how specialist linguistic/textual competence and wider historical/social-scientific questions should constrain one another.

## IV. Later historiography: area studies changes the political frame

Later histories of American China studies place this 1950s–60s reorganization within:

- postwar/Cold War area-studies expansion;
- foundation/government/university infrastructure;
- transition from European Orientalism toward American regional/social-scientific programmes;
- continuing disputes over philology, history, social science and regional expertise.

A useful historiographic control is `Cycles of Cathay: Sinology, Philology, and Histories of the Song Dynasty ...`, which reads Wright together with Fairbank, postwar area studies, Naitō historiography and later critiques of Orientalism.

Public source:
https://quod.lib.umich.edu/f/frag/9772151.0002.004/--cycles-of-cathay-sinology-philology-and-histories

Therefore do not narrate:

`PHILOLOGY / ORIENTALISM -> MODERN HISTORY -> PROGRESS`.

Prefer:

`REGIONAL OBJECT + SOURCE COMPETENCE + GENERAL DISCIPLINES`
-> `JURISDICTIONAL RENEGOTIATION`.

## VI. Relation to Lovejoy

Do not claim a direct Lovejoy -> Wright method transmission without actor/network evidence.

The stronger institutional comparison is:

- early Lovejoy moved among Orientalist philology, comparative religion and philosophical/historical questions before a stable history-of-ideas jurisdiction;
- midcentury Wright debates how Sinology, history and social sciences should be recombined after regional/area-study field construction;
- JHI itself can serve as a carrier for this second jurisdictional negotiation.

This is a history of **changing scholarly resolutions and carriers**, not one method lineage.
