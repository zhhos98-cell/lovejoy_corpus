# Batch 187 — Lovejoy at the 1904 St. Louis Congress: Marillier reappears inside the adjacent philosophy-of-religion programme

Date: 2026-08-26
Status: synced
Scope: person-specific pre-1905 control. Test whether the Paris/Marillier line remained confined to Lovejoy's 1898–99 Paris notebook environment or re-entered a documented institutional field in which Lovejoy himself held a formal role before 1905.

## Core result

The official published proceedings of the 1904 International Congress of Arts and Science at St. Louis create a new, tightly bounded institutional bridge.

On **21 September 1904 at 10 a.m.**, the Congress programme lists **Professor A. O. Lovejoy, Washington University**, as **Secretary of Section A: Metaphysics** within the Department of Philosophy. The same programme separately lists **Section B: Philosophy of Religion** for **3 p.m. the same day**, chaired by Thomas C. Hall, with Otto Pfleiderer and Ernst Troeltsch as speakers and **W. P. Montague**, not Lovejoy, as secretary.

Troeltsch's published paper in that adjacent Philosophy of Religion section explicitly contrasts national research traditions in the psychology of religion. After saying German psychology had contributed relatively little and that French work had attacked complex religious facts more boldly, he states that the works of **Marillier, Murisier, and Flournoy** had considerably advanced the problem.

This gives a person-specific institutional fact that was not previously registered in the corpus:

> **Five years after Lovejoy's documented active participation in Léon Marillier's Paris comparative-religion seminar, Marillier was explicitly named as a significant contributor to the psychology/science of religion in the printed proceedings of a philosophy congress at which Lovejoy himself held a formal section office.**

The evidence does **not** show that Lovejoy attended Troeltsch's 3 p.m. session, heard the reference to Marillier, helped organize that section, or renewed personal contact with Marillier. The programme itself supplies the crucial negative control: Lovejoy's formal responsibility was Metaphysics; Montague was secretary of Philosophy of Religion.

The result is therefore best coded as:

`DIRECT PERSON-SPECIFIC INSTITUTIONAL PROXIMITY / MARILLIER REAPPEARANCE / NO ATTENDANCE OR TRANSMISSION CLAIM`.

---

## 1. Lovejoy has a formal role in the Congress programme

The official programme places the relevant sessions inside **Department 1 — Philosophy**. Under **Section A. Metaphysics**, scheduled in Hall 6 at 10 a.m. on 21 September, it lists:

- Chairman: A. C. Armstrong, Wesleyan University;
- Speakers: A. E. Taylor, McGill University; Alexander T. Ormond, Princeton University;
- **Secretary: Professor A. O. Lovejoy, Washington University.**

The proceedings repeat the same attribution at the head of the Metaphysics section itself.

Primary source:

- Howard J. Rogers, ed., *International Congress of Arts and Science*, vol. 1, St. Louis Congress proceedings, programme and Section A — Metaphysics.
- Project Gutenberg transcription: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/38267/38267-h/38267-h.htm

Evidence status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / FORMAL ROLE**.

---

## 2. Philosophy of Religion is institutionally adjacent but formally distinct

The same printed programme immediately follows Metaphysics with **Section B. Philosophy of Religion**, scheduled in Hall 1 at 3 p.m. on 21 September 1904.

It lists:

- Chairman: Thomas C. Hall, Union Theological Seminary;
- Speakers: Otto Pfleiderer, University of Berlin; Ernst Troeltsch, University of Heidelberg;
- **Secretary: W. P. Montague, Columbia University.**

This distinction is important. It blocks an easy but unsupported inference that Lovejoy was secretary for the religion session merely because he held office in the same Department of Philosophy and on the same day.

What the programme establishes is narrower and stronger:

`Lovejoy = formally present in Congress philosophy apparatus`

and

`Marillier = explicitly discussed in an adjacent philosophy-of-religion paper`

without

`Lovejoy = organizer / attendee / hearer of that paper`.

Evidence status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / NEGATIVE CONTROL**.

---

## 3. Troeltsch explicitly names Marillier in the printed paper

Troeltsch's paper, `Main Problems of the Philosophy of Religion: Psychology and Theory of Knowledge in the Science of Religion`, surveys the modern empirical psychology of religion.

His comparison of national traditions is unusually useful for the present project. He says that German theologians and psychologists had remained tied to older philosophical psychology or psycho-physiology, whereas French research had approached complex religious facts more boldly. He then names **Marillier, Murisier, and Flournoy** as scholars whose works had materially aided the problem.

The importance is documentary rather than genealogical. Marillier is not reconstructed by us as a hidden background figure; he is explicitly named in a major philosophy-of-religion contribution printed from the same Congress in which Lovejoy held a formal philosophical role.

Evidence status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / NAMED SCHOLARLY REFERENCE**.

---

## 4. The same religion section also articulates a comparative-historical problem close to Lovejoy's existing research field

Pfleiderer's paper in the same Philosophy of Religion section treats religion as both psychological experience and historical fact. It explicitly discusses historical comparison among religions, Indian parallels, Max Müller's comparative mythology, ethnology, and analytical/critical/comparative methods.

This is useful as an institutional-field control: the 1904 St. Louis philosophy-of-religion programme was visibly operating with the same broad family of objects that had surrounded Lovejoy's late-1890s comparative-religion work — historical comparison, India, primitive religion, psychology, and causal/developmental explanation.

But this is **not** evidence that Lovejoy adopted these claims from Pfleiderer or attended the session. The overlap describes the Congress environment, not a transmission path.

Evidence status: **DIRECT PRIMARY CONTEXT / NO RECEPTION CLAIM**.

---

## 5. Why this changes the Paris line slightly

Before this control, the strongest person-specific Paris sequence was:

`1898–99 Lovejoy in Paris`

→ `active auditor in Marillier comparative-religion seminar`

→ `005 session-linked comparative-religion notebook`

→ `1902 Religion and the Time-Process`.

The 1904 Congress adds a later institutional recurrence:

`1898–99 Marillier seminar`

→ `Lovejoy returns to US teaching`

→ `1901 Buddhism lectures / 1902 explicit Buddhist self-reuse`

→ **`1904 Lovejoy = Metaphysics secretary at St. Louis Congress; adjacent Philosophy of Religion paper explicitly names Marillier`**.

The safe historical inference is not that a Marillier school followed Lovejoy across the Atlantic. It is that **Marillier's research remained legible inside the philosophy-of-religion discourse of an American congress in Lovejoy's own immediate institutional environment**. Paris was therefore not an isolated name that vanished from the scholarly field once Lovejoy left France.

This matters especially because the Congress took place in St. Louis while Lovejoy was professor at Washington University. The connection is person-specific and local without requiring a speculative correspondence network.

---

## 6. A useful negative result: Lovejoy's formal Congress identity is now Metaphysics, not Philosophy of Religion

This batch also clarifies disciplinary movement.

At the 1904 Congress, Lovejoy's assigned formal role is **Metaphysics**. That sits beside, rather than inside, the Philosophy of Religion section. Combined with the already documented 1901 Buddhism syllabus and 1902 comparative religion article, this discourages a simple story of either disciplinary continuity or abrupt abandonment.

A more accurate pre-1905 picture is multi-track:

- specialist Buddhist research remains reusable in teaching and print;
- comparative religion remains a live analytical reservoir;
- Lovejoy simultaneously occupies increasingly general philosophical and history-of-philosophy roles.

The evidence supports **scale expansion and coexistence of genres**, not a clean replacement of `Oriental/Buddhist studies` by `Western philosophy` at a single date.

## 7. Evidence ledger

### DIRECT PRIMARY

1. 1904 Congress programme: Lovejoy is Secretary, Section A — Metaphysics, Department of Philosophy.
2. Same programme: Philosophy of Religion is a separate section later the same day; W. P. Montague is its secretary.
3. Troeltsch's printed Philosophy of Religion paper explicitly names Marillier, Murisier, and Flournoy as important French contributors.
4. Pfleiderer's printed paper discusses historical comparison of religions, India, Max Müller, ethnology, and comparative/critical method.

### STRONG INSTITUTIONAL INFERENCE

- Marillier's work was present in the published philosophy-of-religion discourse of a Congress where Lovejoy held a formal philosophy role in his own city and university environment.
- The 1904 field therefore supplies a later American institutional recurrence of the Marillier name after Lovejoy's Paris period.

### HOLD / DO NOT CLAIM

- Lovejoy attended the 3 p.m. Philosophy of Religion session.
- Lovejoy heard Troeltsch name Marillier.
- Lovejoy selected Troeltsch or Pfleiderer as speakers.
- Lovejoy organized the Philosophy of Religion section.
- Troeltsch's 1904 paper influenced Lovejoy's later work.
- Lovejoy and Marillier corresponded or renewed direct contact in 1904.

---

## 8. Immediate consequences for the pre-1905 reconstruction

1. Add the 1904 Congress to the person-specific chronology between the 1902 article and the 1904 Bruno paper.
2. Treat it as an **institutional recurrence control**, not as a missing influence link.
3. Preserve the explicit programme distinction between Metaphysics and Philosophy of Religion whenever the Congress is cited.
4. In future retrieval, search Washington University / Congress committee papers for correspondence or local planning documents that could establish whether Lovejoy had any role beyond Metaphysics.
5. Continue separately pursuing the unrecovered 1901 `Syllabus: The Philosophy of Buddhism`; this Congress evidence cannot substitute for that missing object.
