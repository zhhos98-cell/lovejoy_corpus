## Core result

Southern Illinois University Carbondale’s **Open Court Publishing Company Records, MSS 027** are now a high-priority archive for the project.

The collection contains 424 boxes, dates 1886–1998 (bulk 1887–1920), and is explicitly described as documenting correspondence, article/book manuscripts, editorial materials and business records for *The Open Court*, *The Monist* and Open Court books. Languages include Chinese, Japanese and **Pāli** as well as English, French and German.

The finding aid itself emphasizes Open Court/Paul Carus’s role in circulating Eastern thought, especially Buddhism and Taoism, and records a network including figures such as Anagarika Dharmapala, Soyen Shaku, D. T. Suzuki, Max Müller and Vivekananda.

Crucially, **Arthur O. Lovejoy is included in the collection-level controlled-name/subject indexing**.

This does not yet identify a Lovejoy letter or manuscript. The name appears in collection-wide metadata inherited on many child records, so it must not be converted into item-level correspondence evidence.

## 3. Why Open Court matters for the 1906 endpoint

Lovejoy’s `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` appeared in *The Monist* 16.3 (1 July 1906), pp.357–382, while he was at Washington University, St. Louis.

The issue itself is revealing as a publication environment. It includes:

- Friedrich Hirth, `Origin of the Mariners’ Compass in China`;
- Lovejoy, `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`;
- Lawrence H. Mills, `The Bible, the Persian Inscriptions, and the Avesta`;
- alongside philosophy/science pieces.

This is contextual evidence only, but it demonstrates that Lovejoy’s 1906 comparative-ethnological intervention appeared in a journal issue that materially mixed philosophy with Asian/Oriental textual and historical subjects.

Open Court therefore offers a concrete American print infrastructure at the later end of the sequence:

> **Harvard / JAOS specialist Oriental philology → Paris sciences religieuses → American return → Open Court/*Monist* comparative-philosophy publication.**

## 6. Important negative control: a 1906–08 general correspondence folder is not Lovejoy evidence

One surfaced general folder covering **1906–1908** lists correspondents including Jean Réville and `Professor Herman Oncken`, but not Arthur O. Lovejoy.

`Herman Oncken` must not be misread as Lovejoy merely because Lovejoy’s middle name is Oncken.

## 7. Stronger American infrastructure model

The project can now distinguish at least three American regimes:

1. **Harvard/Cambridge specialist teaching and graduate philosophy:** Lanman, Toy, James, Sanskrit/Pāli, Sāṃkhya/Vedānta, public/club lectures.
2. **American Oriental Society / JAOS:** specialist learned-society publication; Lovejoy’s 1898 technical Buddhist article is direct evidence.
3. **Open Court / Monist:** a print/editorial network combining philosophy, comparative religion, Asian textual traditions, Buddhist organizations and public intellectual circulation.

Paris remains different because EPHE `sciences religieuses` concentrated specialist knowledge and ethnographic document criticism inside a coordinated research/teaching institution.

Lovejoy’s trajectory crosses all of these infrastructures without requiring a single national or teacher-to-student influence chain.
