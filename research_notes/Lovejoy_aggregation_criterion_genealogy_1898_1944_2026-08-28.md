# Lovejoy — aggregation-criterion genealogy, 1898–1944

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **CANONICAL GATE-A MATRIX / UNIT-IDEA IDENTITY TECHNOLOGY RECONSTRUCTED**

## Governing question

The current branch has separated two proof gates:

- **Gate A — aggregation/equivalence warrant:** why do heterogeneous historical tokens count as instances of one analytical unit/type/idea?;
- **Gate B — relation/mediation warrant:** once units are identified, what relation connects them and what stronger inference is licensed?

This dossier reconstructs Gate A longitudinally.

The main result is not a linear maturation toward one criterion of `same idea`.

> **Lovejoy repeatedly uses different invariants to aggregate historical materials depending on object and scale: classification, system-function, logical import, causal mechanism, semantic denotation, pairwise overlap, configurational pattern, proposition/corollary profile, and thin conceptual core. The recurrence problem is comparatively stable; the identity technology is plural and object-indexed.**

This substantially changes how `unit-idea` should be historicized.

# II. 1898 — two different invariants already coexist

Notebook 004 blocks any claim that Lovejoy's earliest identity practice is simply lexical.

### Classification-level nonidentity

Warren's `upādāna = taṇhā` identity claim is tested against canonical classifications and rejected.

Invariant required:

`same classificatory item / same defined relation`.

Semantic proximity is not enough.

### System-function convergence

Elsewhere `upādhi`, `āsava`, and `saṁyojana` can differ in derivation and connotation yet converge, at system scale, on essentially the same fundamental idea/function of attachment.

Invariant shifts to:

`systemic role / function`.

Thus from the beginning:

`lexical sameness`
≠ `classificatory sameness`
≠ `functional sameness`.

There is no single identity predicate without a scale.

---

# III. 1902 — invariant becomes logical import / presupposition

`Religion and the Time-Process` tracks recurrent structures smaller than whole philosophical systems.

Lovejoy distinguishes:

- philosophers' temperament;
- logical import of their ideas;
- formal/doctrinal presentation;
- historical transmission.

The historical carrier can therefore be an underlying **presupposition or logical/value relation** appearing in heterogeneous systems.

This creates a stronger abstraction:

`different doctrine/system forms`
→ `same recurrent logical/presuppositional element`.

But the criterion is now more analyst-dependent than canonical lexical classification.

The question becomes:

> How much of a doctrine's historical meaning can change while its `logical import` is judged recurrent?

1902 does not formalize the threshold.

---

# IV. 1904 — identity through dialectical/problem location

In `The Dialectic of Bruno and Spinoza`, Lovejoy insists on locating a thinker both logically and historically.

The relevant invariant can be a position in a **conceptual/dialectical problem-space** rather than shared wording or direct source.

Thus two historical formations can be grouped by their relation to the same abstractly distinguished philosophical question while their genealogies differ.

This strengthens the distinction:

`logical/dialectical equivalence`
≠ `historical/genetic equivalence`.

Again the historian's problem-space contributes to defining the aggregation.

---

# V. 1906 — causal mechanism becomes a cross-cultural equivalence criterion

`The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` supplies the strongest early aggregation experiment.

Lovejoy decomposes local reports and practices until he isolates a recurring mechanism:

- impersonal efficacy;
- transfer/localization;
- communicability;
- quasi-mechanical operation;
- practical manipulation/control.

Historically unrelated, morphologically different cases can then be grouped because they instantiate this mechanism.

Invariant:

> **causal architecture rather than outward form or common genealogy.**

This is analytically powerful because it permits transverse comparison across independent histories.

It is also Gate A's clearest early overreach risk:

`local mechanism similarity`
→ `same comparative concept`
→ `fundamental primitive philosophy`
→ collective actor-level attribution.

Lovejoy explicitly qualifies the programme as incomplete, but procedural provisionality does not itself prove cross-cultural equivalence or representativeness.

---

# VI. 1908–09 — proposition-level identity beneath movement labels

The pragmatism papers relocate aggregation from anthropological mechanism to contemporary philosophical contention.

The named movement is decomposed into propositions/contentions that can be:

- logically independent;
- compatible;
- incompatible;
- recombined.

Invariant:

`explicit philosophical contention / proposition-level commitment`.

Shared historical label is denied classificatory force.

Yet 1909 prevents atomization from becoming the endpoint: historically distinctive novelty can reside in a **new compound** of compatible elements.

Thus Gate A can operate at two levels:

- proposition identity;
- configurational identity/novelty.

---

# VII. 1915 — recurrence becomes programmatic before identity is operationalized

The Thilly review explicitly asks what `units` history of philosophy should use.

Lovejoy moves:

`system`
→ Windelbandian `problem`
→ `separate ideas / dialectical motifs`.

These motifs are said to:

- recur repeatedly;
- appear more or less disguised;
- enter very different combinations/problems.

But he immediately says much remains to be done in their:

- discrimination;
- enumeration;
- examination;

and the conception requires further explanation/justification.

Therefore 1915 establishes a key asymmetry:

> **the recurrence claim is explicit before the equivalence criterion is explicit.**

This is the most important programme-level hinge in Gate A's genealogy.

---

# VIII. 1923–24 — Lovejoy develops negative aggregation controls

The early 1920s do not simply expand recurrence; they aggressively police false identity.

## 1923 Rousseau

`state of nature` has multiple distinguishable senses.

Invariant must be semantic/denotational, not merely lexical.

Rule:

`same phrase` ↛ `same doctrine`.

## 1924 Romanticisms

The broad label can cover heterogeneous complexes.

Lovejoy permits pairwise overlap:

- A shares x with B;
- A shares y with C;
- x need not occur in C.

Thus a macro-historical family need not possess one common invariant.

This creates an explicit distinction between:

- **overlap/family aggregation**;
- **recurrent-component identity**.

The latter is stronger and requires separate proof.

---

# IX. 1932 — configuration itself can be the invariant

`The Parallel of Deism and Classicism` prevents Gate A from becoming permanently atomistic.

Lovejoy analyzes an Enlightenment idea-complex into elements, but the explanatory object is its systematic unity/pattern.

Deism and classicism can instantiate a parallel organized set of preconceptions across different domains.

Invariant:

`configuration / pattern of relations among components`.

Thus the historian can recognize sameness/homology above the level of the individual unit.

This reveals a distinct aggregation technology:

`same pattern`
without requiring
`every component token to be identical`.

---

# X. 1936 — proposition plus inferential profile supplies a practical unit technology

*The Great Chain of Being* narrows the units used in the book to especially definite/explicit cases:

- a specific proposition or principle;
- propositions treated as its corollaries.

Invariant becomes:

`propositional content + corollary / inferential profile`.

This is the most precise practical recognition technology yet recovered.

It is stronger than lexical matching and more determinate than a generic `dialectical motif`.

But it leaves crucial degrees open:

- how many corollaries may vary?;
- can function change?;
- can a proposition acquire incompatible implications and remain the same?;
- how much abstraction is permitted?

The criterion is usable, not formalized.

---

# XI. 1941 — the invariant can be thinned

The `infinite` micro-case shows that Lovejoy can distinguish several rich positive meanings while identifying a shared negative element: absence of limit/completion.

Invariant:

`minimal conceptual core`.

This differs sharply from 1936's explicit proposition/corollary profile.

It permits recurrence under greater semantic variation by reducing the thickness of the common content.

That move creates a central risk:

> **the thinner the invariant, the easier recurrence becomes to establish.**

The historian's choice of abstraction level therefore becomes part of the proof burden.

---

# XII. 1941 — semasiology is a precondition, not the identity rule itself

Lovejoy requires recurrent terms to be studied semasiologically before basic/germinal ideas are identified across labels and phrasings.

This establishes a strong negative rule:

`word recurrence` ≠ `idea recurrence`.

It does not fully state the positive rule:

`what sufficient conceptual invariant = same idea?`

Semasiology removes false lexical identities; it does not uniquely determine the surviving conceptual equivalence class.

Thus Gate A remains open even in the mature explicit methodology.

---

# XIII. 1944 — equivalence becomes an empirical working hypothesis

Against Spitzer, Lovejoy explicitly treats:

- recurrence of discriminable ideas;
- compound character of historical thought

as working hypotheses whose truth/range are questions of historical fact to be tested by texts.

This is epistemically important because recurrence is revisable, not metaphysically guaranteed.

But empirical status does not provide the equivalence rule.

The historian still chooses whether two textual tokens represent:

- the same proposition;
- a modified proposition;
- the same thin core;
- a functional analogue;
- a patterned homology;
- or mere resemblance.

Thus:

> **verification commitment and identity criterion are independent methodological requirements.**

---

# XIV. No single equivalence technology emerges

The longitudinal evidence supports several Gate-A technologies:

1. **classificatory identity**;
2. **system-function identity**;
3. **logical/presuppositional identity**;
4. **dialectical/problem-position identity**;
5. **causal-mechanism identity**;
6. **propositional identity**;
7. **overlap/family aggregation**;
8. **configurational/pattern homology**;
9. **proposition + inferential-profile identity**;
10. **thin common-core identity**.

These are not reducible to one rule.

The most defensible mature interpretation is therefore:

> **`unit-idea` names a historiographical role — a component sufficiently discriminated for the present inquiry to be tracked across contexts — more than it names one invariant formal ontology or one fixed equivalence algorithm.**

This does not make the method arbitrary. Each case can still be better or worse warranted.

It does mean the warrant must be reconstructed locally.

---

# XV. Stable problem, variable criterion

The most striking continuity is not a stable criterion but a stable question:

> **How can a historian recognize something as recurrent when its surface appearance, companions, labels or historical host change?**

Actor-vocabulary anchors:

### 1915

`dialectical motifs` recur `more or less disguised`.

### 1936

the `same idea` may appear `sometimes considerably disguised` in diverse intellectual regions.

### 1941

basic/germinal ideas must be identified so they can be recognized under different contexts, labels and phrasings.

Thus:

> **recognition-under-transformation is stable; the invariant used to license recognition changes with the object.**

This is currently the strongest Gate-A developmental finding.

---

# XVI. Consequence for formal logic

Formal logic can contribute to:

- classification audit;
- explicit partition;
- compatibility/incompatibility;
- inferential restriction.

But no formal relation-space uniquely chooses among the heterogeneous historical equivalence technologies listed above.

The Gate-A genealogy must therefore remain multi-source:

- philology / semantic usage;
- comparative religion / mechanism comparison;
- history of philosophy / problem-centred historiography;
- formal-logical classification;
- contemporary philosophical controversy;
- later semasiology and intellectual history.

A direct Harvard logic-training edge, if eventually recovered, would not collapse these sources into one.

---

# XVII. Consequence for Skinner

The strongest contextualist challenge is now extremely precise.

Lovejoy's mature method can be:

- semantically careful;
- relation-pluralist;
- empirically revisable;
- context-aware;

and still face a Gate-A problem.

Skinner's challenge bites where Lovejoy allows the historian to select an invariant — proposition, common core, function, motif — that survives changes which an actor/use-centred history might treat as constitutive of meaning.

The disagreement is therefore:

> **Which transformations preserve historical identity, and who is entitled to choose the invariant?**

This is stronger than the old binary `context vs timeless ideas`.

## Current shorthand

> **Lovejoy's history-of-ideas programme has a stable recognition problem but no single stable equivalence criterion. Across his career, sameness can be classificatory, functional, logical, mechanistic, propositional, configurational, or common-core. The unit-idea is best historicized as a role within a flexible aggregation practice, whose local identity warrant must be reconstructed case by case.**
