# Batch 34 — Lovejoy 005 only: `Endokannibalismus` as an exact bibliographic bridge from Steinmetz → Marillier/Mauss → Lovejoy

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope lock: **MS38_004_001_061_005 only**.

## Research move

This batch isolates one discovery from the broader source-concordance pass because it is materially stronger than a generic overlap of authorities.

A direct reinspection of Lovejoy 005 PDF p.62 / notebook p.125 resolves a previously uncertain bibliography line as:

> **`Endokannibalismus — Steinmetz.`**

This is not merely a Steinmetz name-hit. It is the exact title of Rudolf S. Steinmetz’s recent 1896 specialist study. The same work was reviewed by **Léon Marillier** in *Revue de l’histoire des religions* 34 (1896), beginning p.113, and was used repeatedly by **Marcel Mauss** in his 1896 critique of Steinmetz.

The resulting documentary chain is:

> **Steinmetz, `Endokannibalismus` (1896)**
> → **Marillier review, RHR 34 (1896), p.113**
> → **Mauss 1896 uses the work within the punishment / mortuary religion / taboo controversy**
> → **Lovejoy 005 p.62 copies the exact title into `Sacrifice / Bibliography`**
> → **Lovejoy’s subsequent pages decompose cannibalism, sacrifice, funerary killing, magical transfer, propitiation and communion into distinct mechanisms.**

No direct teacher-to-student handoff is claimed.

## 2. Bibliographic identity of Steinmetz’s work

Contemporary reviews identify:

**Rudolf S. Steinmetz, *Endokannibalismus* (Wien, 1896).**

A contemporary American Journal of Sociology notice describes it as a reprint from the *Mittheilungen der anthropologischen Gesellschaft in Wien*, Band XXVI. A contemporary *Monist* notice gives Vienna, Anthropologische Gesellschaft, 1896, 60 pages.

Mauss calls it Steinmetz’s `récent opuscule, Endokannibalismus`, and cites specific sections/pages from the separate printing.

The precise bibliographic carrier should therefore be rendered conservatively as:

> Rudolf S. Steinmetz, *Endokannibalismus* (Vienna, 1896), offprint / separate issue from *Mittheilungen der Anthropologischen Gesellschaft in Wien*, vol. XXVI.

## 3. Marillier reviewed the exact title in 1896

The table of contents of *Revue de l’histoire des religions*, volume 34 (1896), gives:

> `R Steinmetz Endokannibalismus M L Marillier — 113`

The next item in the table of contents begins at p.116. It is therefore a strong bibliographic inference that Marillier’s notice occupies **pp.113–115**; publication-grade page span should still be checked against the scan before citation.

This matters because the title in Lovejoy 005 was not simply somewhere in the European ethnographic literature. It had been **personally reviewed by the teacher whose 1898–99 EPHE conference Lovejoy attended actively**.

## 4. Mauss 1896 makes `Endokannibalismus` methodologically relevant

Mauss does not cite the title in passing. He uses it repeatedly in *La religion et les origines du droit pénal d’après un livre récent*.

Among the relevant moves:

- Mauss says the exposition method of Steinmetz’s larger project can be seen in the recent *Endokannibalismus*;
- he cites it when discussing the statistical / comparative method;
- later he criticizes Steinmetz for accepting indigenous or ethnographer interpretations too readily, explicitly saying the same problem occurs in *Endokannibalismus*;
- in the clan-solidarity discussion he cites Steinmetz’s `endocannibalisme`, then distinguishes ritual eating of relatives, magical transfer, funerary sacrifice and sacrificial substitution rather than treating them as one type.

The key methodological issue is therefore close to Lovejoy’s later operation in 005: **an outward form does not identify the causal or religious mechanism that generated it.**

## 5. Exact-title match changes the status of the source-network argument

Before this correction, the strongest source evidence was:

- Brough Smyth in Marillier/Mauss and Lovejoy;
- Codrington in Marillier/Mauss and Lovejoy;
- general evidence that Marillier circulated references and note-files.

Those facts establish a common ethnographic infrastructure.

`Endokannibalismus` adds a different kind of link:

1. it is a **recent** publication, not a canonical nineteenth-century ethnographic compendium;
2. it belongs directly to the **Steinmetz controversy** named in the EPHE report;
3. Marillier **reviewed the exact work**;
4. Mauss **argued with the exact work**;
5. Lovejoy **entered the exact title** into the bibliography opening his sacrifice/cannibalism research sequence.

This moves the evidence from `same library` toward `same active controversy and bibliographic object`.

## 6. 005’s use is active, not derivative

The later 005 pages do not simply reproduce a Steinmetz type. They repeatedly split the cannibalism/sacrifice complex apart.

Relevant 005 operations include:

- ritualized killing does not by itself prove sacrifice;
- eating human flesh can be medicinal, retaliatory, affectionate, magical, propitiatory or sacramental;
- selected organs may be eaten because a property is thought transferable;
- funerary companion-killing can provide attendants or communication rather than communion;
- human sacrifice to dangerous water spirits can be propitiation rather than sacramental union;
- animal-for-human substitution is rejected as a universal developmental law;
- ceremonial regularity is insufficient to establish original religious meaning.

Thus the exact-title connection does not reduce Lovejoy to Steinmetz. It clarifies the **problem-space against which Lovejoy’s classificatory resistance becomes visible**.

## 7. Relation to 005 pp.16–19

The early future-life pages and the later sacrifice/cannibalism pages now connect through Steinmetz more tightly.

At pp.16–19 Lovejoy asks whether post-mortem consequences derive from:

- moral desert;
- ritual or taboo violation;
- manner of death;
- clan solidarity;
- social/religious exclusion;
- missionary contamination of testimony.

At p.62 and after, the bibliography explicitly brings in Steinmetz’s *Endokannibalismus*, and Lovejoy then asks whether ritual practices derive from:

- funerary provision;
- clan relation;
- magical efficacy;
- property transfer;
- expiation / propitiation;
- sacramental communion;
- agricultural fecundation;
- social incorporation.

The notebook can therefore be read as one sustained operation: **decomposing broad ethnological categories into historically and causally distinct mechanisms.**
