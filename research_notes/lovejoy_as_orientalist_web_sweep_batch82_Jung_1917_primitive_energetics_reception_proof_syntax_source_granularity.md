# Batch 82 — Jung 1917: `primitive energetics` migrates into analytical psychology while Lovejoy's proof syntax survives at lower source resolution

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: establish the earliest **securely recovered** direct Jung reception of Lovejoy 1906 and ask what exactly is transmitted: term, causal thesis, evidence, or inferential architecture. This follows Batch 81's immediate-reception disaggregation model.

## Core result

A direct primary-text reception can now be fixed **by the 1917 revision of Jung's analytical-psychology papers**.

In the second-edition material gathered as *Collected Papers on Analytical Psychology* (editor Constance E. Long; editor's preface dated June 1917; later reprinted 1920), Jung argues that the modern idea of energy/conservation must correspond to a primordial image in the `absolute unconscious`. He immediately states that this conclusion **requires proof** that a comparable image existed historically and remained effective across millennia. He then adduces the worldwide `dynamistic religions` centered on universal magical power and says that the American investigator Lovejoy aptly calls the underlying notion **`primitive energetics`**. The footnote identifies *The Monist*, vol. XVI, p.363.

This is a stronger reception result than `Jung borrowed Lovejoy's phrase`.

The inferential sequence is:

`Jung psychological hypothesis: energy/conservation is a primordial image`

→ **stronger historical claim requires independent historical evidence**

→ cross-cultural dynamistic religion / magical-power conception

→ Lovejoy's `primitive energetics` supplies a named conceptual witness

→ Jung converts the ethnological category into evidence for a **primordial psychic image / collective-unconscious genealogy**.

The striking result is therefore:

> **The proof syntax survives while the proof object changes.**

Lovejoy 1906 used native vocabulary/grammar as an independent `vera causa` for a causal conception before applying it to customs. Jung 1917 uses comparative-religion evidence, including Lovejoy, as an independent historical warrant before treating modern energetic thought as the re-emergence of an ancient psychic image.

But the source granularity is much lower in Jung. Lovejoy's proposition-relative distinctions among observer, lexical occurrence, gloss, grammar, semantic abstraction, and causal application disappear. Jung takes the synthesized cross-cultural `power-concept` as a package.

The safest formula is:

> **Jung preserves Lovejoy's demand that a strong explanatory proposition be historically warranted, but he does not preserve Lovejoy's fine-grained architecture for grading the warrants themselves.**

Or, more compactly:

> **proof syntax persists; source granularity collapses.**

## 2. The Lovejoy passage is embedded inside an explicit proof demand

The passage begins from Robert Mayer and the modern scientific idea of energy/conservation.

Jung asks where such an apparently new idea obtains its compelling psychic force. His answer is that the energy/conservation idea must be a **primordial image** lying dormant in the absolute unconscious.

He then states the evidentiary consequence. This conclusion obliges the investigator to prove that a similar primordial image actually existed in the history of the human mind and remained operative over very long periods.

Only after establishing that proof burden does Jung turn to comparative religion:

- the most widely separated `primitive religions` are said to be founded upon a universal magical power;
- Tylor and Frazer are said to have misconstrued this as animism;
- the power in question is said not fundamentally to mean soul/spirit;
- Lovejoy is introduced as the scholar who aptly names it `primitive energetics`.

This is not casual name-dropping. Lovejoy enters exactly where Jung needs to convert a psychological/archetypal hypothesis into a historical claim.

### Reception function

Lovejoy's 1906 article functions as:

- a conceptual nomenclature source;
- a comparative-religion authority;
- part of the historical evidence that an energy/power image is not merely a modern scientific invention.

It does **not** function here as a model of citation criticism or linguistic method.

## 3. The footnote is direct and specific

The Project Gutenberg text marks Lovejoy with footnote `[241]`.

The footnote itself reads, in the electronic reproduction:

`The Monist, vol. xvi. p. 363.`

This is enough to identify Lovejoy 1906 unambiguously. The reference is slightly different from later Jung bibliographies, which often cite the whole article or other pages, but p.363 is inside Lovejoy's North-American lexical proof and is therefore a plausible local locator.

### Bibliographic caution

The electronic text abbreviates the note to journal/volume/page rather than printing Lovejoy's name/title in the footnote. The main text itself names Lovejoy, so identification is secure.

## 4. Lovejoy's category changes historical job in Jung

### Lovejoy 1906

Lovejoy's immediate target is a theory of `primitive philosophy` / causation.

He argues:

- animism/personal agency is not the most fundamental category;
- an impersonal, communicable, productive efficacy must be established independently;
- native lexical and grammatical evidence supplies the `vera causa`;
- customs and rites can then be re-read as applications of the category.

So the target proposition is broadly:

> **this causal conception existed and helps explain the rites.**

### Jung 1917

Jung's target proposition is different:

> **the modern energy/conservation image has an archaic psychic ancestry and therefore can re-emerge from the collective/absolute unconscious.**

Lovejoy's comparative category becomes an evidentiary bridge between:

- ethnographic `primitive power`;
- modern scientific energy;
- Jung's psychological doctrine of primordial images.

Thus the same intellectual object undergoes a reception-level function migration:

`Lovejoy: explanatory category for primitive causal practice`

→

`Jung: historical witness for an archetypal energy-image`.

This is reception as **repurposing of evidentiary function**, not just adoption of terminology.

## 5. A striking formal continuity: Jung reproduces the higher-claim / independent-warrant move

Batch 80's core formula was:

> `mechanism migration is accompanied by warrant migration`.

Jung's argument has an analogous formal structure:

1. psychological theory generates a high-level proposition;
2. Jung refuses to let the proposition stand as introspective speculation alone;
3. he says historical existence over millennia must be proved;
4. comparative ethnology/religion is recruited as the independent channel;
5. Lovejoy's category is one part of that historical proof.

This is not evidence that Jung consciously adopted Lovejoy's `vera causa` method. The logical structure may be generic scientific rhetoric and Jung has his own methodological sources.

But as reception history it matters because it shows that what travels from Lovejoy is not simply a noun. The 1906 object is inserted into another argument at **the point where evidentiary independence is demanded**.

The strongest safe wording is therefore:

> **Jung did not preserve Lovejoy's source-critical apparatus, but he received Lovejoy at a proof-bearing location in his own argument.**

## 6. The major loss: Lovejoy's source-resolution hierarchy disappears

Batches 77–78 reconstructed Lovejoy's 1906 hierarchy:

- an old missionary can attest a lexical application while failing as translator;
- an observer can record external ritual fact while failing on ontology;
- a modern ethnographer can be `clear and trustworthy` for distribution yet insufficient for semantic core;
- grammar/native explanation can carry a stronger proposition than observer gloss;
- regional packets have unequal evidentiary resolution.

None of this is visible in the local Jung 1917 use.

Jung speaks broadly of:

- `primitive religions` in widely separated regions;
- universal magical power;
- Tylor/Frazer misinterpretation;
- Lovejoy's `primitive energetics`.

The category has therefore become **citation-compressed**.

This confirms Batch 81's differential-transmission model:

- substantive category: HIGH transmissibility;
- cross-cultural evidentiary conclusion: HIGH;
- proof syntax: PARTIAL survival;
- source-function granularity: LOW survival.

## 7. The direction of abstraction is reversed

The deepest transformation can be stated as a reversal.

### Lovejoy

`native words / grammatical uses / observed customs`

→ abstract a causal category

→ `primitive energetics`.

### Jung

`modern scientific energy`

→ hypothesize a primordial psychic image

→ use `primitive energetics` as historical confirmation

→ return to modern psychic energetics/archetypal theory.

The Lovejoy category therefore changes from **endpoint of ethnographic abstraction** to **middle term in psychological genealogy**.

This is an unusually concrete example of how an idea changes not only meaning but **evidentiary position** when it enters another discipline.

## 8. Later Jung intensifies the reception, but edition control remains necessary

Later Jung texts collected in *On Psychic Energy* / *On the Nature of the Psyche* quote Lovejoy much more extensively. Searchable modern editions reproduce Lovejoy's claims that mana-family concepts signify `the efficacious, the powerful, the productive` and a force working according to regular/intelligible laws, and Jung then objects that it is strictly inaccurate to call the primitive's mana an abstract `concept`; `a concept of primitive philosophy`, he says, is itself a modern scholarly construction.

This later stage is highly relevant because Jung both **accepts Lovejoy's substantive causal characterization and criticizes Lovejoy's epistemic representation of native abstraction**.

However, publication history is complex:

- Jung repeatedly revised these essays;
- modern *Collected Works* texts can incorporate later states;
- exact first appearance of each Lovejoy quotation/criticism must be reconstructed from German editions before making a year-specific genealogy.

Therefore Batch 82 uses **1917 only** as the secure chronological anchor and records the fuller later critique as a future collation target.

Jung's later mana discussions commonly cite Nathan Söderblom's *Das Werden des Gottesglaubens* alongside Lovejoy.

This does **not** establish a Söderblom→Lovejoy reception relation.

Current status:

- Söderblom 1916: major dynamism/mana synthesis, secure;
- Jung uses Söderblom as a broad comparative survey and Lovejoy for `primitive energetics`, secure in later texts;
- direct Lovejoy citation inside Söderblom: **not yet recovered**.

Thus Söderblom remains HOLD as a recipient of Lovejoy.

The possibility is historically attractive because Söderblom and Lovejoy shared Marillier's 1898–99 EPHE environment, but that makes evidentiary caution more important, not less.

## 10. Revised reception model after Batches 81–82

The 1906 paper now has at least four distinct reception forms:

### Leuba 1912 — inferential re-audit

Lovejoy's causal claim is retained closely enough to test whether its evidence supports `mechanical regularity`.

### James/Kallen 1911 — packet assimilation

Lovejoy becomes one authority among several for primitive magic/mana.

### Marett/Jevons node 1909 — adjacent debate without visible uptake

The proof-sufficiency problem is live, but Lovejoy is not presently a named center.

### Jung 1917 — evidentiary repurposing

Lovejoy's category becomes historical proof for a different high-level proposition: the archaic/primordial ancestry of psychic energy.

The combined model is:

> **reception is not transmission of a theory as a whole. It is reassignment of components to new evidentiary jobs.**

That formulation now mirrors what the project has already shown inside Lovejoy's own research practice: sources, claims, and concepts migrate by function.

## 11. Article-level payoff

A potentially strong article sentence is:

> **The later life of `primitive energetics` repeated the very operation by which Lovejoy had produced it. Sources changed evidentiary jobs. In 1906 native words and grammatical usages served as independent warrants for a causal category; by 1917 Jung was using Lovejoy's category itself as an historical warrant for the primordiality of psychic energy. What survived was therefore not simply a theory but a movable evidentiary object.**

A second sentence, more cautious and methodological:

> **Jung preserved a proof-bearing use of Lovejoy while collapsing the source hierarchy behind Lovejoy's proof: proof syntax persisted, source granularity did not.**
