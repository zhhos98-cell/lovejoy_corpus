## 1. Marillier's 1893/1894 `Survivance` memoir is now directly identified

Persée exposes Léon Marillier, **“La survivance de l'âme et l'idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés,”** *Annuaires de l'École pratique des hautes études*, 1893, pp. 1–46, DOI `10.3406/ephe.1893.19382`. The work also appeared as a separately printed Paris/Imprimerie nationale item in 1894; the BnF catalogue marks it as consultable on Gallica.

The opening states the exact thesis that structures the first third of notebook 005: the common theory that belief in immortality arose from a need for moral justice is to be rejected/tested; Marillier distinguishes the existence of moral rules among non-European peoples from the separate question whether afterlife destiny is conceived as divine recompense.

The 1898–99 EPHE report explicitly says Marillier's Monday conference returned to this earlier memoir and defended its conclusion that the other life is conceived as a **continuation**, not compensation/reparation/punishment, of earthly life. Lovejoy is named in the same report among auditors who took an active part.

## 2. A previously uncertain notebook abbreviation can now be resolved at research-note level

In corrected notebook 005 pp. 1–15:

- PDF p. 5 contains `V. citations given in M. Surviv.`
- PDF p. 6 contains `V. Surviv.`
- later, PDF p. 29 has an inserted slip explicitly headed **`Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`**.

Given the exact title of Marillier's memoir, the 1898–99 course report's explicit description of that memoir, Lovejoy's active participation in Marillier's conference, and the later unambiguous `Marillier — Survivance` note, the best current reading is that **`M. Surviv.` / `Surviv.` refers to Marillier's `La survivance de l'âme et l'idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés`.**

This is strong enough to use as a resolved bibliographic reference in the research apparatus. The diplomatic transcription itself should retain Lovejoy's abbreviation; expansion should be stored as metadata/annotation rather than silently substituted into the manuscript text.

## 3. Marillier's earlier EPHE course summaries match specific early pages of 005

A surviving EPHE course report from 1893 describes Marillier's teaching on:

- myths and legends concerning the **origin of death**;
- the claim that death is treated as accidental/non-natural among the peoples under discussion;
- lands of the dead as continuations of the world of the living;
- post-mortem rank determined by rank in life rather than moral merit;
- the afterlife as continuation rather than moral repair;
- Melanesian myth, including Codrington and comparative mythological material.

Source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1893_num_7_3_19386

This is strikingly close to corrected 005 pp. 7–15, where Lovejoy writes that death is not regarded as a natural/necessary phenomenon, gathers origin-of-death legends, cites Brough Smyth and Codrington, and then moves to Greenland/Eskimo future-life distinctions while repeatedly separating ritual/magical conditions from moral judgment.

The relationship is therefore not merely that Lovejoy attended one 1898–99 course whose topics resemble his notebook. **Notebook 005 is embedded in a longer Marillier teaching/publication program whose conceptual vocabulary and example structure recur directly in the notebook.**

## 4. The next year's Marillier course confirms the continuity of the research programme, but not Lovejoy's attendance

The EPHE 1899–1900 report shows Marillier continuing:

- agrarian human sacrifice among non-Aryan Indian groups, especially Khonds/Orissa;
- magical/fecundative power of blood;
- expiatory sacrifice and expulsion of evils;
- critical engagement with Frazer's *Golden Bough*;
- origin/nature of death and disease;
- the amoral character of non-European eschatology;
- Christian interpolation in American eschatological myths.

Source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1899_num_13_9_19524

Lovejoy is **not** named among active auditors in this 1899–1900 report and by then had begun teaching at Stanford. This source is therefore evidence for Marillier's programme and for the intellectual sequence surrounding 005, not evidence of Lovejoy's attendance in 1899–1900.

## 5. Lovejoy's history-of-religion publication strand continues beyond 1906

The earlier working chronology risked making 1906 `Primitive Philosophy` the endpoint of Lovejoy's early religion work. Wilson's bibliography and primary journal metadata show a broader 1907–1908 publication sequence:

### 1907 — “The Entangling Alliance of Religion and History”

- *Hibbert Journal* 5 (1907), 258–276.
- Wilson summarizes it as an argument about Christianity's unusually strong dependence on historical propositions, traced in part to Jewish ancestry and the conversion of historical episodes into mythology.
- A modern *Church History* article independently cites the paper at pp. 258, 259, 270.

### 1907 — “The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought”

- *American Journal of Theology* 11 (1907), 228–249.
- JSTOR issue metadata confirms the article.
- Wilson describes a source-based reconstruction of the antecedents of Christian ethical inwardness in Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ben Sira, Ecclesiastes, Wisdom of Solomon, etc.
- This is directly relevant to notebook 005's Hebrew `nephesh/ruach`, eschatology, and comparative Jewish material, though a manuscript→article relation has not yet been tested.

### 1908 — “The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus”

- *The Monist* 18.4 (Oct. 1908), 597–609, DOI `10.5840/monist190818440`.
- It is an essay-review of William Benjamin Smith's *Der Vorchristliche Jesus*.
- Wilson says Lovejoy rejects Smith's larger theory that primitive Christianity emerged through a vast syncretism whose distinctive elements were largely Gnostic; Lovejoy argues the evidence does not sustain the proposed genealogy.
- William Benjamin Smith replied in *The Monist* 19.3 (1909), 409–420, under the title **“Professor Lovejoy on ‘Der Vorchristliche Jesus.’”**

## 6. Revised chronology: the comparative-religion/historical-religion strand lasts at least to 1908

A better current sequence is:

1. **Harvard 1895–98** — formal Sanskrit/Buddhist-text, comparative-religion, Hebrew-religion training.
2. **Paris 1898–99** — Lévi private sacred-text reading + Marillier EPHE comparative religion; notebooks 004/005 now sit in this formation/research zone.
3. **1901** — public teaching, `The Philosophy of Buddhism` syllabus.
4. **1906** — `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`, theoretical abstraction around efficacy/power.
5. **1907–08** — continuing published history-of-religion work on Jewish ethical development, religion/history, and origins/syncretism in early Christianity.

Thus the shift is not a clean abandonment of religion after 1906. What changes is the **object and scale**: direct Indic/Buddhist philology becomes less visible, while comparative and historical analysis of religion continues.

## 7. Methodological significance

The 1908 Smith review is particularly suggestive because Wilson's synopsis again shows Lovejoy testing a claimed grand genealogy (`vast and slow syncretism`) against the actual evidentiary fit of its components. This resembles operations already visible in 004 and 005: refuse a named system or ritual form as a self-evident unit; separate elements, motives, mechanisms and chronological layers; then ask whether the proposed historical arrangement is actually supported.

This is a lead, not yet proof of a single continuous method. The next step is a close read of the 1907 Jewish article and 1908 Smith review against notebooks 004/005.
