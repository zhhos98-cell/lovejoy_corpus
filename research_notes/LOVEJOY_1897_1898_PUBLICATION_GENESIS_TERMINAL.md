# Lovejoy 1897–1898 publication genesis — terminal synthesis

Last synchronized: 2026-09-05  
Status: **DEFAULT 1897–1898 PUBLICATION ROUTE**

## Direct documentary sequence

- **10 Apr 1897, AOS final circular:** `Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`. The same circular separately lists, from answers to circulars, members and friends whose presence was hoped for; Lovejoy is not among those thirty-six names. This establishes **no positive attendance response represented in the Final Circular**, not a formal declination.
- **Proceedings reporting the Baltimore meeting of 22–24 Apr 1897:** Lovejoy absent; communication read by title; normalized content-level title `On the meaning of the Buddhist technical terms upādāna and upādāna-kkhandhā`. Exact typography remains visual HOLD. **Carrier-date control:** the Proceedings were prepared/printed after the meeting; the title recorded there cannot yet be dated diplomatically to 22–24 Apr itself.
- **1898, JAOS:** `The Buddhistic technical terms upādāna and upādisesa`.

The second named technical object changes between the recoverable 1897 Proceedings title carrier and the 1898 publication. The currently recovered evidence does **not** establish the exact day on which the Milinda title was replaced by the technical-terms title.

The 10 Apr circular now supplies a same-document administrative control: **paper/title registration and positive attendance response were distinct channels**. Combined with the later Proceedings record of Lovejoy's absence, this excludes an in-person oral retitling by Lovejoy at Baltimore as the mechanism of the recoverable title change. Live mechanisms are a remote authorial correction/revision, officer/editorial reframing, post-meeting Proceedings normalization, or another unrecovered intermediate carrier.

## Core model

Do not model this as one unchanged manuscript repeatedly retitled. The evidence is better described as:

> **object promotion, demotion, and reallocation across documentary states.**

Observed object fates:

- `Milinda-pañha`: organizing public title-object in the 10 Apr circular -> source/witness inside the later technical argument;
- `upādānakkhandha`: Proceedings title-level co-object -> internal controversy/evidentiary object in the 1898 article;
- `upādisesa`: not named in the recoverable Proceedings title -> promoted to 1898 title-level co-object;
- khandha psychology: large notebook worksheet -> explicitly omitted analytical infrastructure;
- Sāṃkhya/Buddhism chronology: large research branch -> essentially outside the 1898 paper;
- relation distinctions: notebook working operations -> retained in printed architecture.

## Printed architecture

**pp.126–129:** Warren/Senart; distinguish attachment relation from khandhas as objects subject to attachment. Principal 004 loci: pp.22–23, 48–50, 57–59, 65, 69–70.

**pp.130–131:** karma = particular condition/manner of rebirth; upādāna = fact of rebirth; non-strict temporal sequence; 1–2 / 3–9 / 10–12 architecture. Principal loci: p.30, pp.32–36, p.53.

**pp.131–133:** composite/borrowed provenance does not imply meaningless arrangement; anti-ontological ignorance; impermanence; full khandha taxonomy explicitly omitted. Principal loci: pp.10–12, 20, 24–31, 34–46, 49–55.

**pp.133–136:** `upādi/upādisesa` settled chiefly through usage; ethical rather than physical residue; provisional conclusion retained. Principal loci: p.47, pp.56–71.

## Publication filter

1. relation judgments survive better than large taxonomies;
2. functional distinctions survive better than speculative genealogies;
3. usage-based adjudication survives better than etymological speculation;
4. compositeness survives while strong origin claims are reduced;
5. print lowers conclusion strength to the burden required by the article.

Notebook p.62, `This is all that is required for the purposes of my paper`, is the clearest actor-level claim-jurisdiction boundary.

## Evidence firewall

Secure: 10 Apr circular title; same-circular separation between Lovejoy's registered paper title and the positive expected-presence list in which he is not named; the later Proceedings title carrier reporting the Baltimore meeting; Lovejoy's absence/read-by-title status as recorded in the Proceedings; change in the second named technical term between the Proceedings carrier and 1898; close 004-to-print correspondence; explicit omission of substantial notebook analysis.

Strong inference: the Proceedings title state is closer to the `upādāna / upādānakkhandha` cluster than the 10 Apr Milinda title made visible; 1898 is a recomposed extraction from a broader research field. After the 10 Apr printed state, the title-change mechanism must run through a remote authorial, officer/editorial, Proceedings-normalization, or other non-in-person carrier rather than Lovejoy orally retitling the communication at Baltimore.

HOLD: **when between 10 Apr and the preparation/printing of the Proceedings the title/object changed**; whether the technical-terms title was already the title used on 22–24 Apr; manuscript identity across the circular, Proceedings carrier, and 1898; whether 004 is a direct draft; whether `upādisesa` was already present in the 1897 communication; whether the change was authorial revision, officer/editorial reframing, Proceedings normalization, substitution, or evidence of two papers.

Attendance/title firewall:

> **TITLE REGISTERED != POSITIVE ATTENDANCE RESPONSE; ABSENCE FROM THE POSITIVE-PRESENCE LIST != FORMAL DECLINATION; TITLE REGISTERED != ABSTRACT OR MANUSCRIPT RECEIVED.**

Carrier-date firewall:

> **MEETING DATE != AUTOMATIC DATE OF THE TITLE PRINTED IN POST-MEETING PROCEEDINGS.**

The 1897 meeting report itself says Vol. XVIII Second Half would be issued after the Proceedings of the meeting could be printed. Until a contemporaneous meeting program, title slip, correspondence, minute-book entry, or equivalent witness is recovered, do not compress the title change into a secure `10 Apr -> 22 Apr` revision interval.
