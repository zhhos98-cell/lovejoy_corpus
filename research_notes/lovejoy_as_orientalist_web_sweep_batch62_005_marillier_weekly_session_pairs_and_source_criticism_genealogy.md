## 1. Fresh palaeographic correction: p. 36 is June 19, not June 18

`Marillier June 19. Surviv. 5.`

The canonical OCR had rendered the date as `June 18`, but the final digit is a cursive **9**, not 8. This matters because 19 June 1899 was a **Monday**, exactly the weekday assigned in Marillier’s retrospective report to the survivance conference.

The sheet itself continues the future-life/survivance dossier: post-mortem status, continuing existence, and Callaway/Zulu material. The date/topic/weekday therefore align independently.

Evidence level: **direct name + direct date + direct topic abbreviation + independent weekday/course match**.

Caution: `Surviv. 5.` is transcribed diplomatically. The final `5.` may be a time or another internal notation; no interpretation is made here.

## 4. Material implication: seminar time was re-filed topically

The dates are **not** laid out chronologically in the physical notebook.

- June 12 and June 19 survivance sheets sit in the earlier future-life dossier around MS pp. 55–57.
- June 6 opens the sacrifice/Africa sequence around MS p. 93.
- June 20 survives much later as an inserted sheet around MS p. 187.

Therefore the notebook’s architecture is not best understood as a diary or chronological classroom notebook. The stronger material inference is that **session-generated paper was inserted or retained under topical dossiers**.

This matters because it explains the hybrid structure that has repeatedly caused difficulty in transcription:

`seminar/session time → separate sheet/slip → topical insertion → later source accumulation → analytic reclassification`.

That architecture is exactly what one would expect from a working research notebook in which conference notes, bibliography, source excerpts, and private analytic work remain materially interleaved.

The safest formulation is:

> MS38 005 is a hybrid dossier-book whose topical files contain identifiable strata generated in direct temporal relation to Marillier’s conferences.

It is **not** a lecture transcript.

## 5. Stronger than Mauss’s retrospective testimony: Marillier’s own 1888–1889 course report

The most important new external source in this batch is Marillier’s first EPHE free course report, 1888–1889. This gives a direct institutional description of his method roughly a decade before Lovejoy’s Paris year.

Primary source:
Léon Marillier, `XI. Cours libre`, *Rapport sur l'École pratique des hautes études*, 1888–1889, pp. 238–239.
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1888_num_4_1_19334

The report says that Marillier:

1. compared the evidentiary value of different orders of testimony concerning religions of non-European peoples: direct testimony, beliefs, myths, rites, and customs;
2. distinguished classes of documents — travellers’ accounts, missionary reports, official enquiries, etc.;
3. taught the **rules of criticism** applicable to those classes of evidence;
4. gave his auditors a **summary and reasoned bibliography** of the subject;
5. then moved from documentary criticism to psychological explanation.

This is a direct primary antecedent for the procedure that Mauss later remembered as Marillier’s `critique des faits`.

It also maps remarkably closely onto operations visible in 005:

- missionary/contact contamination is repeatedly treated as a source-critical problem;
- provenance and chronology of ethnographic testimony matter;
- bibliography is physically accumulated on slips and page-specific references;
- outwardly similar rites are not accepted as equivalent evidence;
- the observational report and the observer’s interpretation are separable;
- classification precedes higher causal/developmental explanation.

This does **not** prove that every one of these habits originated with Marillier. It does establish that documentary criticism and reasoned bibliography were explicit features of Marillier’s teaching programme long before Lovejoy entered it.

## 6. The method is continuous across Marillier’s career

The 1888 report is not an isolated pedagogical manifesto.

- In 1892–1893 Marillier described Melanesian institutions, rites, and legends as surviving after the beliefs that generated them had begun to dissolve, while critically reviewing rival totemic theories.
- In 1893–1894 he explicitly conducted `Etude critique` of Melanesian heroic/divine legends and compared versions and analogous traditions.
- In 1894–1895 he performed critical examination of flood traditions and explicitly flagged biblical/missionary influence.
- In 1896–1897 he insisted that union rites in marriage must be carefully distinguished from magical rites of purification/preservation.
- In Lovejoy’s 1898–1899 year, the report defines the sacrifice conference by determining the different **reasons** for human victims across expiatory, funerary, anthropophagic, and magical forms.

## 7. Mauss 1903 gives a concrete afterlife of the same paper practice

A further primary source makes the continuity unusually visible:

Marcel Mauss, `L'origine des pouvoirs magiques dans les sociétés australiennes. Étude analytique et critique de documents ethnographiques`, EPHE Annuaire 1903, pp. 1–55.
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1903_num_17_13_19612

At the opening Mauss says the published study is a **specimen of the critical work regularly carried out for several years in one of his conferences**. The purpose is to separate the value of the author’s testimony from the sociological import of the recorded fact. He also comments on the near-completeness of their printed-document survey and identifies the remaining bibliographic lacunae.

This is particularly useful for the Lovejoy argument because it shows what `critique des faits` looked like as a paper operation after Marillier:

`bibliographic exhaustiveness → author/testimony criticism → fact reconstruction → analytic inference`.

Again, Mauss’s ultimate sociological explanation differs from Lovejoy’s later causal-efficacy analysis. The procedural family resemblance does not require doctrinal identity.

## 8. Updated transmission claim

After Batch 62 the evidence supports the following proposition at high confidence:

> Lovejoy’s 1898–1899 Paris work was not merely contemporaneous with Marillier’s teaching. MS38 005 preserves a sequence of dated strata that tracks the actual Monday/Tuesday conference rhythm reported by the EPHE, including two explicitly labelled Monday survivance sheets and an explicit Marillier sheet dated Tuesday 20 June. Marillier’s own 1888 course report shows that source criticism, differentiation of document classes, rules for evaluating traveller/missionary/official testimony, and reasoned bibliography were constitutive features of his pedagogy a decade earlier. Mauss later explicitly identified `critique des faits`, ethnographic bibliography, and document seminar work as Marillier’s tradition. The most defensible claim of influence is therefore a transmission of documentary procedure and evidentiary discipline, not a transfer of a single doctrine.

## 9. Consequence for the article’s microhistory

The article can now narrate a concrete conversion process rather than a vague intellectual influence:

### Stage A — classroom/research programme
Marillier structures the year around survivance and sacrifice, with explicit source-critical habits already institutionalized in his teaching.

### Stage B — paper capture
Lovejoy dates and labels sheets (`Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`; `Marillier June 19. Surviv. 5.`; `Marillier — […] — June 20`; `Sac. June 6.`).

### Stage C — topical re-filing
The dated sheets are not preserved in calendar order; they are embedded in the relevant thematic dossiers.

### Stage D — independent source work
Lovejoy works through Trumbull, Kingsley, Callaway, Steinmetz, etc., sometimes at exact-page and preserved-sequence level.

### Stage E — reclassification
He keeps the reported facts while changing their evidentiary function: communion becomes only one possible mechanism among intrinsic efficacy, sympathetic transfer, protection, propitiation, funerary provision, social incorporation, etc.

### Stage F — abstraction
By 1906 one branch of this mechanism-sensitive work is abstracted into the published category of impersonal/transmissible/direct efficacy (`Primitive Energetics`).

The important causal claim is therefore not “Marillier taught Lovejoy Primitive Energetics.” It is:

> Marillier’s research environment trained and materially organized a mode of documentary criticism within which Lovejoy’s own mechanism-sensitive classifications could be produced.
