## Core refinement

The earlier six-state model for `Meaning and Understanding` needs one additional split.

`S2 — circulated/submitted state`

must become:

### S2a — collegial draft circulation

Privately or semi-privately circulated manuscript read by colleagues outside a proven formal journal-referee role.

For Skinner this is now DIRECT because the published 1969 article thanks Maurice Mandelbaum and others for sending critical comments on earlier drafts and pointing out mistakes.

### S2b — formal editorial/referee circulation

Submission receipt, executive-editor routing, reader/referee assignment, internal memoranda, revision request, acceptance/rejection and scheduling.

For Skinner this remains OPEN at the manuscript-specific level, but the institutional container and generic workflow are now concrete:

- 1969 front matter routes `Correspondence and Contributions` to the Executive Editor, Richard T. Vann;
- Samuel James 2010 cites a 13 June 1966 Louis Mink internal memorandum on a named submission from `History and Theory Papers, History and Theory Offices, Wesleyan University`.

Therefore:

`S2a COLLEGIAL READER`
!=
`S2b FORMAL REFEREE/EDITOR`.

Mandelbaum is securely S2a. Whether he also occupied S2b for Skinner is an archival question.

## I. Austin is a genuine S3→S4 textual delta

A 2026 methodological discussion based on Adrian Blau's previously unexplored copy of Skinner's 1968 conference paper states that the 1968 version does **not** cite J. L. Austin, while the 1969 published version does.

This matters because John Dunn's April 1968 `The Identity of the History of Ideas` directly uses Austin and Skinner later explicitly credited Dunn with recognizing the relevance of Austinian speech-act theory for intellectual-historical interpretation.

The secure structure is therefore:

`DUNN 1968 USES AUSTIN`
+
`SKINNER LATER ACKNOWLEDGES DUNN'S AUSTINIAN CONTRIBUTION`
+
`SKINNER PSA 1968 VERSION: NO AUSTIN CITATION`
->
`SKINNER H&T 1969 VERSION: AUSTIN ENTERS APPARATUS`.

This is a real versioned change.

What remains open:

`DUNN CAUSED THE 1968→1969 AUSTIN INSERTION`.

Possible routes include:

- Dunn discussion;
- Skinner's own parallel reading;
- collegial draft readers;
- conference feedback;
- H&T editorial/referee process;
- some combination.

Only correspondence, annotations or revision instructions could assign clause-level causation.

## II. Why Austin matters beyond citation counting

The addition is not merely a bibliographic ornament.

Austin becomes part of the philosophical apparatus through which Skinner later specifies linguistic action, intention and illocutionary force.

Thus the conference/published transition may involve not only:

- title change;
- pruning of concrete opponents/examples;
- polemical sharpening;

but also **philosophical apparatus enrichment**.

Current version pattern:

`1968 CONFERENCE STATE`
- more specific named targets (e.g. Hood);
- concrete Gombrich mentality/perception example;
- no Austin citation in the currently controlled typescript;

`1969 PUBLISHED STATE`
- more portable mythology/taxonomy language;
- some concrete examples/targets pruned or reduced;
- Austin enters the apparatus.

This supports a working hypothesis:

### `PORTABLE GENERALIZATION + PHILOSOPHICAL APPARATUS ENRICHMENT`

between S3 and S4.

Do not attribute this pattern to editorial pressure until S2b evidence exists.
