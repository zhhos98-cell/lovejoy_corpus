# Pocock–Skinner correspondence, 1968–69: preservation history and the `Kuhn-Skinner-Pocock` programme

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **PUBLISHED LETTER QUOTATIONS + CORRESPONDENCE-PRESERVATION HISTORY SECURE / FULL LETTER SERIES STILL PRIVATE**

## Core result

The October 1968 `joint manifesto` proposal was not an isolated remark. Published controls show that Pocock continued to formulate a shared but differentiated methodological programme through 1969, while also exploring a recognizably Austinian/action vocabulary of his own.

Richard Whatmore's 2016 introduction reports that over the following years Pocock seriously contemplated a methodological monograph under titles including:

- `The Cave of Speech`;
- `How to Do Things to People with Words`.

A **December 1969** Pocock letter then refers to the `Kuhn-Skinner-Pocock` approach and describes a shared problem in terms of how people do things to one another with words and respond to such attempts.

This extends the programme-consciousness chronology:

`8 OCT 1968 — JOINT MANIFESTO?`
->
`1969 — KUHN-SKINNER-POCOCK APPROACH`
+
`HOW TO DO THINGS TO PEOPLE WITH WORDS`.

The important result is not a unified Cambridge doctrine. It is a documented network in which participants explicitly named methodological affinities while differentiating their preferred explanatory orientations.

---

## I. Austinian/action language is distributed across the network

The emerging chronology now has several distinct Austin/action edges:

1. Dunn 1968 directly uses Austin and later receives Skinner's explicit credit for seeing speech-act theory's relevance.
2. Skinner's currently controlled 1968 PSA version does not cite Austin; the 1969 publication does.
3. Pocock's methodological planning shortly afterward uses the title `How to Do Things to People with Words`, transforming Austin's formulation toward rhetoric, persuasion and political interaction.
4. Pocock's December 1969 formulation treats the problem reciprocally: doing things to others with words and responding to others' verbal actions.

Therefore the relevant historical unit is not:

`SKINNER INVENTS SPEECH-ACT CONTEXTUALISM`.

It is closer to:

`AUSTINIAN RESOURCE`
-> differently appropriated within
`DUNN / SKINNER / POCOCK DISCUSSION AND TEXT NETWORK`.

Do not infer that every verbal similarity derives directly from Austin without source control; the actor-level Dunn/Skinner and Pocock evidence is already strong enough without overclaim.

---

## II. The `Kuhn-Skinner-Pocock` label shows actor-level comparative self-positioning

Pocock's December 1969 letter is particularly valuable because he names an approach by combining three actors rather than a later `Cambridge School` label.

Whatmore reports that Pocock thought all three were addressing a common question, while distinguishing Kuhn's scientific paradigm model from the rhetoric/persuasion problem Pocock wanted to pursue in political thought.

Thus actor-level grouping already contains internal differentiation.

The relation rule is:

`NAMED PROGRAMME AFFINITY`
!=
`METHOD IDENTITY`.

This is stronger than later retrospective school labels because the differentiation is contemporaneous.

---

## III. Correspondence survival/provenance is now much clearer

A 2025 study of Pocock's correspondence history explains why these letters are accessible at all.

Richard Whatmore discovered that few of Pocock's letters from the period had been retained either by Pocock or by their recipients. Quentin Skinner was an important exception. Skinner allowed Whatmore to copy letters exchanged between them from the **1960s through the publication of *The Machiavellian Moment* in 1975**.

Therefore the currently cited correspondence has a concrete preservation chain:

`POCOCK -> SKINNER LETTERS`
-> retained in `SKINNER PRIVATE PAPERS`
-> `SKINNER ALLOWS WHATMORE TO COPY`
-> selected quotations enter published scholarship
-> Skinner later uses the larger 1965–2020 correspondence corpus in his 2025 JHI memoir.

This is materially different from a formally deposited public archive.

Add firewall:

`PUBLISHED QUOTATION FROM PRIVATE CORRESPONDENCE`
!=
`PUBLIC ARCHIVAL ACCESS TO FULL SERIES`.

---

## IV. Loss itself affects later historiography

Because many Pocock letters were lost while Skinner retained a substantial run, the surviving archive is structurally asymmetric.

Potential consequence:

`SURVIVING CORRESPONDENCE NETWORK`
may overrepresent
`RELATIONS PRESERVED BY SKINNER'S FILES`.

This is not a reason to distrust the letters. It is a reason to treat archival survival as part of canon/network formation.

New useful distinction:

`HISTORICAL NETWORK`
!=
`SURVIVING DOCUMENTARY NETWORK`.

The correspondence history therefore connects directly to the project's canon-memory infrastructure line.

---

## V. Programme formation without a joint manifesto

The joint manifesto did not materialize as a coauthored foundational text.

Later scholarship emphasizes real divergence between Pocock and Skinner, and the December 1969 letter already encodes different emphases.

This gives a useful anti-teleological pattern:

`SHARED PROGRAMME CONSCIOUSNESS`
+
`RECIPROCAL SCRIPT/LETTER CIRCULATION`
+
`DIVERGENT UNIT / RELATION PREFERENCES`
->
`SEPARATE PUBLICATION TRAJECTORIES`.

The absence of a joint manifesto is therefore not evidence that there was no shared methodological conversation.

Conversely, shared conversation does not justify collapsing their methods into one Cambridge School doctrine.

---

## VI. Relation to Skinner 1969's canonical success

The network now makes a new question possible:

> Why did Skinner's 1969 article, rather than a joint programme or Pocock's planned methodological monograph, become the portable canonical manifesto?

Potential variables to test later:

- Skinner's compact taxonomy of `mythologies`;
- publication timing and venue;
- polemical rhetoric;
- subsequent reprinting/anthologization;
- Pocock's failure to complete a planned method monograph;
- dispersion of Pocock's method across scripts/articles/letters;
- later Tully packaging;
- differential archival/documentary survival.

This is a canon-formation question, not a priority contest.
