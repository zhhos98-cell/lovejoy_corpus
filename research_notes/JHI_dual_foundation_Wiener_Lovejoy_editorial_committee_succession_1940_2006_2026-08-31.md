# JHI's dual foundation: Lovejoy, Wiener, the editorial coalition, and executive succession, 1940–2006

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **MULTI-SOURCE RETROSPECTIVE CONTROL / FOUNDING AGENCY DISAGGREGATED / FIRST-ISSUE MASTHEAD IMAGE STILL DESIRABLE**

## Core correction

The institutional founding of the *Journal of the History of Ideas* should no longer be narrated simply as:

`LOVEJOY FOUNDS JHI`
→ `WIENER LATER SUCCEEDS HIM`.

Three independent retrospective controls, written from different institutional positions, support a more differentiated reconstruction:

> **Lovejoy supplied the strongest intellectual/programmatic authority and briefly occupied the public chief-editorship; Philip P. Wiener supplied much of the organizational initiative and day-to-day editorial labor and then became the durable executive center of the journal; an interdisciplinary editorial committee and the ACLS History of Ideas machinery supplied a broader field-building coalition.**

The useful founding model is therefore:

`PROGRAMMATIC / SYMBOLIC FOUNDER`
+
`OPERATIONAL / ORGANIZATIONAL FOUNDER`
+
`INTERDISCIPLINARY EDITORIAL COALITION`
+
`ACLS FIELD-BUILDING AUTHORIZATION`.

These are overlapping but non-identical agencies.

---

## I. Donald R. Kelley 2000: first-issue division of labor

Donald R. Kelley, `JHI 2000`, *Journal of the History of Ideas* 61.1 (2000), 153–156, gives an unusually specific retrospective of the first issue.

Kelley states that the first issue appeared under Lovejoy's editorship, with **Philip Wiener as `managing editor` who did most of the editorial work**.

He also reconstructs the initial editorial committee as deliberately interdisciplinary. The ten members were:

- Crane Brinton;
- Gilbert Chinard;
- Morris R. Cohen;
- Francis Coker;
- Richard McKeon;
- Perry Miller;
- Marjorie Hope Nicolson;
- John Herman Randall, Jr.;
- J. Salwyn Schapiro;
- Louis B. Wright.

Kelley classifies the committee as including historians, philosophers, literary scholars and a political theorist.

Most of these figures contributed to the journal very early, making the committee more than honorary masthead decoration.

### Implication

The first journal regime is best represented as:

`LOVEJOY — PUBLIC / PROGRAMMATIC EDITORIAL AUTHORITY`
+
`WIENER — MANAGING / EXECUTIVE LABOR`
+
`CROSS-DISCIPLINARY COMMITTEE — DISTRIBUTED CONTENT INFRASTRUCTURE`.

This immediately explains why `JHI practice` cannot safely be identified with Lovejoy's own preferred unit-idea method.

## II. 1979 Lovejoy biographical supplement: organizational initiative came from City College / Wiener

The Lovejoy entry in the 1979 *International Encyclopedia of the Social Sciences* biographical supplement supplies a complementary institutional memory.

It first characterizes the Johns Hopkins History of Ideas Club as an `invisible college` which fostered scholarly development.

But when the account reaches JHI it makes a specific distinction:

- the **organizational initiative** for founding the journal came from **City College of New York**;
- chiefly from Philip P. Wiener, then an instructor;
- Lovejoy served for about a year as editor in chief after publication began in 1940.

This is not inconsistent with Lovejoy's well-established intellectual role. It distinguishes:

`WHERE THE PROGRAMMATIC PREHISTORY WAS BUILT`
from
`WHERE PERIODICAL ORGANIZATION WAS OPERATIONALLY MOBILIZED`.

Thus the geography of institutional formation is at least two-centered:

`JOHNS HOPKINS — CLUB / PROBLEM PROGRAMME / LOVEJOY AUTHORITY`

and

`CITY COLLEGE — WIENER / EDITORIAL ORGANIZATION / JOURNAL OPERATIONS`.

The ACLS national committee adds a third scale between them.

## III. Warren Breckman 2007: Lovejoy as driving force, Wiener as first durable editor

Warren Breckman, then a member of the new JHI editorial team, published a retrospective history of the journal in 2007.

His account uses two formulations which are highly useful when kept distinct:

1. Lovejoy was the **driving force behind the founding** and his historical/methodological writings strongly shaped the young field;
2. the philosopher **Philip Wiener became the first editor** and carried that task for more than forty years, before Donald R. Kelley took over in 1985.

Breckman also insists that JHI never functioned simply as a mouthpiece for Lovejoy, even during Lovejoy's lifetime.

He emphasizes the journal's flexible and ecumenical character and notes that only a small fraction of JHI articles actually performed the long-range tracking of unit-ideas in the form Lovejoy's most programmatic methodological language might lead one to expect.

Most strikingly, Breckman suggests that the more authentic institutional realization of Lovejoy's encyclopedic/synthetic ideal may have been **Wiener's 1973–74 *Dictionary of the History of Ideas*** rather than the ordinary run of JHI articles.

This gives a new internal differentiation:

`JHI = FLEXIBLE / PLURAL PERIODICAL EXECUTION`

while

`DHI = MORE CONCENTRATED REFERENCE-ARCHITECTURE REALIZATION OF LOVEJOY'S SYNTHETIC VISION`.

That distinction is especially useful after the new Dictionary global-canon dossier.

## IV. Founding agency should be typed, not adjudicated as one 'true founder'

The sources produce different founder attributions because they answer different institutional questions.

### Lovejoy

Strongest for:

- Hopkins Club and prehistory;
- public intellectual programme;
- anti-departmentalization argument;
- first issue's manifesto;
- name/symbolic authority;
- first-year chief editorship;
- ACLS committee chairmanship in official retrospective.

### Wiener

Strongest for:

- City College organizational initiative;
- managing-editor labor from the beginning;
- very long editorial continuity;
- operational maintenance of JHI Inc.;
- International Society;
- 1961 methodological programme;
- Dictionary;
- global/editorial networking.

### Editorial Committee

Strongest for:

- cross-disciplinary coalition;
- early contributor recruitment;
- distributed intellectual scope;
- protection against one-man methodological orthodoxy.

### ACLS

Strongest for:

- national field authorization/planning;
- History of Ideas Committee, 1929–39;
- developmental-committee infrastructure;
- incubation of the journal as one field-building outcome.

The historical question therefore should not be:

`Who really founded JHI?`

but:

> **Which work of founding was performed by which actor and institution?**

This follows the project's broader relation-specific jurisdiction rule and prevents one retrospective label `founder` from collapsing intellectual, administrative, financial, editorial and legal acts.

## V. Executive succession is exceptionally concentrated

The next striking feature is how few executive regime changes JHI experienced.

### Regime 1 — Wiener, 1940–1985

Kelley's 2005 farewell states that Wiener edited the journal for **forty-five years** before Kelley replaced him, with Kelley's first supervised issue appearing July 1985.

Wiener's academic location changed:

- City College of New York;
- later Temple University;

and the journal's practical/institutional address moved with the editorial operation.

### Regime 2 — Donald R. Kelley, 1985–2005

Kelley reports:

- journal moved from Temple to University of Rochester in 1985, with support from J. Paul Hunter and Lewis White Beck;
- Kelley succeeded Wiener;
- Robin Ladrach joined in 1986 as assistant and later associate editor;
- Kelley and Ladrach moved the journal to Rutgers in 1991, supported institutionally by Richard McCormick;
- Kelley served about twenty years.

Kelley describes much of the journal's change during this period as coming `from below` through changing submissions/intellectual culture rather than strong conscious editorial engineering.

### Regime 3 — team editorship, 2005/06 onward

In 2005/06 the journal moved to the University of Pennsylvania and University of Pennsylvania Press.

For the first time, executive authority moved from one exceptionally long-serving editor to a **team**:

- Warren Breckman;
- Martin J. Burke;
- Anthony Grafton;
- Ann E. Moyer;

with Robin Ladrach continuing as managing editor.

The 2006 editorial statement explicitly recommits the journal to diversity in:

- regional coverage;
- chronological range;
- methodological approaches;

and defines intellectual history expansively across philosophy, literature/arts, natural/social sciences, religion and political thought.

Thus 2005/06 is a real governance-form change:

`LONG SINGLE-EDITOR REGIMES`
→ `DISTRIBUTED EXECUTIVE TEAM`.

## VI. Philosophers → historians is a real but incomplete regime shift

Breckman identifies a second long-term change.

The first JHI era was heavily shaped by philosophers. In his retrospective, he interprets the early historical approach to philosophy partly as a corrective to idealism and pragmatism within the philosophical field.

From Kelley onward, historians dominate executive editorship more clearly.

But Breckman cautions that the shift did **not** produce as radical a break in JHI's course as a disciplinary replacement model would predict.

So the regime history is better:

`PHILOSOPHER-HEAVY EDITORIAL CORE`
→ `HISTORIAN-HEAVY EXECUTIVE CORE`

with persistent interdisciplinary breadth and internal methodological plurality.

This should be tested against board composition longitudinally rather than inferred from editor profession alone.

## VII. The History of Ideas Series: a second editorial output of the JHI coalition

Princeton/History of Ideas Series front matter preserves a later Editorial Committee:

- John Herman Randall, Jr., chairman;
- George Boas;
- Gilbert Chinard;
- Paul O. Kristeller;
- Arthur O. Lovejoy;
- Marjorie H. Nicolson;
- Philip P. Wiener.

The series included works such as:

- Cassirer, *Rousseau-Kant-Goethe*;
- Nicolson, *Newton Demands the Muse*;
- Benjamin Nelson, *The Idea of Usury*;
- Derk Bodde, *Tolstoy and China*;
- J. H. Hexter, *More's Utopia*;
- Aram Vartanian, *Diderot and Descartes*.

This is another institutional form generated by the same editorial ecology, positioned between journal article and Dictionary-scale reference canon.

It further weakens a journal-only genealogy:

`JHI COALITION`
→ `PERIODICAL`
+
`BOOK SERIES`
+
later `DICTIONARY`
+
`INTERNATIONAL SOCIETY`.

A complete history of JHI must therefore reconstruct a multi-output publishing ecology, not only the quarterly.

## VIII. New institutional morphology

The best current high-level map is:

`HOPKINS CLUB`
= local discussion/invisible college

`ACLS HISTORY OF IDEAS COMMITTEE`
= national research planning / field authorization

`LOVEJOY`
= programmatic authority / public first-year leadership

`WIENER + CITY COLLEGE`
= organizational initiative / managing labor / long executive infrastructure

`EDITORIAL COMMITTEE`
= interdisciplinary coalition

`JHI INC.`
= durable legal/publishing organization

`HISTORY OF IDEAS SERIES`
= monographic extension

`INTERNATIONAL SOCIETY`
= international conference network

`DICTIONARY`
= reference-canon architecture

`KELLEY / TEAM REGIMES`
= historiographical self-reflection + later governance transformation.

This is now a history of **infrastructures for moving ideas across scholarly jurisdictions**, not merely the institutional biography of Lovejoy's method.

## IX. Evidence tensions to preserve

Different retrospectives use `editor`, `founder`, and `driving force` differently.

- Kelley 2000: Lovejoy editor; Wiener managing editor who did most editorial work.
- 1979 biographical supplement: organizational initiative City College/chiefly Wiener; Lovejoy editor-in-chief for about a year.
- Breckman 2007: Lovejoy driving force; Wiener first editor for forty-plus years.
- Kelley 2005: Wiener editor for forty-five years; Lovejoy original founder.

Do **not** harmonize these by silently choosing one title.

Instead code the disagreement as evidence about institutional roles:

`FORMAL TITLE`
!= `ACTUAL LABOR`
!= `ORGANIZATIONAL INITIATIVE`
!= `PROGRAMMATIC AUTHORITY`
!= `RETROSPECTIVE FOUNDER MEMORY`.

Highest-value remaining primary control:

- direct image of the 1940 first-issue masthead / volume information;
- JHI Inc. founding papers;
- ACLS journal-launch correspondence;
- Wiener papers if surviving;
- City College institutional records.
