# Batch 136 — Joseph Trumbull Stickney as an American Indianist control in Lovejoy's Paris year

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: test whether the immediate Paris Indianist environment around Lovejoy contained an American near-peer whose trajectory could clarify how Sylvain Lévi's teaching worked for foreign students and whether there is any presently visible transmission route from Harvard/US study into the 1898–99 Paris curriculum.

## Core result

Joseph Trumbull Stickney is now the strongest American near-peer control yet identified for Lovejoy's Paris year, but **not** a documented intermediary between Lovejoy and Lévi.

Stickney was Harvard A.B. 1895, then spent the following years in Paris studying toward the Doctorat ès Lettres. In the exact 1898–99 EPHE year when Lovejoy was active in Marillier's seminar, Stickney appears simultaneously at the center of both major Indianist teaching layers:

- in Alfred Foucher's V-Section `Religions de l'Inde`, he was an `élève titulaire`, followed the Nyāya course, and himself took charge of a series of conferences on **Sāṃkhya according to Garbe**;
- in Sylvain Lévi's IV-Section Sanskrit teaching, he possessed a solid knowledge of Sanskrit and Indian philosophy and took a **constant and very active** part in the Bhagavad-Gītā reading, planning a translation before illness interrupted the work.

This is unusually close to Lovejoy's own archive. Notebook 004 is saturated with Sāṃkhya/Buddhism chronology, Garbe/Jacobi-type genealogy questions and Buddhist technical vocabulary; while Lovejoy was working in the Paris religious-sciences environment, another American Harvard-trained student was literally presenting Sāṃkhya from Garbe and reading the Bhagavad-Gītā intensively with Lévi.

The strongest methodological gain, however, comes one year later. The 1900–01 Sanskrit report explicitly says that **outside the two regular conferences** (`en dehors de ces deux conférences`) Lévi continued with Stickney the Bhagavad-Gītā reading left unfinished the previous year. This is direct contemporary evidence that Lévi's pedagogy included individualized or supplementary textual work beyond the advertised formal courses.

That makes Wilson's report about Lovejoy — desired Pāli-Buddhism course unavailable, followed by occasional private sacred-text reading with Lévi — institutionally recognizable in a much stronger way. It still does not prove that Lovejoy and Stickney knew one another or that Lovejoy learned of Lévi through Stickney.

---

## 1. Stickney is a Harvard-to-Paris near-peer, but not a Harvard classmate of Lovejoy

Biographical notices consistently give Stickney as entering Harvard in 1891 and graduating with high classical honors / magna cum laude in 1895. He then returned to Europe and spent the next years in Paris, eventually receiving the Doctorat ès Lettres in 1903.

Useful controls:

- Harvard-era / posthumous biographical summary in the 1913 *Little Book of Modern Verse*;
- Poetry Foundation biography;
- University of Toronto Representative Poetry Online biographical record.

The chronology matters because a tempting but incorrect shortcut would be to call Stickney and Lovejoy Harvard classmates. They were not.

- Stickney: Harvard undergraduate through June 1895, then Paris.
- Lovejoy: Berkeley A.B. 1895, entered Harvard graduate study in autumn 1895, Harvard A.M. 1897, Paris 1898–99.

Thus there is **no demonstrated Harvard campus overlap**. Their relation is better described as:

`adjacent Harvard trajectories → simultaneous Paris presence by 1898–99`.

This still matters as a transmission ecology, but it blocks a false direct-network inference.

---

## 2. 1898–99: Stickney is inside Foucher's Nyāya course and gives the Sāṃkhya/Garbe series

The primary EPHE V-Section report for 1898–99 names the most engaged students in Foucher's course on Indian logical doctrines according to Nyāya:

`MM. Lacôte, Mauss et Stickney, élèves titulaires`.

It then states that Stickney had taken charge of:

`une série de conférences sur le système philosophique du Sânkhya d'après les travaux du professeur Garbe`.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1898_num_12_8_19504

This is a particularly strong control for notebook 004 because Garbe is not a generic Indianist name in this project. The live late-1890s Sāṃkhya–Buddhism chronology and dependence problem is one of the most distinctive parts of Lovejoy's early Buddhist work.

The correct claim is therefore not that Lovejoy learned Sāṃkhya from Stickney. It is:

> **In Lovejoy's documented Paris year, an American Harvard-trained student in the same EPHE Indianist ecology was actively teaching a Sāṃkhya series based on Garbe while Lovejoy's own archive preserves intensive Sāṃkhya/Buddhism work.**

That substantially thickens the period context while remaining below the threshold of personal contact.

---

## 3. 1898–99: Stickney is also Lévi's most visible Bhagavad-Gītā student

The primary IV-Section Sanskrit report for 1898–99 says Lévi's first weekly conference was devoted to the Bhagavad-Gītā. It singles out Stickney:

- solid knowledge of Sanskrit;
- solid knowledge of Indian philosophy;
- constant and very active participation;
- intended translation of the poem, interrupted by illness late in the year.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1900_num_1_1_2251

The same report says Stickney had begun preparing a doctoral thesis partly on Indian gnomic literature and predicts that he would honor the École's teaching.

This is a much closer analogue to Lovejoy than a generic list of French Sanskritists. Stickney was:

- American;
- Harvard-trained;
- resident in Paris;
- advanced but still a student;
- working directly with Lévi on a major Sanskrit text;
- simultaneously active in the religious-sciences Indian-philosophy conference.

He therefore becomes an excellent control for what a foreign advanced student could actually do inside Lévi's Paris teaching network in 1898–99.

---

## 4. 1899–1900: the relation continues in the V Section

The next `Religions de l'Inde` report names Stickney as the `élève titulaire`. He continued the explanation of Manu with Foucher, while philosophical Nyāya readings were added at Hubert's request.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1899_num_13_9_19527

This shows that Stickney's Indianist role in 1898–99 was not a short curiosity produced by one semester. He remained structurally embedded in the EPHE Indian-religions network.

Again, this later persistence is contextual. It should not be used to infer that he mediated Lovejoy's 1898 arrival.

---

## 5. 1900–01 gives the strongest formal-course/private-work control yet found

The IV-Section Sanskrit report for 1900–01 is especially important because after describing Lévi's two regular conferences it explicitly adds:

`En dehors de ces deux conférences, M. Lévi a poursuivi, avec M. Stickney, la lecture de la Bhagavad-Gîtâ, restée en suspens ...`

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1901_num_1_1_2303

Even with the currently truncated web extraction, the evidentiary structure is unequivocal:

`two regular advertised conferences`

+

`outside those conferences, continued text reading with an individual advanced student`.

This is the closest contemporary institutional control we have for Wilson's Lovejoy story.

It demonstrates that a student's real pedagogical relationship with Lévi could exceed the public programme. Therefore:

`no formal Pāli course in the 1898–99 programme`

is perfectly compatible with

`supplementary or individualized sacred-text reading`.

The analogy has a strict limit: the report does **not** say Stickney met Lévi at Lévi's home. We should not use Stickney's supplementary Bhagavad-Gītā reading to prove the location or content of Lovejoy's reported private sessions.

---

## 6. The relationship ultimately generated a durable textual object

The BnF France–South Asia heritage portal explains that the later French Bhagavad-Gītā translation published under Sylvain Lévi and Joseph Trumbull Stickney grew out of Stickney's student work, directed and subsequently revised/enriched by Lévi.

Institutional source:

https://heritage.bnf.fr/france-southasia/en/node/15744

This provides a later outcome control: the intense student–teacher textual work visible in the annual reports was not merely classroom ephemera. It generated a manuscript translation that survived Stickney's early death and was eventually published in 1938.

The later edition should not be back-projected to date every stage of the collaboration. The annual EPHE reports remain the correct chronological anchors.

## 7. Search for a direct Lovejoy–Stickney link: current result is negative but narrow

Targeted searches of:

- JHU ArchivesSpace Lovejoy collection descriptions / correspondence index;
- Harvard HOLLIS archival discovery;
- general indexed web material;

have not surfaced a `Lovejoy–Stickney`, `Trumbull Stickney–Lovejoy`, or other direct correspondence component.

This is **not** evidence they never met. It is only a current digital-exposure negative.

The strongest present formulation is:

> **Stickney is a same-year American near-peer and pedagogical control, not yet a transmission node.**

That distinction should be maintained throughout the paper.

## 8. Harvard has a small retrospective Stickney research collection

Harvard University Archives exposes:

**Richard Sherman Nutt collection on Trumbull Stickney, 1892–1954 — HUM 420**

Extent: one document box / 0.17 cubic feet.

HOLLIS describes it as documenting Nutt's research into Stickney's life and influence, principally 1947–54.

This is not Stickney's own fonds, and there is no present positive Lovejoy component. But it becomes a reciprocal archival lead if we later need to reconstruct:

- Stickney's Paris friendships;
- reproduced letters;
- student chronology;
- relationship with Lévi;
- Harvard-to-Paris advice networks.

Because the collection is later research material, it should remain lower priority than the primary EPHE records and Lovejoy's own 1898 letters.

---

## 9. Consequence for the 'how did Lovejoy know to expect Pāli?' question

Batch135 shifted the problem from whether Pāli study with Lévi was plausible to **how Lovejoy learned to expect it**.

Stickney now supplies one concrete transmission ecology without yet supplying transmission proof:

- Harvard-trained American in Paris since 1895;
- highly visible in Lévi/Foucher Indianist teaching by 1898–99;
- directly teaching Sāṃkhya/Garbe material;
- working intensively with Lévi on Bhagavad-Gītā;
- later receiving supplementary text work outside formal conferences.

This means an American student network is no longer an abstract possibility. It existed materially inside the very courses relevant to Lovejoy.

But the missing edge remains:

`Stickney / other American student → Lovejoy`.

The next digital task should therefore be a **student-network reconstruction**, not another generic Lévi biography search. Priority candidates include Stickney's Harvard and Paris correspondents, W. S. Andrews and other Anglophone/American names in the Marillier attendance list, and any recent EPHE students whose letters survive digitally.

---

## Evidence cautions

1. Stickney and Lovejoy were not Harvard classmates; do not collapse adjacent Harvard trajectories into co-presence.
2. Same place/year and overlapping research problems do not establish acquaintance.
3. Stickney's Sāṃkhya series is direct evidence of the Paris problem field, not evidence Lovejoy attended it.
4. `En dehors de ces deux conférences` is strong evidence for supplementary teaching with Stickney, but not for a home setting.
5. The later joint Bhagavad-Gītā publication proves durable collaboration, not the exact timing of every translation decision.
6. No positive Lovejoy–Stickney correspondence has surfaced in the current indexed archival layer.
