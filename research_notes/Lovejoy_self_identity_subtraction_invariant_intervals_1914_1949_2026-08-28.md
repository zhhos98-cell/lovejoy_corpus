# Lovejoy — self-identity subtraction and invariant intervals, 1914–1949

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **RESTART-SAFE CHECKPOINT / POSITIVE + NEGATIVE IDENTITY BOUNDS / GATE-A REFINEMENT**

## Core result

The new `self-identity subtraction test` can be paired with negative identity thresholds to reconstruct not one guessed essence but an **invariant interval** for a Lovejoyian unit.

Two evidentiary operations provide opposite bounds:

1. **positive subtraction bound** — when Lovejoy explicitly says two differing formulations are the `same`, `essentially same`, `precisely` the same conception, preserve an `essential character`, or otherwise persist as one idea, any feature that differs across the pair is not safely necessary to minimum identity;
2. **negative break bound** — when Lovejoy explicitly treats a transformation as a contrast, reversal, breach, nonidentity, or destruction of the relevant principle, the lost/reversed feature is a candidate constitutive lower bound.

Thus, instead of asking:

`What is the timeless essence of unit X?`

ask:

> **How thick can X be before Lovejoy's own positive identity judgments force us to subtract features, and how thin can X become before his negative judgments say the identity has broken?**

Analyst notation:

`positive same-case subtraction`
→ **upper bound on invariant thickness**

`negative break-case threshold`
→ **lower bound on constitutive content**.

The space between them is the current **invariant interval**.

This is a reconstruction of Lovejoy's historical practice, not a claim that he formalized such intervals.

# I. Primitive Energetics, 1906→1914 — mechanical regularity is above the identity floor

Direct primary controls:

- Lovejoy 1906, `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`;
- Leuba 1912, *A Psychological Study of Religion*, p.75;
- Lovejoy 1914 review of Leuba, pp.218–220.

## Positive subtraction

Leuba accepts a widespread `non-personal Power or Potency` but explicitly rejects Lovejoy's stronger description of that power as operating by `quite regular and intelligible laws`. He stresses unpredictability and the absence of a stable quantitative relation between ritual means and effects, and prefers `magical force` over `mechanical Power`.

Lovejoy in 1914 nevertheless says Leuba's theory of the origin and content of primitive `impersonal power` is **essentially** Lovejoy's 1906 theory and that Leuba's `dynamism` is **precisely** the conception Lovejoy called `primitive energetics` / `manitouism`.

Therefore the following features are demonstrably **subtractable without destroying Lovejoy's own same-theory judgment**:

- the label (`dynamism` vs `primitive energetics` / `manitouism`);
- strict mechanical terminology;
- strong regularity/predictability;
- stable quantitative means–effect relation.

Likely also nonconstitutive at identity level:

- demonstrable pre-animistic chronological priority, since Lovejoy calls this only probable, not demonstrable, while strongly preserving theory identity.

## Current lower-bound candidate

What remains common and actor-explicit is thinner:

> **a conception of impersonal power/potency not reducible to personal-agent ontology.**

Lovejoy also treats its cross-cultural lexical/phenomenal expression and importance as central to his own theory, but the current evidence does not yet show how much of localization, transfer, ritual manipulation, or diffusion is strictly identity-essential.

### Invariant interval

Too thick:

`impersonal power + transfer/localization + manipulability + strict mechanical regularity + pre-animistic priority`.

Safely subtractable:

`strict mechanical/quantitative regularity`, `exact label`, `demonstrable chronological priority`.

Current floor candidate:

`impersonal power/potency contrasted with personal agency`.

Negative break case still needed to establish the floor more sharply.

# II. Plenitude / Chain — Schelling supplies the subtraction bound; Whitehead supplies the break bound

Direct mature controls are already locked in the repository from *The Great Chain of Being*.

## A. Schelling / temporalized Chain — radical transformation remains the same `essential character`

Lovejoy's temporalization history changes the traditional complex profoundly:

- static inventory → temporal program;
- simultaneous fullness → realization distributed through cosmic history;
- completed plenum → `flying goal`;
- top-down emanative direction → bottom-up/ascent developmental direction;
- God outside/above completed series → God can enter or be identified with process/becoming;
- some inferential/theological accompaniments change.

Yet Lovejoy explicitly says the transformation does not alter the Scale's `essential character` and identifies surviving generativeness, diversity-production, and maximal fullness pressure.

### Positive subtraction result

The following are not safely identity-essential for plenitude/Chain persistence:

- static ontology;
- simultaneity;
- completed actuality at one time;
- downward vs upward genetic direction;
- one fixed theological relation of God to the series;
- one fixed full inferential profile.

## B. Whitehead — limitation crosses the lower boundary

Lovejoy's final treatment contrasts the traditional principle of fecundity/fullness with Whitehead's positive `principle of limitation` / selection/particularization.

The constitutive reversal is approximately:

`possibility/diversity presses toward realization/fullness`
→
`limitation/selection is primitive and positive`.

### Negative break result

What cannot be subtracted without losing plenitude identity is not simultaneity or a fixed cosmology. It is the **orientation of the operator**:

> **positive anti-selective pressure toward realization, generativeness, diversity and fullness.**

### Invariant interval

Upper subtraction bound:

remove temporality, direction, completion-status, theological rationale, substantial inferential accompaniments.

Lower break bound:

retain positive fullness/generative orientation; reverse it into primitive limitation/selection and Lovejoy marks contrast rather than persistence.

This is currently the cleanest two-sided invariant interval in the corpus.

# III. Locke — a probable additional subtraction: modal force may be above the identity floor

Current status remains **mediated / direct Lovejoy footnote image not yet locked**.

Existing reliable scholarship reports Lovejoy's qualification that Locke does not insist on the `a priori necessity` of plenitude and continuity, while Lovejoy nevertheless retains Locke within the Chain history.

If the original footnote is confirmed directly, it supplies another subtraction:

`necessity`
→ `probability / weaker completeness`
while
`positive fullness/gradation expectation`
remains.

This would mean that **modal force itself is not constitutive**, strengthening the plenitude interval:

`necessary fullness`
not required;
`orientation toward fullness`
required.

Until direct image lock, treat this only as a borderline mediated extension of the Schelling/Whitehead result.

# IV. 1941 `infinite` — positive common-core subtraction without a known negative actor case

Direct primary: Lovejoy, `The Meaning of Romanticism for the Historian of Ideas` (1941), pp.262–263.

Lovejoy distinguishes multiple senses/applications of `infinite` but says they share an historically important negative element: absence of limitation/completion.

## Positive subtraction

Lovejoy's own grouping explicitly permits subtraction of much positive semantic content:

- mathematical/metaphysical/aesthetic/theological application can vary;
- positive elaborations and entailments vary;
- uses need not be mutually identical.

The common-core grouping survives at the thinner level:

`not limited / not completed`.

This is M2 rather than strict M1 identity, so the correct interpretation is not that all rich meanings are one proposition. Lovejoy deliberately chooses a thin common conceptual component.

## Current interval

Upper subtraction bound:

most positive elaboration/application can be removed.

Lower floor:

negative no-limit/no-completion element.

But no direct Lovejoy negative comparison has yet been recovered in which a superficially related `infinite` usage lacks that core and is explicitly excluded. The lower boundary is therefore actor-stated as a common element, not yet stress-tested by a refusal case.

# V. 1938/1948 — Lovejoy explicitly allows changing facets, roles, combinations, conflicts and attitudes

Evidence status: **exact Lovejoy loci identified; current wording controlled through Nico Mouton's 2023 open-access transcription/discussion; direct 1948 page image remains desirable for publication-grade quotation**.

Mouton identifies two especially important Lovejoy statements:

- `The Historiography of Ideas`, p.9: a persisting idea may exhibit different facets at different stages, play different historical roles, enter different combinations and conflicts, and be met with different attitudes by successive users;
- 1948 `Author's Preface`, p.xii: Lovejoy stresses `identity in the differences` and differences against a background of identity.

These statements provide a general mature subtraction licence broader than any single unit case.

If the transcription is accurate, the following are **not automatically identity-essential**:

- facet currently salient;
- historical role;
- co-components;
- conflict/alliance profile;
- user's evaluative/affective attitude.

This is a very strong ceiling on invariant thickness.

But it does **not** specify what must remain in every case. It is therefore a general positive-subtraction principle, not a complete identity criterion.

The central unresolved question becomes exactly:

> **what feature remains stable after facets, roles, alliances, and attitudes have all been allowed to vary?**

That answer must be recovered unit by unit.

# VI. 1949 — whole-complex identity is explicitly less stable than component persistence/recurrence

Primary locus: Lovejoy, `Historiography and Evaluation: A Disclaimer`, JHI 10.1 (1949), p.142. Current exact passage is securely located and reproduced in Nico Mouton's open-access article; direct JSTOR page image remains desirable for publication-grade quotation.

Lovejoy says later actors select different components of an original complex, combine them with ideas from other sources, and generate divergent new configurations. The whole complex seldom persists as initially constituted, though persistence or recurrence of individual components can be discerned.

## Subtraction consequence

At the **component** scale:

- whole-complex configuration is not identity-essential;
- persistence/recurrence of a component can survive changed co-components and new configurations.

At the **complex** scale:

- Lovejoy explicitly refuses strong persistence of the whole as the normal case.

This supplies a scale-dependent subtraction rule:

> **features belonging to a surrounding complex cannot automatically be imported into the invariant of the component being tracked.**

And it blocks the reverse move:

`same components recur`
↛ `same whole complex persists`.

This is a direct bridge between Gate A object typing and invariant thickness.

# VII. Result: Lovejoy's identity practice is asymmetric

Across the best positive cases, Lovejoy permits surprisingly large historical transformation:

- labels change;
- modalities weaken;
- temporal ontology changes;
- direction of development reverses;
- positive semantic elaborations change;
- roles, accompaniments, alliances/conflicts and attitudes can change;
- whole configurations can dissolve and recombine.

Yet his negative cases show that not everything is dispensable.

For units with a clear operator, Lovejoy's lower boundary appears to track **directional/relational structure** more often than rich doctrinal packaging:

- plenitude: fullness/generative orientation;
- continuity: degree-along-common-character / no irreducible qualitative jump;
- unilinear gradation: one privileged ascending ordering;
- Primitive Energetics, provisional: impersonal power vs personal-agent ontology.

This suggests a stronger but still provisional hypothesis:

> **Lovejoy often preserves identity by thinning historical formulations toward a relational, directional, functional, or generative operator, while treating temporal mode, rationale, application, co-components and some inferential consequences as variable.**

Do not generalize this into one universal ontology of unit-ideas. Lovejoy's 1941 `infinite` common-core case is semantic rather than obviously operator-like, and the Great Chain's three canonical units are themselves heterogeneous logical types.

# IX. Methodological payoff: replace `essence` with bounded case law

The invariant-interval method changes the unit-idea problem materially.

A critic or defender should not begin by asking whether Lovejoy's units have timeless essences.

For a concrete unit:

1. collect Lovejoy's explicit positive sameness/persistence judgments;
2. subtract every changed feature that Lovejoy demonstrably tolerates;
3. collect explicit negative/nonidentity/breach/reversal judgments;
4. identify the feature whose removal/reversal Lovejoy treats as identity-destroying;
5. leave the interval open where only one side is evidenced;
6. separately test whether the resulting thin invariant has sufficient explanatory/historical warrant.

This produces a jurisprudence of actual identity decisions instead of supplying Lovejoy with a criterion he never stated.

### Important warning

A successfully reconstructed thin invariant solves only `I`.

It does not automatically solve:

- `P` — whether the target actor/text actually has/uses/affirms the unit;
- `V` — whether the principle accurately describes its domain;
- `R` — whether sampled cases make it representative/fundamental;
- Gate B — genealogy, causation, historical operation, psychological association, logical relation to other units.

The thinner the invariant becomes, the more important those independent burdens become.

## Compact conclusion

> **Lovejoy's own same/different judgments can be used to bracket, rather than stipulate, the identity content of his units. Positive same-cases tell us what can be subtracted; negative break-cases tell us what cannot. Plenitude is the cleanest example: staticity, simultaneity, developmental direction, theology and much inferential packaging can change, while the positive generative pressure toward fullness survives; when that orientation reverses into primitive limitation, Lovejoy marks a contrast. `Invariant interval` is therefore a more evidence-controlled way to reconstruct Lovejoyian identity than either `timeless essence` or unrestricted family resemblance.**
