# JHI Blog — Lovejoy secondary-literature generation map + Buddhism-index evidence

Date: 2026-08-20
Status: HISTORIOGRAPHICAL GAP CONTROL / DEEP SWEEP ADDENDUM

## New high-value finding: Wilson's 1982 annotated bibliography makes the gap unusually visible

Daniel J. Wilson, *Arthur O. Lovejoy: An Annotated Bibliography* (Garland, 1982), claims near-complete coverage of Lovejoy's writings and secondary literature through June 1981. Its structure separates Lovejoy's primary works from secondary scholarship and includes a subject index.

Under `Buddhism`, the index points only to entries **19** and **243**:
- no. 19: `The Buddhistic Technical Terms upadana and upadise[s]a` (JAOS 19, 1898, 126–136), annotated as a highly technical attempt to correct previous interpretations;
- no. 243: `Syllabus: The Philosophy of Buddhism` (Washington University Association, 1901), four lectures: `The Place of Buddhism among the Philosophies of India`; two lectures on `Two Essential Formulas of Buddhism`; `Buddhist Ethics and its Goal`.

No secondary-study entry is indexed under `Buddhism` in Wilson's otherwise extensive bibliography through its June 1981 cutoff.

This is not proof that no one anywhere discussed Lovejoy's Buddhism, but it is strong bibliographical evidence that **the Buddhist line had not become a recognized topic of Lovejoy scholarship in the pre-1982 secondary canon**.

This sharpens the gap:

> **Wilson the biographer knew the comparative-religion training; Wilson the bibliographer could point to Lovejoy's Buddhist publications; yet Buddhism still did not generate a secondary-literature strand in Lovejoy studies. The materials were visible without becoming a historiographical problem.**

This is stronger than saying `Buddhism was forgotten`.

## Generational map of Lovejoy scholarship

### Generation 1 — insiders / memorial-methodological consolidation (1940s–1960s)
Dominant questions:
- What is the history of ideas?
- What is Lovejoy's philosophical method?
- How do time, critical realism, dualism, and historiography fit together?
- What was Lovejoy's role as teacher and founder?

Representative nodes:
- Lovejoy, `Reflections on the History of Ideas` (1940).
- 1948 JHI / commemorative treatments by Boas, Mandelbaum, Nicolson, etc.
- 1963 *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* symposium: John Herman Randall Jr.; Philip P. Wiener; Lewis S. Feuer and others.

Result: Lovejoy becomes legible primarily as philosopher + founder + theorist of historiography.

### Generation 2 — intellectual biography and consolidation (late 1970s–1980s)
Dominant node:
- Daniel J. Wilson, *Arthur O. Lovejoy and the Quest for Intelligibility* (1980).
- Wilson, *Arthur O. Lovejoy: An Annotated Bibliography* (1982).

Wilson recovers the early comparative-religion biography in considerable detail: Everett, Toy, Lanman, Buddhism, Paris, Lévi. But the developmental synthesis routes that material chiefly into Lovejoy's rational religion / quest-for-intelligibility trajectory and into Christian/Jewish historical essays.

Important consequence: **the source material is not suppressed; it is narratively subordinated.**

### Generation 3 — critique and re-reading of Lovejoyian method (roughly 1970s–2000s)
Key problems:
- unit-ideas as reification;
- context and historical meaning;
- long-range history;
- relation between philosophy and intellectual history;
- Skinnerian or post-Skinnerian critique.

By 2006 John Patrick Diggins already resists a simple caricature and discusses Lovejoy's Chinese aesthetic/Confucian material. Thus `Lovejoy also studied non-Western things` is not itself novel.

### Generation 4 — rehabilitation / practice-before-programme (2010–2023)
- Leo Catana (2010): grand methodological declarations in *Great Chain* are partly disciplinary rhetoric; actual Bruno practice remains continuous with nineteenth-century histories of philosophy. Crucial permission to reconstruct **practice before programme**.
- Carl Knight (2012): reinterprets unit-ideas as small conceptual components; argues the method has more analytical/contextual potential than caricature suggests.
- Daniel Wickberg (2014): recovers Lovejoy's history of ideas as a form of cultural history and challenges teleological stories of methodological supersession.
- Nico Mouton (2023): explicit apologia for long-range Lovejoy; emphasizes diachronic + synchronic work, decomposition/recombination, contextual attention. Uses `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism` as evidence that Lovejoy revised claims when new evidence appeared.

Result: the historiography has already reopened Lovejoy's method, context, long-range practice, and even mature non-European source material.

## What remains open after these four generations

The missing move is not another rehabilitation. It is a **reconnection of historiographical compartments**:

1. Wilson's biographical comparative-religion material;
2. the technical Buddhist/Indological publication and notebook practice;
3. the later disciplinary genealogy of Lovejoyian history of ideas.

Notebook 004 is unusually powerful because it permits the missing connection to be made at the level of **actual scholarly operations**, not merely biography:
- what Lovejoy read;
- where he inserted his own judgment;
- how he distinguished terms, functions, system, and genealogy;
- how a live dispute with Senart entered the 1898 article;
- how manuscript reasoning was compressed into publication.

Best formulation:

> **The novelty is not to add Buddhism to Lovejoy's list of interests. It is to reconstruct what his Buddhist work consisted of and then ask why this technically recoverable practice never became part of the genealogy through which historians of ideas learned to narrate Lovejoy.**

## Two disciplinary afterlives become even stronger

Specialist Buddhist scholarship continued to cite the 1898 article. Modern examples include Bhikkhu Anālayo's comparative work, which quotes Lovejoy 1898 on `upādāna`, and specialist work on `upādi/upadhi` and nirvāṇa that retains Lovejoy in its bibliography.

Meanwhile Lovejoy studies concentrated on philosophy, unit-ideas, Romanticism, intellectual history, and later Chinese material.

Thus:

> **Buddhist studies retained the article; Lovejoy studies retained the founder.**

Notebook 004 lets us reconstruct the practice hidden between those two afterlives.

## Recommended novelty language

Most secure:

> `Lovejoy's early engagement with Buddhism was not unknown to his biographer. But Wilson's own exhaustive 1982 bibliography is revealing: Buddhism appears in the index only as two Lovejoy primary works, the 1898 article and the 1901 syllabus, not as a secondary field of Lovejoy scholarship. The materials were visible without becoming part of the historiographical genealogy of his method.`

More compressed:

> `The Buddhist Lovejoy was bibliographically visible but historiographically inert.`

Use the compressed line only in internal drafting unless its rhetoric fits JHI Blog tone.

## Guardrails

- Bibliographic absence is not absolute proof of total scholarly silence.
- The 1982 cutoff must be stated if the bibliography is used quantitatively.
- Diggins/Mouton mean non-Western material in mature Lovejoy is not wholly ignored.
- Wilson means comparative religion in Lovejoy's biography is not a new discovery.
- No causal claim from Indology to unit-idea method without explicit evidence.
- Retrospective Windelband self-attribution remains a competing/parallel genealogy and should be acknowledged rather than displaced.
