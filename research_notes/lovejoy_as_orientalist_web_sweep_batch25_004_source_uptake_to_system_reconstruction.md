## Core finding

The strongest methodological evidence in 004 does not lie in treating every first-person-sounding sentence as Lovejoy’s own argument. Much of the opening historical discussion is demonstrably copied or closely abstracted from Garbe, Rhys Davids, Warren and other authorities. The historical object is the movement between layers:

> **source uptake → doctrinal discrimination → system reconstruction.**

Three manuscript moments make that movement directly observable.

1. **PDF p.17:** Lovejoy explicitly breaks from the Rhys Davids digest with **“To R.D.’s remark it should be added …”** and proposes a more discriminating test for a genuinely Sāṁkhyan parallel. The central issue is not merely eternity of matter/self, but the **separateness of Self and World**, the thorough-going dualism on which the Sāṁkhya doctrine of release depends.
2. **PDF p.18:** the notebook rejects an initial reading of Buddhist silence as merely the **“philosophical agnosticism of a purely practical teacher.”** The refusals to answer are said to rest on an objection to the **form of the questions put**, while Buddhism remains a doctrine of salvation based on insight. No matching wording has yet been located in Rhys Davids; classify this as an **unattributed, likely Lovejoy analytical synthesis**, not securely original prose.
3. **PDF p.20:** Lovejoy defines the aim of the **“present effort”** as understanding Buddhism’s general philosophical position, correlating it with the Three Characteristics, and understanding its **technical working out** in `paticca-samuppāda` in relation to current Indic ideas by analysing the twelve notions and their complicated interrelation.

The 1898 JAOS paper then secures this transformation in Lovejoy’s published voice: terms and perhaps ideas may be largely borrowed, while their **arrangement and application** are original and characteristic; compositeness does not make the formula a meaningless heap.

## 1. Source uptake comes first

### Garbe 1894, direct

PDF pp.4–5 / manuscript pp.8–9 are headed `The Method of the Sankhya / Garbe p.150`. They closely reproduce Richard Garbe, _Die Sâmkhya-Philosophie_ (1894), p.150ff., on `pramāṇa`, the three sources of knowledge, and logical material adopted from Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika.

### Rhys Davids 1896, direct

PDF pp.12–14 closely digest T. W. Rhys Davids, _Buddhism: Its History and Literature_ (1896), pp.24ff. They include the question whether Buddhism was based on Sāṁkhya, Garbe’s chronology, Rhys Davids’s objection that a large textual gap remains, and the conclusion that one may infer pre-Buddhist thinkers resembling later Sāṁkhya without proving the mature system existed as a whole.

PDF pp.14–16 continue through the _Brahmajāla_ sixty-two heresies. Rhys Davids already supplies an important classificatory operation: the opinions need not represent mutually exclusive schools; propositions can coexist and recombine.

This is an important source-control result. **Decomposition and recombination are already present in Lovejoy’s source environment.** The claim cannot be that he invented these operations ex nihilo.

### Warren / Oldenberg / Childers

The notebook repeatedly works through Warren’s _Buddhism in Translations_, Oldenberg, Childers and translations of the Piṭakas. The observable labour is therefore selection, juxtaposition, supplementation and reconstruction across a mediated Orientalist source field.

## 2. Authorial hinge: “To R.D.’s remark it should be added”

PDF p.17 is the cleanest sentence-level break from source digest. Lovejoy argues that eternity of Matter and Self is not Sāṁkhya’s most diagnostic feature. What is central to its doctrine of release is the **separateness of Self and World — “in a word its thorough-going dualism.”** He then proposes _Majjhima-Nikāya_ 63, “the soul is one thing & the body another,” as a more likely reference to something genuinely Sāṁkhyan.

The operation is visible:

1. receive Rhys Davids’s historical discussion;
2. identify an insufficient diagnostic feature;
3. redefine the doctrinal core;
4. search the Buddhist source field for comparison at that more specific structural level.

The question becomes not simply “are these doctrines similar?” but **“which feature is central and diagnostic enough to support historical comparison?”** This is doctrinal discrimination, not yet a mature `unit-idea` method.

## 3. Question-form rather than mere agnosticism

PDF p.18 summarizes four issues on which Buddhism refuses straightforward assertion: infinity, eternity of the world, soul/body relation and future life. It then rejects the immediate interpretation of this as merely the philosophical agnosticism of a practical moral teacher. The note says the refusals arise from an objection to the **form of the questions put**; Buddhism itself is a salvation doctrine based on insight and must identify the origin of suffering/existence before prescribing removal.

Exact phrase searching has not located this wording in Rhys Davids’s 1896 book. That absence does not prove sole authorship; another immediate source may remain unidentified. Current status: **likely Lovejoy synthesis, source unresolved**.

The operation is nonetheless analytically clear. A question may carry presuppositions the system rejects. Silence/refusal therefore cannot simply be coded as absence of theory. The interpreter must reconstruct why the question is malformed from within the system.

## 4. The notebook states its own programme

PDF p.20 / manuscript p.71 is the most explicit methodological statement in 004. Under `Nidānas`, Lovejoy says the task is:

- understand Buddhism’s **general philosophical position** correlated with the Three Characteristics;
- understand the **technical working out** of that general conception in `paticca-samuppāda`;
- read that working-out in connection with **current Indic ideas**;
- analyse the twelve notions and their **complicated interrelation**;
- approach this through careful analysis of the texts in the **“present effort.”**

The notebook therefore explicitly moves between two analytical levels:

> **general philosophical position ↔ technical articulation in an interrelated conceptual formula.**

This explains why later pages can acknowledge non-Buddhist origins of individual components while insisting on the intelligibility of their Buddhist configuration.

## 5. Senart supplies the stress-test

By PDF pp.48–52 the notebook directly engages Émile Senart’s 1896 interpretation of the twelve nidānas. Senart gives Lovejoy a difficult case because two propositions are partly conceded: components are historically heterogeneous/inherited, and the sequence is not homogeneous as strict temporal order.

The disagreement concerns the inference drawn from these facts. At PDF p.49 the clean transcription says it is false to infer that the formula is therefore unintelligible or accidentally ordered; a **discoverable technical system** gives the arrangement. Some exact manuscript wording remains medium-confidence, so the 1898 article is the stronger authority for the final formulation.

Lovejoy’s move is therefore to reconstruct coherence **after granting compositeness**, not to rescue coherence by denying historical borrowing.

## 6. The 1898 article secures the transformation

Lovejoy’s 1898 JAOS paper provides four secure authorial operations.

### Semantic discrimination

Against Senart, `upādāna` is not a collective abbreviation for the five skandhas. The skandhas are conditions/objects subject to attachment; `upādāna` is the attachment or habitual volitional relation to them.

### Causal discrimination

Lovejoy distinguishes:

- `karma`: cause of the **particular condition** of rebirth;
- `upādāna`: cause of the **fact of rebirth as such**.

A semantic problem is solved by asking what causal role each concept performs within the system.

### Reconstruction after borrowing

The article explicitly says the terms, **perhaps even the ideas**, are doubtless largely borrowed, but their **“arrangement and application”** are original and characteristic. The causation formula is not `shreds and patches` merely because it is historically composite.

### General position and technical equipment

Impermanence / the Three Characteristics is treated as complementary to dependent origination; together the two formulas constitute nearly all of Buddhism’s indispensable theoretical equipment. This is the published version of the p.20 notebook programme.

## 7. Strongest source-controlled thesis

The responsible claim is:

**004 records Lovejoy appropriating a contemporary comparative-philological apparatus and converting it into a practice of system reconstruction. He receives decomposed doctrines, translated texts and competing genealogies from Garbe, Rhys Davids, Warren, Oldenberg and Senart; he then increasingly discriminates among doctrinal features by their diagnostic and causal functions, asks when the form of a question misrepresents the system being studied, and reconstructs a characteristic arrangement from historically composite materials.**

The continuity with later history of ideas should therefore be tested as continuity in **rules of inference**:

1. **Similarity is not enough:** identify a structurally diagnostic resemblance before inferring genealogy.
2. **Questions are not neutral:** reconstruct the presuppositions that make a question answerable or malformed within a system.
3. **Components do not define the whole:** historical borrowing does not determine the function acquired in a new configuration.
4. **Compositeness is compatible with intelligibility:** reconstruct the arrangement and causal/logical work performed by components.
5. **Systematic meaning can be distributed:** a general position can be technically articulated across several formulas/categories rather than stated in one proposition.

This is stronger and safer than projecting mature `unit-idea` terminology backward.

## 8. Relation to 005

The contrast now looks useful rather than inconvenient.

- **004:** source digest → doctrinal discrimination → semantic/causal function → system reconstruction. Dominant problems: genealogy, conceptual composition, question-form, intelligibility.
- **005:** dossier circulation → source criticism → determinant/mechanism → reclassification. Dominant problems: contamination, differential outcomes, ritual mechanism, causal efficacy.

The two notebooks should not be collapsed into one primitive method. They expose two different workshops in which heterogeneous evidence is turned into analytical distinctions.
