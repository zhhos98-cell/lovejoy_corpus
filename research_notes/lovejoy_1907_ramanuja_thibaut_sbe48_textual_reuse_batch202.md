# Batch 202 — Lovejoy 1907 and Thibaut's Rāmānuja SBE 48: proposition-level textual reuse and direct-reading control

Date: 2026-08-26
Status: synced / PRIMARY-TEXT-TO-PRIMARY-TEXT CONTROL
Scope: follows Batch201's extraction of Lovejoy's 1907 Deussen/Vedānta review. Batch201 could safely establish that Lovejoy knew and explicitly cited George Thibaut's 1904 *Sacred Books of the East* vol. 48, but stopped short of claiming direct textual consultation. This pass collates the quoted Rāmānuja language in Lovejoy's review against Thibaut's English *Śrībhāṣya* text.

## Core result

The evidence ceiling can now be raised substantially.

Lovejoy's 1907 description of Rāmānuja contains a cluster of phrases placed in quotation marks:

- `self of all the world`;
- `knowledge for its essential nature`;
- `free from all shadow of imperfection, comprising within itself numberless auspicious qualities, immediately realizing all its purposes`;
- plus the paraphrase that beings are `real modes or parts` of ultimate reality.

These are not generic summaries that merely resemble Rāmānuja. They map closely, and in the final clause nearly verbatim, onto George Thibaut's 1904 translation of **Rāmānuja's Śrībhāṣya**, SBE 48. Crucially, the matches occur after Thibaut's short translator's Introduction, inside the translated/commentarial text beginning `Rāmānuja's Śrībhāṣya / First Adhyāya`.

The strongest safe conclusion is therefore:

> **Lovejoy's 1907 review rests on direct or near-direct targeted consultation of Thibaut's SBE 48 text, not merely on bibliographic awareness that the translation existed.**

Because Lovejoy explicitly footnotes SBE 48 immediately after the quoted characterization, and because several distinctive formulations can be matched across the First Adhyāya, the hypothesis of purely second-hand knowledge becomes much weaker.

This does **not** establish exhaustive reading of the roughly eight hundred-page volume. The evidence supports targeted textual reading sufficient to extract and synthesize multiple doctrinal formulations.

---

## 1. Carrier and text boundary: the matched language is not merely from Thibaut's Introduction

Project Gutenberg's transcription of SBE 48 identifies:

- George Thibaut as translator;
- Rāmānuja as commentator;
- SBE volume 48, 1904.

Its structure is explicit:

`INTRODUCTION`

followed by

`VEDĀNTA-SŪTRAS / WITH / RĀMĀNUJA'S ŚRĪBHĀSHYA / FIRST ADHYĀYA`.

The translated Śrībhāṣya begins immediately thereafter.

The decisive Lovejoy phrase matches occur well inside that translated First Adhyāya, not in the prefatory bibliographic discussion. Therefore the review's Rāmānuja characterization cannot be explained simply by Lovejoy glancing at Thibaut's title page or introductory notice.

---

## 2. Match A — `knowledge for its essential nature`

### Lovejoy 1907

Lovejoy describes Rāmānuja's ultimate reality as having:

`knowledge for its essential nature`.

### Thibaut SBE 48

In the First Adhyāya the text explicitly says that scriptural texts `declare indeed that knowledge is the essential nature of Brahman`, and immediately elaborates that `knowledge constitutes the essential nature of a knowing subject`.

The lexical relation is unusually close:

`knowledge is the essential nature of Brahman`

→ Lovejoy:

`knowledge for its essential nature`.

This is stronger than doctrinal similarity. It is a direct English-phrase correspondence inside the very translation Lovejoy footnotes.

**Relation status:** `P-TEXT-REUSE / CLOSE VERBAL CONDENSATION`.

---

## 3. Match B — `self of all the world`

### Lovejoy 1907

Lovejoy says Rāmānuja finds ultimate reality in a:

`self of all the world`.

### Thibaut SBE 48

The First Adhyāya repeatedly formulates the same relation in near-identical English:

- Brahman `constitutes the Self of the entire world`;
- `Brahman the Self of the world`;
- `the Self of the whole world`;
- meditation on the highest Brahman `of which the world is a mode, which has the world for its body, and which is the Self of the world`.

Lovejoy's wording is therefore a compact normalization of a recurrent Thibaut formulation.

**Relation status:** `P-TEXT-REUSE / RECURRENT FORMULA CONDENSATION`.

---

## 4. Match C — `real modes or parts`

Lovejoy inserts an explanatory clause: all beings are `real modes or parts` of the world-Self.

No exact Thibaut string `real modes or parts` was recovered. But the underlying relation is explicit and recurrent in SBE 48.

Thibaut's Rāmānuja says, for example, that the world is a **mode** of Brahman, has Brahman as its Self, and stands to Brahman as body to embodied Self. Elsewhere intelligent and non-intelligent beings collectively constitute Brahman's body/form.

Thus this clause is best classified differently from the phrases Lovejoy places in quotation marks:

- not an exact quotation;
- a doctrinal compression of Thibaut's body/mode ontology.

**Relation status:** `P-TEXT-SYNTHESIS / DOCTRINAL PARAPHRASE`.

This difference should be preserved because it shows Lovejoy alternating between textual quotation and analytic restatement.

---

## 5. Match D — the strongest near-verbatim cluster

### Lovejoy 1907

Lovejoy says ultimate reality is:

`free from all shadow of imperfection, comprising within itself numberless auspicious qualities, immediately realizing all its purposes`.

### Thibaut SBE 48

In the First Adhyāya, the translated text describes the highest Brahman as:

- free from all shadow of imperfection;
- of measureless excellence;
- comprising within itself numberless auspicious qualities;
- all-knowing;
- immediately realising all its purposes.

Lovejoy's wording preserves the sequence and nearly all the distinctive lexical material, while omitting two intervening descriptors (`of measureless excellence`, `all-knowing`).

This is not merely parallel philosophical language. It is a recognizable **selective quotation/abridgment** from Thibaut's English.

**Relation status:** `P-TEXT-REUSE / NEAR-VERBATIM SELECTIVE QUOTATION`.

This is the strongest direct-reading control in the present pass.

---

## 6. The matches occur at multiple points in the First Adhyāya

The phrase cluster is not confined to one convenient sentence.

Recovered locations include:

- the `knowledge is the essential nature of Brahman` formulation in an early First-Adhyāya discussion;
- the full `free from all shadow of imperfection ... numberless auspicious qualities ... immediately realising all its purposes` cluster later in the same Adhyāya;
- the `Self of the whole world` / world-as-mode / world-as-body formulations in a still later section.

This matters for the inference ceiling.

The evidence supports more than:

`Lovejoy copied one sentence found in a review or table of contents`.

It is consistent with targeted consultation of multiple passages in the first major division of SBE 48, whether by continuous reading, index-assisted reading, or selective reference checking.

It does **not** tell us which of those reading procedures he used.

---

## 7. Lovejoy's own footnote closes the attribution chain

Immediately after his Rāmānuja characterization Lovejoy writes that a satisfactory translation of Rāmānuja's commentary on the Vedānta Sūtras is now available `by Thibaut`, with the footnote:

`Sacred Books of the East, Vol. 48, 1904.`

The evidentiary chain is therefore unusually clean:

`Lovejoy quotation / paraphrase`

→ `explicit Thibaut attribution`

→ `exact SBE volume/year`

→ `distinctive English phrase matches inside SBE 48`.

This sharply reduces the need to posit an unidentified intermediate source.

An intermediary remains logically possible, but it is no longer the parsimonious explanation.

## 8. Revised evidence grade for Lovejoy's Rāmānuja knowledge

### Batch201 grade

`DOCUMENTED BIBLIOGRAPHIC KNOWLEDGE`:

Lovejoy knew that Thibaut's SBE 48 existed and described it as a satisfactory translation.

### Batch202 grade

`DIRECT / TARGETED TEXTUAL CONSULTATION STRONGLY SUPPORTED`:

Lovejoy reproduces and condenses distinctive English formulations found inside the translation/commentary he explicitly cites.

### Still not established

`EXHAUSTIVE READING OF SBE 48`.

His joke about the `eight hundred painful pages` in fact makes the limited inference especially important. A reader can consult, excerpt, and reason from selected passages without traversing every page.

## 9. The Rāmānuja recommendation is therefore evidence-based, not merely programmatic

Batch201 showed Lovejoy urging greater scholarly attention to Rāmānuja and asking for an accessible outline. Without textual collation, one possible reading remained that he knew only the existence and broad reputation of Rāmānuja's system.

Batch202 removes much of that ambiguity.

His comparison of Rāmānuja with contemporary idealism is built on textual propositions he can quote or closely paraphrase from SBE 48:

- Brahman as world-Self;
- knowledge as essential nature;
- real world/body/mode relation;
- positive auspicious qualities and realised purposes.

Thus Lovejoy's differentiation:

`Śaṅkara / Advaita`

versus

`Rāmānuja / qualified, world-inclusive monism`

is grounded in a textual encounter with Rāmānuja's translated commentary, not merely in Deussen's book under review.

This gives the 1907 review a **two-source architecture**:

1. Deussen/Woods/Runkle as the reviewed Śaṅkara object;
2. Thibaut/Rāmānuja as an independently consulted comparative control.

That architecture should be retained explicitly.

---

## 10. This changes the role of footnotes in the early corpus

The Thibaut footnote is not ornamental bibliography. It points to a source whose language is operational inside Lovejoy's argument.

This supplies another control for the repository's citation-role taxonomy:

`named bibliographic item`

can be tested against

`verbal reuse / proposition reuse`.

Where the two align, the evidence can be upgraded from `availability` or `citation` to `textual consultation`.

For the present case:

`Thibaut SBE 48` = `DIRECTLY CONSULTED COMPARATIVE SOURCE`, strongly supported.

This is materially stronger than the generic category `work cited`.

## 11. Relation to the 1904 Bruno–Spinoza Indic reuse

Batch192 established that Lovejoy's 1904 Bruno–Spinoza essay operationally reused Indic/Vedānta material in a Western metaphysical genealogy.

Batch202 adds a later but methodologically complementary fact:

in 1907, when comparing rival Vedānta systems, Lovejoy does not rely on a single representative authority. He checks a second translated primary/commentarial source and extracts propositions from it to oppose the field's overconcentration on Śaṅkara.

The common operation is not `India influenced Lovejoy` in the abstract.

It is more exact:

> **Lovejoy uses non-Western textual material propositionally and comparatively, with source-specific controls, rather than as a generic civilizational label.**

This can now be shown in more than one early text.

---

## 12. Relation to the `reasons > conclusions` method statement

Batch201 identified Lovejoy's explicit claim that an illuminating analysis of the reasons leading a philosopher to conclusions matters more than even accurate exposition of the conclusions themselves.

Batch202 shows that this was not merely a desideratum directed at Woods.

Lovejoy's own review already performs a miniature version of the practice:

- he accesses a rival system beyond the reviewed manual;
- extracts specific propositions;
- reconstructs its ontological relation among Brahman, world, knowledge, and qualities;
- compares that structure to contemporary philosophical problems;
- uses the comparison to challenge the representative status of the dominant classification.

The methodological statement and the review's source practice therefore reinforce one another.

This is stronger evidence of **practice/criterion alignment** than either passage alone.

## 13. Evidence ceiling

### DIRECT PRIMARY A

Arthur O. Lovejoy, review of Paul Deussen, *Outline of the Vedānta System of Philosophy, according to Shankara*, *The Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* 4.1 (3 January 1907), pp. 23–24.

Relevant Lovejoy wording:

- `self of all the world`;
- `knowledge for its essential nature`;
- beings as `real modes or parts`;
- `free from all shadow of imperfection ... numberless auspicious qualities ... immediately realizing all its purposes`;
- footnote: `Sacred Books of the East, Vol. 48, 1904`.

### DIRECT PRIMARY B

George Thibaut, trans., *The Vedānta-Sūtras with the Commentary by Rāmānuja*, *Sacred Books of the East*, vol. 48 (1904), Project Gutenberg transcription.

Controlled First-Adhyāya matches:

- `knowledge is the essential nature of Brahman` / `knowledge constitutes the essential nature of a knowing subject`;
- Brahman `constitutes the Self of the entire world` / `Self of the whole world`;
- world as a `mode`, body, and world whose Self is Brahman;
- `free from all shadow of imperfection ... comprising within itself numberless auspicious qualities ... immediately realising all its purposes`.

### EVIDENCE GRADE

`DIRECT / TARGETED TEXTUAL CONSULTATION STRONGLY SUPPORTED`.

### DO NOT CLAIM YET

- that Lovejoy read all of SBE 48;
- that he read the Sanskrit rather than Thibaut's English;
- that every phrase in his Rāmānuja paragraph comes from one contiguous passage;
- that he consulted SBE 48 without use of its indexes or other navigational aids;
- that no intermediate source reproduced any of the phrases;
- that Rāmānuja directly influenced Lovejoy's own metaphysics;
- that his reading of Rāmānuja was philologically or philosophically complete;
- that his contrast with Śaṅkara accurately represents either system in modern scholarly terms.
