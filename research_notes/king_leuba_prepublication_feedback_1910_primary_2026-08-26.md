# Irving King ↔ James H. Leuba — prepublication feedback inside *The Development of Religion* (1910)

Date: 2026-08-26
Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / PREPUBLICATION INTERLOCUTION**

Primary object:

Irving King, *The Development of Religion: A Study in Anthropology and Social Psychology* (New York: Macmillan, 1910), especially printed pp.38–39.

## Core discovery

King's 1910 book contains direct evidence that James H. Leuba read at least one relevant discussion before publication and corrected King's representation of Leuba's position.

At printed p.38 King condenses Leuba's earlier distinction between psychology of religion and the historical/social study of religion, then questions what he takes to be the primacy of the subjective state.

A footnote begins:

> `Professor Leuba has read this discussion, and states that his position was...`

The continuation at printed p.39 says Leuba's position had been taken with **quite a different problem in mind**. King then explicitly says that he **retains the criticism** nevertheless because it helps bring out King's own point more clearly.

## 1. Historical significance

This is not later reception reconstructed from citation proximity.

It establishes a direct prepublication feedback relation:

`King drafts criticism of Leuba`
→ `Leuba reads the discussion`
→ `Leuba says his position addressed a different problem`
→ `King records that correction in print`
→ `King retains his criticism for a narrower argumentative purpose`.

The relation is therefore **author–interlocutor feedback before the 1910 book appeared**.

## 2. What the disagreement concerns

King's target is the relation between:

- individual subjective religious experience;
- overt action/activity;
- corporate/social religion;
- historical and social products such as doctrines, beliefs, rites and ceremonies.

King wants to resist a simple primacy of inner religious states and instead argue that conscious attitudes emerge from antecedent activities and social processes.

Leuba's correction, as reported by King, does not make King withdraw the passage. Instead King narrows the status of the criticism: he acknowledges that Leuba's original statement addressed a different problem while retaining the contrast as a device for clarifying King's own action-centered model.

Thus the printed book itself contains **registered disagreement about the proper problem-address of a proposition**.

## 3. Relation to the 1910 review and 1911 reply

The later sequence becomes denser:

`prepublication: Leuba reads King manuscript/discussion and corrects the problem-address of King's criticism`
→ `King 1910 publishes the correction but retains his argument`
→ `Leuba 1910 reviews King in Psychological Bulletin`
→ `King 1911 explicitly identifies Leuba as the friendly reviewer who criticized the book's narrow adherence to the action point of view`
→ `King 1911 defends the restricted viewpoint as a route to clarity while restating his genetic programme`.

The direct text of Leuba's 1910 review remains unrecovered, so do not assume the review simply repeats the prepublication objection. But the two primary controls now show that King and Leuba were already engaged in a live exchange over **which problem a formulation answered and how broad a viewpoint the science of religion should adopt**.

## 4. Relation to Lovejoy 1906 reception

King's chapter VI directly uses Lovejoy's `primitive philosophy` paper for:

- comparative category formulation;
- semantic correction of Fletcher;
- evidence résumé.

King simultaneously reclassifies the impersonal-power field as not inherently religious. The King–Leuba prepublication exchange occurs in the same book, though not at the Lovejoy citation itself.

Do not claim Lovejoy caused the King–Leuba methodological dispute.

The useful historical conclusion is ecological:

> **Lovejoy's 1906 category entered an American book whose production already involved direct argument with one of the psychologists who would later regrade Lovejoy's own hypothesis.**

The American reception field was therefore interactive before the published review/reply chain.

## 5. JHI-facing value

This is probably too detailed for the Blog body, but it materially strengthens the epilogue claim that the American scientific study of religion was not a passive citation network.

A compact safe formulation:

> **King's 1910 book itself records Leuba correcting the problem to which King had assigned one of Leuba's propositions; King printed the correction while preserving his own argument. The later review-and-reply exchange therefore grew out of an already interactive field of proposition-level disagreement.**

This is an external period control for the project's broader interest in claim-address, relation and evidentiary license, not a transmission claim from the notebooks.
