# MS38_004_001_061_005 — provenance and revised relation to Lovejoy 1906

Status: **provenance strongly established; publication relation remains under test**  
Date of revision: 2026-08-18

## 1. Provenance: Paris 1898–99, Léon Marillier / EPHE

Notebook 005 should no longer be provisionally dated `c.1901–1906`.

The following evidence converges on **Paris, academic year 1898–1899**:

- The stationer’s leaf is from **Cabanon, 162 rue Saint-Jacques, Paris**.
- Internal dates include `Dec. 14`, `May 29`, and `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`.
- The back leaf includes another `V. Marillier` reference.
- The official EPHE Section des sciences religieuses report for 1898–99 names **Lovejoy** among the auditors who **“took an active part in the work”** of Léon Marillier’s conferences.
- Marillier’s first conference treated beliefs in survival of the soul and argued that the earliest afterlife is continuation of earthly life, not moral compensation/punishment.
- His second conference treated human sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy, with Bantu southern Africa, Guinea coast, North American Indigenous and non-Aryan Indian comparisons, distinguishing expiatory, funerary, anthropophagic, magical, agrarian and fecundative sacrifice.
- Those are the organizing problems of 005 from beginning to end.

The most defensible formulation is therefore:

> `MS38_004_001_061_005` is strongly datable to Paris 1898–99 and very likely preserves Lovejoy’s working/course-research archive from active participation in Léon Marillier’s EPHE comparative-religion conferences, supplemented by independent reading and later inserted notes.

It should not yet be called a verbatim lecture notebook because much of the material is clearly Lovejoy’s own classification, cross-referencing, reading, and argument.

## 2. The opening of 005 is directly Marillier-shaped

The first thirty PDF pages repeatedly argue that:

- terrestrial rank/status continues into the next life without implying moral desert;
- magical or ritual capacity can persist after death without being virtue;
- modes of death can sort post-mortem destinations;
- “survivance” precedes an idea of inherent immortality;
- death is not originally treated as a natural necessity but requires a special causal explanation;
- myths explain the accidental introduction of death;
- second death/annihilation may limit post-mortem existence;
- missionary/contact evidence must be source-critically separated from earlier beliefs.

Marillier’s official EPHE report and his earlier published report on `La survivance de l’âme et l’idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés` state almost exactly the same research problem: afterlife as continuation rather than moral recompense, rank after death following terrestrial rank, death requiring explanation, and comparative use of Melanesian/American evidence.

The notebook’s `Surviv.` abbreviations and `Marillier — Survivance` slip now have an identifiable referent.

## 3. The second half is equally close to Marillier’s sacrifice course

005 later classifies sacrificial and anthropophagic practices into distinct mechanisms:

- funerary provision/continuity;
- expiation/propitiation;
- communication with the dead;
- substitution;
- magical protection;
- transferable vitality/power through blood or body parts;
- agricultural/fecundative efficacy;
- sacramental/communion union;
- initiation/incorporation rather than sacrifice;
- ritual anthropophagy versus non-sacramental cannibalism.

This maps closely to Marillier’s official 1898–99 course description. The geographical overlap is also striking: Bantu/southern Africa, Guinea/West Africa, North America, and Indian comparisons.

## 4. Revised 005 → 1906 model

The 1906 *Monist* paper, **“The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy,”** should now be understood as a possible **later theoretical abstraction from a Paris comparative-religion archive**, not as the immediate publication product of a notebook from c.1901–1906.

The strongest conceptual/lexical bridges remain:

- PDF p. 105: `quasi-mechanical and magical rather than sacramental`;
- fetish vs charm/amulet: indwelling spirit versus efficacy intrinsic to an object;
- blood and body parts as transferable protective/curative/life-giving efficacy;
- refusal to make modern natural/supernatural categories native categories;
- repeated distinction between visible ritual form and underlying causal mechanism;
- critique of single-origin or single-communion theories;
- decomposition of apparently homogeneous ritual complexes into historically distinct motives.

Secondary and reception evidence for the 1906 paper independently describes Lovejoy’s published thesis in terms of **impersonal/quasi-mechanical/communicable power**, “regular and intelligible laws,” and “primitive energetics.” This still makes 005 a highly relevant precursor.

But the new chronology changes the historical question from:

> Was 005 written to prepare the 1906 paper?

into:

> How did Lovejoy transform the empirical/classificatory comparative-religion work he carried out under Marillier in Paris in 1898–99 into the more abstract theory of efficacy/causation published in 1906?

That is a stronger and more historically specific problem.

## 5. Three-stage transformation to test

A provisional production genealogy is now:

### Stage A — Marillier/EPHE, 1898–99

Empirical comparison and negative classification: do not infer morality, sacrifice, communion, animism, or supernatural agency merely from outward form. Separate different mechanisms and historical layers.

### Stage B — Lovejoy’s notebook 005, 1898–99

Lovejoy reorganizes the ethnographic evidence into sharper conceptual distinctions: naturalistic continuity vs moral eschatology; alimentary/propitiatory/magical/fecundative/sacramental sacrifice; fetish vs intrinsic efficacy; ritual form vs causal mechanism.

### Stage C — Lovejoy 1906

Potential abstraction of those distinctions into a general proposition about primitive causal thought: efficacy/power may be conceived as impersonal, transferable and quasi-mechanical, and need not be reduced to personal spirits or the “supernormal.”

The final step remains to be demonstrated from the primary article.

## 6. What is now secure / likely / unresolved

### Secure

- Lovejoy was an active participant in Marillier’s EPHE conferences in 1898–99.
- 005 is a Paris notebook.
- 005 contains internal dates and explicit Marillier references consistent with the same academic year.
- The course themes and 005 themes closely correspond.

### Highly likely

- 005 was produced in close connection with Marillier’s conferences during the 1898–99 Paris year.
- Marillier’s `Survivance` work is one of the controlling sources for 005’s first section.

### Unresolved

- Which passages are direct lecture notes, which are Lovejoy’s independent library work, and which are later insertions.
- Whether any part of 005 was added after Paris.
- Exact examples/sources transferred from 005/Marillier into the 1906 paper.
- Whether a surviving 1906 draft/typescript makes the transformation explicit.
