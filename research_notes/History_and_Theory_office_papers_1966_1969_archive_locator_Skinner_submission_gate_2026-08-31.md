# *History and Theory* office papers, 1966–69: archive locator for the Skinner submission gate

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **OFFICE-PAPER CORPUS DIRECTLY ATTESTED IN PUBLISHED SCHOLARSHIP / SKINNER FILE NOT YET LOCATED / PUBLIC FINDING AID STILL ABSENT**

## Core result

The open question `S2b — Skinner editorial/referee circulation, c.1966–69` now has a concrete archival target rather than a hypothetical institution-level request.

Samuel James's 2010 *Modern Intellectual History* article on Louis O. Mink directly cites:

> `History and Theory Papers, History and Theory Offices, Wesleyan University, Middletown, CT`

and uses this corpus for a **13 June 1966 Louis Mink memorandum on a submitted methodological paper** (`Stanley Paluch—'The Covering Law Model of Historical Explanation'`).

James separately cites `LMP/Wesleyan` for Louis Mink personal papers, proving that his citation to `History and Theory Papers` is not simply a loose label for Mink's own archive.

Therefore:

`1960s HISTORY AND THEORY EDITORIAL WORKING PAPERS EXISTED / WERE ACCESSIBLE TO A SCHOLAR`

and their contents reached the level of:

`NAMED SUBMISSION`
-> `INTERNAL EDITORIAL MEMORANDUM`
-> `DATED 1966`.

This is exactly the document class needed to test Skinner's manuscript routing.

What remains unproven:

- whether the office papers survive unchanged in 2026;
- whether they are catalogued publicly;
- whether a Skinner file is present;
- whether Mink, Mandelbaum, Vann or Nadel formally handled Skinner's submission;
- whether referee reports survive.

## I. Why this changes the archive strategy

Previous public finding-aid searches showed:

- Wesleyan's named `History and Theory` annual-report series begins only in 1970–71;
- Richard Vann's named papers begin 1972;
- obvious Mandelbaum named collections do not expose 1966–69 professional correspondence.

Those negative controls created an apparent catalog gap around the crucial years.

James 2010 now demonstrates that a different layer existed:

`JOURNAL OFFICE WORKING PAPERS`

rather than

`UNIVERSITY ANNUAL REPORTS`
or
`INDIVIDUAL NAMED PAPERS`.

So do **not** infer from the absence of a public ArchivesSpace collection that the editorial corpus never existed.

## II. Direct precedent: 1966 submission memorandum

James footnote 72 cites:

Louis Mink, memorandum on `Stanley Paluch—'The Covering Law Model of Historical Explanation,'` 13 June 1966, `History and Theory Papers, History and Theory Offices, Wesleyan University, Middletown, CT`.

This proves a document type very close to the desired Skinner evidence:

`SUBMISSION`
-> `EDITORIAL READER`
-> `INTERNAL MEMORANDUM`
-> `JOURNAL OFFICE FILE`.

Thus the Skinner query should be formulated at document level:

- manuscript receipt / cover letter;
- internal memorandum;
- referee assignment/report;
- author revision request;
- rejection/acceptance letter;
- publication scheduling;
- correspondence around vol.8 no.1.

## III. Institutional chronology needs a title/function distinction

A Wesleyan 2008 interview with Brian Fay states that:

- George Nadel founded *History and Theory* in 1960;
- the journal came to Wesleyan in **1965**;
- Richard Vann of Wesleyan's History Department was `the editor from 1965 until 1993`.

This creates a useful control against over-literal reading of masthead labels.

Earlier repo reconstruction from 1969 front matter had distinguished:

- George H. Nadel — editor;
- Richard T. Vann — executive editor;
- Louis O. Mink — associate/editorial role;
- Maurice Mandelbaum — editorial committee.

The Wesleyan institutional retrospective suggests that operational editorship may be described differently in later institutional memory.

Therefore add firewall:

`PRINTED MASTHEAD TITLE`
!=
`LATER INSTITUTIONAL DESCRIPTION OF OPERATIONAL EDITORSHIP`.

Both must be preserved until the 1969 front matter is directly transcribed and role functions reconstructed.

This title discrepancy is potentially informative rather than an error: journals can distribute formal titles, office labor and intellectual authority differently.

Skinner's published acknowledgment already proves:

`MANDELBAUM READ EARLIER DRAFT + SENT CRITICAL COMMENTS`.

The office papers could answer a distinct question:

`WAS MANDELBAUM ALSO FORMALLY INVOLVED IN H&T EDITORIAL/REFEREE PROCESS?`

These must stay separate.

If the journal file names Mandelbaum, then two relations may overlap:

- `S2a COLLEGIAL DRAFT READER`;
- `S2b FORMAL EDITORIAL/REFEREE ROLE`.

But one role cannot be inferred from the other.
