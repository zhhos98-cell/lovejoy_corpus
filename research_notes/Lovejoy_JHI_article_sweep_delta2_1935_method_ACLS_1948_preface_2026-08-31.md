## 1. 1935 preface makes the historiographical machine explicit

Direct searchable full-text control: Arthur O. Lovejoy and George Boas, *Primitivism and Related Ideas in Antiquity* (1935; later reprint text collated online).

The preface says that in `the historiography of ideas` the object to be exhibited is the fortunes of distinct `unit-ideas` and their relations of congruity/opposition rather than intact philosophical `systems`. The procedure therefore requires analysis/anatomizing of texts, separating passages pertinent to different ideas, and regrouping passages that may originally have occurred together. This organization can even place different portions of one text in different chapters.

But Lovejoy and Boas immediately refuse to make the method a Procrustean bed. Plato, Aristotle, Lucretius, Cicero, Cynics and Stoics can receive more integrated treatment where individual influence warrants it. Thus the actor-level procedure is already:

`ANALYTICAL DECOMPOSITION`
+
`REGROUPING BY IDEA/RELATION`
+
`EXCEPTIONS FOR HISTORICALLY IMPORTANT WHOLES/AGENTS`.

This is stronger than the later caricature `unit-ideas = atomization regardless of context`.

## 2. The Prolegomena explicitly distinguishes analyst categories from mixed historical reality

At the opening of the Prolegomena, `primitivism` is described as a name used by historical/philosophical writers for two distinct tendencies that had different origins but often fused/interpenetrated historically. Lovejoy/Boas therefore analytically split:

- `chronological primitivism`;
- `cultural primitivism`.

They insist these answer different questions. Chronological primitivism concerns the temporal distribution of good/value across world/human history; cultural primitivism concerns dissatisfaction with civilization and preference for a simpler mode of life.

Crucially, after producing an elaborate analytical taxonomy, the Prolegomena concedes that actual historical beliefs are not always sharply discriminated and often combine logically different assumptions; historical cases therefore cannot always be classified with confidence and precision under the analytical heads.

This creates an actor-level architecture:

`ANALYST CONSTRUCTS CLEAR DISTINCTIONS`
→ `HISTORICAL BELIEFS MAY MIX THEM`
→ `CLASSIFICATION CONFIDENCE VARIES`
→ `DISTINCTIONS STILL GUIDE THE HISTORICAL SURVEY`.

This is an important mature control for notebook 004. The claim is structural recurrence, not direct methodological continuity.

## 3. `state of nature` is decomposed by target sense

The same Prolegomena enumerates multiple senses of `state of nature` (technological, economic, marital, dietetic, political/juristic, ethical, etc.) and explicitly warns that the generalized phrase usually implies only one or a few of them in a specific writer. The historian must identify which sense the writer chiefly has in mind, because the motivation and historical development vary with the selected sense.

This is a mature version of the project's `same label != same relation/object` firewall.

## 4. India and Western Asia receive an explicit editorial role: `Supplementary Essays`

The preface thanks W. F. Albright and P.-E. Dumont for `Supplementary Essays` that extend the perspective of the history of primitivism into two important regions where the subject had not hitherto received special study.

The two essays are:

- Albright, `Primitivism in Ancient Western Asia`;
- Dumont, `Primitivism in Indian Literature`.

This is the strongest actor-level role classification yet found for the non-Western material in the 1935 project:

`GREEK/ROMAN MAIN DOCUMENTARY ARCHITECTURE`
+
`WESTERN-ASIAN / INDIAN SUPPLEMENTARY REGIONAL EXTENSION`.

The volume also says the projected series concerns opinions held `chiefly in the Occident` about civilization/progress. Contemporary Nature review (1936) similarly described the undertaking as presenting direct evidence of views `throughout Western civilization` while noting the supplementary essays.

Therefore the strongest current formulation is **asymmetric inclusion**, not exclusion:

> India is deliberately recruited into the history of primitivism, but the editorial architecture marks it as a supplementary regional extension of a project whose main historical address is chiefly Occidental.

## 5. ACLS is a material bridge, not merely later JHI sponsorship

The 1935 volume explicitly acknowledges American Council of Learned Societies grants supporting preparation and publication.

ACLS's official centennial history describes its research-planning committees as vehicles for incubating new fields, creating trans-institutional scholarly networks, supplying seed money, surveys and research infrastructure. It identifies JHI as one of the journals produced through this mechanism and states that JHI was begun by ACLS in 1940 on the initiative of a committee chaired by Lovejoy.

This creates a new institutional chain that is parallel to, but not identical with, the Hopkins Club genealogy:

`HOPKINS CLUB / LOCAL RESEARCH PROGRAMME`
+
`ACLS GRANTS / TRANS-INSTITUTIONAL RESEARCH INFRASTRUCTURE`
→ `1935 DOCUMENTARY HISTORY OF PRIMITIVISM`

and later:

`ACLS RESEARCH-PLANNING COMMITTEE CHAIRED BY LOVEJOY`
→ `JHI 1940`.

Do **not** infer that the same ACLS committee funded the 1935 volume and founded JHI unless archival committee records establish this. The safe claim is continuity of institutional ecology and funding/planning form.

## 6. 1948 Author's Preface supplies a direct anti-teleological rule

Searchable copies and John Herman Randall's near-contemporary 1950 review preserve Lovejoy's Author's Preface claim that the general conceptions expressed in the methodological essay and elsewhere **grew out of rather than preceded most of the inquiries into special topics**. Lovejoy explicitly says the collected essays are not examples of imposing a predetermined method on refractory material; rereading them instead revealed common assumptions and procedures whose validity must be judged by results in specific cases.

This is one of the most important controls for the proposed article.

It authorizes the historical question:

`SPECIAL INQUIRIES`
→ `RECURRENT OPERATIONS / PROBLEMS`
→ `RETROSPECTIVE METHODOLOGICAL GENERALIZATION`,

while blocking:

`MATURE METHOD`
→ `read backward as a fixed programme already present in 1898`.

Thus the Buddhist notebooks can be made method-historically relevant without calling them a proto-unit-idea method. They become an unusually early special inquiry in which later-recurring problems of classification, relation, sequence and warrant are observable.

Direct 1948 edition image lock remains desirable before publication quotation; the proposition itself is independently attested by the 1950 Randall review and multiple later scholarly discussions.

## 7. Revised article thesis

The strongest thesis now has three levels rather than one intellectual-development line.

### Level A — case practice

1898 Buddhist philology shows Lovejoy doing relation-specific reconstruction inside a non-Western conceptual/textual system.

### Level B — object conversion

1906–1935 converts `primitive/primitivism` into a family of cross-cultural and historiographical objects. By 1935 the historian explicitly creates analytical categories, decomposes texts, regroups passages, preserves uncertainty where historical beliefs mix categories, and recruits India/Western Asia as supplementary regional extensions.

### Level C — infrastructure / field formation

The Hopkins Club makes primitivism a recurrent seminar problem; the 1935 project is materially supported by ACLS; ACLS later incubates JHI through a Lovejoy-chaired committee; JHI's 1940 programme explicitly lists primitivism among model objects and institutionalizes cross-disciplinary migration as a field problem.

The new strongest formulation is:

> **Lovejoy's history of ideas was not simply a methodological doctrine applied to preselected Western materials. It emerged through special inquiries, cooperative documentary projects, and institutions that repeatedly had to decide how historical materials were to be decomposed, compared, and reassigned across disciplinary and geographical jurisdictions. His early Buddhist work matters because it exposes one such problem-space before the `history of ideas` existed as a named institutional programme; the primitivism project then shows those operations being formalized while Asian materials acquire a new, explicitly supplementary comparative role; JHI subsequently generalizes cross-jurisdictional movement into a field programme.**

This remains a genealogy of **problem and jurisdiction**, not a causal line `Buddhism -> JHI`.
