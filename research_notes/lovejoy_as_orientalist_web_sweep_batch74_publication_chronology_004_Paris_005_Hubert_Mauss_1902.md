# Batch 74 — Chronology audit: 004 print, Paris 1898–99, 005, Hubert–Mauss, and why the evidence does not support a simple before/after influence story

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: publication and participation windows around the 004→Paris→005 transition, with a forward control to Lovejoy 1902. The purpose is to identify which methodological relations can be chronological transmission claims and which must remain parallelism, reinforcement or later continuity.

## Core result

The chronology materially changes the safest historical narrative.

Lovejoy’s Buddhist article is not simply an unambiguous `pre-Paris` published baseline. Bibliographic controls identify it as appearing in **JAOS 19, second half (1898)**; library serial metadata identifies the second half as **July–December 1898**. Lovejoy’s documented active participation in Marillier’s EPHE conference belongs to the **1898–99 academic year**. Those windows overlap too much to establish, from publication date alone, whether every argument in the printed Buddhist article preceded his Paris work.

At the same time, notebook 004 itself and its source-transformations supply clear evidence that the level-separating philological operator was already under construction before the published endpoint. Therefore the historical claim does not need a clean publication-before-Paris sequence.

The correct temporal model is:

> **004 gives a prepublication philological working apparatus; the JAOS article appears during the July–December 1898 publication window; Lovejoy is securely an active participant in Marillier’s 1898–99 EPHE work; 005 shows the same proof-level architecture operating on comparative-religion/ethnographic materials.**

This supports transport/reinforcement across a transition, not a claim that Paris first created the operator after the Buddhist work was fully complete.

---

## 1. The JAOS article is second-half 1898, not simply `1898`

Several independent bibliographic controls converge:

- Daniel J. Wilson’s annotated bibliography identifies `The Buddhistic Technical Terms upadana and upadisesa` as JAOS 19, **Second Half (1898)**, pp.126–136.
- Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek / Bayerische Staatsbibliothek identifies the physical object as `JAOS. 19. 1897/98, Half 2`, published in 1898.
- CiNii/HathiTrust serial metadata identifies `v.19:no.2` and `v.19:2` as **July–December 1898**.

This is a meaningful tightening because the printed article cannot be used as if it were securely dated to early 1898.

### Consequence

`1898 article` ≠ `demonstrably before Paris`.

Without a submission date, proof date, correspondence, or exact travel date, the publication itself only gives a half-year window.

---

## 2. The article’s byline/affiliation does not solve the chronology

The recovered primary text identifies Lovejoy as `Harvard University, Cambridge, Mass.`

That affiliation is historically useful, but it should not be converted into a publication-day location. Journal affiliation can reflect the institutional location under which the article was submitted or prepared and does not by itself prove that Lovejoy remained physically at Harvard when the second half was issued.

Thus the affiliation strengthens the Harvard/Orientalist provenance of the work but does not establish a day-by-day Harvard→Paris sequence.

---

## 3. EPHE supplies the secure Paris anchor: academic year 1898–99

The official Marillier report is unambiguous about Lovejoy’s status during 1898–99: he is listed among the `auditeurs ayant pris une part active aux travaux`, separately from `élèves titulaires`.

This gives a secure institutional interval but not an exact first date of attendance.

The safest temporal statement is therefore:

> **By the 1898–99 EPHE reporting year Lovejoy was an active participant in Marillier’s conference work.**

Do not infer from that annual label an exact arrival month unless an independent travel, enrollment or correspondence record is recovered.

---

## 4. 004 remains a genuine earlier working-stage control even when print chronology overlaps

The lack of a clean article-before-Paris publication sequence does not erase the 004→005 argument.

Notebook 004 gives direct prepublication working evidence for operations subsequently stabilized in the JAOS article:

- Hardy p.394 is turned into the `fact of continued existence / manner of continued existence` allocation question;
- canonical usage is used to separate attachment from its objects;
- `upādisesa` is reconstructed contextually as an ethical state;
- textual criticism and version/recension questions are kept separate from semantic judgment;
- compositional and Sāṃkhya-dependence hypotheses are advanced only as distinct historical propositions.

The direct manuscript→print chain is therefore analytically prior even if its exact calendar endpoint relative to Paris cannot yet be fixed to a month.

The stronger wording is `prepublication 004 apparatus`, not automatically `pre-Paris published method`.

---

## 5. Hubert–Mauss 1899 is contemporary Paris-field evidence, but its own publication window needs caution

The standard bibliographic citation for Hubert and Mauss’s `Essai sur la nature et la fonction du sacrifice` is *L’Année sociologique* 2, 1899, pp.29–138. Mauss bibliographies and modern editions give 1899; the volume is commonly labelled `2 (1897–1898)` because it reviews/worked over that sociological year.

Library metadata is not perfectly uniform: some Hathi/CiNii records date the volume object to 1898, while standard article bibliographies and later scholarly references cite the essay as 1899. This is exactly the kind of bibliographic ambiguity that should prevent a fine-grained access claim.

### Consequence

We can securely say:

- Hubert–Mauss belongs to the same immediate late-1890s Paris comparative-religion field;
- its method is a strong contemporary control for genealogy-from-analogy and proof-threshold problems.

We cannot yet say:

- Lovejoy necessarily had access to the printed essay while producing 005;
- the essay was printed before a particular 005 notebook entry;
- 005 derives from Hubert–Mauss.

The direct institutional transmission claim remains Marillier, not Hubert–Mauss.

---

## 6. Chronology therefore differentiates three kinds of relation

### A. Direct working continuity: secure

`004 manuscript operation → Lovejoy 1898 JAOS article`

The notebook and printed article share specific source loci and specific reformulations. Hardy p.394 is the clearest microgenesis.

### B. Institutional reinforcement / domain translation: strong but not one-to-one

`004 philological proof partition → 1898–99 Marillier conference environment → 005 ethnographic/comparative proof partition`

The institutional middle is secure; direct assignment of a specific methodological sentence to Marillier remains unproved.

### C. Paris-field triangulation: strong contemporary control, no reading claim

`Marillier ↔ Hubert–Mauss ↔ 005`

This shows that the proof-threshold problem was not an anachronistic reconstruction but an explicit methodological problem in the immediate Paris field. It does not prove textual dependence.

---

## 7. The chronology makes the transatlantic model stronger, not weaker

A simplistic chronology would have been:

`Harvard method complete → Paris adds anthropology topics → Lovejoy carries method forward`.

The evidence now supports a more interesting and historically realistic model:

> **The philological method was still being worked out, published and reformulated at the same moment that Lovejoy entered a Paris environment organized around analogous problems of testimony, comparison, causal classification and genealogy. The transition is therefore an overlap zone rather than a clean handoff.**

This fits the archive better and removes the need to manufacture a decisive moment of conversion.

The analytic novelty of the paper can lie precisely in this overlap: 004 and 005 show two research genres coexisting around the same researcher while their proof architectures converge.

## 8. 1902 is securely downstream, but proposition-level collation remains unfinished

JSTOR securely identifies Lovejoy’s `Religion and the Time-Process` in *The American Journal of Theology* 6.3 (July 1902), pp.439–472, stable object `3153919`. Hathi/ISSN controls confirm the 1902 volume is preserved and public-domain access exists through JSTOR.

The current web pass did **not** yet recover a proposition-level full text from JSTOR XML. Therefore Batch 74 does not use the 1902 essay to manufacture an exact endpoint quotation.

Existing repo work has already flagged 1902 as a likely scale-expansion bridge, including the relation between Buddhist/Vedāntic materials and larger histories of temporal/eternal concepts. That remains a research hypothesis/control until the exact passages relevant to resemblance, borrowing, internal logic and historical genealogy are directly collated.

## 9. Paper-level chronological formulation

A defensible paragraph is now:

> **The transition from Lovejoy’s Buddhist philology to his Paris comparative-religion work should not be narrated as a clean succession of completed stages. His article on `upādāna` and `upādisesa` appeared only in the second half of 1898, the same broad interval in which he entered the 1898–99 EPHE environment. Notebook 004 nevertheless preserves the prepublication construction of the article’s philological distinctions, while notebook 005 shows an analogous partition of proof applied to ethnographic testimony and ritual comparison. Paris is therefore best treated neither as the origin of Lovejoy’s analytic method nor as a merely topical detour, but as an overlap zone in which an already developing philological inference discipline was translated and reinforced within another research genre.**

Compact formulation:

> **The Harvard-to-Paris transition is an overlap zone, not a handoff.**

This is our analytical formulation.

---

## 10. Evidence cautions

1. `Second Half 1898` is secure; `July–December` is supported by Hathi/CiNii serial metadata. This still does not date Lovejoy’s individual article to a specific day or month.
2. No submission or proof date for the JAOS article has yet been recovered.
3. No exact travel/arrival date for Lovejoy in Paris has yet been recovered in this pass. Use the official 1898–99 participation interval only.
4. The Harvard affiliation on the article should not be treated as a physical-location stamp at publication.
5. *L’Année sociologique* vol.2 dating has bibliographic variation between volume-year labels and standard article citation. Do not use it to assert that Lovejoy saw Hubert–Mauss before a specific 005 entry.
6. Hubert–Mauss remains a field control, not a reading/influence claim.
7. 1902 is bibliographically secure downstream evidence but direct proposition-level collation remains pending.
