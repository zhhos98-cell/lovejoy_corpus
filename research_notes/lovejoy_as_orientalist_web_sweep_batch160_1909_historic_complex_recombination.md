## Core result

Batch159 substantially narrowed the Lovejoy-specific methodological delta. Oldenberg and Jacobi already supplied diagnostic/source controls in the Indological field; Marillier already supplied an anti-unilateral, anti-morphological mechanism discipline in comparative religion. Notebook 004 and notebook 005 are therefore best treated as a site of **cross-disciplinary convergence, selection, and redeployment**, not as the birthplace of the constituent operators.

Lovejoy's 1909 `Pragmatism and Realism` supplies a strikingly explicit published criterion for where historical novelty may nevertheless reside:

> a doctrine may be historically distinctive **even when its constituent parts are not novel**, because the novelty can lie in their combination and in the mutual transformation produced by that combination.

Lovejoy's own phrase is a `new compound in intellectual chemistry`.

This does not prove that the notebooks caused the 1909 formulation, nor that Lovejoy was retrospectively theorizing his own 1896–1906 research practice. It does, however, materially strengthen the post-Batch159 reconstruction: **Lovejoy himself explicitly regarded recombination as a legitimate locus of historical distinctiveness.**

## 1. Named doctrines are historical complexes, not logical bricks

Lovejoy opens by denying that `pragmatism` is a well-defined substantive entity, a `logical brick` that can be handed around, analyzed, and broken into pieces without alteration or loss of identity.

He generalizes the point to named historical doctrines. His immediate example is Christianity: as a name for a collection of historical phenomena, `Christianity` has always contained a large diversity of elements. One can arbitrarily select one element, pronounce it `essential` or `fundamental`, and then prove almost any desired compatibility or incompatibility with another named doctrine.

This is not merely terminological skepticism. Lovejoy is specifying a historical-object problem:

`named -ism`  
≠ `single stable logical essence`  
= `historically changing complex of motives/elements/tendencies`.

That formulation is directly relevant to the Batch159 attribution problem. Once Oldenberg/Jacobi/Marillier show that the individual analytic operators were already available in adjacent disciplinary fields, the historical object to explain is not an isolated Lovejoyian rule but a **configuration of inherited rules and motives in one working practice**.

---

## 2. `Essentiality` and distinctiveness require historical discrimination

Lovejoy then states a criterion for treating one feature as more essential than another in a historical complex.

For Christianity, there is no objective reason to privilege one characteristic unless it can be historically shown to be:

1. the sole teaching of the actual originator;
2. the most emphasized teaching of that originator; or
3. a teaching not previously expressed or emphasized — a genuinely novel contribution.

He applies the same problem immediately to pragmatism, calling it a `historic complex of mixed philosophical motives and tendencies`.

The methodological warning is very close to the project-level `diagnostic specificity` problem, but it is now Lovejoy's own published language: one must not choose an arbitrary trait from a heterogeneous complex and then use it to establish whatever affinity one prefers.

The important post-Batch159 point is not that Lovejoy invented this discriminative discipline in 1909. Oldenberg and Jacobi already provide field controls. What is new here is that Lovejoy **explicitly theorizes the historical status of the complex itself** and asks which feature, if any, gives that complex objective distinctiveness.

---

## 3. Complete enumeration before affinity

Lovejoy's procedure is unusually explicit.

Before deciding what pragmatism essentially is, one should first make a `complete enumeration` of the important ideas or logical motives actually emphasized by people calling themselves pragmatists.

Only after that inventory should one ask whether:

- any one motive separately is historically novel/distinctive; or
- **the fact of their combination** is historically novel/distinctive.

Without these precautions, a comparison between pragmatism and another `-ism` does not concern an objective historical thing. It concerns only a set of propositions arbitrarily selected by the analyst.

This gives a published Lovejoyian version of a discipline already visible materially in the notebooks:

`inventory heterogeneous materials`  
→ `do not let a surface label define the object`  
→ `identify discriminating relations/elements`  
→ `test historical distinctiveness at the level of component and combination`.

The notebook comparison remains documentary rather than terminological: 004/005 do not contain this 1909 formula as such.

## 4. Novelty can belong to the combination rather than the constituents

Lovejoy's objection to W. P. Montague is especially important. Montague, he says, treats pragmatism too atomistically and fails to recognize that its instrumentalism is transformed by conjunction with another motive, nominalism.

Lovejoy describes pragmatism as a `medley of diverse logical motives`. Some are incompatible; several can nevertheless be harmonized. The interpreter should take as many elements together as logic permits and allow the synthesized elements to **modify and interpret each other**.

The decisive formulation follows:

> a doctrine may be more or less novel even though its constituent parts are not novel; it may be a `new compound in intellectual chemistry`.

He then gives the substantive example: pragmatism's distinctiveness consists precisely in combining instrumentalism with the method of nominalism.

This is the strongest primary support yet recovered for treating **recombination itself** as a plausible Lovejoy-specific historical delta once constituent-rule priority has been withdrawn.

The logic is not additive:

`old element A + old element B`  
≠ merely `A beside B`.

Rather:

`A in conjunction with B`  
→ `A transformed by B`  
→ `B interpreted through A`  
→ potentially distinctive compound C.

Project shorthand: **combinatorial novelty / recombination criterion**. These are analytical labels, not Lovejoy's terms.

## 6. Relation to notebook 004

After Oldenberg 1897 and Jacobi 1897, notebook 004 should not bear a novelty claim for:

- generic-versus-characteristic discrimination;
- composite inheritance without necessary incoherence;
- usage over speculative etymology;
- source chronology/genre control;
- skepticism about negative coincidence;
- comparison by question/function rather than verbal/formal resemblance.

What 004 still documents at unusually fine resolution is **selection and recomposition**:

- Rhys Davids, Warren, Senart, Hardy, Oldenberg, Müller, Dahlmann and other source propositions enter the same manuscript workspace;
- Lovejoy changes their evidentiary weights;
- he tests one source's proposition against another kind of evidence;
- he redistributes causal/semantic functions before producing the much narrower 1898 article.

The 1909 article makes this type of historical distinctiveness conceptually intelligible without requiring novelty at the level of every constituent operation.

## 7. Relation to notebook 005 and Marillier

Marillier 1896 now proves that external resemblance does not warrant a single explanatory interpretation and that cannibal practices can have heterogeneous causes and plural origins.

Notebook 005 therefore does not originate the anti-morphological rule. Its documentary value lies in the range and persistence of its redeployment:

- survivance and eschatological determinants;
- sacrifice and mixed motives;
- provision/communication/propitiation;
- blood and direct transferable efficacy;
- foundation rites;
- fetish versus charm/amulet;
- agrarian/fecundative rites;
- anthropophagy;
- initiation/circumcision.

Across this material, one can observe a Marillierian mode of discrimination being combined with Lovejoy's broader habits of source control, classificatory discrimination, and genealogy/function separation.

Again, `combination` here is an archival reconstruction. The 1909 essay supports the legitimacy of looking for historical distinctiveness at that level; it does not prove a direct Marillier → 1909 conceptual genealogy.

## 8. 1906 and 1909 perform different abstractions

The 1906 `Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` and the 1909 `Pragmatism and Realism` should not be collapsed.

### 1906

Lovejoy takes one recurring causal architecture — impersonal, diffusive, transferable, quasi-mechanical efficacy — and elevates it into a broad comparative category. Batch157 called this **mechanism universalism without genealogical monism**.

### 1909

Lovejoy addresses the historical/logical identity of named doctrines. He says a historical complex may be distinctive because of the configuration of its motives even if the motives themselves are old.

Thus the two moves are complementary:

`1906: abstraction of a recurrent mechanism across heterogeneous cases`  
`1909: explicit theory of distinctiveness through recombination within a heterogeneous historical complex`.

The first concerns cross-case causal category formation; the second concerns the historical identity of a doctrine/configuration.

## 9. Christianity is already inside the 1909 methodological example

The intended next extension had been Lovejoy's 1908 `The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus` against W. B. Smith's 1909 reply. The public volume routes are secure, but a proposition-level primary collation of that debate has not yet been completed because the currently reachable web derivatives are unreliable.

The 1909 pragmatism article nevertheless supplies a genuine cross-domain extension now: Lovejoy uses **Christianity itself** as his first example of why a named historical formation cannot be assigned an arbitrary essence.

This does not substitute for the Smith controversy. It does show that by 1909 the historical-complex problem was explicitly being generalized beyond a single philosophical `-ism`.

The Lovejoy–Smith pair remains worth retrieving if an exact primary-text route becomes stable, but it is no longer required to establish that Lovejoy was applying the complex/essentiality problem outside the 004/005 immediate source fields.

## 10. Strongest revised thesis after Batch160

> **The early Lovejoy notebooks are historically valuable not because they preserve inference rules unavailable in the contemporary Indological or comparative-religion fields, but because they document the assembly, reweighting and redeployment of heterogeneous analytical practices within one research trajectory. Oldenberg and Jacobi supply diagnostic and source-critical controls; Marillier supplies anti-unilateral mechanism analysis. In notebooks 004 and 005 these practices meet different source ecologies and are recomposed in local acts of classification. The 1906 article promotes one recurrent causal mechanism into a comparative category; by 1909 Lovejoy explicitly defines historical distinctiveness as something that may belong to a combination even when its constituent motives are not new — a `new compound in intellectual chemistry`. The defensible developmental claim is therefore not operator invention but cross-domain recombination and transport.**
