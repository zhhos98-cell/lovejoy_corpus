# Lovejoy as Orientalist / comparative religion — web sweep batch 13

Date: 2026-08-18  
Focus: the publication ecology of Lovejoy’s 1906/1908 *Monist* articles; Open Court / Paul Carus as an Orientalist-comparative-religion infrastructure; new archive lead at Southern Illinois University.

## 1. Why this matters

Lovejoy’s 1906 “The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy” and 1908 “The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus” were not published in a disciplinary vacuum. *The Monist*, under Paul Carus and Open Court, was simultaneously a philosophy journal and a major American conduit for comparative religion, Buddhism, Asian philosophy, philology, and translations.

This does **not** by itself prove that Carus, Suzuki, Dharmapala, or other Open Court figures shaped Lovejoy’s arguments. The evidentiary value is institutional and editorial: it identifies a publication network in which Lovejoy’s comparative-religion articles circulated and generates a new archive target for correspondence/manuscripts.

## 2. The 1906 issue itself is an Orientalist/comparative-philology neighborhood

Oxford’s primary issue table for *The Monist* 16.3 (1 July 1906) shows the sequence:

- Friedrich Hirth, **“Origin of the Mariners’ Compass in China,”** pp.321–330;
- editorial article on Mach, pp.331–356;
- Arthur O. Lovejoy, **“The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy,”** pp.357–382;
- Lawrence H. Mills, **“The Bible, The Persian Inscriptions, and the Avesta,”** pp.383–387;
- followed by mathematics, philosophy of God, etc.

Source: Oxford Academic, *The Monist* vol.16 issue 3.

Lovejoy’s article therefore sits literally between a China-history/technology article and an Avesta/Persian/Biblical philology article. This is contextual evidence for the journal’s mixed philosophy/Oriental-studies ecology, not evidence that these neighboring papers influenced Lovejoy.

## 3. The 1908 issue is even more explicit

The primary issue table for *The Monist* 18.4 (1 Oct. 1908), where Lovejoy published the W. B. Smith essay-review, includes among other pieces:

- D. T. Suzuki, **“A Brief History of Early Chinese Philosophy”**;
- an article on the classification of religions;
- Berthold Laufer, **“The Jonah Legend in India”**;
- Karl Borinski extracts reviewing *Der Vorchristliche Jesus*;
- Lovejoy, **“The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus,”** pp.597–609.

The complete public-domain volume is independently preserved at Internet Archive as item **`monistquart18hegeuoft`**, 694 pages, with full-text/OCR/PDF download options. This gives a concrete primary retrieval route for Lovejoy’s 1908 article even though Oxford’s article PDF is paywalled.

## 4. Paul Carus / Open Court was directly involved in transmitting Buddhist and Asian thought

Authoritative modern biographical/institutional sources independently document the Open Court network:

- Association for Asian Studies records that after the 1893 World’s Parliament of Religions, Carus recruited D. T. Suzuki (through Shaku Sōen) to work in Illinois; Suzuki translated Asian religious texts and wrote early English work for Carus’s journals, including *The Monist*.
- Open Court published Buddhist and Chinese materials, and Suzuki’s *Outlines of Mahāyāna Buddhism* grew from essays written for *The Monist*.
- The Southern Illinois University Open Court archive describes the company as a forum for philosophy, science and religion and specifically notes its role in printing and spreading **Eastern thought in the West**.

Again, this is publication-network evidence. A direct Lovejoy–Carus/Suzuki intellectual relation requires correspondence or editorial records.

## 5. New archival lead: SIU’s Open Court Publishing Company Records (MSS 027)

Southern Illinois University Carbondale holds **MSS 027, Open Court Publishing Company Records**, a very large archive (424 boxes) covering 1886–1998, predominantly 1887–1920.

The collection includes:

- editorial/business correspondence;
- article and book manuscripts and production material for *The Open Court* and *The Monist*;
- promotional and financial records;
- material in **Chinese, English, French, German, Japanese and Pāli**.

Most importantly for this project, the collection-level person indexing explicitly includes **Arthur O. Lovejoy (1873–1962)**. The same index includes Anagarika Dharmapala, Max Müller, Lucien Lévy-Bruhl, Shaku Sōen, D. T. Suzuki, Swami Vivekananda, Josiah Royce and many others.

Current evidentiary status:

- **confirmed:** Lovejoy is indexed as a person represented in MSS 027 at collection level;
- **confirmed:** MSS 027 contains editorial correspondence/manuscripts for *The Monist* under Carus;
- **unresolved:** which exact Lovejoy folder(s), dates, letters, manuscripts, or editorial exchanges survive.

The web index has not yet surfaced a folder titled directly “Lovejoy, Arthur O.” Search-engine results mostly inherit the collection-wide person index. We should therefore not claim correspondence contents until the folder-level finding aid is searched or SIU is queried.

## 6. Why this archive may be unusually high-yield

If Lovejoy submitted the 1906 and/or 1908 papers through normal editorial channels, MSS 027 may preserve some combination of:

- submission letters;
- editor-author correspondence with Paul Carus/Open Court staff;
- article manuscript/typescript or corrected proof;
- requests for revision;
- offprint/payment/production correspondence;
- correspondence mentioning ethnographic or Orientalist sources.

This is an inference from the collection’s documented scope, not a claim that these Lovejoy items exist.

The archive could also allow us to reconstruct whether Lovejoy’s relation to the Carus/Suzuki world was merely publication-based or involved a more substantive comparative-religion network.

## 7. A potentially important publication-ecology distinction

The developing project now has at least three institutional ecologies for Lovejoy’s early Orientalist/comparative work:

1. **Harvard, c.1895–98:** Lanman/Warren/Everett/Toy — formal language, Buddhist-text, comparative-religion, Hebrew-religion formation.
2. **Paris/EPHE, 1898–99:** Lévi + Marillier — private Buddhist reading plus systematic comparative religion/ethnographic classification.
3. **Open Court / *The Monist*, 1906–08:** an editorial-publication network that actively circulated Asian philosophy, Buddhism, comparative religion and philology.

The third should not be collapsed into the first two. It is evidence about **circulation, editorial selection and scholarly neighborhood**, and potentially about correspondence if MSS 027 yields direct records.
