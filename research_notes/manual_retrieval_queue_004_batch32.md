### Hermann Oldenberg, _Buddha_, true 3rd enlarged ed. (1897)

**PRIMARY_TEXT_RECOVERED / TRUE EDITION CONTROLLED.**

The previously uploaded `buddhaseinleben03oldegoog` remains the **1890 second edition** and is retained only as an earlier-edition baseline.

#### Printed pp.272–273 — Senart / upādāna control

Oldenberg says Buddhism continually works with inherited `Schlagworte`, but combines them so that thoughts, even if often unclear thoughts, remain recognizable. He then directly rejects Senart's equation of `upādāna` with the five `upādānakkhandha` and cites canonical texts distinguishing them: `upādāna` is `chandarāga`, desire/attachment **with respect to** the five khandhas.

This is an exact prior field articulation of a major semantic move later visible in Lovejoy 004/1898.

#### Printed pp.443–455 — edition-specific Sāṁkhya/Buddhism excursus

The full excursus is now controlled.

- **p.443:** Oldenberg softens his earlier skepticism under the impact of Garbe, Jacobi, Senart, and Dahlmann, but resists the strongest dependency claims.
- **p.444:** old keywords, keyword-groups, and whole category-series can be placed in the service of **new fundamental ideas**; a loose older worldview can be recast into a strict new systematic form and modified even at its foundations. Gradual genesis can therefore yield a formation that looks `aus einem Gusse`.
- **p.445:** comparison should seek `Hauptgedanken` in which Buddhism shares **characteristic directions** of later Sāṁkhya without prematurely equating preparatory thought-circles with a named school.
- **pp.445–446:** generic eternal-Self/world or body/mind parallels may be accidental, poorly reported, or even contrary to characteristic Sāṁkhya doctrine.
- **p.447:** the Buddhist causal chain and Sāṁkhya evolution answer **totally different questions** — the arising of suffering in psychic processes versus the evolution of the universe from the world-ground.
- **p.448:** related concepts and neighboring positions can be natural contacts between systems using related conceptual tools; the evidence permits little more.
- **p.449:** the five senses plus manas matching on both sides `beweist natürlich wenig`; comparison fails when it reaches the **really characteristic conceptions of Sāṁkhya**.
- **p.450:** Buddhism's lack of guṇas cannot support reconstruction of a guṇa-less Sāṁkhya prototype; that hangs too heavy a weight on a hook that cannot carry it.
- **pp.451–453:** negative coincidence is weak; Aśvaghoṣa's relatively late testimony about Arāḍa must be tested against older sources and the literary/poetic character of the _Buddhacarita_.
- **pp.454–455:** positive canonical usage defeats Senart's speculative `sakkāya = satkārya` etymology; the direct Sāṁkhya allusion is not established.
