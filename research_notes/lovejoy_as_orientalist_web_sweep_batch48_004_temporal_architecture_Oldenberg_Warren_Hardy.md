## Core result

Lovejoy’s 1898 three-life partition should **not** be treated as an original discovery.

The article says that the formula, although **not a strict temporal sequence**, may broadly be divided as:

- links **1–2**: previous existence;
- links **3–9**: present existence;
- links **10–12**: future existence.

This yields a **2–7–3** partition and places the temporal transition **after `upādāna` (9), before `bhava` (10)**.

At first sight this differs from the later scholastic/Visuddhimagga-style **2–8–2** allocation, in which `bhava` remains part of present karma-existence and the future begins at `jāti`. But a pre-Lovejoy Oldenberg/Waddell line already places the conceptual boundary after the ninth link: Waddell 1895 explicitly says the life-history runs through link 9 and then quotes/criticizes Oldenberg’s rendering of link 10 `bhava` as **rebirth / continuance for another existence**.

The safest result is therefore:

> **Lovejoy selects and recomposes an available Oldenberg-style placement of `bhava` on the renewal/future side, rather than inventing the 2–7–3 partition. What is distinctive in 004 → 1898 is how that temporal placement becomes integrated with Hardy’s two-factor causal distinction and with Warren’s warning against reading the formula as a strict homogeneous temporal sequence.**

This is a particularly clean example of the larger project thesis: Lovejoy’s contribution is **configuration of inherited interpretive resources**, not priority for each component.

## 1. 004 already gives `bhava` a renewal/continuance role

Corrected 004 PDF p.21 / MS p.73 defines `bhava` from the Nidāna-Saṁyutta as the three existences (`kāma-bhava`, `rūpa-bhava`, `arūpa-bhava`) and then adds Lovejoy’s interpretive gloss:

- `bhava` = existence of a being in general;
- it is specifically taken/formalized in `jāti` and `jarāmaraṇa`;
- Childers’s `continued existence` is said to be **precisely what it means in the paticca-samuppāda formula**.

This is significant because it places `bhava` conceptually beside the **renewal/continuance** problem before the notebook reaches the explicit Hardy fact/manner question on p.33.

The sequence of notebook work is therefore:

1. p.21: `bhava` = continued existence, specifically worked out through `jāti / jarāmaraṇa`;
2. pp.22–23: separate `upādāna` from `taṇhā` and test attachment categories;
3. pp.27–28: map links 3–8 as one present sentient/psychological complex and discuss a new `nāmarūpa` with rebirth;
4. p.33: Hardy p.394 becomes the explicit `fact` vs `manner` of continued existence problem.

This ordering strongly suggests that the published temporal boundary and the published causal bifurcation were being built together in 004.

## 2. Lovejoy’s 1898 structure: `upādāna` is the hinge, `bhava` belongs to renewal

The published article makes `upādāna` the causal hinge between the existing khandha-combination and a new one.

Lovejoy argues:

- present khandhas provide the field/object of attachment;
- `upādāna` is the chronic identification of will/interests with those conditions;
- whether the dissolution of the present group is followed by formation of a new group depends on whether `upādāna` persists;
- karma determines the **particular condition** of rebirth;
- `upādāna` determines the **fact of rebirth as such**.

This causal allocation makes the 9→10 boundary intelligible in his reconstruction:

`present attachment (9)` → `continued/new existence (10)` → `birth (11)` → `ageing/death (12)`.

Thus the 2–7–3 temporal partition is not an independent chronological thesis added to the semantic argument. It is structurally coupled to Lovejoy’s interpretation of `upādāna` as the transition-producing cause and `bhava` as renewed/continued existence.

## 3. Oldenberg/Waddell show that the 9→10 boundary was already available before Lovejoy

A pre-1898 control is L. A. Waddell’s 1895 discussion of the nidāna chain in _The Buddhism of Tibet or Lamaism_ and his closely related JRAS treatment.

Waddell says, in substance, that **up through link 9 the evolution is clearly the life-history of a person**. He then turns to link 10, `bhava`, and quotes/criticizes Oldenberg’s rendering along the lines:

> from clinging comes rebirth and the continuance of being for another existence.

Waddell himself objects to treating `bhava` simply as rebirth, so he is not evidence for unanimous acceptance of the boundary. His value is precisely as a contemporary witness that the **Oldenberg interpretation placing `bhava` on the renewed-existence side was already in circulation by 1895**.

Evidence-layer caution:

- the Waddell passage has been recovered through public digital text/search in the current research run;
- Oldenberg’s exact English/German locus for this `bhava` rendering should still be checked directly before final quotation in a paper;
- Waddell is **not** yet a demonstrated Lovejoy source. Use him as field/reception control, not transmission evidence.

## 4. The competing scholastic model places `bhava` on the present side

Warren’s _Buddhism in Translations_ (1896) prints the Visuddhimagga-style three-life analysis. In that standard scholastic framework, the causal complex is not divided exactly as Lovejoy divides it.

The model treats the previous-life factors as including ignorance/karma/desire/attachment/`bhava`-type karma-existence and then explains the present/future causal series through a distinction among causal and resultant factors. In later standard shorthand this becomes the familiar **2–8–2** arrangement:

- past: 1–2;
- present: 3–10, including `bhava`;
- future: 11–12.

Important qualification: Warren is publishing/translating a **commentarial source model**. Do not convert that fact into the claim “Warren personally held 2–8–2” without further authorial evidence.

The historiographical value is that Lovejoy’s 1898 **2–7–3** was not mechanically inherited from the most obvious scholastic allocation available in Warren’s source packet.

## 5. Warren 1893 may explain Lovejoy’s warning against a strict temporal sequence

Lovejoy’s 1898 temporal paragraph contains an important qualification: the twelve links do **not** express a strict temporal sequence.

A pre-Lovejoy Warren discussion, quoted contemporaneously by Waddell, says that much difficulty arises from using `cause` too strictly in dependent origination; **time is being allowed too important a role whereas it should be secondary**, and `cause`/condition must be understood loosely/flexibly and in different senses.

This is extremely close to the methodological warning Lovejoy later publishes.

But source control is not yet complete:

- Waddell’s 1895 article is a contemporary relay for Warren’s statement;
- the direct Warren locus is **Proceedings of the American Oriental Society, April 6–8, 1893, p. xxvii**;
- Lovejoy 1898 independently cites **Warren, JAOS vol. XVI, p. xxvii** nearby for the claim that desire and attachment stand in a relation of identity.

That last fact makes direct exposure to Warren’s p.xxvii discussion highly plausible, but the exact 1893 page must be recovered before asserting that Lovejoy’s “not strict temporal sequence” sentence directly depends on Warren.

Current inference ladder:

### Secure
- Warren p.xxvii is a source Lovejoy cites elsewhere in the same 1898 discussion.
- Waddell 1895 quotes Warren as warning that time is secondary and `cause` must be used flexibly.
- Lovejoy 1898 says the nidānas do not express strict temporal sequence.

### Strong but not yet direct

### Not established

## 6. A three-source recomposition model now fits 004 especially well

The temporal/causal architecture can be reconstructed without awarding Lovejoy priority for any single ingredient:

### Oldenberg line

Supplies an interpretation in which `bhava` is renewal/continued existence and the practical transition can fall after link 9.

### Hardy line

Supplies the two-factor causal architecture:

- `upādāna` → renewed existence;
- karma → manner/state of that existence.

004 turns this into `fact` vs `manner`; 1898 turns it into rebirth-per-se vs particular condition.

### Warren line

Supplies a live warning that `paccaya/cause` should not be read as one strict temporal relation and that time is secondary; Warren’s 1896 book also preserves the rival scholastic causal/three-life framework.

### Lovejoy 004 → 1898

Combines these into one configuration:

- links 3–8 form the sentient/psychological complex of the present organism;
- link 9 `upādāna` is the chronic volitional attachment that makes another existence occur;
- link 10 `bhava` is the renewed/continued existence;
- links 11–12 express its birth and dissolution;
- the whole chain is causal but not a single homogeneous temporal succession;
- karma and `upādāna` perform distinct causal jobs.

This is exactly the kind of source transformation the 004 archive can demonstrate at unusually fine resolution.

## 7. Later Poussin control: the standard model remained different and multi-dimensional

La Vallée Poussin’s 1905/06 paper explicitly takes the **past/present/future distribution** of nidānas as a dogmatic problem and says there is an orthodox doctrine. His 1913 treatment makes the standard distribution clear and also distinguishes it from other analytical dimensions such as `kleśa / karman / vipāka`.

This later control is useful for two reasons:

1. it confirms that temporal allocation was itself a specialist interpretive problem, not a trivial reading of the list;
2. it reinforces that **temporal placement, causal role and doctrinal category must not be collapsed**.

Lovejoy’s 2–7–3 should therefore be described as one reconstruction among competing late-19C/early-20C arrangements.

## 8. Revised paper-level payoff

The temporal architecture gives the strongest concrete version yet of the project’s “configuration” thesis.

We no longer need to say abstractly that Lovejoy recombined sources. We can show a specific configuration whose ingredients have different upstream histories:

- `bhava` as renewed/continued existence: Oldenberg line;
- `upādāna` as cause of renewal while karma determines manner: Hardy;
- temporal causality not strict/homogeneous: Warren environment;
- standard scholastic alternative with `bhava` on present side: available through Warren/Visuddhimagga;
- Lovejoy’s notebook: `bhava` → continued existence, 3–8 diagram, then Hardy fact/manner question;
- Lovejoy’s article: 1–2 past / 3–9 present / 10–12 future plus causal-role distinction.

The Lovejoy-specific historical object is therefore the **assembled explanatory architecture**, not an isolated proposition.

That makes “configuration” literal rather than metaphorical.
