# AOS 1897 membership, History of Religions Section, and Lovejoy status

Date: 2026-09-01

## New state-bearing observation

The American Oriental Society chronology now needs a distinction between three different statuses:

1. announced scholarly communication;
2. ordinary Society membership;
3. membership in the newly organized Section for the Historical Study of Religions.

These cannot be collapsed into a single institutional belonging claim.

## Lovejoy and AOS

Current evidence:

- The 10 April 1897 Final Circular announced:
  `Mr. ARTHUR O. LOVEJOY, Harvard University; Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha.`
- The 1898 JAOS membership list records:
  `ARTHUR ONCKEN LOVEJOY (Harvard University) ... 1897.`

Interpretation:

Lovejoy entered AOS membership in 1897. The membership date independently confirms that 1897 was an institutional transition point in his Orientalist/public scholarly position.

Do not infer:

- that Lanman nominated Lovejoy;
- that Toy sponsored Lovejoy;
- that AOS membership itself proves delivery of the Milinda paper.

## History of Religions Section firewall

The AOS created a Section for the Historical Study of Religions around the 1897 meeting cycle. The 1898 proceedings list the Section and its officers/membership structure.

Lovejoy is recorded as an AOS member but is not listed among the Section members.

Therefore:

`AOS member` != `member of History of Religions Section`.

This distinction matters because Lovejoy's Harvard training placed him close to the same network:

- Toy: comparative religion instructor;
- Lanman: Pali instructor;
- AOS religious-studies institutional development.

The evidence supports proximity, not a direct organizational role.

## Research significance

The emerging pattern matches the wider Lovejoy project method:

Institutional adjacency is not itself proof of transmission.

The same evidentiary discipline used for:

`comparison -> genealogy`

applies here:

`shared institution/network -> personal influence`.
