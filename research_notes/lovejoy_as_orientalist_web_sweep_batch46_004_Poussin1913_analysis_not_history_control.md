Focus: isolate a methodological passage in La Vallée Poussin’s 1913 _Théorie des douze causes_ that sharply clarifies the historiographical status of the `004 → 1898 → 1902/1907` Lovejoy argument. This is a **later field control**, not an upstream source for 004.

## Core finding

La Vallée Poussin’s 1913 preliminary note states almost exactly the distinction that our 004 source-control work has forced us to formulate, but in explicit methodological language:

> the work of analysis does **not necessarily have historical value**; analysis is indispensable for understanding ideas because we can only understand them by reconstructing them for ourselves according to a **logical or at least intelligible development**, yet that reconstruction may be incapable of giving more than a plausible or probable account of the **real historical development**.

This is a major historiographical control.

It means that the distinction we have been recovering from Lovejoy — between genealogy/provenance and logical/configurational function — was not an idiosyncratic or uniquely Lovejoyan problem. By 1913 a leading Buddhist scholar could state the separation between **logical reconstruction** and **historical reconstruction** in programmatic terms while discussing the very same twelve-link formula.

The paper’s contribution must therefore be documentary and chronological rather than a uniqueness claim:

> 004 shows Lovejoy operationalizing this separation in a late-1890s notebook before it appears in his own more general published language of categories, presuppositions and logical sequence.

## 1. Poussin 1913 explicitly separates three different research questions

The preliminary note to _Bouddhisme, études et matériaux: Théorie des douze causes_ (1913) makes an unusually clean division.

Poussin says that one can, with reasonable security:

1. determine the **general meaning** of the Pratītyasamutpāda as an explanation of suffering/rebirth;
2. determine its explicit or implicit **philosophical scope**.

But two other questions are much more difficult:

3. the **origins of the formula**;
4. the **exact original value of the terms** once assembled into the formula.

For the origins one encounters incompletely explained/systematized data and categories that may have been originally independent; for the exact terms one encounters later definitions and systematizations that can be incoherent or artificial.

Poussin therefore deliberately limits his task rather than allowing one sort of evidence to answer all four questions.

This is a mature and explicit version of a **division of evidentiary burdens**.

## 2. His footnote makes the history/logic distinction programmatic

The key methodological footnote explains the principle guiding his canonical analysis.

Poussin assumes that canonical definitions themselves result from processes of arrangement and exegesis analogous to those visible in post-canonical literature; the twelve-link chain was not created ex nihilo and was not from the first complete together with a complete exegesis.

But he immediately adds the decisive qualification:

- analytical reconstruction is **indispensable to intelligibility**;
- it reconstructs ideas according to a **logical or at least intelligible development**;
- it does **not necessarily have historical value**;
- it may be unable to establish the **real development** beyond plausibility/probability.

The distinction is therefore not simply `history versus philosophy`. It is:

`logical/intelligible reconstruction` ≠ `demonstrated real historical development`.

The two are legitimate but evidentially different operations.

## 3. This is strikingly close to the Lovejoy route, but chronologically later

### 004 / 1898

Lovejoy grants that terms may be borrowed, the formula composite and the order historically heterogeneous, while still asking what semantic and causal relations make the arrangement intelligible.

In 1898 he explicitly separates derivational questions from semantic questions when he says that if both etymologies of `upādi` are possible, the meaning must be settled **without reference to etymology**.

### 1902

In `Religion and the Time-Process`, structural affinity between Aristotle and Vedānta does not establish eastern transmission if an internal Greek genealogy suffices. Logical resemblance and historical derivation carry different burdens of proof.

### 1907

In `The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought`, Lovejoy explicitly distinguishes **logical sequence** from chronological sequence, noting that texts of the same date can embody different stages of reflection and that older intellectual forms survive alongside newer ones.

### Poussin 1913

Poussin formulates the general methodological warning directly within Buddhist philology: logical/intelligible reconstruction is necessary for understanding ideas, but it does not by itself reconstruct the real historical sequence.

The chronology matters. We should not use Poussin to explain Lovejoy’s 1890s notebook. We should use him as a **field-level control** showing that the distinction Lovejoy was already operationalizing became an explicit methodological problem in subsequent Buddhist studies.

## 4. Poussin’s bibliography makes the reception gap more interesting

In the same preliminary note Poussin says he will rely heavily on predecessors, naming especially **Oldenberg, Senart and Oltramare**. In the note on pre-/extra-Buddhist origins he lists Garbe, Jacobi, Senart, Oldenberg, Walleser, Pischel, Rhys Davids and Kern. For the history of Western interpretation of the Pratītyasamutpāda he sends readers to **Paul Oltramare, _La formule bouddhique des douze causes_ (1909)**, calling it an excellent summary, and names Burnouf, Spence Hardy, Childers, Kern, E. Hardy, Warren and Rhys Davids among the earlier interpreters.

In the currently indexed Wikisource text, **Lovejoy does not appear in these visible survey lists**.

This cannot be converted into a claim that Poussin or Oltramare had never read Lovejoy. But it changes the priority of the reception search:

> Oltramare 1909 is now the single most valuable direct reception control because Poussin 1913 explicitly treats it as a summary of the Western interpretation history.

If Oltramare surveys 1844–1909 interpretations and omits Lovejoy 1898, that would be a much stronger statement about Lovejoy’s position in the specialist memory of the problem than generic search-engine silence.

## 6. Poussin’s 1910 review of Oltramare gives a Harvard/Lanman network control

La Vallée Poussin reviewed Oltramare’s pamphlet in _JRAS_ 42.1 (January 1910), pp.201–206, DOI `10.1017/S0035869X00081958`.

One note is institutionally interesting: discussing Pāli reference practice, Poussin says that “the true light” had recently come from **Cambridge (Mass.)** and jokes that abandoning older sutta/vagga/samyutta notation would please **“our excellent friend Professor Lanman.”**

This is not evidence that Poussin knew Lovejoy’s 1898 paper, and it comes too late to explain 004. But it confirms that the post-1898 European Buddhist specialist network remained directly connected to Lanman’s Harvard environment — the same environment in which Lovejoy had received Sanskrit/Buddhist-text training.

Use this only as network context, not as a transmission chain.

## 7. Stronger revised methodological claim

After this control, the paper should distinguish three levels very explicitly.

### Level A — period field repertoire

By Senart 1896, Oldenberg, later Poussin, etc., Buddhist studies already contains:

- term filiation;
- textual stratification;
- borrowing/source genealogy;
- system reconstruction;
- warnings about artificial scholastic coherence;
- warnings about over-reading historical development from logical reconstruction.

### Level B — Lovejoy’s documentary microgenesis

004 uniquely lets us watch **his** use of that repertoire at work:

- a Rhys-Davids comparison is explicitly supplemented and reweighted;
- Hardy’s two-factor statement becomes a fact/manner analytic question and then a historical-causal explanation;
- Senart’s composite formula is granted historically while his semantic/systematic inference is restricted;
- competing `upādi/upadhi` genealogies are bracketed when usage must decide meaning.

### Level C — Lovejoy’s later generalization

1902/1907 show similar evidentiary separations at a larger scale:

- resemblance vs transmission;
- logical sequence vs chronological sequence;
- persistent presuppositions/logical motives vs intact named systems.

The project contribution is therefore:

> **not the invention of the distinction between history and logical reconstruction, but the archival reconstruction of how Lovejoy learned to perform that distinction in one technical field and then redeployed it elsewhere.**

## 8. A useful anti-teleological consequence

Poussin 1913 is especially valuable because it blocks a teleological reading of 004.

If a major Buddhist scholar can formulate the same general methodological tension in 1913 without this being “Lovejoyan history of ideas,” then the operation itself belongs to a wider scholarly problem. Lovejoy’s later history-of-ideas method cannot be inferred merely from the presence of that operation.

What the Lovejoy archive adds is continuity of **his own practice**, supported by:

- named notebook sources;
- publication transformation in 1898;
- explicit self-reuse in 1902;
- explicit logical/chronological distinction in 1907.

That is a much stronger historical argument than resemblance between early and late terminology.
