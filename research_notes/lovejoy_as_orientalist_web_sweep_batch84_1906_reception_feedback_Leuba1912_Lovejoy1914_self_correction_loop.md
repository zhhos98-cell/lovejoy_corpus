# Batch 84 — Reception becomes feedback: Leuba 1912 → Lovejoy 1914 and the self-correction loop around `Primitive Philosophy`

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: extend Batches 81–83 from one-way reception to a documented feedback structure. The central object is Arthur O. Lovejoy's review of James H. Leuba, *A Psychological Study of Religion*, *International Journal of Ethics* 24.2 (Jan. 1914), 216–220.

## Core result

The reception history of Lovejoy's 1906 `Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` is no longer adequately modeled as a one-way line from Lovejoy to later readers.

A direct bibliographic primary object now establishes that **Lovejoy himself re-entered the reception of the 1906 paper**:

`Lovejoy 1906`
→ `Leuba 1912 reads and criticizes Lovejoy`
→ **`Lovejoy 1914 reviews Leuba`**
→ Lovejoy attempts to correct Leuba's understanding of his 1906 position.

The existence, authorship, date and pagination of the 1914 review are direct primary facts: JSTOR lists `A Psychological Study of Religion: Its Origin, Function and Future by James H. Leuba`, review by Arthur O. Lovejoy, *International Journal of Ethics* 24.2 (Jan. 1914), pp.216–220, stable 2376508. The complete volume is also exposed as a public-domain Google Books object.

The **substantive statement that Lovejoy uses the review to correct Leuba's misconception of his position in the 1906 paper is presently secondary-controlled**, through Daniel J. Wilson's annotated Lovejoy bibliography. The JSTOR XML/PDF endpoint is visible but the automated retrieval returned a cache miss, so the exact wording of Lovejoy's correction has not yet been directly collated.

This distinction is crucial. We can now write securely that a feedback relation exists, but we must not yet specify the exact proposition Lovejoy says Leuba misunderstood.

The historiographic consequence is nevertheless substantial:

> **`Primitive energetics` was not merely transmitted and transformed downstream. Lovejoy monitored at least one important transformation and publicly intervened in it.**

This turns the reception history from a citation tree into a **feedback system**.

## 2. Wilson identifies the review as a correction of Leuba's reading of Lovejoy 1906

Daniel J. Wilson's annotated bibliography summarizes the 1914 review as follows at the proposition level:

- Lovejoy calls Leuba's work a `valuable book`;
- he concentrates especially on the third and fourth parts, concerning empirical apologetics and a religion of the future;
- **he attempts to correct a misconception by Leuba of Lovejoy's own position in `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`;**
- he also criticizes the book's organization and style.

Wilson is a strong bibliographic locator and synopsis, but this is not a substitute for the review text itself.

## 3. What Leuba 1912 had actually done to Lovejoy

Batch 83 directly controlled the relevant Leuba passages.

Leuba uses Lovejoy 1906 in several separable ways:

1. he cites the paper as a criticism of Marett deserving attention;
2. he reproduces Lovejoy's proposition that the crucial force is conceived as working according to `quite regular and intelligible laws` and is therefore better described as `Primitive Energetics`;
3. he challenges that stronger characterization because Wakonda / Manitou / Mana remain unpredictable and ritual means do not disclose a definite, stable quantitative relation to effects;
4. he nevertheless recommends Lovejoy's paper as a `good summary of the evidence`;
5. he rejects `manitouism` as nomenclature in favor of `dynamism`;
6. he uses the personal/impersonal cleavage to distinguish religion from magic more sharply than Lovejoy's 1906 paper does.

Leuba therefore did not perform one simple `misreading`. He redistributed Lovejoy's article across at least four propositions:

- existence of impersonal power;
- causal regularity / quasi-mechanical operation;
- classification as religion versus magic;
- terminology (`manitouism` / `dynamism`).

Any one of these could be the target of Lovejoy's 1914 correction. Until the review text is retrieved, **we cannot choose among them**.

## 4. Why the feedback loop matters methodologically

Batches 79–83 showed repeated **function migration**:

- sources move to new evidentiary jobs inside Lovejoy;
- claims move from notebook to print only after acquiring new warrants;
- readers then reuse Lovejoy's category for new arguments;
- Leuba changes evidentiary channel again when moving from ethnographic existence to psychological genesis.

The 1914 review adds a new operation:

> **the producer of the original evidentiary object can return and contest the downstream reassignment of that object.**

This means a reception graph should not treat published concepts as packets that leave the author and then circulate independently. At least here, circulation produces a response from the original author.

The relevant structure is:

`claim production`
→ `reader decomposition`
→ `warrant challenge / function reassignment`
→ **`authorial correction`**
→ further reception.

The missing 1914 text now matters disproportionately because it may tell us **which component Lovejoy considered essential enough to defend**.

## 5. The most important unresolved question is now exact, not generic

Before this batch the question was:

> Did Lovejoy's proof architecture disappear in reception?

After Leuba and the 1914 review, the sharper question is:

> **When Lovejoy saw Leuba disaggregate the 1906 paper, which disaggregation did Lovejoy himself regard as a misconception?**

Possible candidates, not conclusions:

- `regular/intelligible laws` and the strength of mechanical causality;
- whether impersonal efficacy should be classified as religion or magic;
- whether `manitouism` is merely a label for a power concept or a proposed minimum definition of religion;
- the relation between `primitive energetics` and Marett's pre-animism.

These are **retrieval hypotheses only**. None should be written as Lovejoy's actual correction without direct collation of pp.216–220.

---

## 6. Reception is now bidirectional

The minimum secure chronology is:

### 1906 — Lovejoy

Publishes the `vera causa` / impersonal-power argument in *The Monist*.

### 1912 — Leuba

Directly cites Lovejoy; retains non-personal power and evidence survey, contests mechanical regularity, changes the terminology, and later changes warrant channel when addressing psychological origin.

### 1914 — Lovejoy

Publishes a review of Leuba's book. Wilson's annotated bibliography identifies part of the review as an attempt to correct Leuba's misconception of the 1906 position.

This is not merely `influence`.

It is a **documented author–reader–author feedback sequence**.

## 7. Historiographic payoff

A safe article sentence is already possible:

> **The early reception of `primitive energetics` was not wholly external to Lovejoy. Leuba's 1912 book explicitly decomposed the 1906 argument, accepting the evidence for impersonal power while contesting its mechanical regularity and preferring a different classificatory vocabulary. Two years later Lovejoy reviewed Leuba's book; a contemporary bibliographic primary fixes the review, while Wilson's annotated bibliography identifies it as in part an attempt to correct Leuba's understanding of Lovejoy's 1906 position. The exact correction still requires direct collation, but the reception relation had already become a feedback loop.**

A stronger formulation should wait for pp.216–220.
