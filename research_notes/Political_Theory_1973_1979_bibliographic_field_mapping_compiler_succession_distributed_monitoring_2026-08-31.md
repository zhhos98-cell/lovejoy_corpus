# *Political Theory* 1973–1979: bibliographic field mapping, compiler succession, and distributed monitoring

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT NAMED-COMPILER CONTROL / DIRECT READER-SUPPLEMENT MECHANISM / DIRECT 1977 RESEARCH-ASSISTANT SUCCESSION / DIRECT 1979 SELECTION-RULE CONTROL / FIELD-MAPPING INFRASTRUCTURE LOCKED**

## Core result

The early *Political Theory* bibliographies were not merely anonymous back matter or a passive index of whatever the journal happened to publish. Direct evidence now supports a more concrete institutional model:

`NAMED COMPILER LABOR`
+
`CORE JOURNAL SURVEILLANCE`
+
`READER-SUPPLIED SUPPLEMENTS`
+
`CROSS-DISCIPLINARY SELECTION`
+
`EDITORIAL RECLASSIFICATION`
=
`FIELD-MAPPING INFRASTRUCTURE`.

The visible labor history begins with **Linda Marasco**, named by JSTOR as compiler of the inaugural February 1973 `Political Theory Articles` bibliography. By 1977 Marasco is still producing the journal's bibliographic infrastructure, now jointly with **Cary J. Nederman**, whom SAGE identifies as a Columbia philosophy student and **research assistant for Political Theory**. By 1979 Nederman independently publishes `A Bibliography of Articles in Political Theory, 1974–1978` and explicitly states both the source universe and the criterion of editorial selection.

This yields a new institutional distinction:

`BIBLIOGRAPHY`
!= `NEUTRAL INDEX`.

It is an analytic and communications apparatus that constructs a visible `political theory` field out of materials dispersed across multiple disciplines and journals.

At the same time:

`BIBLIOGRAPHIC INCLUSION`
!= `ENDORSEMENT`
!= `ARTICLE PUBLICATION`
!= `SOURCE OF A LATER PT ARTICLE`.

JSTOR's direct issue record for *Political Theory* 1.1 identifies:

`Bibliography: Political Theory Articles`, pp.112–124

Author/compiler:

**Linda Marasco**.

This corrects a possible reading of the bibliography as anonymous editorial machinery.

At least at launch, bibliographic surveillance is attached to identifiable scholarly labor.

Direct JSTOR record:

https://www.jstor.org/stable/i209858

Stable bibliography item:

https://www.jstor.org/stable/191086

Evidence ceiling:

- Marasco is directly named for the inaugural bibliography;
- do not automatically assign every 1973–74 unsigned SAGE bibliography record to her without item-level authorship control.

`ONE NAMED COMPILER INSTANCE`
!= `AUTHORSHIP OF EVERY ADJACENT BIBLIOGRAPHY`.

## II. May / November 1973: the bibliography describes a permanent surveillance list plus distributed supplementation

A direct machine-readable vLex rendering of the May 1973 `Political Theory Articles` bibliography states that it lists articles appearing in enumerated journals and explicitly invites readers to send information about political-theory articles published in reviews **not on the journal's permanent list**.

The visible permanent list includes journals from several institutional and disciplinary locations, among them:

- *American Political Science Review*;
- *British Journal of Political Science*;
- *History and Theory*;
- *Journal of the History of Ideas*;
- *Journal of Politics*;
- *Mind*;
- *La Pensée*;
- *Il Pensiero Politico*;
- *Philosophical Quarterly*;
- *Philosophy*;
- *Philosophy & Public Affairs*;
- *Political Studies*;
- *Politics & Society*;
- *Polity*;
- *Praxis*;
- *Review of Politics*;
- *Western Political Quarterly*.

The November 1973 bibliography repeats the same basic architecture: a permanent list of monitored journals plus a reader invitation for relevant items outside that list.

Direct vLex control:

May 1973 DOI 10.1177/009059177300100212.

November 1973 DOI 10.1177/009059177300100412.

Thus the information mechanism is not simply:

`EDITORS READ FIXED JOURNALS`.

It is:

`CORE MONITORING LIST`
+
`DISTRIBUTED READER REPORTING`.

This is direct evidence for a partially **crowd-supplied / network-extended surveillance architecture** in 1973.

Do not infer actual contribution volume from the invitation alone. The source proves an open supplementation channel, not how often readers used it.

`INVITATION TO SUPPLY ITEMS`
!= `MEASURED READER CONTRIBUTION RATE`.

## III. February 1974: the monitored field is described as more than 100 major journals

The February 1974 `Political Theory Articles` abstract states that the bibliography lists political-theory articles appearing in **over 100 major journals** and again invites readers to submit relevant material from reviews not normally included.

Direct SAGE record:

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/009059177400200109

This confirms continued use of the distributed supplementation mechanism after the first volume.

However, do not mechanically interpret:

`1973 ENUMERATED PERMANENT LIST`
vs
`1974 OVER 100 MAJOR JOURNALS`

as proof that coverage expanded from N to >100 because:

- the 1973 text distinguishes the permanent core list from supplementary sources;
- the 1974 abstract may describe the full monitored universe rather than only the permanent core;
- no controlled version ledger yet counts source journals issue by issue.

Safe claim:

> **By 1974 the journal publicly characterized its article-surveillance field as extending across more than 100 major journals, with reader supplementation still built into the mechanism.**

## IV. The surveillance field is visibly broader than the journal's own research-object output

The companion 1973–77 regional-object census establishes that early PT's substantive article-title layer remained strongly concentrated in Euro-American historical and contemporary objects.

Yet the May 1974 bibliography directly monitors materials including:

- multiple *Gandhi Marg* articles on nonviolence, peace, movement and ends/means;
- James M. Glass, `The Yogin and the Utopian: Nirvana and the Discovery of Being`;
- an article connecting Voegelin, the Old Testament and the Ancient Near East.

Therefore:

`BIBLIOGRAPHIC FIELD MAP`
was in at least some respects geographically / conceptually broader than
`PT OWN SUBSTANTIVE ARTICLE-TITLE DISTRIBUTION`.

This is not evidence that those materials were central, equally weighted, or absorbed into PT's own article programme.

It is evidence that **information intake and original-publication output occupied different field boundaries**.

Core rule:

`INFORMATION HORIZON`
!= `PUBLICATION-OUTPUT HORIZON`.

This is analogous to but distinct from the APSR distinction between accepted pipeline and printed output.

## VI. 1977: bibliographic labor becomes visibly shared with a journal research assistant

SAGE directly identifies the May 1977 bibliography:

`Recent Books in Political Theory: 1974–1976`

by:

- Linda Marasco;
- Cary J. Nederman.

The same SAGE author page gives a useful institutional snapshot:

- Marasco had recently received her PhD from **Columbia University** and was teaching at Fordham;
- Nederman was studying philosophy at **Columbia University** and was a **research assistant for Political Theory**.

Direct SAGE record:

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/009059177700500211

This directly connects bibliography production to the journal's junior scholarly labor infrastructure.

The sequence is now:

`NAMED COLUMBIA-BASED COMPILER`
-> `COMPILER + PT RESEARCH ASSISTANT CO-PRODUCTION`.

This is compatible with a succession / training / handoff model, but does not by itself prove mentorship or formal transfer of duties.

Preserve:

`CO-AUTHORSHIP + RESEARCH-ASSISTANT STATUS`
!= `MENTORSHIP RELATION PROVEN`.

## VII. 1979: Nederman makes the selection mechanism explicit

Cary J. Nederman, `A Bibliography of Articles in Political Theory, 1974–1978`, *Political Theory* 7.4 (Nov. 1979), 563–580.

Direct machine-readable vLex text gives unusually strong methodological control.

Nederman states that:

- the bibliography supplements the journal's earlier 1970–73 bibliography;
- it covers January 1974 through December 1978;
- it was compiled from the contents of **more than fifty English-language journals**;
- source disciplines included **political science, philosophy, history, classics, sociology and anthropology**;
- articles were selected according to the editor's estimation of their relevance to current debates among political theorists;
- omissions/errors were expected in an enterprise of this scale;
- an editor's note thanks **Kurt Fliegel, Linda Bandy and James Wray Goulding** for suggestions in compilation.

Direct vLex:

https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/bibliography-of-articles-in-874229666

Direct SAGE metadata:

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/009059177900700413

This is decisive for the status of the bibliography.

It is not a neutral database dump.

The operation is:

`MULTIDISCIPLINARY SOURCE CORPUS`
-> `EDITORIAL RELEVANCE JUDGMENT`
-> `POLITICAL-THEORY BIBLIOGRAPHIC FIELD`.

Therefore:

> **The bibliography actively constructs political theory as a cross-disciplinary problem-field by selecting and reclassifying material produced elsewhere.**

## VIII. 1979 categories show analytic reclassification, not mere journal listing

The directly visible 1979 bibliography organizes selected material under historical/intellectual categories including:

- `Ancient and Medieval`;
- `Modern-German`;
- `Modern-British and American`.

Within those headings, material is drawn from journals such as:

- *Journal of the History of Ideas*;
- *Journal of the History of Philosophy*;
- *American Political Science Review*;
- *Political Studies*;
- *Canadian Journal of Political Science*;
- *Ethics*;
- *Classical Journal*;
- *Greek, Roman and Byzantine Studies*;
- *Social Theory and Practice*;
- *Philosophical Forum*;
- *Telos*;
- *Diogenes*;
- *Political Theory* itself.

Thus journal provenance is not the final classification principle.

A JHI article, APSR article, classics article and philosophy article can be removed from their original disciplinary containers and reaggregated under a PT historical-theoretical category.

This is exactly the kind of operation the UNIT×RELATION project needs to distinguish:

`SOURCE VENUE`
!= `RECLASSIFIED FIELD ROLE`.

The bibliography is a `U11 ANALYTIC CONSTRUCT / CLASSIFICATORY APPARATUS` built partly from `U5 SOURCE / RESEARCH-INFRASTRUCTURE` material.

Do not claim that these headings exhaust the full 1979 bibliography based on the currently machine-readable segment; only the directly visible categories are locked here.

## IX. Comparison with the 1973 journal programme

The February 1973 `Editorial Statement` promised bibliographic awareness as part of the new journal's role architecture.

The bibliography evidence shows what that promise meant operationally:

1. a named compiler;
2. a permanent core monitoring list;
3. reader-supplied out-of-list notices;
4. surveillance of a much larger multi-journal field;
5. continued bibliographic labor across years;
6. research-assistant participation;
7. later explicit relevance judgments and historical categorization.

Therefore the journal's `international / pluralist` infrastructure was not exhausted by the articles it accepted.

It also included an **information layer that made dispersed arguments mutually visible**.

This sharpens the earlier carrier model:

`ARTICLE CAPACITY`
+
`CONTROVERSY CAPACITY`
+
`REVIEW CAPACITY`
+
`BIBLIOGRAPHIC FIELD-MAPPING CAPACITY`.

Specialist-carrier formation therefore involved the construction of a **disciplinary observatory**, not merely a new outlet for manuscripts.

## X. Relation to CSPT and embedded differentiation

The bibliography's permanent/source list includes APSR, JHI, H&T, philosophy journals, political-science journals and European journals.

That architecture is consistent with the repo's `embedded differentiation` model:

*Political Theory* builds its own specialist communications infrastructure while continuously monitoring and importing material from surrounding disciplines and venues.

It does not define political theory by institutional secession from them.

Compact model:

`SPECIALIST CARRIER`
-> `MONITORS PARENT / NEIGHBORING VENUES`
-> `SELECTS / RECLASSIFIES THEIR OUTPUT`
-> `MAKES A POLITICAL-THEORY FIELD VISIBLE`.

This is a different relation from citation influence or author migration.

`BIBLIOGRAPHIC FIELD MAPPING`
!= `INTELLECTUAL INFLUENCE`.

## XI. Relation to global-history / civilizational-object problem

The bibliography mechanism helps explain an otherwise puzzling finding from the 1973–77 regional-object census.

Early PT can simultaneously have:

- a narrow substantive article-title distribution for internally addressed non-Western intellectual traditions;
- a wider bibliographic information horizon that notices Gandhi-related, Buddhist/Nirvana and other materials outside the journal.

Thus globality itself must be role-typed.

At least three layers can differ:

`GLOBAL / INTERNATIONAL INFORMATION SURVEILLANCE`
!= `GLOBAL ORIGINAL-ARTICLE JURISDICTION`
!= `GLOBAL INTERNAL-TRADITION HISTORIOGRAPHY`.

The first may precede or exceed the second without causing it.

Do not infer that bibliographic visibility of Gandhi-related work caused Haksar's later Rawls/Gandhi article.

`FIELD AVAILABILITY`
!= `TRANSMISSION TO SPECIFIC AUTHOR`.

---

## XII. Relation to Lovejoy / classification history

There is a structural comparison available here, but no genealogy should be claimed.

Lovejoy's notebooks and later historical practice repeatedly separate source collection from analytic regrouping. The PT bibliography similarly takes heterogeneous source material and constructs a field through selector/classificatory operations.

Safe structural comparison:

`HETEROGENEOUS SOURCE INFRASTRUCTURE`
-> `ANALYTIC REAGGREGATION`.

Hold:

- Lovejoy -> PT influence;
- JHI -> PT bibliography genealogy;
- identical epistemic purpose across the cases.

The value is comparative method history, not transmission.
