# Lovejoy as Orientalist / comparative religion — Batch 149

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: use the newly recovered full primary `Endokannibalismus` (Batch 147) to re-read Mauss's contemporary criticism and Lovejoy 005's final anthropophagy block. The question is no longer whether these writers recognize multiple motives; all three do. The live question is **which explanatory level is allowed to govern the classification of a surviving practice**.

## Core result

The Steinmetz → Mauss → Lovejoy comparison becomes considerably sharper once Steinmetz's own pp.36–60 are read rather than reconstructed from later summaries.

Steinmetz and Lovejoy are not separated by a simple contrast between `one cause` and `many causes`. Steinmetz explicitly rejects `Simplomanie`, distinguishes motives, tests negative conditions, and admits later animistic, magical, ceremonial and communion-like refunctionalizations. His distinctive commitment appears one level higher: he retains a **universal historical hierarchy of origins**. The reconstructed first cause — ordinary nutritional corpse-eating made possible by omnivory, need and the absence of later inhibitions — remains explanatory prior; later religious meanings are classified as secondary additions to an older custom.

Mauss changes the level of explanation. When he renames `endocannibalisme` as `l'anthropophagie rituelle des parents`, he does not primarily ask what first caused humans to eat corpses. He asks what **social relation and ritual mechanism** constitute the surviving practice: kinship solidarity, transmission of the dead person's virtues, the common meal, and communion. His citation to Steinmetz p.52 is therefore an act of reclassification, not merely a paraphrase of Steinmetz's own conclusion.

Lovejoy 005 changes the level again. Kin relation, ceremoniality, ingestion of a body part and transfer of qualities are each treated as insufficient by themselves to force the case into `sacramental communion`. The notebook redistributes anthropophagic cases among famine, revenge, affection/kin relation, propitiation, sympathetic/transferable efficacy and sacramental union. The operative question is increasingly:

> **what causal bearer/mechanism is doing the explanatory work in this case?**

The sequence is therefore best stated as:

> **origin hierarchy → relational/ritual reclassification → mechanism-level decomposition.**

This formulation does not require a direct Mauss → Lovejoy reading claim. It reconstructs a live Paris controversy whose components are independently connected to Marillier and whose exact source title is physically present in 005.

No new GLA/GAL/COV identifier is created in this batch.

## 1. Steinmetz: anti-`Simplomanie` does not eliminate origin hierarchy

Batch 147 recovered the original 1896 text and corrected an easy historiographical mistake.

Steinmetz explicitly insists that motives must be distinguished. He attacks the tendency to select a plausible motive and make it sufficient for the whole phenomenon, calling that tendency `Simplomanie`. His §§16–22 do not amount to a single hunger claim; they test a cluster of enabling/negative conditions — omnivory and desire for meat, disgust, fear of the dead, sympathy with the corpse, solidarity and hunger.

Printed p.46 is particularly important because it rejects hunger as a complete explanation of continuing endocannibalism. Peoples practicing the custom are not simply those in the most desperate economic conditions.

Yet the conclusion retains a strong historical ranking. For Steinmetz:

1. ordinary corpse consumption is reconstructed as primitive/primary;
2. animistic explanations, magical transfer, ceremonial reduction and communion can preserve, reinterpret or ennoble the custom later;
3. those later motives do not become the origin of the institution merely because they explain some surviving cases.

Thus Steinmetz combines **local explanatory pluralism** with **global genealogical priority**.

That combination, not an absence of nuance, is the correct point of comparison.

## 2. Steinmetz p.52 §26: vital-force transfer is explicitly a later explanatory layer

Section 26 asks whether animism could have caused endocannibalism.

Steinmetz's answer is negative at the origin level but permissive at the later-function level. The idea that eating the dead appropriates the person's soul or vital force can:

- furnish a new motive;
- preserve an existing practice;
- make an older custom intelligible in a changed conceptual environment;
- generate reduced/ceremonial forms.

This is crucial because it means `transfer of vital force` is already present in the primary source, but its evidentiary status is historical and subordinate:

> **real later motive, not original cause.**

The later historiography should therefore not credit Mauss or Lovejoy with first noticing transfer. Their difference lies in what they allow that mechanism to do classificatorily.

## 3. Mauss: p.52 is redeployed at a different explanatory level

Mauss's contemporary critical essay explicitly cites Steinmetz p.52 §26 when discussing what he calls more precisely `l'anthropophagie rituelle des parents`.

The change of name is analytical. `Endocannibalism` classifies the act by the inside/outside relation of eater and victim. Mauss instead specifies:

- the victim is a relative;
- the act is ritual/funerary;
- the social unit is the family/clan;
- the dead person's `vertus` pass into survivors;
- the common meal distributes or realizes a religious solidarity.

Mauss then places these practices under communion/family religious unity and treats human funerary sacrifice as later than, or distinct from, this communion complex.

The important point after Batch 147 is that Mauss's p.52 citation does **not** mean Steinmetz himself had classified the practice this way. Steinmetz had made transfer-of-vital-force a secondary animistic motive within an origin history. Mauss elevates the relational/ritual mechanism into the relevant explanatory class for the surviving practice.

That is an **explanatory-level shift**.

## 4. Mauss's criticism of Steinmetz elsewhere confirms that the issue is the rubric before counting

Mauss acknowledges the seriousness of Steinmetz's comparative method and even cites `Endokannibalismus` §§16–22 as an example of a method that presents contrary cases and variation.

His criticism is instead that statistical rubrics can remain too broad. In the funeral field he asks why Steinmetz does not separate more rigorously:

- funeral customs;
- cults of the dead proper;
- ordinary dead from deified ancestors;
- sacrifice from a terminal mourning rite;
- magical vengeance/prophylaxis from other corpse practices.

Thus one can possess counterexamples, motive tables and reliability judgments and still compare unlike causal relations under the same heading.

This is the key transition toward the operation later visible in 005:

> **classification controls what comparison can prove.**

## 5. Lovejoy 005 pp.106–119: ritual form repeatedly loses diagnostic force

The corrected final transcription makes the notebook's operation unusually explicit.

### pp.106–107 — ceremoniality does not create a religious/sacramental class

Lovejoy starts by asking whether human-flesh eating is intrinsically religious. Famine, revenge, hatred, affection and acquisition of qualities are all live motives. Public performance, repetition, solemnity or fixed ceremonial rules can regulate a practice without proving a sacramental origin.

Thus `ritualized` is not itself a sufficient causal category.

### p.108 — transfer of qualities is separated from sacrifice/communion

Human flesh or selected organs can be credited with efficacy; acquisition of courage, intelligence or strength may fit sympathetic/transferable efficacy rather than offering to a deity or mystical union.

The exact causal mechanism matters more than the mere fact of ingestion.

### pp.109–111 — propitiation is not communion

A victim's dangerous spirit, a hostile water power, and a friendly/protective deity generate different sacrificial relations. A human victim cast into dangerous water can be propitiatory without being a communion meal.

Again the visible sacrificial act is decomposed by relational/causal structure.

### p.117 — a synthetic distributional judgment

Lovejoy's working conclusion is that alimentary sacrifice and expiatory/propitiatory sacrifice are common, while sacramental union is comparatively exceptional. The point is not that communion never occurs; it is that it cannot function as the default genus for heterogeneous rites.

### p.119 — selected body parts

The final sustained anthropophagy discussion gives the cleanest mechanism allocation:

- head/brain → intelligence;
- heart → courage/breath/life;
- sexual organs → sexual power.

Lovejoy assigns this primarily to sympathetic/transferable efficacy, while keeping open the possibility that one-part eating can historically derive from eating the whole body. Historical derivation and present explanatory mechanism are therefore analytically separable.

## 6. The exact three-stage shift

| Problem | Steinmetz 1896 | Mauss 1896–97 | Lovejoy 005 |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is the first question? | Under what primitive conditions could corpse-eating originate? | What social/ritual relation constitutes the surviving kin practice? | What mechanism/bearer makes this particular case work? |
| Multiple motives? | Yes; explicitly, anti-`Simplomanie` | Yes; criticizes broad rubrics | Yes; extensive decomposition |
| Transfer of qualities/vital force | real but later animistic motive preserving/reinterpreting older custom | central mechanism inside ritual kin communion | one mechanism among several; often sympathetic/transferable efficacy rather than communion |
| Kin relation | compatible with later ritual development | becomes classificatorily central (`parents`, family solidarity) | affection/kin relation does not by itself settle communion class |
| Ceremonial form | can be secondary/reduced later form | helps specify ritual relation | ceremoniality alone is non-diagnostic |
| Communion | later religious interpretation/high cultural stage | strong explanatory class for ritual kin eating | possible but comparatively exceptional; must be demonstrated case-by-case |
| Genealogy vs function | origin hierarchy remains architectonic | current social/ritual relation reorganizes the class | historical derivation and causal function repeatedly separated |

This matrix is the strongest current way to avoid both caricature and false priority.

## 7. Where Marillier now matters

Marillier sits in the middle of this controversy through three independently secure routes:

1. he reviewed Steinmetz's exact `Endokannibalismus` in RHR 34 (1896), now correctly bounded at **pp.113–115**;
2. Mauss's critical essay explicitly cites Marillier's 1896 Melanesian taboo dossier as an assembled body of texts;
3. Lovejoy is later officially documented as actively participating in Marillier's 1898–99 conferences on human sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy, and 005 physically records Steinmetz's title.

The missing three-page Marillier review is therefore not a generic `influence` source. It can decide where Marillier himself stood in the explanatory-level shift:

- did he retain Steinmetz's nutritional-origin hierarchy?
- did he already privilege current ritual/social mechanism?
- did he separate transfer, communion, magic and funerary relation?
- did he reject the universal inference while accepting some local causal claims?

Batch 148 corrects all older `pp.113–118` references: the review is **pp.113–115**.

## 8. Consequence for the 005 / `Primitive Energetics` argument

The new primary Steinmetz control helps explain why the later Lovejoy abstraction is not merely another universal origin theory of the older evolutionist kind.

005 repeatedly extracts a cross-cutting causal relation — direct/intrinsic/transferable efficacy — from cases that belong morphologically or historically to different rites. A mechanism can recur across blood, body parts, charms, foundation rites, agriculture and anthropophagy without implying that all those institutions share one historical origin.

This is exactly where the distinction between **genealogy** and **function** matters:

> recurring causal mechanism ≠ single institutional origin.

The later 1906 category can therefore be studied as an abstraction across differently derived practices rather than a claim that those practices all descend from one primordial rite.

This remains a developmental interpretation and should not be projected back as explicit 1898–99 terminology.

## 9. Strongest paper formulation after Batch 149

> **Steinmetz's _Endokannibalismus_ was already methodologically plural: he attacked single-motive explanation, tested contrary conditions, and allowed animistic and magical motives to refunctionalize an older practice. What remained architectonic was his hierarchy of origins, in which ordinary nutritional corpse-eating preceded the later religious meanings attached to it. Mauss shifted the explanatory level by redescribing endocannibalism as the ritual anthropophagy of relatives and making kin solidarity, communion and the transfer of the dead person's virtues central to the surviving form. Lovejoy's 005 notebook decomposed the field again. Kinship, ceremoniality and ingestion ceased to be diagnostic on their own; transfer of qualities became one causal mechanism beside hunger, revenge, propitiation and sacramental union. The Paris story is therefore not a progression from crude monism to pluralism, but a tightening of the relation between genealogy, classificatory rubric and causal mechanism.**
