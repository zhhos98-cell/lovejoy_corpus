## 1. 1941 is not a silent year: Lovejoy's alphabetical interval is explicitly missing

The 1941 run is unusually clear. RU7091 shows:

`B228/F6 — Correspondence Has - Haz, 1941. Correspondence He - Sg for 1941 is missing.`

The range `He-Sg` includes the surname Lovejoy.

This becomes `GAP0003`.

The correct statement is not `Lovejoy's 1941 correspondence is missing`. The collection-level evidence does not establish that any Lovejoy item existed that year. The secure statement is that the annual filing segment in which such a letter would ordinarily appear is missing, making a 1941 presence/absence test impossible from that run.

The finding aid also contains `B230/F1 — Note on missing 1941 folders`, which should be requested together with any provenance/transfer documentation. That note may explain scope or history of the loss and is therefore a higher-value next step than generic name searching.

---

## 2. 1948 and 1949 form a second preservation break

For 1948 the surviving run resumes at:

`B293/F1 — Correspondence Sm, 1948. Correspondence A - Sm for 1948 is missing.`

Lovejoy falls inside A-Sm, creating `GAP0004`.

The next folder sequence ends with:

`B293/F16 — Correspondence X - Z, 1948. Most general correspondence files for 1949 are missing.`

This creates `GAP0005`.

The 1949 statement is broader than the 1941 and 1948 alphabetical gaps. It does not expose a surviving L-position container from which a negative result can be produced. The project therefore does not synthesize a hypothetical 1949 L box/folder by extrapolating from adjacent years.

This distinction is important:

- 1941: named missing alphabetical interval covering Lovejoy;
- 1948: named missing alphabetical interval covering Lovejoy;
- 1949: broad loss of most general correspondence, with no safe annual L locator recoverable from the public guide.

All three are preservation evidence, not Lovejoy-document evidence.

## 3. The surviving 1942–47 run is exact and requestable

Between the 1941 and 1948 gaps, the annual filing scheme supplies six finite tests:

`1942 B238/F1 -> 1943 B250/F2 -> 1944 B260/F5 -> 1945 B270/F4 -> 1946 B280/F9 -> 1947 B289/F7`.

These folders vary in how the L range is subdivided (`Ll-L`, `Li-L`, `Lin-L`, or all `L`), but Lovejoy falls inside each literal range.

This sequence should be requested as one tranche together with the already-known 1940 positive folder. The purpose is not simply to find more Lovejoy names. If items exist, each should be captured for:

- literal date;
- sender and recipient;
- signer/addressee;
- original/carbon/draft/photostat or other material form where determinable;
- annotations and routing marks;
- enclosures;
- folder sequence;
- whether the item belongs to a stable bilateral exchange or a broader Science Service editorial workflow.

The 1940 folder provides a positive internal control for what a verified Lovejoy presence looks like in this filing regime.

## 4. 1950–62 is a nearly continuous finite test series

After the 1948–49 preservation break, every year from 1950 through 1962 has a surviving exact L-position container in the public guide.

The map is encoded in `lovejoy_science_service_post1940_opportunity_map_batch108.csv` and operationalized in two request tranches:

### Tranche C: 1950–56

`B297/F1; B302/F9; B308/F2; B311/F11; B316/F7; B322/F1; B327/F7`.

### Tranche D: 1957–62

`B333/F5; B339/F6; B344/F9; B349/F7; B353/F8; B358/F2`.

No current public description in these folders explicitly names Lovejoy. That silence is intentionally not coded as a negative result.

A genuine negative result requires either:

1. complete physical inspection of the folder, or
2. a faithful item-level digital surrogate showing the complete folder contents.

Only after such inspection could a row move from `exact_alphabetic_candidate_container` to `inspected_no_lovejoy_item`.

The edited container list clearly demonstrates that many annual correspondence folders include more people than the few correspondents named in the description. Therefore repeated web/name searching is now lower-yield than direct inspection of the exact candidate containers.

This is recorded as `COV0033` rather than as a negative component census.

## 6. The archive model now distinguishes five annual states

Across the Science Service work from Batches105–108, annual evidence can now be classified as:

1. `explicit_name_presence` — e.g. 1927–29, 1940;
2. `exact_alphabetic_candidate_container` — a surviving folder in which Lovejoy would alphabetically fall, but not a positive hit;
3. `repository_declared_missing_expected_range` — e.g. 1941 and 1948;
4. `repository_declared_general_correspondence_gap` — e.g. 1949;
5. `primary_folder_inspected` — not yet reached in this Science Service run.

This is more useful than a binary hit/no-hit model because it separates three very different reasons why a Lovejoy item is not currently visible: selective archival description, uninspected surviving material, and actual documentary loss.

## 7. Retrieval order

`lovejoy_science_service_request_tranches_batch108.csv` now defines the practical order:

1. **SS108A** — 1940 positive control + 1942–47 candidate run; highest priority;
2. **SS108B** — 1941/1948/1949 preservation-history work rather than ordinary folder requests;
3. **SS108C** — 1950–56 surviving annual L run;
4. **SS108D** — 1957–62 surviving annual L run.

This is intentionally more economical than requesting every L folder in one undifferentiated batch. The first tranche can establish whether Lovejoy continued to appear after 1940 and can reveal the material/documentary form of any later exchange before the later years are scanned.

## Evidence rules added

> **A complete annual filing map can turn descriptive silence into finite retrieval opportunities without turning those opportunities into positive presence claims.**

> **Missing ranges interrupt a correspondence chronology epistemically; they do not supply missing documents historically.**
