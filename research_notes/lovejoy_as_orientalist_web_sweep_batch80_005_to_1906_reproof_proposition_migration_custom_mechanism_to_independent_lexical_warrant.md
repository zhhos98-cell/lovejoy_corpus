# Batch 80 — 005 → 1906 is `reproof`, not mere republication: mechanisms migrate only after acquiring a new warrant

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: proposition-level comparison between notebook 005 and Lovejoy's 1906 `Fundamental Concept`, following Batch 79's source-function migration. The unit of comparison is no longer an author or topic but a **claim**: what mechanism is proposed in 005, and what additional evidence is used in 1906 when that mechanism enters publication?

## Core result

The strongest 005→1906 continuity is not that Lovejoy copies notebook conclusions into print. Several recurring mechanisms are **reproved**.

Notebook 005 often reaches a causal interpretation by decomposing ambiguous customs:

`same rite/material` → compare cases → eliminate rival meanings → isolate a plausible mechanism.

The 1906 paper then seeks a second, independent route:

`native vocabulary / grammar / indigenous explanation` → establish that the causal conception exists → return to the rite as an application.

Thus some of the most striking 005 mechanisms migrate into the published theory only after Lovejoy changes their evidentiary basis.

The pattern is especially clear in four cases:

1. **selected organs / transferred qualities**;
2. **blood and reproductive/agricultural fertility**;
3. **fetish / amulet and efficacy not reducible to indwelling spirit**;
4. **quasi-mechanical action distinct from personal/sacramental relation**.

A fifth case — source misclassification — shows the same transformation at the level of witness criticism: 005 uses reporter/contact history to qualify a report; 1906 separates a reporter's observed fact from the reporter's translation and ontology.

The best formula is:

> **005 isolates mechanisms comparatively; 1906 re-establishes selected mechanisms through independent linguistic evidence.**

Or more compactly:

> **mechanism migration is accompanied by warrant migration.**

This is a stronger developmental argument than lexical continuity or shared bibliography because it specifies what changes between manuscript research and publication.

---

## 1. Selected organs: from comparative efficacy inference to an indigenous `manitou` transfer explanation

### 005

In the cannibalism/body-part sequence, especially pp.108 and 119, Lovejoy refuses to treat consumption of selected organs as automatically sacramental communion. The observed selection itself becomes a clue to mechanism:

- head/brain → intelligence;
- heart → courage or life;
- sexual organs → sexual power.

The underlying explanation is sympathetic or transferable efficacy. The material body part carries or concentrates a property that can pass to the eater.

At the notebook stage this is largely a **comparative-mechanism inference**. Lovejoy places several cases together and asks what relation best explains selection.

### 1906

At p.371, the Jones Algonkin packet gives a more direct evidentiary route. Lovejoy reports that eating the heart of a brave enemy is understood as absorbing the enemy's `manitou` into oneself.

This is a major change in warrant:

`selected heart + desired courage/strength`

is no longer only interpreted by the comparative scholar as property transfer.

The native term and explanation make the transfer relation explicit:

`heart consumption` → `absorption of manitou`.

### Migration

`005 comparative body-part pattern`

→ **transferable-quality mechanism inferred**

→ `1906 native lexical/causal explanation`

→ **mechanism independently anchored in indigenous semantic evidence**.

This is one of the cleanest cases of reproof.

## 2. Blood and fertility: from fecundative sacrifice to a general productive-energy mechanism

### 005

The agricultural block, pp.99–105, repeatedly separates blood/life transfer from alimentary or sacramental sacrifice.

Examples include:

- blood placed on seed-corn or ground;
- spring/planting human sacrifice;
- victims/body parts used to secure crop fertility;
- plant-soul sacrifice interpreted as **quasi-mechanical/magical rather than sacramental**.

The central local mechanism is fecundative transfer: life or productive efficacy is moved into the crop.

### 1906

The published paper gives the mechanism a new conceptual carrier.

In the North-American section Lovejoy connects reproductive blood, women's ritual potency, fertility and agriculture to a localized but transferable life-energy. At p.377 he explicitly says agricultural charms and sacrifices can be understood as transfers of energy from beings or things with surplus power to the crop.

The publication therefore does more than repeat the notebook classification.

It says, in effect:

`blood makes crop fertile`

is one application of an independently evidenced category whose linguistic existence has already been established.

### Migration

`005 fecundative custom cluster`

→ **local quasi-mechanical fertility mechanism**

→ `1906 wakonda/manitou linguistic category`

→ **cross-cutting productive-energy explanation**.

The published abstraction is broader, but the notebook supplies the empirical problem it needs to solve.

## 3. Fetish versus charm/amulet: from causal ontology discrimination to lexical evidence of powerful objects

### 005

The blood/fetish/charm block at pp.84–90 makes one of the notebook's sharpest ontological distinctions.

Lovejoy separates:

- **fetish** — efficacy explained by a spirit inhabiting/present in the object;
- **charm / amulet / stone / plant / bodily substance** — efficacy may belong to the thing itself for a given purpose.

This distinction is important because it breaks the shortcut:

`object is powerful`

→ `therefore a spirit/person must inhabit it`.

At this stage the conclusion is extracted from comparative use and classification of objects.

### 1906

The Algonkin `oki` material provides a different evidentiary route. An anomalous object found in an unusually hard-to-kill animal is itself called `oki` and credited with exceptional force/efficacy; Lovejoy connects such a powerful object to the origin of amulet and some fetish practices.

Crucially, the linguistic evidence allows Lovejoy to say that the native power-term can apply **to the efficacious object/property itself**, not only to a personal spirit supposed to inhabit it.

### Migration

`005 spirit-inhabitation vs intrinsic efficacy distinction`

→ `1906 concrete lexical application of oki/manitou to efficacious object`

→ **anti-animist causal ontology gains native-language support**.

The category is still historically problematic, but the proof no longer depends only on analyst-defined object classes.

## 4. `quasi-mechanical`: a notebook classification becomes the published causal category

### 005

At p.105, Lovejoy considers sacrifice of a plant-soul to itself and says the process would be **quasi-mechanical and magical rather than sacramental**: the action is not yet addressed to the plant as a personal deity.

Earlier pages similarly distinguish direct action on natural forces from bargaining/propitiation directed to gods.

Here `quasi-mechanical` is a local classificatory adjective for one mechanism among several.

### 1906

At pp.359–361 the same opposition becomes the conceptual center of the paper:

- the fundamental category is impersonal and quasi-mechanical;
- practical life seeks well-adjusted quasi-mechanical relations with sources of power;
- Frazer's sharp magic/religion division fails because the same transferable efficacy can operate inside practices classified on either side.

### Migration

`005 local mechanism label`

→ `1906 general causal category`.

But Batch 76 adds the decisive epistemic transformation: the general category is not presented as a mere induction from such rites. Lovejoy first seeks independent linguistic evidence that it is a `vera causa`.

Therefore the trajectory is not:

`one notebook phrase → one published doctrine`.

It is:

`local comparative discrimination`

→ `cross-case mechanism abstraction`

→ `independent lexical verification`

→ `published generalization`.

## 5. Reporter/source criticism: from provenance weighting to statement decomposition

### 005

Future-life pages pp.16–19 treat reporter date, missionary intercourse, borrowing and internal mixture as evidentiary variables. A report may preserve useful material while being incapable of proving an indigenous moral genealogy.

The primary unit of criticism is often the **report** and its contact history.

### 1906

The published paper works at a smaller scale.

- M. V. P. can be trusted for external custom but not for translation of the native term or classification as prayer;
- Dorsey's informant/custom relation can be retained while his personification of `wakonda` is rejected;
- old missionary `god/spirit` glosses can be discarded while the lexical occurrence itself remains useful;
- McGee can be `clear and trustworthy` for usage while insufficiently discriminating about semantic core versus connotation.

The unit of criticism is now the **statement extracted from the source**.

### Migration

`005 report-level qualification`

→ `1906 proposition-level qualification`.

This is not necessarily a chronological intellectual advance page by page; 005 is a longitudinal notebook. But across the two objects the published method is visibly more explicit about assigning separate proof functions to separate parts of a witness.

## 6. Why this is not simple confirmation bias

At first glance 1906 might look like Lovejoy simply finding linguistic evidence for mechanisms already favored in 005. The primary text complicates that reading.

The publication preserves adverse evidence:

- Codrington's personal-origin clause;
- Masai prayer to a distinct personality;
- McGee's mixed semantic glosses;
- older missionary usages that do not by themselves establish an abstract force.

Lovejoy then ranks what those statements can prove.

The published category is therefore certainly selective and, as Batch 78 showed, regionally asymmetric. But it is not built by silently discarding every contrary witness.

The more exact critique is:

> **Lovejoy reweights adverse evidence rather than eliminating it, then permits the richer North-American packet to carry more interpretive weight than the thinner regional packets.**

This is why `reproof` is more useful than `confirmation` as the descriptive label.

## 7. The published source apparatus changes the direction of inference

This is the deepest change.

### Notebook direction

`custom / rite / material`

→ infer likely mechanism.

Example:

`heart eaten`

→ maybe property transfer rather than communion.

### Published direction

`native power-term / grammar / semantic explanation`

→ establish mechanism as independently real

→ return to the custom as an application.

Example:

`manitou = impersonal transferable property`

+

`native explanation that heart-eating absorbs manitou`

→ heart-eating becomes an application of the established mechanism.

Thus 1906 reverses the evidentiary direction at crucial points.

This is exactly what Lovejoy means by refusing to infer the causal conception merely from the customs it explains.

The transition can therefore be described as:

> **from abductive mechanism extraction to cross-evidential mechanism confirmation.**

`Abductive` is our analytical vocabulary, not Lovejoy's.

---

## 8. Relation to 004 and 1902

The proposition-level migration makes the wider continuity more concrete.

### 004

Usage/context can support a semantic result while etymological/genealogical questions remain separately open.

### 005

Comparative morphology can support a mechanism hypothesis while source provenance and historical origin remain separately tested.

### 1902

Philosophical affinity can be acknowledged while historical transmission is denied absent an independent warrant.

### 1906

A causal mechanism can be abstracted from customs, but its existence is then independently tested through language before the customs are used as corroborating applications.

Across all four:

> **the stronger proposition is not allowed simply to inherit the evidence of the weaker proposition.**

That is now the most exact statement of the recurring inference syntax.

## 9. Historiographic payoff

A strong paper paragraph now becomes possible:

> **Notebook 005 and the 1906 Monist article do not merely share topics or examples. They often share mechanisms while changing the evidence by which those mechanisms are warranted. In the notebook, selected body parts are grouped as cases of transferable qualities, agricultural blood as fecundative efficacy, and charms as objects whose power need not derive from an indwelling spirit. In the published article, these claims are given a second basis: William Jones's Algonkin speakers explain enemy-heart consumption as absorbing `manitou`; grammatical and lexical evidence defines that power as an impersonal property; `oki` can name an efficacious object; agricultural rites become applications of an independently established productive energy. The move from notebook to article is therefore not simple extraction. It is reproof: Lovejoy rebuilds the warrant for mechanisms that comparative analysis had first isolated.**

This formulation has two advantages:

1. it avoids claiming 005 is a literal draft of the article;
2. it makes the 1906 linguistic first stage historically intelligible as a response to the inferential vulnerability of custom-based comparison.

## 10. Evidence cautions

1. The 005 notebook is longitudinal and some later pages cannot yet be securely dated before every stage of the 1906 paper's composition. `Migration` here means cross-document transformation, not a day-by-day compositional sequence for every page.
2. The Jones enemy-heart explanation is direct 1906 primary evidence; not every 005 body-part example has yet been source-matched to the same ethnographic group.
3. The fetish/charm comparison is conceptual and causal; do not imply that the exact `oki` case already appears in 005 unless separately localized.
4. The agricultural correspondence is strong at mechanism level but the 1906 paper gathers additional North-American linguistic and menstrual/fertility material not securely present in the same notebook passage.
5. `reproof`, `warrant migration`, and `abductive mechanism extraction` are our analytical labels.
6. Batch 78's asymmetry correction remains essential: independent linguistic proof is not equally rich in every region.

---

## 11. Highest next move

The next step should test whether the same reproof pattern survives at the **published reception** level:

- Did contemporary reviewers/readers notice Lovejoy's independent linguistic warrant, or merely treat the paper as another `mana` theory?
- Did later users (Leuba, King, Marett, Jung, Söderblom, etc.) preserve the `vera causa` / cross-evidential structure or extract only the substantive `primitive energy` category?

This matters because it would distinguish:

`what Lovejoy thought the proof was`

from

`what later history remembered as the theory`.

That gap could help explain why the source-critical architecture disappeared while `mana/manitou/primitive energetics` remained citable.
