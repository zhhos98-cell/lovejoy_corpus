## Decision

**GO. The JHI Blog threshold is comfortably met.**

The present problem is no longer insufficient evidence. It is **selection and compression**.

The JHI Blog currently asks for well-researched short essays on intellectual history broadly conceived, especially the generation, communication and transformation of ideas; posts are typically **600–2,000 words** and may present new research. Its editors explicitly assess pertinence, compelling evidence, depth of research and writing. The current Lovejoy corpus exceeds that evidentiary threshold by a wide margin.

Official pages checked 2026-08-18:

- `https://www.jhiblog.org/write-for-us/`
- `https://www.jhiblog.org/style-guide/`

The correct publication strategy is therefore **not to wait for another archival breakthrough before pitching/drafting**. Harvard scans, RAI MS 99 images, Marillier’s Steinmetz review, Oltramare 1909, etc. remain valuable for the later journal article or for strengthening footnotes, but none is a structural blocker for the blog.

## 2. The two possible JHI Blog essays are both already viable

### Route A — 005 core: the stronger archival/blog story

**Recommended.**

The hook is unusually visual and documentary:

- a Paris stationer’s notebook from 162 rue Saint-Jacques;
- an inserted `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June` slip;
- an official EPHE report explicitly naming Lovejoy among auditors who `took an active part in the work`;
- the two conference blocks map onto the notebook’s two dominant research blocks;
- exact-page source extraction can be shown at manuscript level;
- Lovejoy’s 1906 article later dates the relevant conclusions to 1900 and says they were strongly influenced by Marillier.

This is more immediately legible to a general intellectual-history readership than a technical nidāna/upādisesa argument.

#### Best exact-source example: Kingsley

The cleanest 2,000-word case is Mary Kingsley:

- Lovejoy cites `Travels in West Africa` p.511 exactly;
- Kingsley’s source case explains a `suhman` through an indwelling spirit;
- Lovejoy immediately makes the amulet a contrast class in which there need be `no spiritualistic idea`;
- on the next manuscript page he cites Kingsley p.525 and reopens the causal status of blood;
- Hubert–Mauss use the same book at a different page (p.454) and for a different causal architecture.

This is a nearly ideal JHI Blog microhistory because one can literally show:

> **same book → exact page → classificatory transformation.**

It makes the broad intellectual-history question concrete: an idea/category is not simply received from a source; it is made through selective reading and reclassification.

#### Optional second micro-example: Trumbull

If space permits, use only one short paragraph:

- `Blood Covenant` p.118 and p.129 are explicit notebook anchors;
- intervening examples follow Trumbull’s source sequence;
- Lovejoy retains the cases while redistributing their explanatory function away from automatic covenant/communion toward curative, protective and transferable efficacy.

Formula:

> **same pages, new mechanism.**

Do not give Trumbull equal weight to Kingsley in the blog. It belongs in the later article if word count tightens.

## 3. Why a full 004+005 transatlantic essay should NOT be the first blog post

The full corpus now supports a much larger argument:

`Harvard / American Lectures / Sanskrit-Pāli philology`  
→ `transnational Senart–Jacobi–Oldenberg controversy`  
→ `Paris / EPHE / Marillier sciences religieuses workshop`  
→ `005 comparative-religion reclassification`  
→ `1906 Primitive Energetics`.

That is probably the **journal article**, not the 2,000-word blog post.

Trying to fit both notebooks, Harvard, American Lectures, Senart, RHR, Marillier, Mauss, Kingsley, Trumbull and 1906 into one JHI Blog piece would convert a strong archival discovery into a compressed literature survey. The corpus is now too rich for that structure.

The first blog should therefore have **one documentary center**.

Recommended division of labor:

- **005 = primary object / narrative spine**;
- **004 = 150–250 word chronological control**, only to prevent the false claim that Paris invented Lovejoy’s analytical practice;
- **1906 = short endpoint/coda**, not the organizing text;
- **later article = full transatlantic operator-migration argument**.

## 4. Recommended JHI Blog argument now

### Working title

**A Notebook in Paris: Arthur Lovejoy Before the History of Ideas**

Alternatives:

- `Arthur Lovejoy’s Paris Notebook`
- `How Arthur Lovejoy Read Before the History of Ideas`
- `From Term to Rite: Arthur Lovejoy’s Early Intellectual Workshop` — better for the later article than the first blog.

### One-sentence claim

> **A surviving Paris notebook and the records of Léon Marillier’s EPHE seminar show the young Arthur Lovejoy not simply receiving comparative-religion theories but actively reclassifying their evidence; exact citations to Mary Kingsley reveal how a spirit-inhabited ritual object became a boundary case for distinguishing spirit-mediated from intrinsic efficacy, a classificatory labor later compressed into Lovejoy’s 1906 account of `Primitive Energetics`.**

### Necessary causal correction

Add one short Harvard sentence/paragraph:

> Lovejoy did not arrive in Paris methodologically blank. His immediately preceding Buddhist notebook already separates provenance from semantic function. Paris changed the objects and scale of that evidentiary discipline: terms in texts became rites in ethnographic reports.

This keeps Marillier important without making Paris the origin of `decomposition`.

## 7. Claims the blog should not make

1. `Buddhism caused Lovejoy’s later history-of-ideas method.`
2. `Marillier taught Lovejoy decomposition from scratch.`
3. `005 is simply a transcript of Marillier’s lectures.`
4. `Lovejoy borrowed a fixed Marillier/Mauss source packet.`
5. `Mauss directly influenced Lovejoy.`
6. `Primitive Energetics was unknown to historians before this archive.` Meylan already treats it substantially.
7. `The archive reveals proto-unit-ideas.`
8. `Lovejoy invented decomposition, source criticism, term filiation or mechanism-sensitive analysis.` Contemporary scholars already did all of these in different ways.
9. `RHR 37 was Lovejoy’s route to Rhys Davids or Trumbull.` Co-location/circulation is all that is established.
10. `Every page of 005 dates to Paris 1898–99.` The notebook is longitudinal and was reused later.

### Final readiness judgment

> **JHI Blog: ready now.**

The key editorial move is to stop treating additional evidence as a prerequisite for the blog. The repository already contains more evidence than the format can absorb.
