# Lovejoy as Orientalist / comparative religion — batch 42

Date: 2026-08-18  
Focus: re-read the **argumentative burden** of Senart 1896 against notebook 004 and Lovejoy 1898 after direct recovery of Senart pp. 288, 293, 295. The central correction is historiographical: Senart is not merely the author of a mistaken `upādāna = upādānakkhandha` gloss or a loose claim that the nidāna formula is “meaningless.” He already possesses an explicit programme of **term analysis, filiation, stratification and source-layer reconstruction**. The question therefore becomes much narrower: what is Lovejoy doing that Senart’s own historical-philological method does not already do?

## Core result

The strongest distinction is now one of **evidentiary burden**, not method versus no method.

Senart explicitly proposes studying Buddhist metaphysics through the **analysis and filiation of terms** in order to recover successive stratifications, semantic confusions and scholastic accretions. Lovejoy accepts much of this historical problem-setting: inherited terminology, compositeness, heterogeneous categories, non-homogeneous temporal ordering and borrowing are all compatible with his reconstruction. Where he parts company is in the **inference that filiation/compositeness is allowed to support**.

The compact formulation is:

> **Filiation can explain where a term or formula came from; it does not exhaust what that term or formula does in a later arrangement.**

Or, more historically:

> **Senart asks what the filiations of terms reveal about stratification; Lovejoy asks what system-level intelligibility remains once that stratification is granted.**

This is a stronger and more source-controlled claim than saying that Lovejoy “restored coherence” or “distinguished genealogy from function” in the abstract.

## 1. Senart p. 288: the nidāna formula already has a historical anatomy

Senart does not treat the twelve links as an undifferentiated accident. On p. 288 he gives them an internal historical anatomy.

He identifies links 8–9, `tṛṣṇā` and `upādāna`, as the **core** of the nomenclature because they condense the Four Truths’ account of the origin of suffering. The next three terms could attach naturally to that core. The rest, in his reconstruction, consists principally of:

1. borrowing from Sāṁkhya, especially the derivation of the evil/existence from `avidyā` and `saṃskāras`;
2. detailed repetition of the skandhas, which he thinks are already included collectively in `upādāna`.

Thus Senart’s historical claim is not simply `formula = patchwork`. It is a proposed **layered composition with a recoverable nucleus and later acquisitions**.

This matters for 004 because Lovejoy is working against a rival reconstruction that is already analytical and historical. He cannot be credited with discovering either layering or the need to decompose the formula.

## 2. Senart p. 293: semantic comparison is used to support a genealogy

Senart’s discussion of `upadhi / upādhi / upādi` further shows how his method works. He compares Buddhist and Sāṁkhya usages, invokes Childers and Garbe, and argues that Buddhist `upadhi` cannot be detached from the Sāṁkhya philosophical term because of their close semantic affinity. He also treats the northern `sopadhiśeṣa / nirupadhiśeṣa` forms and southern `sa-upādisesa / anupādisesa` forms as evidence that southern `upādi` corresponds to northern `upadhi`.

The operation is therefore:

`semantic/formal affinity → term filiation → historical stratification/borrowing`.

Senart is not unaware of recombination. In the surrounding argument he can say that a borrowing from Sāṁkhya was used on the basis of an older Buddhist formula and that Buddhist nomenclature fixed the borrowed material into a new frame. But his explanatory emphasis remains historical: the terms are evidence with which to reconstruct layers, borrowings and corruptions.

## 3. Senart p. 295: an explicit methodological manifesto

Page 295 is the decisive source-control passage. Senart turns the preceding case study into “conclusions pratiques.” He warns against attributing to many Buddhist nomenclatures the weight of deliberate philosophical speculation. They may instead reflect views distorted by what he calls the “tyrannie des cadres et des mots”; Buddhist metaphysics can become less the echo of creative thought than a scholasticism imprisoned by an absorbing phraseology.

Most important, Senart then states explicitly that this material should be studied **“par l’analyse et la filiation des termes”**, rather than by generous commentaries too ready to discover conscious and profound speculation in it.

He immediately converts that rule into textual history: even the most authoritative books already combine **several successive stratifications**; the Pāli canon is separated from origins by a meaningful interval; tradition passed through a more fluid period and through unregularized dialectal stages capable of creating or facilitating lexical confusion. He then says that after Kern, Garbe and Jacobi the influence of Sāṁkhya-Yoga on Buddhism can hardly be doubted, while the conditions and character of that influence remain to be determined.

This requires a major historiographical correction to earlier versions of our 004 argument:

- **term decomposition is not Lovejoy-specific**;
- **historical layering is not Lovejoy-specific**;
- **borrowing plus later recombination is not Lovejoy-specific in its broadest form**;
- **the use of terminology as evidence for historical formation is emphatically Senart’s own programme**.

The point at issue is therefore not whether Lovejoy “has genealogy” and Senart does not. Both do.

## 4. 004 p. 49: Lovejoy grants Senart’s historical premises but blocks one inference

Notebook 004 p. 49 is now the principal manuscript hinge.

Lovejoy concedes a remarkable amount:

- the formula is composite;
- its terms are not original to Buddhism but are brought together from existing sources;
- objective and subjective categories are mingled;
- the relations are strange from a Western logical standpoint;
- the sequence is not homogeneous in strict temporal relation.

Yet he then says it is **entirely false** to infer that the formula is therefore unintelligible or that its terms have merely been assembled in a more or less accidental order. The surviving corrected transcription gives the conclusion, with some verbal uncertainty, as: **“There is a discoverable technical system which gives the arrangement.”**

This is the crucial difference in evidentiary burden:

`composite provenance` does **not** entail `absence of present systematic relation`.

The two propositions require different evidence. Senart’s filiation work may establish the first; it cannot by itself establish the second.

This should replace any earlier claim that Lovejoy’s novelty was simply “recognizing coherence.” Oldenberg and others also sought coherent readings. The more precise operation is that Lovejoy **restricts the explanatory reach of genealogical evidence**.

## 5. 1898 p. 127: the published article attacks the semantic premise before the historical conclusion

The published article makes this evidentiary structure unusually visible. Senart’s historical inference about the derivative/composite/practically meaningless character of the formula depends in part on the supposed repetition generated by `upādāna = upādānakkhandha = five skandhas`.

Lovejoy replies that both the premise and the conclusion are erroneous, but his order of proof matters. He first tests the semantic identification against Sutta usage and distinguishes:

- the skandhas as such;
- the skandhas as subject to attachment;
- `upādāna` as the attachment/grasping relation itself.

Only after functional/semantic identity has been tested can alleged repetition carry historical weight.

The rule is therefore not anti-genealogical. It is:

> **before using recurrence as evidence of composition, establish that the recurrent items are semantically/functionally identical in the relevant configuration.**

This is one concrete way in which Lovejoy constrains Senart’s historical inference.

## 6. Hardy supplies a second instance of the same evidentiary discipline

The Hardy p. 394 discovery from batch 35 now belongs naturally in this argument.

Hardy supplies the two-factor architecture of renewed existence: `upādāna` produces renewal while karma controls its manner. 004 turns this into a `fact` versus `manner` question. Lovejoy 1898 stabilizes the distinction as:

- karma → particular condition of rebirth;
- `upādāna` → fact of rebirth as such.

He then gives the distinction a historical explanation: karma is inherited moral-causal vocabulary that assumes rebirth, whereas the Buddhist possibility of terminating rebirth creates a separate explanatory problem.

Again, provenance does not dictate function. An inherited term may remain in the new system while its explanatory labour is redistributed relative to another term.

Thus the Senart and Hardy microhistories converge on the same rule without requiring a claim that Lovejoy invented the ingredients:

> **historical origin and present explanatory role must be established separately.**

## 7. The second half of 1898 makes the rule explicit at the lexical level

Batch 41 showed that Lovejoy stages an explicit fork over `upādi / upadhi`:

- Childers / Rhys Davids derive `upādi` from `upādā / upāda` and connect it to the five skandhas;
- Müller / Dahlmann support or are used to support the alternative relation of Sanskrit `upadhi` to Pāli `upādi`.

Lovejoy’s response is methodologically explicit: **both derivations may be etymologically possible, so the meaning of `upādi` must be settled without reference to its etymology.** He turns to actual textual usage.

This is probably the sharpest published statement of the evidentiary division uncovered by the Senart comparison:

`derivation/filiation evidence ≠ sufficient semantic evidence`.

The Sutta-Nipāta tests then yield an ethical/residual-attachment interpretation in contexts where a physical “skandhas remaining” interpretation would be incoherent. 004 p. 66 reaches the same conclusion with unusually high transcription confidence: positive `upādisesa` applies to an Anāgāmin with residual impurity; negative `anupādisesa` applies to the Arahat before or after physical death, so the distinction cannot simply encode persistence of the five skandhas.

## 8. Revised comparison: Senart and Lovejoy are closer than a foil model suggests

The historiographical picture should now be symmetrical enough to avoid caricature.

### Senart

- decomposes a formula historically;
- reconstructs a nucleus and later additions;
- tracks term filiations;
- identifies borrowings and successive stratifications;
- warns against reading later scholastic nomenclature as transparent evidence of original conscious speculation;
- uses philology to expose historical accretion and semantic confusion.

### Lovejoy

- accepts substantial compositeness and borrowing;
- checks whether alleged repetitions are actually semantic/causal identities;
- grants that terms may have pre-Buddhist or non-Buddhist histories while asking what role they acquire in a Buddhist arrangement;
- when etymological genealogies underdetermine meaning, brackets them and turns to usage;
- permits a composite formula to possess a discoverable technical organization.

The difference is not `history` versus `system`. It is a disagreement over **how far historical genealogy may determine semantic and systematic interpretation**.

## 9. 1902: a plausible scale expansion of the same evidentiary rule

`Religion and the Time-Process` (1902) remains the strongest later control because Lovejoy explicitly self-cites the 1898 Buddhist paper in his dependent-origination discussion.

At the larger comparative scale he again separates two questions that surface resemblance can tempt the historian to collapse. The affinity between Aristotle’s changeless/self-sufficient deity and Vedānta does not itself establish eastern transmission; Lovejoy argues that the Greek conception can be generated from an internal Greek genealogy. Likewise, resemblance in Neoplatonic otherworldliness does not by itself require Oriental influence.

The operational parallel with 1898 is precise enough to state but should not be inflated into a direct causal claim:

- **1898:** etymological/filiational relation does not by itself determine semantic function;
- **1902:** conceptual resemblance does not by itself determine historical transmission.

In both cases Lovejoy divides evidentiary burdens and refuses to let one type of relation answer a second historical/semantic question automatically.

This is the cleanest current bridge from 004/1898 to early history-of-ideas practice. It is an operational continuity, not proto-`unit-idea` terminology.

## 11. Revised paper-level claim

The strongest current formulation is:

> **Notebook 004 places Lovejoy inside a late-1890s philological field already committed to decomposition, term-filiation and historical stratification. Its distinctive evidentiary value is therefore not that it reveals those operations before Lovejoy’s later history of ideas, but that it shows him limiting what genealogical evidence is allowed to prove. Senart could use the filiation of terms to reconstruct accretion and to distrust apparent system; Lovejoy could grant the same accretion while requiring independent semantic and causal evidence before concluding that repeated or borrowed elements were functionally identical or that a composite arrangement was unintelligible. In 1898 he makes the same division explicit when etymology fails to settle usage; in 1902 he applies an analogous control when conceptual resemblance fails to establish historical transmission.**

This is narrower than an “early unit ideas” thesis but substantially more defensible and more documentable.
