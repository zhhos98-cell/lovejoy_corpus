# Batch 89 — Leuba 1912 partitions authorities by proposition: King and Lovejoy as simultaneous adversaries and evidence sources

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: deepen Batches 81–87 from article-level disaggregation to **source-function partition** inside one receiving text. Primary object is James H. Leuba, *A Psychological Study of Religion* (1912), especially pp.49–52 and 74–76. King 1911 and Coe 1910 provide controversy controls; Leuba's 1910 *Psychological Bulletin* review at vol. VII p.301 remains a primary-text HOLD.

## Core result

Leuba's 1912 book does something more granular than accepting or rejecting individual authors. It assigns the **same author different epistemic functions in different proposition-spaces**.

The clearest pair is Irving King:

- in chapter II, King's theory of religion as a socially generated `valuational attitude` is a **THEORETICAL TARGET**. Leuba argues that King's graded series of higher, more permanent and more inclusive values produces only differences of degree and therefore fails to mark a qualitative boundary between religion and the rest of life; he connects this failure directly to King's difficulty in distinguishing religion from magic and other practical/aesthetic activities;
- in chapter IV, King's chapter VI, `The Mysterious Power`, is an **EVIDENCE AGGREGATOR**. Leuba says King brings together the philological and other data bearing on impersonal power, and directs readers to King ch. VI as a good summary of the evidence.

Lovejoy is partitioned almost exactly the same way:

- Lovejoy's stronger claim that primitive power is conceived as operating by regular and intelligible laws, hence `Primitive Energetics`, is a **THEORETICAL TARGET / WARRANT TARGET**. Leuba accepts the power category but disputes the mechanical strength of the causal relation because the means used to invoke Wakonda / Manitou / Mana do not disclose a definite, stable quantitative relation to effects;
- Lovejoy's 1906 article is nevertheless an **EVIDENCE SUMMARY**. In the same footnote in which King ch. VI is recommended, Leuba also recommends Lovejoy's shorter article as a good summary of the evidence.

This produces a stronger reception-mechanics rule:

> **evidentiary competence does not entail theoretical assent.**

Or, from the citation side:

> **citation-level survival can conceal proposition-level defeat.**

The recipient is not merely decomposing an argument; he is **decomposing authorities into locally usable functions**.

## 1. King as theoretical target: Leuba pp.49–52

Leuba reconstructs King's project as an attempt to explain how a religious attitude differentiates itself from other conscious attitudes that are also valuational. King's social-psychological thesis makes the group the matrix in which permanent notions of value develop: the stronger and more organized the social group, the more elevated, permanent, and universal the valuations associated with it.

Leuba's objection is not that King lacks a developmental mechanism. It is that the mechanism supplies **continuity without discrimination**.

The argument runs:

1. King himself recognizes that valuational attitudes belong to life generally, not to religion alone.
2. He then marks the specifically religious values by greater significance, permanence, and power.
3. But these predicates form a graded series.
4. Any boundary drawn on such a series yields a difference of degree.
5. Hence the apparatus connects religion with the whole of life but does not adequately differentiate religion from the rest of life.
6. Leuba treats King's difficulty separating religion from magic, aesthetic activity, and other practical activity as a consequence of this problem.
7. Against King's degree-continuum, Leuba insists that commonly called religion and magic contain a **specific qualitative distinction**.

This matters because Leuba is not treating King as an incompetent witness. He is rejecting one **inferential use** of King's materials: the move from a developmental sociology of value to a definition capable of discriminating religion.

### Code

`King / valuational attitude / definition of religion`
→ `THEORETICAL TARGET`
→ `REJECT as insufficient discriminator`
→ `continuity explanatory; boundary-making weak`.

## 2. King as evidence aggregator: Leuba pp.75–76

Only a few chapters later King's function changes completely.

After auditing Lovejoy's stronger regular-law characterization of impersonal power, Leuba introduces King's chapter `The Mysterious Power` and says it brings together the philological and other data relevant to the problem. The footnote then tells readers interested in the point that a good summary of the evidence can be found either in **King ch. VI** or in **Lovejoy's shorter article**.

Thus the same King whom Leuba has just criticized on the definition of religion becomes a recommended route into the evidence dossier for mana / manitou / wakonda / orenda and related impersonal-power concepts.

This is not inconsistency. It is a highly local allocation of authority:

`King is weak as discriminator of religion`
≠
`King is weak as compiler of impersonal-power evidence`.

The two functions are independent.

### Code

`King / ch.VI impersonal-power dossier`
→ `EVIDENCE AGGREGATOR`
→ `ACCEPT / USE`
→ `no transfer of assent to King's general definition of religion`.

---

## 3. Lovejoy as theoretical and warrant target

Leuba gives Lovejoy's 1906 paper unusually precise treatment. He names the article and reproduces the key correction of Marett: the crucial primitive power is not defined by abnormality or portentousness but by efficacy conceived as operating in an intelligible enough fashion to be investigated and controlled; Lovejoy therefore proposes `Primitive Energetics`.

Leuba then separates **category** from **causal strength**.

He does not throw out Wakonda / Manitou / Mana as non-personal power. Instead he asks whether Lovejoy has shown enough regularity to warrant a quasi-mechanical description. His answer is negative: much remains unpredictable, and the ritual means do not indicate a definite and stable quantitative relation between means and effects. The power is therefore better treated by Leuba as magical than mechanical.

This is the Batch81 disaggregation in its cleanest form:

`impersonal-power category` → retained

`regular/intelligible-law strength` → re-audited and weakened

`Primitive Energetics as mechanical characterization` → contested

`evidence dossier` → retained.

### Code

`Lovejoy / mechanical regularity proposition`
→ `THEORETICAL + WARRANT TARGET`
→ `WEAKEN / RECLASSIFY`
→ `mechanical power -> magical force`.

---

## 4. Lovejoy as evidence summary

Immediately after criticizing the stronger causal thesis, Leuba preserves the paper as a documentary resource. His footnote recommends the Lovejoy article beside King ch. VI as a good summary of the evidence.

This is stronger than saying that Leuba only partly agreed with Lovejoy. `Partial agreement` collapses two different epistemic jobs. The same object is:

- inadequate as warrant for a strong mechanical proposition;
- adequate as a compact route into the comparative evidence dossier.

The theory can fail locally while the citation remains useful.

### Code

`Lovejoy / comparative evidence dossier`
→ `EVIDENCE SUMMARY`
→ `ACCEPT / USE`
→ `survives rejection of mechanical thesis`.

## 5. Authority disaggregation: a new reception mechanic

Batches 81–83 established **argument disaggregation**: category, causal thesis, dossier, and proof procedure move at different speeds.

Batch89 adds **authority disaggregation**:

> a recipient can assign different truth-values and different evidentiary functions to different propositions extracted from the same author.

The relevant unit is therefore neither `citation` nor even `author`. It is closer to:

`source × proposition × evidentiary job`.

For Leuba 1912:

| author | proposition-space | source function | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| King | definition / valuational attitude | theoretical model | reject as insufficient discriminator |
| King | impersonal-power data | evidence aggregator | use / recommend |
| Lovejoy | mechanical regularity | causal warrant | reject / weaken |
| Lovejoy | impersonal-power dossier | evidence summary | use / recommend |

This is why a reception history based on raw citation persistence can misread the intellectual event. A citation can remain after the proposition that originally gave it theoretical force has been defeated.

## 6. Reproof and source-function partition belong to the same mechanism

Leuba then makes another move already recovered in Batch83. When he turns from the **existence** of non-personal power concepts to the stronger **genetic/historical** proposition that such concepts could arise independently of animism and perhaps precede it, he explicitly declines to reproduce the entire linguistic/historical dossier and changes evidentiary channel to developmental psychology, especially the child.

So the chapter contains three distinct operations:

1. `audit Lovejoy's mechanical causal thesis`;
2. `retain King + Lovejoy as evidence summaries for ethnographic existence`;
3. `substitute child/developmental psychology when proving genesis`.

The evidence channel changes when the proposition changes. At the same time, the authority attached to each source changes when its evidentiary job changes.

A useful combined formula is:

> **propositions migrate by warrant substitution; authorities survive by source-function partition.**

This is structural description, not evidence that Leuba consciously adopted Lovejoy's reproof method.

## 7. What this does to the King–Leuba 1910/1911 HOLD

King 1911 directly tells us that reviews of *The Development of Religion* had produced misunderstandings of his general standpoint and specific passages, and explicitly identifies J. H. Leuba, *Psychological Bulletin* VII, p.301, alongside George A. Coe's 1910 review.

The still-missing Leuba 1910 review can now be targeted much more precisely. The question is no longer generic `what did Leuba say about King?` It is:

### Target A — definition-of-religion dispute

Does p.301 already contain the critique later developed in Leuba 1912 pp.49–52?

Search for:

- `value` / `valuational attitude`;
- permanent / highest / ultimate values;
- social group as matrix of value;
- religion as difference of degree versus qualitative difference;
- failure to distinguish religion from magic or other life activities.

### Target B — impersonal-power dossier

Does p.301 discuss King's chapter VI `The Mysterious Power`, mana / manitou / wakonda / orenda, or King's use of Lovejoy?

If yes, the 1910 review may already display the later source-function partition. If no, the positive evidentiary use of King in Leuba 1912 may be a later reallocation.

## 9. Historiographic payoff

A publication-ready formulation, subject to facsimile collation for exact quotations:

> **Leuba received authorities propositionally. In the same 1912 book Irving King could fail as a theorist of religion yet remain useful as a compiler of evidence for impersonal power; Lovejoy could fail to establish the mechanical regularity of `primitive energetics` yet remain a recommended summary of the evidence. Reception therefore operated below the level of authors and even below the level of texts. What survived was a locally assigned source function.**

Short form:

> **For Leuba, authority is local to a proposition.**

And a caution against citation census:

> **Citation-level survival can conceal proposition-level defeat.**
