# Batch 92 — Relay without verdict inheritance: Jones → Lovejoy → Leuba → Malinowski and the zigzag of `mechanicality`

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: deepen Batch91's citation-shadow result by tracing one proposition through the North-American `manitou` dossier: whether impersonal power is regular/mechanical. The relevant objects are William Jones 1905, Lovejoy 1906, Leuba 1912, and Malinowski 1915.

## Core result

The reception chain is not a linear transmission of a verdict. The **same dossier supports alternating judgments about mechanical regularity**:

`Jones 1905`
→ impersonal cosmic property + efficacy + transferability
→ **interpretation explicitly not governed by any regular rule; feeling rather than knowledge**

`Lovejoy 1906`
→ recruits Jones as high-resolution grammatical/native evidence for impersonal abstraction and transfer
→ strengthens the comparative thesis into power operating by **quite regular and intelligible laws**, `Primitive Energetics`

`Leuba 1912`
→ retains the impersonal-power category and Lovejoy/King evidence dossier
→ explicitly rejects the mechanical strengthening because ritual means do not show a definite and stable quantitative relation to effects

`Malinowski 1915`
→ rejects sharply abstract native concepts and emphasizes vague/felt force
→ nevertheless calls Algonkin `manitu` in essence a **mechanical factor** conferring mystical power
→ explicitly cites Jones as primary ethnographic source and sends readers to King ch. VI + Leuba ch. IV, but does not name Lovejoy.

The best reception-mechanics conclusion is therefore:

> **relay does not imply verdict inheritance.**

A later author can cite an intermediary for access to a dossier while taking a position that the intermediary explicitly rejected. Source transmission and proposition verdict travel on partly independent tracks.

This also sharpens Batch91's citation-shadow claim. Malinowski is not simply `Lovejoy without the name`. His text recombines components in a configuration that resembles Lovejoy on mechanicality but resembles Jones/Leuba/Marettian psychology on vagueness and anti-abstraction. The downstream object is a **new recombination**, not a hidden copy.

## 1. Jones 1905: impersonal property without regular-law interpretation

William Jones's `The Algonkin Manitou`, *Journal of American Folk-Lore* 18 (1905), 183–190, is unusually diagnostic because it contains both the elements later used by Lovejoy and an explicit limit adverse to Lovejoy's stronger conclusion.

Jones's field/linguistic account says, in substance:

- manitou is associated with a cosmic mysterious property existing throughout nature;
- as a property/abstraction it is expressed with inanimate grammatical gender;
- it can become confused with animate objects when identified with them;
- it is transferable between objects/persons;
- ritual cases such as sweat-lodge practice and eating the heart of a brave enemy illustrate transfer and expected efficacy;
- the property has a lively miraculous efficacy and an emotional sense of mystery.

But Jones's summary ends with a crucial qualification: **its interpretation is not according to any regular rule, but is based on feeling rather than knowledge**.

Thus Jones can license:

`impersonal property`
`omnipresence / diffusion`
`transferability`
`efficacy`

without licensing:

`regular causal law`.

This source-function split is essential for understanding what Lovejoy does next.

---

## 2. Lovejoy 1906 raises Jones's evidentiary resolution, then outruns Jones's own verdict

Batch77–78 already established Lovejoy's exact use of Jones.

Lovejoy first limits seventeenth-century missionary evidence: early sources show concrete applications of `oki/manitou` to unusually powerful persons and things, but do not yet prove a pervasive abstract property. He then notes that **after those old citations had already been transcribed**, Jones's 1905 specialist study appeared.

Lovejoy recruits Jones precisely at this proof threshold because Jones supplies a stronger evidentiary type:

- inanimate grammatical gender when `manitou` denotes property/abstraction;
- omnipresent property language;
- transfer mechanisms explained through native practice;
- specialist study of Sauk/Fox/Kickapoo material.

So Jones materially upgrades Lovejoy's case from:

`concrete lexical application`

into:

`impersonal abstraction + transferable efficacy`.

Yet Lovejoy's final proposition is stronger than Jones's own summary. In direct disagreement with Marett, Lovejoy describes the force as operating according to `quite regular and intelligible laws`, capable of study and control, and calls the system `Primitive Energetics`.

The key historical operation is:

> **Lovejoy accepts Jones's high-resolution evidence while declining Jones's own low-regularity interpretive limit.**

That is not a citation error. It is an inferential strengthening by the receiving author.

### Code

`Jones evidence architecture`
→ `ACCEPT / HIGH VALUE`.

`Jones anti-regularity verdict`
→ `NOT INHERITED`.

`Lovejoy regular-law thesis`
→ `RECEIVER STRENGTHENING`.

---

## 3. Leuba 1912 partially restores Jones's limit

Leuba's response becomes clearer when Jones is put back into the chain.

Leuba quotes Lovejoy's `quite regular and intelligible laws` language and directly questions its appropriateness. His test is empirical and relational: the means used to bring Wakonda / Manitou / Mana into play do not reveal a sufficiently definite and stable quantitative relation between ritual means and effects. Therefore the invoked power is not mechanical in the strong sense but magical.

Leuba simultaneously keeps:

- the existence of nonpersonal power;
- the comparative dossier;
- Lovejoy's paper as a good evidence summary;
- King ch. VI as another good evidence summary.

In proposition space, Leuba's verdict is closer to Jones's final caution than to Lovejoy's strengthening:

`Jones: no regular rule / feeling > knowledge`

`Lovejoy: regular and intelligible laws`

`Leuba: no definite stable quantitative means-effects relation`.

This does **not** prove Leuba derived his criticism specifically from Jones. It establishes a structural return to the source-side limit that Lovejoy had outrun.

### Mechanism

`high-resolution dossier survives`
+
`receiver's stronger causal inference is rolled back`.

---

## 4. Malinowski 1915 moves again: anti-abstraction + `mechanical factor`

Malinowski then produces the strangest combination in the sequence.

On primitive conceptuality he is emphatically anti-rationalist. He criticizes theories that attribute to primitive people:

- clearly defined anthropomorphic spiritual beings;
- a subtle clear opposition between personal agents and impersonal forces;
- sharply formulated magical laws.

For Malinowski, primitive abstract forces and general phenomena are more often felt than clearly conceptualized. The basic religious layer is a vague, undifferentiated sense of mysterious/mystical power.

This part of the argument is compatible with Jones's emphasis on feeling and confusion, and unlike Lovejoy's strong `primitive philosophy` abstraction.

But when Malinowski reaches Algonkin manitu, he states that it can be regarded **in essence as a mechanical factor giving mystical power to objects of religious veneration**.

So his combination is:

`native conceptuality = vague / felt / non-abstract`

plus

`analyst's causal description = mechanical factor`.

That is neither Jones's configuration nor Lovejoy's nor Leuba's.

### Comparison

- **Jones:** vague/feeling + impersonal property + no regular rule.
- **Lovejoy:** abstract category + transferable efficacy + regular/intelligible causal law.
- **Leuba:** impersonal category + evidence retained + mechanical regularity rejected.
- **Malinowski:** vague/feeling + impersonal force + mechanical descriptor restored.

The proposition does not simply move forward. Its components are repeatedly **unbundled and recombined**.

## 5. Malinowski cites the anti-mechanical intermediary while using a mechanical descriptor

Batch91 established the citation architecture: Malinowski's impersonal-power note names Jones among the principal ethnological sources, then points readers to Marett, Hubert/Mauss, Preuss, Durkheim, **King ch. VI**, and **Leuba ch. IV pp.70–84**.

This matters because Leuba ch. IV is not neutral on mechanicality. It explicitly says the power invoked is **not mechanical** in Lovejoy's stronger sense.

Therefore Malinowski's citation to Leuba cannot be treated as evidence that he inherited Leuba's verdict. Quite the opposite: at the proposition level Malinowski's `mechanical factor` statement is in tension with the very chapter he cites.

This yields a general rule:

> **the cited source may be a dossier source without being the verdict source.**

Or more formally:

`citation edge`
≠
`proposition-assent edge`.

This is a downstream version of Batch89's source-function partition. The same work can be cited for data aggregation while its interpretive conclusion is silently declined.

---

## 6. The `mechanical factor` phrase is not simply Jones

The direct Jones text makes an important source test possible.

Malinowski's description of manitu closely follows Jones in several respects:

- cosmic mysterious property;
- impersonal character;
- diffusion through nature;
- confusion when property and object are identified;
- emotional/mysterious efficacy.

But Jones does **not** culminate in a mechanical-law verdict. His published summary explicitly says interpretation follows no regular rule and rests on feeling rather than knowledge.

Therefore Malinowski's `mechanical factor` is not a straightforward translation of Jones's final analytical conclusion.

We still cannot assign it to Lovejoy. Other routes remain possible, including King's synthesis, broader contemporary dynamism discourse, or Malinowski's own analytical interpolation. Direct Lovejoy dependence remains **UNPROVEN**.

The safe finding is narrower and more interesting methodologically:

> **Malinowski combines Jones-derived descriptive material with a causal label that Jones's own concluding qualification does not license straightforwardly.**

---

## 7. This is proposition drift, not merely citation compression

Batch91 introduced `citation shadow`: Lovejoy disappears by name while King and Leuba become named gateways to the dossier.

Batch92 adds **proposition drift through relay**.

A single proposition family changes as follows:

### Stage 1 — source-side descriptive packet

Jones:
`impersonal / diffuse / transferable / efficacious / affectively apprehended`
+
`no regular interpretive rule`.

### Stage 2 — theoretical strengthening

Lovejoy:
`impersonal / transferable / efficacious`
→ `regular/intelligible causal system`
→ `Primitive Energetics`.

### Stage 3 — critical rollback

Leuba:
`impersonal power + evidence dossier retained`
→ `regular/mechanical strength rejected`.

### Stage 4 — recombination after name loss

Malinowski:
`native abstraction weakened even further`
+
`mechanical descriptor restored at analyst level`
+
`Lovejoy citation absent`.

This makes `reception` look less like copying concepts and more like a sequence of **local verdicts over movable components**.

A useful formula is:

> **components travel; verdicts are recomputed.**

And in source-critical form:

> **relay preserves access to evidence more reliably than it preserves the interpretation placed upon that evidence.**

## 8. Implication for citation-network history

A conventional citation graph would record:

- Lovejoy → Leuba;
- Jones → Lovejoy;
- Jones → Malinowski;
- Leuba → Malinowski;
- no Lovejoy → Malinowski edge.

But proposition-level reconstruction shows why that graph is inadequate. The downstream Malinowski sentence occupies a position unexpectedly close to Lovejoy on one dimension (`mechanicality`) while sharply opposing Lovejoy on another (`clear primitive abstraction`). His named intermediary Leuba is close to him on the second dimension but opposite him on the first.

Thus intellectual transmission is not recoverable from adjacency alone.

The more accurate object is a **multidimensional proposition trajectory**:

`source`
× `component`
× `verdict`
× `evidentiary job`
× `citation visibility`.

This is the same ontology suggested by Batch89, now demonstrated across four authors rather than inside one receiving text.

## 9. Evidence cautions

1. Jones 1905 is direct full text and explicitly says the manitou interpretation is not according to any regular rule and rests on feeling rather than knowledge.
2. Lovejoy's recruitment of Jones at pp.370–372 and the grammatical/transfer evidence were directly audited in Batches77–78.
3. Lovejoy's `regular and intelligible laws` / `Primitive Energetics` thesis is direct primary and directly quoted by Leuba 1912.
4. Leuba's rejection of stable mechanical regularity is direct primary.
5. Malinowski's `mechanical factor` wording and anti-abstraction argument are direct primary in the 1915 Polish text.
6. Malinowski directly cites Jones and Leuba ch. IV in the relevant dossier, but that does not identify the immediate source of his `mechanical factor` label.
7. Do not convert structural closeness to Lovejoy into hidden influence.
8. `verdict zigzag`, `proposition drift`, `relay without verdict inheritance`, and `components travel; verdicts are recomputed` are our analytical formulations.

## Working formulation

> **The early history of `primitive energetics` is not a chain of inherited conclusions. Jones supplied Lovejoy with unusually diagnostic evidence for an impersonal, transferable manitou, while explicitly denying that its interpretation followed any regular rule. Lovejoy converted that dossier into a theory of regular and intelligible causal law; Leuba retained the dossier but rejected the mechanical strengthening; Malinowski, citing Jones and Leuba but not Lovejoy, then combined a strongly anti-abstract account of primitive thought with the description of manitu as a mechanical factor. The evidence packet travels more continuously than the verdicts placed upon it.**
