# Batch 77 — 1906 citation architecture: proposition-relative source reliability, statement decomposition, and the source-level enforcement of proof thresholds

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: direct primary audit of the North-American evidentiary packet in Lovejoy, `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` (1906), especially printed pp.363–370, against the proof-threshold and independent-control models established in Batches 68, 70, 73, 75 and 76.

## Core result

Batch 76 showed that Lovejoy's 1906 paper explicitly demands a `vera causa`: the explanatory conception of impersonal/communicable energy must be independently evidenced rather than merely inferred from the customs it is then used to explain.

The present source-by-source audit shows **how Lovejoy enforces that rule at citation level**.

He does **not** assign a single global credibility score to each ethnographic source. Instead, he repeatedly decomposes a witness into distinct statement-types:

- external observation of a custom;
- attested native lexical usage;
- translation into an English/French religious term;
- semantic abstraction of the native category;
- causal or ritual interpretation of the observed act.

The same source can be admissible for one of these propositions and inadmissible for another.

The resulting rule is:

> **source × proposition type → evidentiary competence → proposition licensed.**

This is stronger than generic `source criticism`. It is **proposition-relative source reliability** or, equivalently, **claim-relative admissibility**.

It also deepens Batch 68's formulation `the reporter is part of the datum`. In 005, reporter/date/contact history qualify testimony. In 1906, Lovejoy goes one step finer: **the individual statement made by a reporter is itself decomposed by evidentiary function.** A missionary can be a weak interpreter of native semantics while remaining a useful witness to a concrete application of a word; an old travel account can preserve an external ritual fact while its translation of a native term is rejected; a recent specialist ethnographer can be trusted for distributional usage while still being corrected for collapsing semantic core and secondary connotation.

The source is therefore not the atomic unit of reliability. **The claim extracted from the source is.**

---

## 1. McGee: `very clear and trustworthy information` is still not sufficient semantic analysis

At printed p.363 Lovejoy introduces the Dakotan `wakonda` evidence through W. J. McGee, *Fifteenth Annual Report of the U.S. Bureau of Ethnology*, p.182.

Lovejoy's praise is unusually strong. He says that in the case of the Dakotan term there is **`very clear and trustworthy information as to its use and real meaning`**. McGee supplies a wide distributional field: sun, moon, thunder, lightning, stars, winds, cedar, shamans, mythic monsters, fetishes, ceremonial objects, animals and striking natural places can all be `wakanda`.

McGee then offers a bundle of possible English approximations, including `mystery`, power, sacredness, antiquity, grandeur, animation and immortality.

Lovejoy does not simply accept the authority wholesale. He immediately says that McGee **does not attempt to distinguish the essential meaning of the word from its secondary connotations**. The list is valuable precisely because it gives Lovejoy evidence from which he can reconstruct that distinction himself.

Thus the verdict is internally partitioned:

- **distribution / application of the native term:** HIGH;
- **existence of a broad native category:** HIGH;
- **English one-word gloss:** PARTIAL;
- **analysis of semantic core versus secondary associations:** INSUFFICIENT.

This is crucial. Even a source Lovejoy explicitly labels clear and trustworthy does not thereby receive unrestricted interpretive authority.

### Proposition licensed

McGee licenses the proposition that `wakonda` has a broad, cross-object application associated with unusual efficacy/power. He does **not**, by himself, settle the exact abstract essence of the concept.

## 2. Alice C. Fletcher: specialist evidence can be stronger than its own final interpretation

Lovejoy next turns to Alice C. Fletcher's work on the Omaha, explicitly calling her paper on the import of the totem **valuable**.

Fletcher gives two propositions that strongly support Lovejoy's category:

1. animate and inanimate things are permeated by a common life;
2. this life is communicable or transferable.

This evidence is much closer to the abstract causal conception Lovejoy wants than the older missionary gloss `god` or the loose McGee bundle of partial equivalents.

But Lovejoy still does not simply inherit Fletcher's complete interpretation. Where Fletcher identifies the operative conception too narrowly with a kind of `will-power`, Lovejoy treats that specialization as misleading because the same efficacy is attributed to natural forces and objects that cannot plausibly be reduced to individual volition.

The source therefore divides again:

- **field observation / native-use pattern:** HIGH;
- **common-life and transferability structure:** HIGH;
- **narrow reduction to will-power:** CORRECTED / TOO SPECIFIC.

### Proposition licensed

Fletcher licenses a stronger claim than McGee about communicability and common life. She still does not dictate Lovejoy's final philosophical abstraction.

The evidentiary gain is not `newer source replaces old source`; it is **a better-matched source licenses a stronger proposition**.

## 3. M. V. P. / Osage material: external custom is accepted while translation and interpretation are rejected

One of the cleanest examples in the North-American packet is Lovejoy's use of the anonymous/initialled `M. V. P., Histoire de la tribu des Osages` (1827), based on information concerning Osages brought to Paris.

Lovejoy gives the source a granular credibility judgment. He treats the internal evidence as suggesting an honest and careful observer and regards the account as trustworthy **so far as external facts of custom are concerned**. But he explicitly warns that its translations of native terms and interpretations of the acts described must be corrected by later work, including Dorsey, McGee and Fletcher.

The point is visible in the planting/ritual material. M. V. P. reports an observed ceremony and translates `Houackanda` in personal/deistic language. Lovejoy keeps the external ritual fact while rejecting the theological translation. He further reclassifies what the older observer called a `prayer` as closer to an incantatory operation and treats a bodily/ritual circumstance that the observer records incidentally as potentially causally central.

This produces an exact source-function partition:

- **what happened externally:** ADMISSIBLE;
- **which native word was used in the setting:** POTENTIALLY ADMISSIBLE;
- **translation of that word as a personal deity / master of life:** DOWNGRADED;
- **observer's classification of the act as prayer:** DOWNGRADED;
- **Lovejoy's mechanism reconstruction:** requires comparison with later lexical and ethnographic controls.

### Proposition licensed

M. V. P. can help establish a ritual morphology and a historical usage context. It cannot carry the proposition that the native category is a personal god, nor that the rite operates by petition to such a god.

This is the sharpest current demonstration that **the same document is evidentially competent and incompetent at different levels simultaneously**.

## 4. Roger Williams: a seventeenth-century missionary can preserve lexical application even when the gloss `god` is unusable

In the Algonkin sequence Lovejoy deliberately reaches back to early observers. He notes that the usage of `manitou` and cognate terms can be detected even through the **superficial and unsympathetic observations** of seventeenth-century missionaries/explorers.

Roger Williams's *Key into the Languages of America* (1643) is useful because it reports `Manittoo` in association with excellency, unusual power or efficacy. Williams's own religious vocabulary leads toward the English gloss `god`.

Lovejoy does not discard the witness because the gloss is wrong. Instead he strips the observer's interpretation away from the lower-level fact:

- **attested concrete application of the word:** retained;
- **translation as `god`:** rejected;
- **evidence for a personal supreme being:** rejected;
- **evidence that unusual efficacy/power attracts the term:** retained.

### Proposition licensed

Williams can document the historical application of the native term. He cannot by himself establish the conceptual ontology that Lovejoy reconstructs from it.

This is exactly the kind of operation already visible in 005's missionary-contamination pages, but now executed inside a single citation rather than by accepting/rejecting a whole report.

## 5. Early Jesuit evidence and Bressani: concrete application does not prove the abstract category

Printed p.370 supplies perhaps the most explicit statement of source-function limits.

An early Jesuit report says that `oki` could be used for a powerful or unusual spirit/person, whether good or bad, and could be applied to missionaries, medicine-men/magicians and even persons seen as possessed or abnormal because of extraordinary capacities.

Lovejoy also cites Bressani's *Breve Relatione* (1653), through the *Jesuit Relations*. Bressani describes a hard-to-kill stag or bear in which an unusual bone, stone or serpent is found; the object is called `oki` and treated as an enchantment giving the animal `forza e vigore`, later kept as a powerful object.

Lovejoy then states the evidentiary boundary himself. These early observations show **only the application** of `oki` and `manitou` in concrete cases to persons or things marked by unusual powers. They **do not show** the use of those words as names for a single pervasive property or force possessed by those objects.

This yields a literal proof ladder:

`early lexical occurrence`

→ YES: proves concrete application

but

`early lexical occurrence`

→ NO: does not yet prove abstract universal category.

This is not our reconstruction imposed on the text. Lovejoy explicitly draws the limit.

### Proposition licensed

Seventeenth-century missionary evidence licenses the historical/concrete application claim and nothing stronger. The abstract philosophical category requires another evidentiary channel.

## 6. W. Jones 1905: a new specialist study appears after Lovejoy has already transcribed the old evidence

Immediately after limiting the Jesuit/Bressani packet, Lovejoy notes that **after these citations had been transcribed for the present paper**, an important new exposition appeared: W. Jones, `The Algonkin Manitou`, *American Journal of Folk-Lore* (1905), based on special study of Sac and Fox and Kickapoo material.

The chronology is revealing at the scale of research practice.

Lovejoy had already built an early-observer packet. He did not treat it as sufficient for the abstract category. A specialist field/linguistic study appearing during preparation is then recruited precisely at the point where the older material reaches its evidentiary ceiling.

Even before the rest of the Jones discussion is fully re-collated page by page, Lovejoy's placement of the citation is methodologically clear:

- early sources establish historical concrete use;
- Lovejoy explicitly says they do not establish the abstract pervasive force;
- Jones is introduced as an **important exposition** based on special study at exactly that transition.

### Current evidence limit

The present batch does not yet normalize every proposition Lovejoy extracts from Jones on the following pages. That continuation should be collated directly before assigning a full semantic verdict.

The safe result is narrower: **Jones is recruited as a higher-resolution evidentiary source after Lovejoy explicitly declares the old packet insufficient for the stronger abstract claim.**

## 7. Reliability is not a chronology ladder from `old bad` to `new good`

The source architecture should not be simplified into a Whiggish hierarchy in which early missionaries are bad and modern ethnographers are good.

McGee is recent and highly trusted, yet Lovejoy finds his semantic analysis insufficiently discriminating. Fletcher is valuable and supplies stronger conceptual evidence, yet one interpretive narrowing is rejected. M. V. P. is early but remains trusted for external custom. Williams and Jesuit/Bressani material remain usable for concrete lexical application despite theological misinterpretation.

The actual rule is:

> **fitness of evidence to proposition, not date of source alone.**

Date, observational proximity, linguistic competence, missionary categories, later specialist correction and the exact statement being extracted all interact.

This is a much more precise historiographic finding than `Lovejoy preferred better ethnography`.

## 8. Batch 68 → 76 → 77: the evidentiary atom becomes progressively smaller

The sequence across the project can now be stated clearly.

### Batch 68 — reporter/source as part of the datum

In 005, source date, missionary contact and channels of borrowing alter the evidentiary status of an ethnographic belief report.

Formula:

`reporter / date / contact environment → weight of report`.

### Batch 76 — higher claims require independent evidence

In 1906, the proposed causal category must be a `vera causa`, independently evidenced through native linguistic usage before it is used to explain customs.

Formula:

`independent existence-control → explanatory application → reciprocal corroboration`.

### Batch 77 — statement inside the source becomes the evidentiary unit

A single witness can be trustworthy for observed custom, useful for lexical occurrence, poor for translation, and insufficient for abstract semantics.

Formula:

`source → statement type → competence → proposition licensed`.

The historical trajectory is therefore not merely increasing skepticism. It is **increasing granularity in the allocation of evidentiary authority**.

## 9. Direct concordance with notebook 005

This architecture is already latent in 005.

### Missionary-contact pages

The future-life sequence accepts reported material provisionally while treating missionary contact and borrowed moralization as separate problems. The report can preserve a datum without proving its indigenous genealogy.

### Sacrifice / cannibalism pages

Reports of ceremonial killing or anthropophagy are admitted as descriptions while their interpreters' sacramental classifications are repeatedly withheld. A report can establish that bodies were eaten publicly or according to rule without proving religious motive, communion, or original sacrificial function.

### Blood/fetish pages

A reported material use is retained while its explanatory ontology is reopened: blood in a foundation may be protective efficacy rather than communion; an object may operate as an amulet with intrinsic efficacy rather than a fetish through indwelling spirit.

Thus 005 already separates:

`reported act`

from

`observer/analyst's classification of the act`.

1906 makes the same operation explicit at citation level and adds lexical control.

## 10. Relation to Marillier's document-class pedagogy

The result also lets us refine the Marillier comparison.

Marillier's 1888 programme distinguished travellers' narratives, missionary reports, official inquiries and other testimony/document classes, applying different rules of criticism to each. That is a **document-class level** source-critical grammar.

Lovejoy 005 operates partly at the same level: traveller/missionary provenance changes weight.

Lovejoy 1906 goes further within documents:

> one missionary report can be admitted for a lexical occurrence and rejected for theological translation.

This should not be turned into a direct transmission claim. But it sharpens the institutional-methodological homology:

`document class`

→ `document-specific criticism`

→ **statement-specific evidentiary competence**.

The last step is securely visible in Lovejoy even though its precise pedagogical genealogy remains open.

## 11. The paper's source apparatus can now be treated as an argument, not a bibliography

A major consequence is methodological for our own article.

The citations in 1906 are not merely an archive from which Lovejoy extracted examples. Their sequencing performs epistemic work:

1. broad distributional testimony;
2. criticism of loose glosses;
3. more discriminating specialist testimony;
4. preservation of old concrete attestations despite bad interpretation;
5. explicit refusal to use those old attestations for the stronger abstract proposition;
6. insertion of a new specialist study at the point where the evidentiary burden rises.

Thus a citation can be coded not merely as `source used` but as:

> **source → evidence class → proposition → confidence ceiling.**

This should be the default schema for the next 1906 audit.

## 12. Article-level formulation

A defensible sentence is now:

> **Lovejoy's evidentiary discipline operated below the level of whole sources. In the 1906 `Primitive Philosophy` essay, a single ethnographic witness could be accepted for an observed custom or a native word while rejected as an interpreter of that word's meaning. McGee's usage evidence was `clear and trustworthy` without settling the distinction between semantic core and connotation; seventeenth-century Jesuits could attest concrete applications of `oki` and `manitou` without proving an abstract pervasive force; an early Osage account could be trustworthy for external ritual facts while its translations and causal interpretation required correction. Reliability, in other words, was proposition-relative.**

A shorter formulation:

> **Lovejoy did not grade sources; he graded claims made from sources.**

This compressed version is useful rhetorically, but the longer formulation is more exact: he does also grade sources globally in places; the distinctive point is that global grading does not exhaust his practice.

## 13. Evidence cautions

1. McGee, Fletcher, early Jesuit/Bressani and the introduction of Jones are directly controlled in the 1906 primary text.
2. The M. V. P. source-function analysis was directly recovered in the same primary-text pass, but exact French title/orthography and every page-level wording should be rechecked before publication quotation.
3. Roger Williams is secure as an early Algonkin lexical witness; publication-grade quotation should use the 1906 facsimile or Williams primary rather than relying on OCR punctuation.
4. Jones is securely introduced as an important special study after the older citations had been transcribed. This batch does not yet claim that every subsequent Jones proposition proves Lovejoy's full abstraction; the continuation remains to be collated.
5. Do not convert `proposition-relative source reliability` into Lovejoy's own phrase. It is our analytical name for a directly observable citation practice.
6. The period racial/anthropological vocabulary in Lovejoy 1906 should remain marked as historical language, not silently adopted in present prose.
