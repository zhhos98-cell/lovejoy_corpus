## Core correction

The active Carnap branch had already established that the full Baltimore manuscript `Testability and Reduction` existed by 9 Dec. 1935, three weeks before Lovejoy's 31 Dec. intervention.

A stronger direct-primary control now pushes the relevant architecture back **nineteen months before Baltimore**.

On **15 May 1934**, Carnap wrote Charles Morris that C. I. Lewis had sent him both the published `Experience and Meaning` and, earlier, the manuscript. Carnap enclosed a copy of his own `Notes for an answer to Lewis`; he says he had also sent a copy directly to Lewis. The attached notes themselves are handwritten-dated **11 May 1934**.

Primary source:

Rudolf Carnap to Charles W. Morris, 15 May 1934, with attachment `Notes for an answer to Lewis’ “Experience and Meaning”`, VALEP static correspondence edition:  
https://valep.vc.univie.ac.at/files/Briefwechsel/Briefe/1934/1934-05-15-Carnap-an-Morris-a.html

Archival/editorial locators in VALEP:

- letter: CM; duplicate RC 029-04-15;
- attachment: CM; 2-page typescript/notes; handwritten 11.5.34.

This supersedes any shorthand saying the Lewis-reply/liberalization architecture is known only from December 1935.

## 1. Carnap explicitly accepts Lewis's central criticism by May 1934

Carnap summarizes Lewis's chief thesis as follows in substance:

- the known object is defined in experiential terms;
- knowledge is not exhausted by experience at the present moment;
- the Given supports a probability judgment concerning future experiences;
- future experiences can therefore bear on verification.

Carnap then states directly that the Vienna Circle is **now in agreement** with this view and says Lewis's criticism of their former, Wittgenstein-influenced views is **right**.

Most important causal qualification: Carnap says the **modification and development** of their views on this point was **mainly the effect of opposition made by physicists**.

Therefore the direct actor chronology is:

`earlier Wittgenstein-influenced view`
→ `modification/development under physicists' objections`
→ `Lewis criticism recognized as right and used as occasion for explicit reply`

rather than:

`Lewis criticism`
→ single-handedly causes Carnap's liberalization.

This is an anti-single-influence firewall.

## 2. Hypothesis / future-experience architecture is already explicit in May 1934

Carnap says that on the revised view a statement is an **hypothesis**:

- it is never completely verified by present experiences;
- it also concerns future experiences.

The attachment later states explicitly that:

`knowledge and meaning go beyond present experiences`.

This is highly important for the later Baltimore/Lovejoy dispute. By May 1934 Carnap already denies the crude view that meaning or knowledge is exhausted by what is presently verified.

Operator form:

`present experiential evidence`

≠

`total content of statement / hypothesis`.

A statement projects beyond the present evidential base to possible future observations.

This architecture substantially predates Lovejoy's Baltimore challenge.

## 3. `Verifiable in principle` is logical possibility, not technical executability

Carnap asks directly what `verifiable` or `verifiable in principle` should mean.

His answer is formalized through deducible observation sentences: a sentence is verifiable when appropriate implication-sentences connect it with observation sentences under possible conditions.

He then distinguishes:

`technically possible`

from

`logically possible`.

The relevant possibility is logical: the implicants must be compatible with known physical laws.

This is another pre-Baltimore reason not to flatten Carnap into a crude operational requirement of presently executable testing.

It also provides a direct bridge toward the later 1936 distinction between confirmability and stronger testability.

Warrant ceiling: the May 1934 notes do **not yet by themselves establish the full later reduction-sentence machinery or the final technical fourfold distinction among complete testability / complete confirmability / testability / confirmability**. Those require the December 1935 manuscript and 1936–37 publication.

## 4. Formal/material mode distinction is already part of the Lewis reply

Carnap says the problem of formulation is crucial and distinguishes:

- material mode of speech — talk of things, objects, facts, experiences;
- formal mode of speech — talk concerning sentences, names, denotations.

He notes that some formulations Lewis quotes as characteristic of positivism would not have been used by Carnap's group, including formulations that reduce mind/reality/toothache to constructions or behavior.

Carnap's important qualification is that the **material mode is neither false nor senseless**. Its defect is that it is dangerous because it frequently produces ambiguity and pseudo-problems.

This is a major direct-primary pre-Baltimore control.

It means that by May 1934 Carnap already possesses a jurisdiction distinction between:

`bad/dangerous mode of formulation`

and

`false or meaningless proposition`.

That substantially complicates any narrative in which Lovejoy at Baltimore first forces Carnap to recognize that language-form decisions should not be confused with assertions about meaning or truth.

## 5. Datum versus cognoscendum — the `something more` is explicit

Carnap identifies the chief application as translating the difference between datum and cognoscendum into the formal mode.

The cognoscendum includes a **`something more`** that is asserted but is not immediately verified.

He then describes a physical sentence as an hypothesis from which further observation sentences are deducible and may be verified or falsified later.

Thus the May 1934 reply architecture already contains:

`current datum`
≠
`cognoscendum / total content`

and

`immediate verification`
≠
`future evidential consequences of the hypothesis`.

This is directly relevant to Lovejoy's later distinction between empirical criterion and conceptual meaning, but it should be used as a Carnap-side control, not as evidence of agreement between them.

## 6. Circle-internal differentiation is explicit

Carnap's footnote says the `we` in this issue refers especially to the jocularly named **left wing** of the Vienna Circle: Frank, Hahn, Neurath, Carnap.

He contrasts this wing with Schlick and Waismann as more conservative / Wittgensteinian on relevant questions, while saying that on the point presently under discussion the wings appear to be moving closer together.

This destroys another possible flattening:

`Vienna Circle verificationism`

cannot be treated as one temporally fixed group doctrine even before the American encounter.

The relevant change is intra-Circle, actor-differentiated, and underway before Baltimore.

## 7. January 1935 independent circulation control

Ernest Nagel's letter to Carnap of **5 Jan. 1935** supplies an independent actor-level circulation control.

Nagel reports attending a Vienna Circle meeting where Schlick translated/discussed Lewis's paper and explicitly referred to Carnap's `Notes for a reply to Lewis`. Nagel understood Schlick to imply that Carnap had felt targeted by some of Lewis's criticism and that Schlick himself had never held some of the positions Carnap thought necessary to disavow.

Primary source:

Ernest Nagel to Rudolf and Ina Carnap, 5 Jan. 1935, VALEP:  
https://valep.vc.univie.ac.at/files/Briefwechsel/Briefe/1935/1935-01-05-Nagel-an-Carnap_Ina-a.html

This shows the Notes were circulating inside the Circle before 1935 and that the Lewis controversy was already generating an **internal historical dispute over who had ever held which version of positivism**.

That is unusually relevant to the Lovejoy project because target reconstruction is itself becoming the central methodological problem.

## 8. Revised chronology

The controlled sequence is now:

### Before May 1934

Lewis sends Carnap the manuscript of `Experience and Meaning`, then the reprint.

### 11 May 1934

Carnap's attached `Notes for an answer to Lewis` are dated.

### 15 May 1934

Carnap sends the Notes to Morris and says a copy has also gone to Lewis.

At this stage Carnap already:

- accepts Lewis's central criticism of the former view;
- says change was mainly prompted by physicists' opposition;
- treats statements as hypotheses extending to future experience;
- says knowledge and meaning go beyond present experience;
- distinguishes technical from logical possibility;
- distinguishes formal from material mode;
- says material mode is not simply false/senseless;
- distinguishes datum from cognoscendum.

### 5 Jan. 1935

Nagel reports Schlick discussing Carnap's Notes at a Circle meeting; internal disagreement concerns which older positions Carnap now disavows.

### 28 Oct. 1935

Secondary reconstruction / Creath correspondence locator: Carnap refers to the developing paper as his reply to Lewis.

### 9 Dec. 1935

Carnap sends Quine the full `Testability and Reduction` manuscript for English correction/retyping before Baltimore.

### 31 Dec. 1935

Baltimore: Carnap presents shortened paper; Lovejoy challenges the conventionalist/anti-metaphysical consequence; Carnap replies.

### 1936–37

Published `Testability and Meaning` contains the mature technical architecture: graded confirmation, confirmability versus stronger testability, reduction, explicit methodological scope, language-construction proposal versus assertion, and conventional decision versus objective observational component.

## 9. Causal consequence for Lovejoy–Carnap

The causal ceiling must now be stronger than the previous `three weeks before Baltimore` formulation.

Secure:

> **The central Lewis-reply liberalization and several key bridge distinctions are documented by May 1934, roughly nineteen months before Lovejoy's Baltimore intervention.**

Therefore Lovejoy cannot be treated as the source of:

- Carnap's recognition that present verification does not exhaust a statement;
- the hypothesis/future-experience model;
- the distinction between formal and material mode;
- the claim that material-mode formulations need not simply be false/senseless;
- the basic Lewis-reply project.

What Baltimore can still be:

- a public pressure point;
- an event at which Lovejoy attacks the **remaining anti-metaphysical jurisdiction** of a liberalized empiricism;
- a possible source of local wording/revision after 31 Dec., if manuscript/publication collation proves such changes.

The event-level question therefore sharpens to:

> **How can Carnap liberalize empirical meaning, concede that meaning outruns present verification, and still retain a principled exclusion of metaphysics?**

That is much closer to the objection Carnap's diary attributes to Lovejoy.

## 10. Relation to Verhaegh's reconstruction

Verhaegh correctly emphasizes that `Testability and Meaning` should be read as a comprehensive response to Lewis and an attempt to correct the American narrative about Carnap, not simply as a sudden Baltimore conversion.

The May 1934 direct source lets the repo make the causal statement more finely:

- Lewis's criticism is explicitly accepted as right by Carnap;
- Carnap says the relevant modification was already underway and mainly caused by physicists' opposition;
- Lewis's paper becomes a stimulus for articulation, public correction, and American-facing exposition of the revised position.

Thus:

`physicists / prior internal development`
→ `revised hypothesis/future-experience view`
+
`Lewis criticism recognized as accurate against former view`
→ `explicit reply / American clarification project`
→ `Baltimore public confrontation`.

No single actor owns the shift.

## 11. Implication for Lovejoy 1938

The 1938 Lovejoy essay explicitly knows Carnap may have changed since 1928 and deliberately uses *Scheinprobleme* as an instance of a type of argument.

The 1934 Notes now make that caution historically even more important.

By 1934 Carnap already says:

- knowledge/meaning transcend present experience;
- material-mode statements need not simply be senseless;
- theoretical hypotheses project to future possible observations.

Therefore Lovejoy's 1938 critique should be read as intentionally reconstructing a **broader anti-metaphysical argument-type**, not as a straightforward report of Carnap's current 1938 technical semantics.

This strengthens the current historical diagnosis:

> **Lovejoy's target is partly selected and widened for philosophical consequence. The historically relevant issue is the scope Carnap's empiricist criterion is entitled to govern, not whether Carnap in 1938 still held a crude present-verification theory.**
