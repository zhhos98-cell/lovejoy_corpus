# Batch 73 — 004 proof thresholds: textual witness → semantic relation → doctrinal function → historical genealogy, and the demonstrable migration of an inference operator into 005

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: 004 first, then controlled comparison with 005. This tests Batch 70/72’s claim that the 005 proof ladder may be an operator carried across a change in evidentiary object rather than a method local to comparative ethnology.

## Core result

The parallel 004 pass succeeds, but with one important correction.

The continuity is **not** that Lovejoy always refuses genealogy in 004 and later does the same in 005. In 004 he sometimes does make strong historical-compositional or external-influence claims. What persists is more precise: **he keeps different inferential questions separable and assigns them different warrants.**

A four-level reconstruction works for 004:

1. **Textual-witness admissibility** — which canonical passage, recension, translation, commentary or manuscript witness can carry the lexical claim?
2. **Semantic/classificatory relation** — what does a term mean in use, and which terms/passages may legitimately be grouped as semantically related?
3. **Doctrinal/causal function** — what job does the term perform inside the Buddhist causal or soteriological configuration?
4. **Historical genealogy/dependence** — is the form inherited, borrowed, interpolated, composite, secondary, or historically dependent on another Indic system?

The same asymmetric possibility seen in 005 is already present: **a lower-level result can stand while a higher-level historical result remains uncertain.** The cleanest case is `upādisesa`: Lovejoy can argue strongly from usage that the term denotes an ethical condition while leaving etymology, scribal history and the total textual genealogy explicitly unsettled.

But 004 also shows the converse discipline: where Lovejoy believes independent structural/historical warrants accumulate, he is willing to advance a compositional or influence hypothesis. The operator is therefore not `genealogy skepticism`; it is **proof-level separation**.

This makes `operator migration` substantially more defensible:

> **004 qualifies textual witnesses and semantic relations before doctrinal function and historical dependence; 005 qualifies ethnographic witnesses and formal similarities before mechanism and genealogy. The evidentiary objects change, but the inference syntax remains recognizably four-level.**

## 1. 004 PDF p.33 / MS p.97: Hardy is converted from a source proposition into an analytic allocation problem

Direct manuscript reinspection confirms the compact heading and question:

`Upādāna. Relation to Karma.`

Lovejoy then asks whether upādāna may be called the cause of the **fact of continued existence**, and karma the cause of the **manner of continued existence**, citing Hardy p.394.

Hardy’s primary text is now already recovered in Batch 39: upādāna produces renewed existence while karma controls the manner/state of that existence. Lovejoy’s notebook does not merely copy this. It reformulates the two-factor report as a question of causal allocation.

The inferential levels separate immediately:

- the Hardy passage is a usable witness to a two-factor account;
- `upādāna` and karma are both in the rebirth problem field;
- they need not therefore mean the same thing or perform the same causal function;
- nor does functional proximity by itself establish a Sāṃkhya/`adṛṣṭa` genealogy.

The published 1898 paper stabilizes the allocation: karma explains the **particular condition** of rebirth, while upādāna explains the **fact of rebirth in itself**.

Thus similarity of causal location does not entail semantic identity. Function is separately reconstructed.

## 2. PDF pp.50–52: compositional analysis and genealogy are distinct questions, even where Lovejoy eventually hazards genealogy

The Nidāna block is especially useful because it prevents us from making the new model too tidy.

On p.50 Lovejoy works through apparent repetitions inside the dependent-origination formula: collective reference to the skandhas can recur beside more separate references, and the sequence can be tested for logical and temporal structure. On pp.51–52 he engages Senart’s claim that the enumeration may have been abridged or extended and asks what kind of source-history could explain the formula.

The notebook then moves into stronger historical language. Lovejoy asks whether an outside tradition is necessary for some features and explicitly invokes Sāṃkhya-Yoga / Upaniṣadic affinities; on p.52 he treats parts of the formula as potentially secondary in origin and composite in character.

This is important negatively and positively.

### Negative result

We should **not** describe 004 as a notebook that systematically blocks L4 genealogy. It does not. Lovejoy is willing to make historical-dependence and compositional claims.

### Positive result

He gets to those claims through a layered analysis: repetition, logical role, semantic fit, whether a notion has a legitimate function in the Buddhist formula, and comparison with neighboring Indic traditions. The genealogy is an additional proposition generated from accumulated warrants, not simply another name for formal resemblance.

The 004→005 continuity is therefore about **separate proof burdens**, not a uniform anti-genealogical conclusion.

## 3. PDF p.65: usage establishes the semantic relation between attachment and its objects

The direct manuscript image is unusually clear.

Lovejoy quotes/notes the Khandha-Saṃyutta distinction between the `things subject to attachment` and `attachment` itself: the skandhas are the objects of attachment, while passion/desire in connection with them is upādāna. He immediately uses related Saṃyutta passages to note that pleasure in what is subject to attachment produces desire and, from desire, attachment.

This operation is basic but methodologically important:

`same compound / same textual neighborhood`

is not enough. Lovejoy reconstructs the **relation between terms from their use in propositions**.

This is the 004 equivalent of 005’s insistence that a visible rite/material does not define its mechanism. The semantic bearer is not diagnosed from morphology alone; its relational function in context matters.

## 4. PDF pp.65–66: `upādisesa` gives the cleanest lower-level-stands / higher-level-remains-open case

The Sutta-Nipāta sequence creates the strongest parallel to Batch 70.

On p.65 Lovejoy collects three contexts in which `upādisesa`, `saupādisesa` and `anupādisesa` occur. On p.66 he argues that the negative form cannot mean mere physical death or extinction of the present skandhas: to make death itself the Buddhist summum bonum would contradict the practical/soteriological structure of the doctrine. He therefore states that the word in that passage denotes the **moral condition of freedom from attachment**.

The right page then generalizes the three Sutta-Nipāta cases:

- `upādisesa` does not primarily refer to persistence of the five skandhas;
- it refers to an ethical condition;
- positively, it applies to the Anāgāmin, in whom a small residue remains;
- negatively, it applies to the Arahat, before or after physical death.

This is strong L2/L3 confidence built from contextual use.

Yet the later published article explicitly refuses to convert this into total lexical/text-historical certainty. Lovejoy says the competing derivations are both etymologically possible; therefore meaning must be settled by usage rather than etymology. He calls his evidence relevant but not necessarily conclusive. At the end he says the oldest and probably most numerous texts favor the ethical interpretation while other passages support the usual physical one, so the matter **cannot be regarded as finally settled**.

The proof partition is therefore exact:

> **semantic/doctrinal judgment can be strong while etymological and textual genealogy remain open.**

This is perhaps the single best 004 precedent for 005’s `mechanism plausible; genealogy underdetermined` state.

## 5. PDF p.67: textual criticism is a separate evidentiary operation, not an afterthought

The next notebook page changes register explicitly: `MP. Text criticism`.

Lovejoy compares the scope of Chinese versions with the Pāli text and records a Rhys Davids argument about whether an elaborate work could fit into a very small textual compass. The hurried details need conservative transcription, but the operation is visually secure: recension/version extent is being used to decide what textual witness can support the historical reconstruction.

This matters because it supplies the 004 analogue of 005’s reporter/contact control.

### 004

`recension / canonical passage / translation / commentary / manuscript relation`

conditions what lexical or doctrinal inference the text may carry.

### 005

`reporter / date / contact environment / missionary contamination / authority`

conditions what ethnographic or historical inference the report may carry.

In both notebooks the **carrier of the datum is itself part of the evidence**.

---

## 6. Published 1898 makes the proof partition explicit in prose

The JAOS article provides a primary-text endpoint for the notebook operations.

Three passages matter especially.

### A. Usage over etymology

After listing competing derivations of `upādi/upadhi`, Lovejoy says both are etymologically possible; meaning must therefore be settled by examining use rather than by etymological arguments.

This is a textbook separation of L2 semantic warrant from L4 word-history/derivational warrant.

### B. Evidence can be relevant without being conclusive

He introduces the positive `upādisesa` evidence as points that are relevant but not necessarily conclusive. This is explicit proof calibration rather than retrospective reconstruction on our part.

### C. A prevailing semantic judgment can coexist with unresolved contradiction

His final paragraph says the oldest and probably most numerous texts point toward Oldenberg’s interpretation, but contrary passages remain, and the question cannot be regarded as finally settled. Scribal confusion is offered as a possible explanation, not converted into a demonstrated text history.

Thus the published article validates the notebook-level model: Lovejoy is willing to assign different confidence levels to meaning, textual distribution and historical explanation.

---

## 7. The important correction: 004 proof partition is not equivalent to skepticism

A simple `Lovejoy withheld genealogy` story would now be demonstrably false.

In the Nidāna/Sāṃkhya block he sometimes advances strong historical hypotheses. Elsewhere he historicizes karma as a pre-Buddhist moral-causal category and treats the arrangement/application of inherited material as distinctively Buddhist. The point is not that L4 is always held.

The point is:

> **L4 must be argued as L4.**

Semantic equivalence, causal role, textual attestation and historical borrowing are not interchangeable propositions.

This is exactly what makes the comparison with 005 stronger. In 005 Lovejoy likewise sometimes accepts borrowing, survivals or historical layering; what he repeatedly blocks is the shortcut from a lower-level similarity to those higher-level claims.

## 8. Parallel matrix: 004 and 005

| Proof level | 004 textual/philological object | 005 ethnographic/comparative object | Shared question |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 witness | canonical passage, recension, Chinese/Pāli version, commentary, translation | traveller/missionary/official/ethnographic report, reporter, date, contact setting | can this carrier enter the evidence set, and with what weight? |
| L2 classification/meaning | lexical usage, semantic relation among upādāna/upādi/upadhi, object vs attachment | formal class: afterlife, sacrifice, cannibalism, initiation, blood/fetish | what similarity/category is actually established? |
| L3 mechanism/function | causal role in rebirth, ethical vs physical residue, relation to karma/skandhas | provision, propitiation, communion, incorporation, intrinsic efficacy, taboo, status | what relation makes the phenomenon work in this system? |
| L4 history | etymology, scribal history, composition, borrowing, Sāṃkhya dependence, pre-Buddhist inheritance | borrowing/contact, survival, substitution, common origin, developmental sequence | what historical connection or sequence is independently warranted? |

The principal continuity is therefore structural:

> **witness qualification → relation/classification → functional/mechanistic reconstruction → historical genealogy.**

## 9. Operator migration can now be stated without metaphor

Batch 68 first suggested that an operator survives the move from 004 to 005. Batch 73 can now specify it.

### 004 operator

`textual witness`
→ `usage/semantic relation`
→ `doctrinal or causal function`
→ `textual/historical dependence`

### 005 operator

`ethnographic witness`
→ `formal comparative relation`
→ `social/ritual/causal mechanism`
→ `historical origin/development`

The evidence object has changed from **word/passage** to **report/rite**. The middle-level explanatory language has changed from semantics/doctrine to social-religious mechanism. But the proof architecture is recognizably homologous.

This is stronger than saying Lovejoy “moved from philology to anthropology.” What migrated was a way of **preventing a relation established at one analytical level from automatically doing historical work at another**.

## 10. Relation to Batch 72’s Paris control

Batch 72 found the same proof-threshold grammar in Marillier and Hubert–Mauss:

- local survival may warrant a bounded local history without making totemism a universal developmental necessity;
- a shared animistic/magical substrate does not establish real genealogical kinship;
- analogy does not license genealogical grouping;
- logical anteriority is not historical anteriority;
- similar forms can have contrary functions.

Batch 73 changes the chronology of our interpretation. We no longer need to say Paris **created** a four-level method in a previously undifferentiated Lovejoy. 004 shows that a substantial level-separating habit was already active in his Buddhist/philological work.

A safer historical model is:

> **Harvard/Orientalist philological work supplied an existing practice of witness qualification, contextual semantics and separation of function from derivation; the Paris comparative-religion environment then provided a new evidentiary field in which closely analogous proof-threshold problems were explicitly institutionalized and debated. Notebook 005 shows the operator working after the object changes from texts to ethnographic practices.**

This is a much stronger transatlantic account than either `Paris invented Lovejoy’s method` or `Paris was merely topical exposure`.

## 11. What this does to the larger argument

The current strongest historical sequence is now:

`Harvard Indological/source-critical practice`

→ **004:** textual witness / semantic relation / causal function / historical dependence kept distinct

→ `Paris EPHE / Marillier comparative-religion environment`

→ **005:** reporter / class / mechanism / genealogy kept distinct across ethnographic evidence

→ **1898 print:** usage may settle a prevailing semantic judgment while etymology/text history remain open

→ **1902 scale expansion:** resemblance and transmission are treated as distinct historical propositions; the logical role of a category in a configuration is not exhausted by provenance.

The crucial claim is not priority. It is **transportability of an inference discipline across domains**.

---

## 12. Paper-level formulation

A defensible paragraph is now:

> **The methodological continuity between Lovejoy’s Buddhist notebook and his Paris comparative-religion notebook lies less in doctrine than in the partition of proof. In MS38 004 he distinguishes the authority of a textual witness, the meaning of a term in use, the causal job that term performs within Buddhist doctrine, and the separate historical question of derivation or borrowing. His treatment of `upādisesa` is especially revealing: contextual usage can sustain an ethical interpretation even while etymology, scribal history and contradictory passages remain unsettled. MS38 005 transports the same architecture into a different evidentiary world. Travellers and ethnographers replace canonical passages; rites and beliefs replace lexical items; social and magical mechanisms replace semantic functions. Yet the governing rule remains: a relation established at one level is not automatically evidence for a stronger historical relation at the next.**

Compact formulation:

> **004 and 005 change evidence objects, not inference syntax.**

This is our analytical reconstruction, not Lovejoy’s terminology.

## 13. Evidence cautions

1. The four-level matrices are analytical reconstructions; Lovejoy does not name L1–L4.
2. 004 does not consistently suspend genealogy. Pages 51–52 contain comparatively strong compositional/external-tradition hypotheses. This is a feature of the model, not an exception to hide.
3. The strongest direct 004 example is `upādisesa`, where semantic/doctrinal confidence and textual-historical uncertainty visibly coexist.
4. PDF p.67’s general text-critical operation is visually secure; individual hurried references to exact Chinese/Pāli divisions should be transcribed only if needed for publication.
5. The 1898 JAOS article is a secure primary endpoint; chronology of every notebook entry relative to every 1898 article/fascicle remains a separate question.
6. The Paris relation remains reinforcement/translation into a new problem field, not proof that Marillier taught Lovejoy the level distinction from scratch.
7. `operator migration`, `proof partition`, and `inference syntax` are our analytical labels.
