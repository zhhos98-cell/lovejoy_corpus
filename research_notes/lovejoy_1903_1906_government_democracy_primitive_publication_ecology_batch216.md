## Direct-primary political result

Lovejoy contrasts eighteenth-century `Nature` with nineteenth-century `Evolution`.

The former supplies universal/simple norms equally applicable to all peoples and times; the latter renders political institutions variable, contingent and relative to time, place and circumstances.

He then adds `race and nationality` as a second axis and says the conjunction of:

- necessary stages of social evolution; and
- racial/national genius

has destroyed the unity of eighteenth-century `generic Man`, splitting him `longitudinally and cross-wise`.

The two axes do not have identical force:

- stage-of-culture difference is treated as a hard developmental constraint;
- racial/national difference is treated as an independent political factor but Lovejoy explicitly refuses to say institutional mismatch by race `must` follow and warns that the race category is overworked.

The best semantic classification of `race` is therefore a mixed racial-national-historical population category, not a clean modern biological variable.

## Relativity is bounded by higher ends

The essay rejects natural-rights absolutism but retains a supra-institutional evaluative criterion.

Political forms are judged by concrete consequences and general/permanent social interests, including civil liberty, order, culture, civic virtue, national continuity and the elevation of the `standard of civilization`.

Thus the 1903 fork is now resolved as:

> **general evaluative/rational ends + historically variable institutional suitability**.

This is not full normative relativism.

## Political consequence

The essay applies this framework to colonial policy, female suffrage and Black enfranchisement/disfranchisement. Lovejoy says the considerations may incline one to regret the `hasty` enfranchisement of Black Americans after the Civil War and to sympathize with attempts to disfranchise illiterate Black voters, while declining to give a final explicit judgment on the specific cases.

Do not sanitize this into neutral contextualism. At the same time, do not convert the essay into a fully articulated biological race doctrine unsupported by the text.
