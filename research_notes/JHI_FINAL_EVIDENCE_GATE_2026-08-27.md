## 7. 1898 → 1906 direct Buddhist source-practice continuity

The strongest bridge is now Lovejoy's own repeated use of the same Buddhist source.

In 1898 he says that the original signification of Buddhist technical terms should be sought first in the **Sutta Nipāta**, and uses it to adjudicate `upādisesa` and Nirvāṇa.

In April 1906, reviewing Paul Elmer More's *Shelburne Essays*, he again invokes **Sutta Nipāta 1073–1076** to resist a positive/concrete reconstruction of Nirvāṇa. The same review explicitly values `induction of evidence`, inquiry into `historical conditions and antecedents`, `wide-ranging comparisons`, and the correlation of manifestations across heterogeneous literatures.

The Harvard catalogue now adds a non-causal institutional control immediately before the 1898 article: Lanman's 1896–97 advanced Pāli course explicitly taught the `Sutta Nipāta` among the `Sacred Books of Buddhism`. This strengthens the source-environment chronology without proving that Lovejoy was enrolled in that exact course number or that Lanman's course caused Lovejoy's later source practice.

This establishes:

> **documented Harvard Sanskrit/Buddhist-text training inside a curriculum that formally taught the Sutta Nipāta → 1898 source-controlled Buddhist semantics → 1906 reuse of the same canonical source to police a Nirvāṇa interpretation.**

It does not establish continuous annual Buddhist research, use of the same physical edition, exact enrollment in Pāli 5, or Buddhist causation of later history-of-ideas method.

## 8. 1906 as scalar laboratory

1906 is the strongest hinge because direct primaries within months perform different scalar operations.

### Democracy in the Twentieth Century — February 1906

Lovejoy contrasts eighteenth-century `Nature` with nineteenth-century `Evolution`, then says social-evolutionary stages and race/nationality split Enlightenment `generic Man` `longitudinally and cross-wise`.

The axes are unequal:

- `stage of culture` carries the stronger developmental hierarchy;
- `race/national genius` supplies cross-wise population differentiation, but Lovejoy says institutional mismatch need not follow with necessity and warns that the race category is overworked.

Best semantic judgment:

> **mixed racial-national-historical population category with real racialized political consequences, not a cleanly biological doctrine.**

The argument is applied to colonial policy, female suffrage, and Black enfranchisement/disfranchisement. It must not be sanitized into neutral contextualism.

Political relativity is not normative nihilism: Lovejoy retains higher consequential/civilizational criteria such as general welfare, civil liberty, order, culture, civic virtue, and maintenance/elevation of a `standard of civilization`.

Best formula:

> **general evaluative ends + historically differentiated institutional suitability.**

### Kant's Antithesis — April 1906

Lovejoy decomposes the accepted dogmatism/criticism rubric into predecessor doctrine, Kantian divergence, argumentative novelty, and historical discontinuity. Local audit leads him to reject the macro historical contrast.

Operation:

> **macro classification → local proposition audit → macro classification rejected.**

### More review — April 1906

Source-level Buddhist semantic control remains active while Lovejoy explicitly discusses induction, historical antecedents, comparison, and correlation across literatures.

### Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy — July 1906

The paper moves the opposite scalar direction: heterogeneous local ethnographic and lexical claims are decomposed; a recurring impersonal/quasi-mechanical causal relation is abstracted; the abstraction is then reattributed upward to `primitive philosophy`, `savage thought`, and broad populations.

Operation:

> **local plurality → mechanism abstraction → transverse equivalence → collective cognitive reaggregation.**

Therefore decomposition and totalization are contemporaneous possibilities, not chronological ideological stages.
