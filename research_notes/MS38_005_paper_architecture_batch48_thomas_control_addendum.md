## Core upgrade

N. W. Thomas is now more than a later methodological parallel. Two distinct relations are directly controlled:

1. **same-teacher training relation**: modern biographical scholarship based on Thomas materials identifies him as an EPHE student of `primitive religion` under Léon Marillier after leaving Cambridge in 1894; after two years of supervised study he submitted the Welsh animal-cult thesis in 1897, which was then published in *Revue de l'histoire des religions* 38 (1898), 295–347;
2. **actor-near workshop description**: Thomas himself, writing in *Folklore* 7 (1896), describes Marillier's EPHE pedagogy as `work by the pupils, directed by the lecturer` and gives a concrete procedure of student papers read in class, commented on by Marillier and discussed by other pupils.

This materially strengthens the `workshop/shared problem field` model without turning it into a claim that Marillier transmitted one fixed doctrine.

## 1. Thomas 1896 — direct description of the EPHE research apparatus

Thomas's short correspondence piece, `The École Pratique des hautes Études`, *Folklore* 7 (1896), 200–201, is a direct near-contemporary witness.

He describes the religious section as including a course directed by Marillier on `the religions of savage nations`, then says:

> `The idea of the School is "work by the pupils, directed by the lecturer."`

He gives the practice behind the formula:

- pupils could be asked to write an essay on a specific point;
- the essay would be read at the lecture;
- Marillier would comment on it;
- the other pupils could discuss it `at will`.

Thomas adds, from personal experience, that Marillier was exceptionally lucid, painstaking and willing to assist pupils.

The second page describes the formal student pipeline:

- auditors could attend freely;
- candidates for the title `élève` followed a course for one year so the professor could assess their work;
- after nomination they followed another year;
- afterward they could submit a thesis on `some critical or historical question`;
- an accepted thesis yielded the diploma and could be published under school auspices.

This is unusually strong actor-near evidence for a research culture organized around **directed student investigation, criticism, discussion, and historical/critical problem-solving**, not passive lecture reception.

## 2. EPHE's own 1894–96 records specify the problem field Thomas was describing

The official EPHE reports independently control Marillier's teaching content.

### 1894–95

Marillier's `Religions des peuples non civilisés` includes:

- critical examination of flood myths across Inuit, North American, Mexican/Central American and other fields;
- classification of different myth types;
- explicit control for the influence of Biblical traditions imported by missionaries;
- comparative analysis of marriage rites in Polynesia and Melanesia.

Thus contact/contamination and formal classification were already part of the course apparatus.

### 1895–96

The programme lists:

- flood myths across Oceania and Asia;
- marriage rites in Australia and the Indian Archipelago.

The year-end report says Marillier's new research on Australian family structure and marriage rites contradicted, on several points, Morgan, Fison and Howitt's collective-marriage theory.

The important institutional fact is therefore not merely `Marillier taught primitive religion`. The course repeatedly treated comparative categories as **criticizable propositions** tested against heterogeneous evidence.

## 3. Thomas's diploma relation is now secure

Paul Basu's biographical reconstruction states that Thomas enrolled at the EPHE after Cambridge, studied `primitive religion` under Marillier, and after two years of supervised study combined Welsh folklore with anthropological theory in a diploma thesis on the survival of animal cult in Wales.

Thomas's 1897 diploma thesis was subsequently published, with a slightly modified title, as:

N. W. Thomas, `La survivance du culte totémique des animaux et les rites agraires dans le Pays de Galles`, *Revue de l'histoire des religions* 38 (1898), 295–347.

The EPHE's later chronological diploma table independently lists the 1897 project `Survivance du culte des animaux dans le pays de Galles`; its rendered text gives the student name as `Albert Thomas`, evidently an erroneous historical/index/OCR form when set against Thomas's own bibliography and modern archival biography. Do not use that name form as an identity claim.

Therefore:

> **THOMAS = SAME-TEACHER / SAME-INSTITUTION STUDENT PRODUCT — SECURE.**

What remains open is not the relation but the exact method inside the 1898 published product.

## 5. Revised historiographical language

Avoid:

> `Marillier taught a doctrine that Lovejoy adopted.`

Prefer:

> **The EPHE evidence now documents a directed research apparatus rather than a stable doctrinal school. Thomas himself described Marillier's course as pupil work under lecturer direction, with student essays subjected to teacher commentary and peer discussion, while the formal diploma demanded a critical or historical problem. Across Söderblom, Thomas and Lovejoy, what recurs is therefore better described as a revisable research grammar: separate formal resemblance from origin and operative meaning; test contact or borrowing before genealogical inference; and make inherited classifications answerable to negative cases, provenance and historical change.**

`research grammar`, `practice of redivision`, and `shared problem field` remain project terms, not actor-native labels.
