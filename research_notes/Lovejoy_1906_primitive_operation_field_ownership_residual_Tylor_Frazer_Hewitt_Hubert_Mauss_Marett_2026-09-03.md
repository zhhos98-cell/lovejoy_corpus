# Lovejoy 1906 `primitive` — field-owned operation grammar and the residual after Tylor / Frazer / Marett / Hewitt / Hubert–Mauss

Date: 2026-09-03  
Status: **SOURCE-OWNERSHIP RECUT / 1906 LOVEJOY DIRECT-PRIMARY CONCORDANCE + PRE-1906 FIELD CONTROLS / ORIGINALITY CLAIM NARROWED**

## Core result

The geometric/technical model recently recovered for `primitive` — internal historical layering plus transverse comparison across heterogeneous populations — should **not** be assigned to Lovejoy as an invention.

Major constituent operations were already field-available before 1906:

- **Tylor:** older/lower cultural forms survive inside later/higher living cultures;
- **Frazer:** heterogeneous magical practices can be decomposed into cross-cutting causal laws/mechanisms (similarity and contact/contagion), and magic can be reconstructed as an implicit system of natural law;
- **Marett:** a pre-animistic field of impersonal/supernormal power may be prior to or wider than animism;
- **Hewitt:** `orenda` is a widely distributed mystic potency/efficient cause and is treated as a central category of `savage` philosophy;
- **Hubert–Mauss:** mana / orenda / related power notions can be correlated across regions and languages as a general magical/causal category, including Melanesian `mana`, Huron `orenda`, ancient Indian neuter `brahman`, and other analogues.

Thus:

`VERTICAL INTERNAL STRATIGRAPHY`
+
`HORIZONTAL MECHANISM ABSTRACTION`
+
`IMPERSONAL POWER COMPARISON`

are **field-owned / field-available operators** by 1906.

The Lovejoy-specific documentary residual is narrower:

> **Lovejoy selects and recombines these available operations in order to relocate the impersonal-power dossier from a primarily supernatural / emotional / magic-religion classification problem into a theory of causation and efficacy; he then ranks that causal category above personality and reattributes it as the architectonic `fundamental concept of primitive philosophy`.**

Even this residual is not an originality claim for each ingredient. It is a claim about **selection, reassignment, ranking, scale and epistemic address**.

## I. Tylor — vertical internal stratigraphy is upstream

Primary control:

Edward B. Tylor, *Primitive Culture* (1871), especially Chapters I and III.

Tylor defines `survivals` as customs, processes and opinions carried by habit into a new state of society different from their original home, remaining as evidence of an older condition of culture from which a newer one evolved.

He later describes survivals as `fragments of a dead lower culture embedded in a living higher one`.

The operation is already:

`LIVING / LATER CULTURE`
contains
`OLDER / LOWER CULTURAL STRATUM`.

Therefore the vertical geometry

`ONE PRESENT OBJECT CAN CONTAIN MULTIPLE HISTORICAL AGES`

is not Lovejoy-owned.

This is a direct upstream control for notebook uses such as `Greek primitive`, older non-moral structures beneath later interpretation, and survivant primitive relations inside complex traditions.

Public direct text:

- Wikisource, *Primitive Culture*, ch.1;
- Wikisource, ch.3.

Attribution rule:

`LOVEJOY INTERNAL STRATIFICATION`
!=
`LOVEJOY INVENTED SURVIVAL / EMBEDDED LOWER CULTURE`.

## II. Frazer — horizontal causal-mechanism abstraction is upstream

Primary control:

James George Frazer, *The Golden Bough*, `Sympathetic Magic`.

Frazer explicitly analyzes heterogeneous magical practices into two cross-cutting principles:

- Law of Similarity;
- Law of Contact / Contagion.

He then reconstructs magic as a `spurious system of natural law` and distinguishes theoretical from practical magic.

This already performs:

`MANY LOCAL PRACTICES`
→ `ABSTRACT CAUSAL PRINCIPLE`
→ `CROSS-CASE COMPARATIVE TYPE`.

Frazer further says the magician implicitly assumes universal natural-law regularity even though the abstract theory is supplied by the analyst.

Therefore both:

- horizontal mechanism abstraction;
- analyst reconstruction of an implicit actor logic;

are field-available before Lovejoy.

This matters especially because Lovejoy 1906 directly rejects **Frazer's boundary**, not the entire causal-law grammar.

Lovejoy refuses Frazer's sharp allocation:

`MECHANICAL CAUSATION -> MAGIC`

versus

`PERSONAL / SPIRITUAL RELATION -> RELIGION`.

The Lovejoy delta is consequently not `magic has law-like causal logic`.

It is the reassignment of quasi-mechanical efficacy **across the magic/religion boundary**.

## III. Marett 1900 — pre-animistic impersonal-power field is upstream

Primary bibliographic/direct-volume control:

R. R. Marett, `Pre-Animistic Religion`, *Folklore* 11.2 (June 1900): 162–184.

Marett challenges the sufficiency/priority of animism and opens a pre-animistic field in which `supernaturalism` / awe / supernormal force need not yet be resolved into personal spirits.

The exact later mana formulation develops further, but the pre-1906 problem is already present:

`PERSONAL SPIRITS / ANIMISM`
!=
`EARLIER OR WIDER IMPERSONAL / SUPERNORMAL FIELD`.

Lovejoy 1906 directly takes Marett as a target.

His disagreement is not whether an impersonal pre-animistic field exists.

It is over **what relation should define it**.

Marett's emphasis:

`SUPERNORMAL / AWE / EMOTIONAL-VALUATIONAL EXPERIENCE`.

Lovejoy's reallocation:

`EFFICACY / POWER / PRODUCTIVITY / CAUSAL CONTROL`.

Thus Marett owns much of the preanimistic problem field; Lovejoy changes its diagnostic relation.

## IV. Hewitt 1902 — mystic potency as causal category is upstream

Primary bibliographic control:

J. N. B. Hewitt, `Orenda and a Definition of Religion`, *American Anthropologist* n.s. 4.1 (1902): 33–46.

Hewitt's `orenda` is already:

- mystic potency / power;
- attached to beings and phenomena;
- an efficient cause of phenomena;
- broadly distributed in the world;
- treated in evolutionist language as a central category of `savage` philosophy/religion.

Thus Lovejoy does not own the move:

`NATIVE POWER TERM`
→ `GENERAL CAUSAL POTENCY`
→ `BROAD SAVAGE-PHILOSOPHY CLAIM`.

Hewitt is especially important because Lovejoy's later North-American lexical zone sits inside the exact American ethnological field where `orenda`, `wakonda` and `manitou` were being made into second-order comparative terms.

## V. Hubert–Mauss 1902–03 — cross-cultural impersonal-power reaggregation is directly upstream

Primary control:

Henri Hubert and Marcel Mauss, `Esquisse d'une théorie générale de la magie`, *L'Année sociologique* 7 (1902–03).

Their mana section explicitly correlates power notions across cultural and linguistic fields.

Direct examples include:

- Melanesian `mana`;
- Huron/Iroquois `orenda` via Hewitt;
- ancient Indian neuter `brahman`;
- Assyrian `mâmit`;
- further North-American / Malagasy / magical analogues.

They describe mana as capable of appearing as:

- quality;
- substance/essence;
- force/activity;
- transmissible/contagious potency;
- a causal medium.

Their treatment of orenda explicitly compares it to mana and uses Hewitt's material to analyze causality.

Therefore the operation

`MANA ~ ORENDA ~ OTHER POWER TERMS`
→ `GENERAL CROSS-CULTURAL POWER / CAUSAL CATEGORY`

is already direct and pre-1906.

This removes a major possible Lovejoy priority claim.

## VII. Lovejoy's direct-primary 1906 architecture

Authority:

`research_notes/lovejoy_as_orientalist_web_sweep_batch12_1906_primary_concordance.md`.

- `more fundamental than personality`;
- extraordinarily pervasive / near-ubiquitous;
- central to the reconstructed `primitive philosophy`.

This is the direct Lovejoy state that must now be compared with the upstream field, rather than treated in isolation.

## VIII. Residual 1 — Lovejoy changes the epistemic address of the power dossier

The clearest Lovejoy-specific reallocation is not discovery of impersonal power.

It is **where he places the explanatory center**.

### Marett-like address

`SUPERNORMAL / AWE / EMOTION / RUDIMENTARY RELIGION`.

### Hubert–Mauss address

`MAGIC / COLLECTIVE FORCE / SACRED-SOCIAL RELATION / GENERAL THEORY OF MAGIC`.

### Frazer address

`IMPLICIT NATURAL LAW OF MAGIC`, separated from personal religion.

### Lovejoy 1906 address

`CAUSATION / EFFICACY`
→ crosses magic/religion classification
→ `FUNDAMENTAL CONCEPT OF PRIMITIVE PHILOSOPHY`.

Lovejoy explicitly objects that Marett puts the emphasis on the wrong side: the key is not unusualness/supernormality but efficacy/power/productivity and a force operating according to intelligible, controllable relations.

The historically distinctive object is therefore **reassignment of the same power-material to a different explanatory question**.

This matches the project's broader rule:

`SAME EVIDENCE FIELD != SAME HISTORICAL OBJECT`.

## XI. Residual 4 — a potentially important selection boundary around prestigious textual cultures

Hubert–Mauss directly includes **ancient Indian neuter `brahman`** in the same comparative magical-power series as mana/orenda.

Lovejoy's direct-primary concordance, by contrast, records the 1906 lexical demonstration as organized through:

- North American ethnographic terms;
- Oceanic terms;
- Bantu/African terms.

No Indian/Vedic/Sanskrit branch appears in that direct concordance.

A modern history of `mana` explicitly characterizes Lovejoy as correlating mana/orenda/manitou/etc. while **not including analogous terms drawn from more prestigious textual cultures**, in contrast with Hubert–Mauss.

Current evidence ceiling:

> **This is a real candidate selection boundary, but a full-text literal-negative (`brahman / Vedic / Sanskrit / India` absent from all 26 pages) has not yet been independently closed in the present pass.**

Therefore use:

`LOVEJOY'S POSITIVE PROOF ARCHITECTURE SELECTS ETHNOGRAPHIC PRIMITIVE FIELDS WHERE HUBERT–MAUSS'S COMPARISON WAS WIDER`.

Do not yet write:

`LOVEJOY DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED BRAHMAN BECAUSE INDIA WAS TOO PRESTIGIOUS / CLASSICAL`.

Motivation is unproved.

### Why this target matters

Lovejoy had real Sanskrit/Pāli training and separately treated Indian textual philosophy as technical philosophy.

If the 1906 primitive-power category systematically stops at the boundary of textual Orientalist/philosophical materials that an upstream comparative source had included, the issue becomes:

> **not merely which mechanism is `primitive`, but which evidentiary populations are allowed to bear the predicate `primitive philosophy`.**

This is potentially a major bridge to the project's claim that Oriental studies may function as a **model/boundary condition**, not simply an opponent.

It requires direct full-text negative closure before promotion.

## XIII. Consequence for the larger `primitive` project

The source-ownership recut strengthens a non-biographical thesis.

`Primitive` appears increasingly as a shared technical operator capable of:

- locating old strata inside later forms;
- extracting mechanisms from heterogeneous practices;
- comparing those mechanisms across unrelated histories;
- assigning equivalence across living populations;
- reattributing an analyst's abstraction as actor-level cognition/philosophy.

But different scholars can assign the same evidence to different objects:

- survival;
- magic;
- religion;
- emotion/awe;
- social/sacred force;
- causation;
- philosophy.

The historical problem is therefore not only `who first discovered the primitive layer?`

It is:

> **WHO OWNS WHICH RELATION, AND UNDER WHICH DISCIPLINARY QUESTION DOES THE SAME MATERIAL BECOME `PRIMITIVE`?**

This is precisely a jurisdictional history of comparative classification.
