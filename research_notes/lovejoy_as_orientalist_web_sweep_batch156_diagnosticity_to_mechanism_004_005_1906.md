## Core result

The 004 → 005 → 1906 sequence can now be specified as a change in **what diagnosticity is used to identify**.

In 004, Lovejoy's explicit addendum asks which doctrinal feature has enough discriminatory power to identify a **genuinely Sāṁkhyan system/witness**. Generic eternity is too weak; the system-defining relation is the separateness of Self and World / `thorough-going dualism`.

In 005, the same kind of classificatory problem is repeatedly applied not to a philosophical system but to a **ritual mechanism**. Outward form — blood, killing, eating, ceremoniality, shared food, foundation deposit, body part, sacred animal — is repeatedly declared non-diagnostic. Lovejoy asks what causal bearer actually makes the case work:

- personal divine will;
- dangerous or ancestral spirit;
- provision/communication for the dead;
- propitiation;
- incorporation/social status;
- indwelling spirit;
- intrinsic/direct/transferable efficacy;
- sympathetic relation;
- sacramental union.

By 1906 the diagnostic variable itself becomes the published theoretical object. Lovejoy proposes a `category more fundamental than personality`: **impersonal, quasi-mechanical, diffusive and transferable efficacy**. The article then re-reads terms translated as `god` or `spirit` by asking whether their operative property is actually power/efficacy.

The strongest developmental formulation is therefore:

> **004: diagnose the system by a discriminating doctrinal relation → 005: diagnose the rite by a discriminating causal bearer → 1906: abstract one recurrent causal bearer into a cross-cutting category.**

This is more specific than saying that Lovejoy moved from philology to anthropology or that the same four-level proof ladder simply migrated intact. What changes is the **classification target** and then the **ontological level of the discriminant**.

No new GLA/GAL/COV identifier is created in this batch.

## 1. 004: diagnosticity is used for system identification

Batch153 isolated the manuscript hinge after Rhys Davids:

`To R.D.'s remark it should be added...`

The problem is historical-systemic. A Buddhist passage has been proposed as evidence for early Sāṁkhya. Lovejoy's objection is not just that resemblance is weak. He asks which feature should identify Sāṁkhya in the first place.

- Eternity of Matter and Self is too generic.
- The doctrine's release depends more centrally on the **separateness of Self and World**.
- Therefore a textual witness expressing strong soul/world or soul/body separateness has greater diagnostic value.

The logic is classificatory:

> **choose the feature that best discriminates the target class from neighboring classes.**

At this stage the target class is a philosophical system/doctrine.

## 2. 005 repeatedly makes outward form lose diagnostic force

The clean 005 transcription makes the same problem recur with rituals.

### Sacrifice and syncretism — pp.64–65

Magical sacrifice can act directly on forces of nature rather than on a god's will. Several motives may be mixed, and `delicate analysis` is needed to separate ritual elements. Syncretism can combine sacrifices originally distinct in character.

Thus `sacrifice` as a visible form is not yet the explanatory class.

### Human sacrifice — p.76 onward

The same visible killing can be:

- provision for the dead;
- communication/message-sending;
- protective or magical action;
- propitiation;
- later sacramental interpretation.

The visible victim does not diagnose the mechanism.

### Foundation blood — p.84

Human/animal remains, flesh, bones or blood in a foundation do not prove `union through blood`. They may work through independent protective efficacy.

### Blood/body parts — pp.85–87

Blood can be curative, rejuvenating, protective or transferable. Eyes, bones and other parts can transmit qualities. These effects need not be communion and need not require an indwelling spirit.

### Fetish versus charm/amulet — pp.88–90

This is perhaps the cleanest diagnostic pair in 005:

- **fetish:** efficacy depends on an inhabiting/present spirit;
- **charm/amulet:** efficacy may be intrinsic to the object, with no spiritualistic idea required.

Two objects may look equally `magical` from outside, but the causal bearer differs.

### Agrarian sacrifice — p.105

A plant-soul sacrifice can be `quasi-mechanical and magical rather than sacramental` because the act is not yet addressed to a personal plant deity.

### Cannibalism — pp.106–119

Ceremoniality, publicity and repetition do not make anthropophagy sacramental. Eating body parts for intelligence, courage/life or sexual power is assigned primarily to sympathetic/transferable efficacy rather than communion.

Across these blocks, the repeated question is:

> **what relation or bearer has enough causal specificity to distinguish this case from other outwardly similar cases?**

## 3. The discriminating variable changes from morphology to causal bearer

This produces a sharper account of the 005 method.

The notebook is not simply generating finer labels for ritual forms. It is repeatedly **changing the variable on which classification is based**.

Rejected or downgraded classificatory variables include:

- killing versus non-killing;
- human versus animal victim;
- blood present versus absent;
- eating versus not eating;
- public/ceremonial versus private;
- sacred animal versus ordinary animal;
- foundation deposit versus funerary deposit;
- body part versus whole body.

Preferred discriminating variables increasingly include:

- personal agency versus impersonal efficacy;
- spirit-mediated versus intrinsic efficacy;
- transaction/propitiation versus communion;
- provision/communication versus offering;
- social incorporation versus sacrifice;
- direct transfer of qualities versus mystical union.

The analytical shift can therefore be stated:

> **from classifying by visible rite morphology to classifying by causal architecture.**

This does not mean Lovejoy ignores form. Form remains evidence, but it loses the right to define the genus by itself.

## 4. This is the 005 analogue of the 004 `dualism` move

The 004 and 005 operations are homologous without being identical.

### 004

Low-discrimination feature:
`eternity of Matter and Self`.

Higher-discrimination feature:
`separateness of Self and World` / dualistic relation.

Target:
identify a genuinely Sāṁkhyan doctrine/witness.

### 005

Low-discrimination feature:
`blood`, `killing`, `eating`, `ceremoniality`, `shared meal`, `foundation victim`.

Higher-discrimination feature:
`what causes the expected effect and through what relation?`

Target:
identify the mechanism of the rite/practice.

Thus the continuity is not a repeated doctrine. It is a repeated **search for a feature with enough discriminatory power to bear the classification being made**.

The change in target matters. In 004 the diagnostic feature helps test historical-system identity; in 005 it helps separate current causal mechanisms before historical genealogy is attempted.

## 5. 005 inserts mechanism between form and genealogy

Batch73 modeled both notebooks as proof ladders. The post-Batch153 refinement is that 005 makes the middle level unusually productive.

A typical 005 sequence is:

`outward practice / report`  
→ `reject form as sufficient classifier`  
→ `identify causal bearer/mechanism`  
→ `only then ask origin, survival, borrowing, substitution, or later reinterpretation`.

This explains why the notebook can simultaneously:

- accept that a rite may historically derive from another practice;
- reject the claim that its present mechanism is the same;
- allow several mechanisms to become syncretically combined;
- allow the same mechanism to recur across historically unrelated rites.

The mechanism level therefore creates a two-way freedom:

> **same history does not guarantee same current function; same mechanism does not guarantee same historical origin.**

This is one of the strongest bridges between 005 and the later history-of-ideas problem of provenance versus configuration.

## 6. 1906 promotes one diagnostic mechanism into a fundamental category

The full 1906 primary text gives the next transformation.

Lovejoy opens by attacking anthropomorphic/animistic master theories. He does not deny persons/spirits; he denies that personality is the most fundamental explanatory category for the whole field.

At p.359 he explicitly posits a category **more fundamental than personality**: an impersonal and quasi-mechanical conception of causation.

Its diagnostic properties include:

- energy/power diffused through persons and things;
- unequal localization;
- transfer by contact;
- direct efficacy without volition;
- regulation, insulation and manipulation;
- practical rites oriented to controlling power rather than persuading a person.

This is exactly the kind of causal bearer that 005 had been isolating locally in blood, charms, foundations, agriculture and body-part transfer.

The publication therefore performs an abstraction:

`many local mechanism distinctions`  
→ `identify a recurrent causal family`  
→ `treat that family as a fundamental comparative category`.

This is not a claim that every 005 example generated the paper or that the notebook is a literal draft. It is a proposition-level developmental fit between the notebook's discriminations and the published category.

## 7. Fetish versus amulet becomes a miniature of the 1906 argument

The 005 fetish/charm distinction deserves special status because it compresses the later problem into a local pair.

### Fetish

The object's efficacy is explained by a spirit residing in or associated with it.

### Charm / amulet

The object can work without a spiritualistic hypothesis; efficacy belongs to the object itself.

The visible outcome — an efficacious sacred/magical object — is similar. The causal bearer differs.

The 1906 article generalizes this exact kind of question. Words and objects commonly rendered through `god`, `spirit`, or personal agency are re-read to ask whether the operative concept is instead **power/property/efficacy**.

Thus the shift can be written:

> **005 local diagnostic pair: spirit-mediated vs intrinsic efficacy → 1906 comparative category: personality vs impersonal quasi-mechanical power.**

This is stronger than a lexical continuity around `quasi-mechanical`; it is continuity in what the distinction is used to explain.

## 8. Why `Primitive Energetics` is not another single-origin theory

Batch149 already showed that Steinmetz could be locally plural while retaining a universal hierarchy of origins. The present diagnostic analysis clarifies how Lovejoy's later category differs in structure from such a genealogy.

A causal mechanism can recur across:

- blood rites;
- body-part eating;
- charms;
- foundation practices;
- agrarian/fecundative rites;
- taboo/contact phenomena;
- objects of unusual power.

But recurrence of the mechanism does **not** imply that these institutions descend from one original rite.

The category is cross-cutting:

> **one causal architecture can be instantiated by historically heterogeneous practices.**

That is precisely why mechanism classification can become theoretically general without becoming a universal origin story.

This also helps connect the 1906 paper to the proof discipline of 004/1902: logical or causal comparability can be retained independently of historical identity.

## 9. A three-stage diagnostic transformation

| Stage | Low-value / non-diagnostic surface | Higher-value discriminant | Classification target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 004 Sāṁkhya block | generic eternity / broad doctrinal resemblance | separateness of Self and World / system-defining dualism | philosophical-system identity / textual witness |
| 005 comparative religion | killing, blood, food, cannibalism, ceremoniality, foundation deposit | causal bearer: spirit, intrinsic efficacy, provision, propitiation, incorporation, communion, transfer | ritual mechanism/function |
| 1906 Monist | conventional labels `god`, `spirit`, `magic`, `religion`; heterogeneous practices | impersonal, diffusive, transferable, quasi-mechanical efficacy versus personal agency | fundamental comparative causal category |

The progression is not from simple to sophisticated ideas. It is a shift in analytical scale:

`diagnostic property of one system`  
→ `diagnostic mechanism of individual cases`  
→ `cross-case category defined by causal mechanism`.

## 10. Relation to Marillier

The Marillier evidence remains essential but should not be overdrawn.

His 1897–98 primary texts already insist that outward resemblance does not establish common origin and that multiple analogous practices may belong to different causal classes. His 1898 blood/foundation discussions explicitly distinguish direct magical efficacy, guardian-spirit mechanisms, propitiation and communion.

Lovejoy is independently documented as actively participating in Marillier's 1898–99 EPHE conferences, and 005 occupies the same source/problem field.

Thus Paris supplies a historically secure environment in which **causal discrimination of outwardly similar rites** was already an explicit research practice.

But the 004 result prevents a one-directional origin claim. Lovejoy already had a pre-Paris/Harvard-side habit of asking which feature is diagnostically probative in a textual/doctrinal comparison.

The best formulation is therefore:

> **Paris did not introduce diagnostic comparison ex nihilo; it placed an existing proof habit into a domain where the decisive discriminant increasingly became causal mechanism.**

## 11. Consequence for the larger Lovejoy argument

This sequence gives a more concrete prehistory of later category work.

The archive does not show a young Lovejoy already possessing `unit-ideas`. It shows him repeatedly learning to distrust classes defined by visible resemblance and to ask what underlying relation actually does the explanatory work.

Across different evidence domains:

- 004: a philosophical system is not identified by any shared proposition;
- 005: a religious rite is not identified by any shared morphology;
- 1906: a religious worldview is not adequately classified by conventional personal/spiritual labels.

The recurring move is:

> **replace a surface class with a relation that has greater explanatory discrimination.**

That is a defensible methodological continuity without importing mature Lovejoyan vocabulary backward.

---

## 12. Strongest paper formulation

> **The methodological bridge between Lovejoy's Buddhist and comparative-religion notebooks can be stated more narrowly than a general habit of comparison. In the Sāṁkhya notebook, after copying Rhys Davids's source criticism, Lovejoy asks what feature would actually discriminate a Sāṁkhyan doctrine and substitutes dualistic separateness for generic eternity. In the Paris notebook the same diagnostic problem is transferred from doctrines to rites. Blood, killing, eating, ceremoniality, and foundation deposits repeatedly lose classificatory force; what matters is whether the effect is carried by a spirit, by direct transferable efficacy, by propitiatory transaction, by provision for the dead, by incorporation, or by sacramental union. The 1906 paper then raises one of these recurrent discriminants to the level of a fundamental category: impersonal, diffusive, quasi-mechanical power. The development is therefore not simply from philology to anthropology, but from identifying system-defining properties to identifying mechanism-defining causal relations and finally to abstracting a cross-cutting causal category.**
