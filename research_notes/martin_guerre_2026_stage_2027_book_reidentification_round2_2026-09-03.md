# Martin Guerre — 2026 stage / 2027 book reidentification round

Date: 2026-09-03  
Status: **TERMINAL COMPARATIVE CONTROL / BOOK-STAGE CONVERGENCE / NON-GENEALOGICAL**  
Scope: second and final round returning the twenty-first-century afterlife to two authoritative carriers now being renewed almost simultaneously: the Old Vic's October 2026 stage version and Harvard University Press's 2027 new edition of Natalie Zemon Davis's *The Return of Martin Guerre*. The central question is how a work is declared `the same` while its paratext, form, epistemic emphases, and sometimes textual/musical content change.

## Thesis

The 2026–27 moment creates an unusually clean double reidentification event.

- **Stage:** the Old Vic preserves the title, creators, story lineage, and portions of the score while openly advertising new music, new lyrics, new orchestrations, new staging, and `a whole new identity`.
- **Book:** Harvard University Press preserves Davis's authorship and classic-work status while issuing the first new edition in more than forty years, with a new foreword by Jill Lepore and a new market-facing description.

These carriers solve `same-work identity` in opposite ways. The theatre claims continuity **through extensive revision**; the press claims continuity **through textual authority and paratextual renewal**. Both are therefore useful controls on the historical case itself: sameness is not secured by unchanged substance but by a managed bundle of title, authorship, lineage, recognized content, institutional custody, and audience/reader recognition.

## 1. The stage openly problematizes its own identity

The Old Vic describes *Martin Guerre* (1–31 October 2026) as an `all-new version`, `reimagined for the London stage` with new music, new lyrics, new folk-inflected orchestrations, and in-the-round staging. Its publicity line — `Back, but with a whole new identity` — makes work identity reflexive rather than incidental.

The creators' own 2026 publicity sharpens the epistemic framing: Boublil and Schönberg describe the story as lies and passionate love versus truth, or `perhaps more importantly what we each perceive to be true`. This is actor/publicity vocabulary and may safely be used as evidence that the revived work is being framed around the distinction between truth and perceived truth before opening.

## 2. The public cast list itself produces an unresolved identity signal

As of 3 September 2026, the Old Vic's public cast list gives **both Alex James-Hatton and Shane O'Riordan the role label `Martin`**, while no separate actor is publicly listed as `Arnaud`. Independent theatre listings reproduce this. One report says the two actors are `sharing the role of Martin`.

This is highly suggestive but not yet interpretable with confidence. Possible explanations include alternating performance, division of the two Martin-functions between actors, deliberate spoiler/identity management, or another production convention. Therefore:

- **secure fact:** two actors are publicly credited as `Martin`; `Arnaud` is absent as a separate named cast role;
- **not yet secure:** that the production intentionally uses billing to collapse true and false Martin;
- **October check:** programme, casting schedule, script/score, and first performance must adjudicate the function.

This is worth preserving because the **paratext can itself participate in identification**: before an audience sees the performance, cast metadata tells them which names correspond to which bodies.

## 3. The 2027 Davis edition is a different kind of return

Harvard University Press's 2027 edition is described in current bibliographic/retail metadata as the first new edition of *The Return of Martin Guerre* in over forty years. The US record gives a publication date of 13 April 2027, 240 pages, and a new foreword by Jill Lepore. The 1983/84 JSTOR carrier is 176 pages. Page-count difference alone does **not** establish revision of Davis's body text: typesetting, foreword, front/back matter, bibliography, or other edition design can account for it. Direct collation must wait for the new edition.

`same author + same title + same canonical work + same publisher lineage + new foreword + new edition packaging`.

This differs sharply from the musical, where substantial internal rewriting is already declared.

## 4. Paratextual hardening: Davis's warrant becomes story premise

The most consequential book-side finding is epistemic rather than bibliographic. Current 2027 promotional copy asks why Bertrande `play[ed] along despite knowing the truth`. This presents her knowledge as a narrative given.

That is stronger than Davis's own methodological formulation. In her 1988 defense `On the Lame`, Davis puts `knew` in quotation marks, says the degree of Bertrande's conscious accommodation is hidden from us, and constructs a cumulative evidentiary argument for collaboration. Barnden 2024 likewise characterizes the knowing/complicit Bertrande as Davis's persuasive but unprovable argument/speculation.

Therefore there is a genuine **warrant compression** at the level of twenty-first-century book paratext:

`Davis 1988: private mental state hidden → cumulative evidence → plausible/strong collaboration claim`

becomes

`2027 sales copy: Bertrande knew the truth`.

This does not mean the 2027 edition's scholarly text or Lepore's foreword makes the same epistemic upgrade. Only the market-facing description is currently observable. The distinction must be retained.

## 5. Inverse movement: book paratext closes while stage paratext reopens

This produces the cleanest 2026–27 comparison.

### Book paratext

`Bertrande knows` is hardened into premise.

### Stage paratext

truth is explicitly placed beside `what we each perceive to be true`; the Old Vic tells audiences `You think you know Martin Guerre? Think again`, and says Bertrande's `choices` are at the emotional heart of the show without stating in the official guide exactly when or how she knows the imposture.

So the two authoritative re-releases may be moving in opposite epistemic directions:

`new-edition marketing: uncertainty → compressed certainty`

`new-stage marketing: inherited story knowledge → renewed perceptual uncertainty`.

This is not yet a claim about the performed 2026 script. It is already a claim about two **paratextual regimes of knowledge**.

## 6. Jill Lepore as foreword writer: relevant adjacency, not yet content evidence

The 2027 edition names Jill Lepore as foreword writer. In August 2026, Lepore published `Are You a Human?` in *The New Yorker*, examining proof-of-humanity, biometric identity, bots, and the contemporary demand to prove that an online agent is human. This creates an unusually relevant intellectual adjacency to the Martin Guerre afterlife and to the verification-regime sidecars in this repository.

However, the foreword is not yet available in the sources checked here. We therefore must **not** claim that Lepore interprets Martin Guerre through proof-of-humanity or AI. The only secure proposition is that a historian currently writing publicly about contemporary human verification has been selected to frame the first new edition of Davis's classic identity/imposture microhistory in more than forty years.

## 7. Same-person / same-work / same-book matrix

| Object | Continuity claim | What changes? | Authority that stabilizes sameness | Main epistemic risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Martin Guerre, 1560 dispute | this claimant is the departed Martin | body, age, experience, behavior | witnesses + court | recognition may misbind claimant to remembered person |
| Davis book, 2027 new edition | this is still Davis's *The Return of Martin Guerre* | foreword, packaging, possibly edition matter; body-text changes unverified | author/title/publisher/canon/bibliographic edition relation | paratext may harden historically argued claims |
| musical, 2026 Old Vic | this is still Boublil–Schönberg's *Martin Guerre* | music, lyrics, orchestration, staging, additional material | title, creators, retained lineage/material, institution, audience recognition | revision may alter the knowledge architecture while continuity branding conceals degree of change |

The general control is:

> **Identity over time is not supplied by invariant content. It is produced by authorized continuity rules governing which changes count as compatible with sameness.**

This holds as an analytical comparison across person, book, and performance, not as a claim that those domains are historically identical.

## 8. Relation to Davis's original film/book problem

The 1983 book already began from a carrier transition. Davis said film created a historical laboratory that generated possibilities rather than proofs, while its narrative simplifications also prompted her to return to archives and lawbooks. Forty-plus years later the loop has become reflexive:

`film → book`

→ `book becomes historiographical classic`

→ `musical adapts film + historiographical arguments`

→ `musical repeatedly rewrites its own knowledge distribution`

→ `2026 stage advertises a new identity`

→ `2027 book is itself newly reintroduced to readers through a new foreword and new paratext`.

The book and stage are no longer simply primary/secondary in relation to one another. They have become mutually legible **versions of how the culture manages Martin Guerre as a reusable historical object**.

## 9. Final formulation

**The most revealing twenty-first-century return of Martin Guerre occurs in 2026–27 because the stage work and Davis's book are almost simultaneously asked to prove their own continuity. The Old Vic openly retains the musical by changing it: a work with new lyrics, music, orchestration, staging, and additional material is branded `back` with `a whole new identity`. Harvard University Press retains Davis's book through a different continuity regime: same author, title, publisher/canonical lineage, but a new foreword, new edition packaging, and a market description that compresses Davis's contested inference about Bertrande into narrative certainty. The two returns therefore reverse one another epistemically: the book's paratext hardens what historical scholarship had kept argumentatively qualified, while the stage's paratext foregrounds perception and invites audiences to doubt what they think they know. Martin Guerre survives not as an unchanged story but as a recurrent problem of authorized sameness across persons, texts, performances, and claims.**
