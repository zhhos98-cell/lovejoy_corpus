## 1. Why this matters

The AOS documentary sequence already shows a public shift:

`10 Apr 1897 — Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`

`-> late Apr 1897 — On the meaning of the Buddhist technical terms ...`

`-> 1898 — The Buddhistic technical terms upādāna and upādisesa`.

The 1898 publication uses the Milinda Pañha only as local witness evidence in the `upādāna` section. The next question is whether notebook `MS38_004_001_061_004` already shows this **decomposition of Milinda into proposition-specific evidence**.

It does.

The important result is not that every published Milinda page citation has been exactly matched to a notebook citation. The result is functional: **notebook 004 repeatedly mines the Milinda for distinct problems, and the 1898 publication selectively retains the Milinda functions that support the narrowed upādāna/nidāna argument while dropping other Milinda problems.**

## 2. Milinda is already disaggregated across notebook 004

### A. Flux / time continuity — notebook pp.10–11

The early notebook uses `MP II, 33` and `MP II, 35` inside a problem of absolute flux, temporal sequence and gradual becoming.

Lovejoy asks how a doctrine of radical transition can preserve intelligible temporal sequence without stable units and interprets Nāgasena's response as treating the time-process as an ultimate/inexplicable fact.

Function:

`Milinda witness -> flux / continuity / intelligibility of temporal sequence`.

This line of inquiry does **not** become a principal burden of the 1898 technical-terms article.

### B. Nāmarūpa / rebirth identity — notebook p.28 / MS87

The notebook uses an uncertain `MP II, 2` reference while discussing the individual before dissolution and the rise of a new `nāmarūpa` with rebirth; the same source is used to distinguish immaterial `name` from material `form`.

Function:

`Milinda witness -> nāmarūpa / personal continuity / rebirth configuration`.

This is not published as an independent Milinda problem, though parts of the causal architecture feed the technical-term analysis.

### C. Infinite causal series — notebook p.37 / MS107

The notebook explicitly records `MP II.32, 3`: Nāgasena says the ultimate point of time is not apparent and illustrates recursive causality using a series involving eye/form, consciousness, contact, sensation, desire, karma and renewed production.

Function:

`Milinda witness -> causal recursion / no recoverable first point`.

The broad infinity/first-point issue is not a central burden of the 1898 paper.

### D. Alternate dependent-origination chain — notebook p.32 / MS95

This is the most important retained Milinda locus.

The notebook explicitly gives a Milinda sequence:

`eye + forms`
`-> eye-consciousness`
`-> contact`
`-> sensation`
`-> desire`
`-> attachment`
`-> becoming`
`-> birth / old age ...`.

Lovejoy then notes that the Milinda also gives a more abridged version in which **karma follows directly upon taṇhā**, and uses the comparison to argue about the structure and grouping of nidānas 3–9.

Function:

> **Milinda becomes a witness for the internal causal architecture of the nidāna sequence, especially the relation among sensation, desire, attachment, karma and continued existence.**

This is directly continuous at the level of proof function with the published 1898 use.

### E. Upādāna / karma causal allocation — notebook p.33 / MS97

Immediately downstream, the notebook states the explicit analytic question:

`upādāna = cause of the fact of continued existence?`

vs

`karma = cause of the manner of continued existence?`

The immediate cited secondary control is Hardy p.394; the canonical Quellenforschung dossier already reconstructs the path as:

`Hardy two-factor description`
`-> Lovejoy analytic sharpening`
`-> later textual testing`
`-> publication stabilization`.

The 1898 publication then states:

- karma = cause of the **particular condition** in which one is reborn;
- upādāna = cause of the **fact of rebirth in itself**.

Its two footnotes at precisely this point cite, respectively:

- Samyutta/Warren + **Milinda Pañha p.65**;
- earlier passages + **MP p.33**.

Thus the published Milinda citations are inserted into a causal distinction already explicit in the notebook working apparatus.

Evidence status:

> **FUNCTIONAL CONTINUITY CLOSED; exact notebook-page-to-published-Milinda-page citation identity only partial.**

Do not claim Hardy's two-factor architecture was invented by Lovejoy.

### F. Soul / organ analogy — notebook p.46 / MS131

Lovejoy uses `Milindapañha II.3.6` for the palace-window reductio against a substantial inner soul that uses the sensory organs indiscriminately.

Function:

`Milinda witness -> anti-substantialist self / organ relation`.

This vivid Milinda argument disappears as an independent object from the 1898 technical-terms article.

### G. Vedanā / sensation — notebook p.55 / MS149

In the final nidāna/khandha map the notebook labels `vedanā` as the second khandha, `concerned with enjoyment`, with an explicit `[MP reference]`, and distinguishes pleasant, painful and indifferent forms.

The published article later says the middle nidānas constitute a psychological analysis culminating in sensation, where pleasure/pain determinations first appear; its footnote cites **Milinda Pañha p.60** together with Warren.

This gives a second strong functional continuity:

`notebook: Milinda -> vedanā / affective differentiation inside nidāna map`

`-> publication: Milinda p.60 -> sensation / pleasure-pain proposition in the same causal reconstruction`.

The exact notebook bibliographic page is not legible enough to assert that the note itself already says `MP p.60`; the continuity is proposition/function-level.

### H. Nirvāṇa — notebook p.60 / MS161

The notebook invokes an `MP` locus for a passage on the cessation of the elements / consciousness and underlines the terminal nothingness language.

Function:

`Milinda witness -> Nirvāṇa / cessation / metaphysical interpretation`.

Notebook 004 subsequently explores strong conclusions about Nirvāṇa and complete extinction. The canonical publication-selection dossier already shows that the 1898 article **softens and narrows** this larger metaphysical burden.

This Milinda function is not retained as a Milinda-centered published claim.

## 6. Historiographical payoff

The 10 April title can now be interpreted more precisely. It is not simply evidence that Lovejoy `also read the Milinda`. Notebook 004 shows that the Milinda was a **working evidence reservoir** from which different philosophical and causal problems were extracted.

The 1898 publication does something narrower:

> **It converts selected Milinda materials from a named-text research object into proposition-level witnesses within a corpus-wide semantic argument.**

## 7. Relation to broader proof architecture

This selective retention also clarifies that `publication pruning` is not random omission.

The retained Milinda functions are those that can be integrated into a local proof ladder:

`textual witness`
`-> semantic / causal distinction`
`-> place in dependent origination`
`-> bounded doctrinal conclusion`.

The discarded or softened Milinda functions bear larger burdens:

`whole time-process`
`personal identity`
`ultimate metaphysical status of Nirvāṇa`
`historical/systemic totalization`.

This is consonant with the canonical gradient:

> **the broader the genealogical/metaphysical proposition, the more likely it is to remain manuscript surplus or be softened; the more locally controllable the semantic/causal proposition, the more likely it is to survive and sharpen in print.**
