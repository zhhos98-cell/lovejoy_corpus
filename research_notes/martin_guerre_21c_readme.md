## 1. Executive thesis

The twenty-first-century life of Martin Guerre is not best described as a simple reception history of a famous sixteenth-century imposture case. It is a history of **epistemic relocation**: the same bounded historical affair is repeatedly detached from one explanatory setting and re-used wherever scholars, institutions, technical systems, publishers, filmmakers, or theatre-makers need a compact problem of recognition, identification, proof, continuity, and authorized sameness.

The recurrent question is therefore not simply:

`Who is Martin Guerre?`

It is:

> **By what procedure, from which traces, under whose authority, and at what evidentiary resolution can a claim that this person, text, performance, or record is the same object as an earlier one become warranted knowledge?**

The case remains durable because its basic architecture is portable:

`claimant / object`

→ `available traces`

→ `recognition or retrieval`

→ `comparison`

→ `warrant`

→ `authorized judgement of sameness`.

What changes from one receiving field to another is the carrier of the traces and the authority that licenses the judgement. A village court compares bodies, memories, kinship, household knowledge, speech, and testimony. Identification history studies documents, bodily marks, records, offices, and power. Bertillonage manufactures a retrievable archival representation of a person before the next encounter. Digital identity systems validate credentials, bind applicants to evidence, record proofing paths, and recursively create new reference states. Davis's historical book converts surviving evidence into warranted inference while preserving unresolved interiority. Film and musical theatre can redistribute that interiority directly to viewers and listeners. The 2026 Old Vic revival and the 2027 Harvard University Press edition finally turn the problem back on the cultural carriers themselves: **what makes a radically revised musical, or a newly framed edition of a canonical book, count as the same work?**

The final integrated proposition is:

> **Identity over time is not guaranteed by invariant content. It is produced through historically specific continuity rules that determine which traces count, who may compare them, which changes remain compatible with sameness, and which authority may make the result consequential.**

Martin Guerre is useful because those continuity rules are unusually visible across law, historiography, identification systems, media, and performance.

## 2. What this project is actually about

This project began from a question about the twenty-first-century reappearance of Martin Guerre, especially in relation to the revived musical. It became a history-of-knowledge problem because the case is repeatedly reused not merely for its plot but for its **epistemic structure**.

Three transformations matter most.

### A. From identity to identification

The older philosophical or cultural question asks what identity is. The identification-history literature instead asks who identifies whom, through which signs, documents, institutions, technologies, distances, and relations of authority. Martin Guerre becomes a classic failure case for the problem of connecting a present claimant to a past person.

### B. From identification to verification architecture

Later policy and technical literature can recode the affair into authentication factors, biometrics, liveness, identity matching, digital enrolment, and proof-of-humanity. The case becomes portable by being decomposed into a later protocol. This is analytically powerful and historically dangerous: heterogeneous relations such as spouse testimony, remembered intimacy, embodied familiarity, local reputation, and court evidence can be reassigned to standardized technical slots.

### C. From historical proof to media-specific knowledge

Davis's book, the 1982 film, successive musical versions, the 2026 Old Vic revival, and the 2027 new book edition do not merely tell the same story with different degrees of accuracy. Each carrier redistributes the **proof burden**. A historian must argue from traces. A musical can make a private mental state audible. A publisher can harden an argued interpretation into sales copy. A theatre can deliberately reopen uncertainty and make the audience itself a witness community.

The broad argument therefore moves through three nested problems:

`Who is this person?`

→ `How can a system warrant that this is the same person?`

→ `How do historical and cultural carriers themselves establish that they are still the same object after revision?`

## 3. The main spine: book ↔ film ↔ stage

The book-stage line is the controlling cultural spine of the cluster. The identification/verification material is important because it clarifies the same underlying problem, but the project closes by returning to Davis's book and the musical.

### 3.1 Davis's book is not simply a source text

Natalie Zemon Davis's *The Return of Martin Guerre* should not be treated as a stable scholarly original from which later performance merely departs. The book itself emerged partly through performance pressure. Davis's collaboration with Daniel Vigne and Jean-Claude Carrière on the film made her confront questions of motivation, gesture, timing, and action that archival prose did not force in the same way. She described filmmaking as a kind of **historical laboratory** capable of generating possibilities, though not proofs.

That distinction is foundational:

`performance may generate a possible historical configuration`

≠

`performance may prove that configuration historically`.

The book then became partly corrective. Davis returned to archives, law, religious context, Basque background, and the judge's contradictions partly because cinematic compression had simplified elements she considered historically explanatory. Thus the relation is recursive:

`historical sources → film questions → new historical inquiry → book`.

The book is therefore already a product of intermedial pressure.

### 3.2 The key variable is Bertrande's knowledge

The strongest book-stage control is the problem of what Bertrande knew and when she knew it.

Davis's historical position has a visible proof burden. In her defense against Robert Finlay, she deliberately qualifies the claim that Bertrande `knew`. The degree of her conscious accommodation remains hidden from us; collaboration is argued cumulatively from legal evidence, behavior, timing, household circumstances, and the eventual impossibility of maintaining the deception without some form of accommodation.

This gives a clean three-layer model:

- `E` = surviving evidence;
- `I` = historiographical inference warranted from that evidence;
- `M` = represented mental state assigned by dramatic form.

The historical dispute lives at `E → I`.

The musical can bypass that transition by making `M` directly audible.

### 3.3 1996: inference becomes audience knowledge

Sally Barnden's 2024 analysis makes the transformation unusually clear. In the 1996 musical, conventions such as duet, soliloquy, reprise, and the `I want` structure allow the audience to know what a character privately knows, wants, or recognizes. The audience no longer has to infer Bertrande's knowledge from behavior. The form tells them.

Thus:

`Davis: K_historiographical = strongly argued but evidentially indirect`

becomes

`1996 musical: K_dramatic = explicit to audience`.

This changes much more than factual certainty. It reorganizes agency and consent. If Bertrande recognizes Arnaud, continued intimacy is represented as a choice within the fictional world. If she does not recognize him, the same intimacy can become deception and sexual violation. The adaptive decision is therefore simultaneously epistemic, ethical, and causal.

The cost is dramaturgical. The more the audience knows, the less mystery remains. The musical's history can therefore be read as a history of **epistemic privilege with dramatic costs**.

### 3.4 2007: certainty is partly withdrawn

Later musical versions, especially the 2007 revival, moved back toward ambiguity. Knowledge previously assigned directly to the audience and to Bertrande was partially withdrawn. Jeopardy returned; consent became less straightforward again.

The important point is methodological: this is not merely plot revision. It is **version-level redistribution of epistemic access**.

A useful sequence is:

`film: greater uncertainty`

→ `1996 musical: privileged audience knowledge / explicit Bertrande knowledge`

→ `2007 revival: uncertainty partially restored`

→ `2026 Old Vic: unresolved before opening; paratext emphasizes perception and Bertrande's choices`.

### 3.5 2026 Old Vic: the audience may again become a problem

As of 3 September 2026 the Old Vic production has not opened, so the script and performance architecture remain unverified. Only paratext-level claims are secure.

The production is publicly described as an `all-new version`, with new music, new lyrics, new folk-inflected orchestrations, additional material, semi-staged / in-the-round form, and a renewed emphasis on Bertrande's choices. Boublil and Schönberg's publicity language places truth beside `what we each perceive to be true`. The Old Vic explicitly asks audiences who think they know Martin Guerre to think again.

That language matters because it suggests a move away from simple revelation and toward **perceptual uncertainty**, but the October performance must decide whether that suggestion is actually realized.

The decisive questions are narrow:

1. When does Bertrande know the imposture relative to the audience?
2. Does the audience receive privileged interior knowledge through private songs?
3. Does the production preserve or blur an Arnaud/Martin distinction?
4. Does in-the-round staging position spectators as distributed witnesses rather than omniscient observers?
5. Does the trial produce truth, announce truth, or destabilize truth?
6. Does `perceived truth` remain publicity language or become the production's actual epistemic architecture?

### 3.6 The dual `Martin` cast metadata is itself an identification problem

As of 3 September 2026, the Old Vic's public cast list gives both Alex James-Hatton and Shane O'Riordan the role label `Martin`, while no separate actor is publicly listed as `Arnaud`. External theatre coverage reproduces the same signal and in at least one case describes the actors as sharing the role of Martin.

This fact should be preserved without interpretation before opening. It may reflect alternating performances, division of functions, spoiler management, or deliberate collapse of the true/false Martin naming structure.

Its importance is conceptual: **cast metadata is already an identification apparatus**. Before an audience sees any body onstage, paratext assigns names to performers. If the paratext is itself ambiguous, then work identity and character identity are already being managed before performance begins.

## 4. The 2027 book: re-edition as reidentification

Harvard University Press's forthcoming 2027 edition of *The Return of Martin Guerre* creates the book-side equivalent of the Old Vic revival. Current metadata identifies it as the first new edition in more than forty years and names Jill Lepore as the writer of a new foreword.

The book and musical preserve continuity differently.

### Stage continuity

`same title + same creators + story lineage + retained musical material`

while allowing:

`new music + new lyrics + new orchestration + new staging + additional material`.

The theatre therefore claims sameness **through revision**.

### Book continuity

`same author + same title + same canonical work + publisher/bibliographic lineage`

while allowing:

`new foreword + new edition design + new packaging + potentially new edition matter`.

The press therefore claims sameness **through textual and bibliographic authority**.

This yields a strong comparative control:

> **A work can remain the same through radically different continuity regimes.**

There is no requirement that the amount or kind of preserved substance be identical across media. What matters is which institutionally recognized bundle of title, authorship, lineage, content, custody, and audience recognition is authorized to carry continuity.

### 4.1 Paratextual hardening

The most important 2027 finding so far is epistemic rather than bibliographic. Current promotional copy asks why Bertrande played along despite `knowing the truth`. This turns her knowledge into a narrative premise.

That is stronger than Davis's own methodological formulation, where the degree of conscious accommodation remains inaccessible and collaboration is cumulatively argued. Barnden likewise treats the knowing/complicit Bertrande as Davis's persuasive but ultimately unprovable interpretation.

The twenty-first-century publisher therefore performs a small but important warrant compression:

`private mental state hidden`

→ `cumulative historical argument`

→ `market-facing narrative certainty`.

This should be called **paratextual hardening**.

The claim is currently about sales/publicity copy only. It must not be attributed to the body text or to Lepore's forthcoming foreword before the edition is directly collated.

### 4.2 Stage and book move in opposite directions

The 2026–27 pairing is especially strong because the two paratexts move in opposite epistemic directions.

**Book paratext:**

`uncertain / inferential Bertrande knowledge`

→ `Bertrande knew the truth`.

**Stage paratext:**

`audiences inherit a famous story`

→ `what we each perceive to be true` / `think again`.

So the publisher currently closes uncertainty while the theatre reopens it.

That inverse movement is one of the strongest results of the whole project.

## 5. Martin Guerre as a portable epistemic object

The case moves across fields because later users do not transport the whole historical object. They select a relation-specific subset of the case that solves a new problem.

A useful definition is:

**Portable epistemic object** = a bounded historical case whose evidentiary architecture can be detached from its original setting and re-instantiated in new fields to test theories of recognition, proof, mediation, continuity, or authority.

Martin Guerre is unusually portable because the minimal structure is simple:

`a claimant appears`

→ `people possess incomplete traces of an earlier person`

→ `those traces conflict`

→ `an institution must decide whether sameness holds`.

The case can therefore be recoded without preserving all historical detail.

This portability does **not** imply a single intellectual genealogy. The project distinguishes two histories:

### Concept transmission

`state information / passport / identification at distance`

→ `identification as power relation`

→ `plural regimes of identification`

→ `identification / authentication / verification / identity matching` as increasingly formal institutional categories.

### Case portability

`Davis's Martin Guerre`

→ `imposture / identification failure`

→ `benchmark / anomaly / teaching object`

→ `technical recoding`

→ `policy exemplum`

→ `public-history and theatrical re-entry`.

These histories sometimes intersect and sometimes travel separately. The correct model is **braided transmission**, not a simple chain from Martin Guerre to digital identity.

## 6. Identification becomes a historical object

The identification-history branch is useful because it clarifies the relation that the Martin Guerre affair dramatizes.

### 6.1 Noiriel: from identity to authorized identification

Gérard Noiriel's work on passports and state identification moves the question away from metaphysical identity and toward practical operations of identifying persons at distance. The relevant questions become:

- who has the power to identify?
- through which documents or signs?
- for which administrative purpose?
- over which population?
- with what consequences?

Identification is therefore an operation with an authorized asymmetry.

### 6.2 About and Denis: `régime d'identification`

Ilsen About and Vincent Denis turn identification into an autonomous historical object and explicitly cite Martin Guerre as a precursor case that addressed the problem before `identification` had become a self-conscious category of analysis.

Their useful decomposition separates:

- singularisation;
- differentiation;
- recognition across time or space.

A `régime d'identification` is then the historically specific configuration of naming, signs, documents, images, recognition practices, actors, procedures, authorities, and resistances through which persons become identifiable.

This is an important control because it shows that Martin Guerre did not generate the concept. The case was **reclassified** once the field had formed.

### 6.3 Higgs: failure case at the threshold of the field

Edward Higgs places Davis's Martin Guerre alongside Perkin Warbeck and the Tichborne Claimant as cases in which identification mattered because it failed. He distinguishes such histories from a history explaining how forms of identification themselves change over time.

Martin Guerre therefore sits at an epistemic threshold:

`identification is important inside the story`

→ `identification becomes the object of historical explanation`.

Higgs also warns against an easy modernization narrative in which intimate village recognition simply collapses under mobility and anonymity, producing modern state identification as the inevitable solution.

That warning becomes crucial when later digital-ID literature restores precisely such a village → state → digital developmental sequence.

## 7. Retrospective protocolization

The Center for Global Development's *Identification Revolution* provides the clearest modern technical recoding of the case. It maps the Martin Guerre evidentiary bundle onto the familiar authentication-factor triad:

- appearance → `something you are`;
- Bertrande's testimony → `something you have`;
- detailed personal knowledge → `something you know`.

This is analytically revealing because the historical evidence is not naturally composed of those categories. Spousal testimony is not literally a possession factor. Household intimacy, local memory, witness authority, and juridical proof belong to a different social and evidentiary world.

The later system therefore performs an operation that this project calls **retrospective protocolization**:

> the re-description of historically heterogeneous evidence and relations in the slots of a later standardized decision protocol so that an old case becomes comparable with a contemporary system.

The gain is portability. The loss is historical specificity.

This operator is useful far beyond Martin Guerre. It identifies the exact point at which a later analytical grid can make an earlier object appear as though it had always been composed of the analyst's categories.

## 8. Convergent recoding, not a single citation chain

The closer source check shows that Martin Guerre does not travel through one clean textual lineage from Davis to digital identity. Simon Cole, Higgs, Malcolm Thorburn, Gelb and Diofasi Metz, identification historians, policy writers, and public historians repeatedly recruit the case for related but non-identical purposes.

The best model is **convergent recoding**:

> independent or only partially connected fields repeatedly arrive at Martin Guerre because the case supplies a compact identification problem that can be mapped onto their own operational vocabulary.

This avoids overstating influence.

The same case can support contradictory models:

- **Higgs:** identification failure; caution against simple modernization.
- **Gelb–Diofasi:** developmental benchmark; village → centralized ID → e-village.
- **Daston:** defamiliarizing anomaly; remove modern identity infrastructure and make present certainty strange.
- **French policy:** memorable historical exemplum inserted into an already developed distinction among identification, authentication, biometrics, and liveness.

Portability therefore does not imply doctrinal stability. The same object can authorize competing inferences.

## 9. The policy intersection: French digital identity

The French National Assembly's 2020 report on digital identity is the cleanest point at which the history-of-identification concept line and the Martin Guerre case line become recombined inside a policy object.

The report uses About and Denis for a historical periodization of identification practices and separately cites Davis when invoking Martin Guerre as a memorable case of identity usurpation and fallible recognition. It then moves into digital identification, authentication, biometric comparison, remote enrolment, and liveness.

The evidentiary structure is therefore braided:

`Noiriel → About/Denis → identification-regime history`

plus

`Davis → Martin Guerre → failure case`

→ `policy synthesis`.

This is stronger than claiming a simple genealogy. It shows **recombination of concept lineage and case itinerary**.

## 10. Reference states: the deeper identification problem

The verification-regime material originally seemed to suggest a contrast between interpersonal memory and modern databases. That formulation proved too weak.

The deeper difference concerns **reference states**.

A reference state is the prior representation against which a current claimant can be checked.

In the Martin Guerre affair, the court does not possess a single authoritative representation of the departed Martin created in advance for future re-identification. It must reconstruct him retrospectively from dispersed traces:

- remembered appearance;
- bodily marks;
- shoe lasts / measurements;
- speech and accent;
- household history;
- intimate knowledge;
- kin relations;
- testimony;
- legal narrative.

The task is therefore:

`present claimant → retrospective reconstruction of past person → comparison`.

Modern identification increasingly reorganizes this temporal relation by manufacturing reusable reference states before the next dispute.

## 11. Historical addressability: Bertillon as the key control

Bertillonage provides the strongest pre-digital control because its central innovation is not merely `better bodily evidence` or `more objective photography`. The crucial object is an **indexed, retrievable representation of the person**.

Measurements are converted into a classification formula; frontal/profile photographs, scars, descriptors, and record numbers are attached to a card; filing and cross-indexing create a path from a present body back to a stored record.

The process becomes:

`registration`

→ `classification`

→ `archival address`

→ `future encounter`

→ `query / retrieval`

→ `comparison`.

This project calls the resulting capacity **historical addressability**:

> the capacity of an institution to transform a person encountered in the present into a query that can retrieve an earlier institutional representation of that same person.

Martin Guerre lacked a centralized authoritative address of this kind. Bertillonage deliberately manufactures one.

This is a better long-term axis than the technological sequence:

`memory → paper → biometric → digital`.

The more revealing sequence is:

`distributed recollection`

→ `manufactured historical addressability`

→ `recursively reusable assurance`.

## 12. Recursive reference-state manufacturing

Current identity-proofing standards make the recursive structure explicit.

A modern identity system does not simply compare a claimant to `truth`. It validates documents and authoritative sources that were themselves produced by earlier institutional acts of identification. It verifies that the current applicant is bound to those records and then creates a new subscriber/account state carrying evidence of how the proof was performed.

The system therefore operates recursively:

`earlier institution creates evidence/reference state`

→ `current verifier inherits and validates it`

→ `applicant is bound to it`

→ `new account / credential / assurance state is created`

→ `future systems may rely on that state`.

This project calls the process **recursive reference-state manufacturing**.

It shifts the history of identification away from a simple story of more accurate signs. The key change is the ability to preserve not only identity claims but the **provenance of the proof path** by which an institution became entitled to make those claims.

## 13. Witnesses do not disappear: the jurisdiction shift

A naive modernization account would say:

`village witnesses → objective documents / biometrics / databases`.

Current standards complicate that story. NIST identity-proofing guidance still permits applicant references when conventional evidence or data checks are insufficient. The reference must itself be identified/proofed; the relationship, role, statement, signatures, and legal responsibility can be recorded inside the institutional account.

Thus human testimony survives, but its location changes.

In Artigat, interpersonal knowledge is ordinary and central.

In modern proofing, interpersonal knowledge becomes a bounded exception pathway embedded inside a larger provenance-bearing system.

This project calls that transformation the **witness jurisdiction shift**:

> interpersonal testimony does not disappear; its authority is delimited, proceduralized, attributed, and recorded.

This is one of the two principal anti-teleological corrections controlling the whole cluster.

## 14. Proof of humanity: the limit case

The twenty-first-century verification branch eventually reaches a threshold at which the question is no longer only:

`Are you Martin Guerre?`

or

`Can you prove that you are the same registered person?`

but:

`Can you prove that you are a person at all rather than a bot, synthetic agent, or fraudulent digital actor?`

This is why recent proof-of-humanity discourse is analytically adjacent to Martin Guerre. The old case begins with disputed continuity of a person; the digital limit case asks whether a claimant belongs to the category of human persons before individual identity is even adjudicated.

This branch is useful as a boundary control rather than the main narrative. The project returns from it to the book and stage because the central historical object remains Martin Guerre as a reusable case, not a general history of digital identity.

## 15. Media are epistemic instruments, not neutral carriers

The book-stage research sharpens a wider point: different media do not merely transmit a fixed historical content. They alter the **epistemic geometry** of the case.

### Court record

Produces legally conditioned testimony, procedural statements, contradictions, and judgements. Private mental states remain indirect.

### Historical book

Can collate dispersed evidence, construct cumulative inference, distinguish fact from conjecture, and retain unresolved interiority.

### Film

Must materialize bodies, spaces, gestures, chronology, and motivation. It can make possibilities concrete, but concreteness can exceed proof.

### Musical theatre

Can allocate private consciousness directly through song, duet, reprise, leitmotif, and staging. It can make `what the character knows` audible to the audience even when the historical record cannot.

### Digital surrogate / OCR

Changes access to source witnesses and lowers the cost of inspection, while creating new risks of edition conflation, OCR error, and false equivalence between scan and source.

### Publisher paratext

Can compress an argument into a premise and thereby alter the public warrant level without changing the body text.

### Casting / production metadata

Can identify bodies with character names before performance and can itself introduce ambiguity.

The case survives because its evidentiary architecture reveals those media differences unusually well.

## 16. Same-person, same-book, same-work

The final synthesis becomes reflexive in 2026–27.

The original legal case asks:

`Is this claimant the same Martin who left?`

The 2027 edition asks, structurally:

`What makes this new material carrier still Davis's same book?`

The 2026 revival asks:

`What makes a musical with extensive internal revision still the same Martin Guerre?`

A comparative matrix:

| Object | Continuity claim | What may change? | Authority stabilizing sameness | Main epistemic risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Martin Guerre, sixteenth-century dispute | present claimant = departed person | age, body, behavior, experience, circumstances | witnesses + court | traces may be misbound to claimant |
| Davis book, 2027 edition | new edition = Davis's same canonical book | foreword, pagination, design, packaging, edition matter; body-text changes unverified | author/title/publisher/bibliographic/canonical lineage | paratext may harden qualified historical claims |
| Boublil–Schönberg musical, Old Vic 2026 | heavily revised production = same musical | lyrics, music, orchestration, staging, additional material, knowledge architecture | title/creators/retained lineage/institution/audience recognition | continuity branding may conceal the degree of epistemic rewriting |

This does not claim that person identity and work identity are historically identical problems. It uses them as a structural control.

The comparison supports the final proposition:

> **Sameness is a judgement governed by authorized continuity rules. Different domains authorize different bundles of preserved and transformable features.**

## 18. Controlling vocabulary

These are **project analytical terms** unless a note explicitly assigns them to an actor/source.

### Portable epistemic object
A bounded historical case whose evidentiary architecture can be reused across fields while preserving only a relation-specific subset of the original object.

### Case portability
The itinerary through which Martin Guerre is repeatedly recruited as failure case, benchmark, anomaly, exemplum, teaching object, or media problem.

### Concept transmission
The historical genealogy of identification concepts, institutions, and formal categories independently of whether Martin Guerre travels with them.

### Braided transmission
The recombination of an independently developing concept lineage and case itinerary inside later scholarship, policy, or public discourse.

### Retrospective protocolization
The reassignment of historically heterogeneous evidence to the slots of a later standardized verification protocol.

### Convergent recoding
Independent or partially connected reuses that arrive at similar modern classifications of the case without requiring a continuous direct citation chain.

### Reference state
The prior representation against which a current claimant can be checked.

### Recursive reference-state manufacturing
The process by which one institutional identity judgement becomes inherited evidence for later identity proofing and is itself used to create a new reusable assurance state.

### Historical addressability
The institutional capacity to turn a present person into a query that retrieves an earlier stored representation of that same person.

### Witness jurisdiction shift
The relocation of interpersonal testimony from ordinary central evidence to bounded, attributed, proceduralized, provenance-bearing exception pathways.

### Epistemic constraint
The way Davis's book limits and marks what later representation can historically warrant, even when dramatic form chooses to assign greater certainty.

### Epistemic access
Which actor, historian, audience, institution, or system is permitted to know which proposition, at what point, and by what mechanism.

### Proof-burden redistribution
The movement of a claim from source-based historical inference into cinematic, dramatic, paratextual, or institutional forms with different requirements for justification.

### Paratextual hardening
The compression of a qualified or inferential scholarly claim into a market/publicity premise presented without its original proof burden.

### Authorized sameness
A continuity judgement licensed by a recognized authority using domain-specific rules about which differences are compatible with identity over time.

## 19. Evidence and claim ownership

The cluster depends on strict separation of evidence layers.

### Layer A — direct historical / legal / printed source

Coras editions, legal materials, Davis's cited archival bases, and directly inspected primary witnesses. Claims about sixteenth-century actors should originate here or be clearly identified as later historical inference.

### Layer B — historiographical argument

Davis, Finlay, identification historians, Barnden, Crouzet, the 2023 *Studi Storici* forum, and related scholarship. These sources may make historical claims, methodological claims, or claims about earlier historiography. Keep those functions distinct.

### Layer C — actor / institutional vocabulary

Old Vic production copy, creator statements, HUP/Google Books bibliographic metadata, National Assembly reports, current regulations, NIST guidance, France Identité pages. These are strong evidence for how institutions currently frame operations, but they are not automatically evidence about the sixteenth century.

### Layer D — project analytical vocabulary

`portable epistemic object`, `retrospective protocolization`, `reference state`, `historical addressability`, `witness jurisdiction shift`, `epistemic constraint`, and related terms belong to this project unless explicitly noted otherwise.

### Layer E — unresolved inference

Dual `Martin` billing, possible in-the-round witness effects, the role of Lepore's forthcoming foreword, and the 2026 script's actual knowledge architecture remain open until event-bound evidence appears.

Never silently upgrade one layer into another.

## 20. Anti-teleological controls

Two corrections govern all writing from this cluster.

### Control 1: human testimony does not simply disappear

The movement from village recognition to modern identity systems is not:

`subjective witnesses → objective technology`.

Interpersonal knowledge survives but changes jurisdiction, provenance, responsibility, and relation to other proof mechanisms.

### Control 2: later media do not simply lose historical truth

The movement from archive to book to film to stage is not:

`accurate history → distorted popular representation`.

Different media can:

- harden an inference;
- expose a missing step;
- generate a possible configuration;
- distribute private knowledge;
- withdraw certainty;
- create new witness positions;
- compress warrants into paratext;
- reopen inherited certainty.

The relevant question is not fidelity alone but **what kind of knowledge each carrier makes possible, necessary, or invisible**.

## 27. Compressed abstract

**Martin Guerre's twenty-first-century afterlife is more than the continued popularity of a famous imposture story. The case has become a portable epistemic object repeatedly used to test how persons, records, historical claims, and cultural works are recognized as the same across time. Identification historians recoded the affair from a problem of identity into an operation of identification; policy and digital-ID writers later retrofitted its heterogeneous evidence into modern verification protocols; historical comparison with Bertillonage and current proofing systems shows a deeper transformation from retrospective reconstruction toward manufactured and recursively reusable reference states. Yet the most revealing afterlife remains the relation between Natalie Zemon Davis's book and the musical. Davis's reconstruction preserves a visible proof burden around Bertrande's private knowledge, while musical theatre can assign that knowledge directly to the audience and thereby alter agency, consent, causality, and suspense. The 2026 Old Vic revival and the 2027 Harvard University Press edition make the structure reflexive: the stage advertises a heavily revised work as returning with a `whole new identity`, while the new book preserves canonical continuity through authorship and edition lineage even as its promotional paratext hardens Davis's qualified inference about Bertrande into narrative certainty. Martin Guerre therefore survives not because one stable meaning persists, but because different institutions repeatedly authorize sameness through different continuity rules.**
