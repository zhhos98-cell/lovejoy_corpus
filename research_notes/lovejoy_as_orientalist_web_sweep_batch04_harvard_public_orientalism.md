## 2. Graduate Philosophical Society: a potentially very important Vivekananda intersection

Wilson says Lovejoy **was a member of the Graduate Philosophical Society and its secretary during his second year at Harvard**; in February 1897 he led a discussion on the problem of evil. Wilson cites Lovejoy letters and the “Minutes of the Graduate Philosophical Society of Harvard” in the Lovejoy Papers.

Independently, the primary bibliographic record for Swami Vivekananda’s *The Vedanta Philosophy* states that it was an address before **the Graduate Philosophical Society of Harvard University on 25 March 1896**, originally printed for the Society at Cambridge in 1896. The later 1901 edition includes an introduction by Charles Carroll Everett. Public catalogues and scans also state that Professor Lanman and Professor John Henry Wright assisted in revising the stenographic report; the original lecture included Sanskrit quotations followed by translations.

This creates a concrete institutional intersection:

- the society before which Vivekananda delivered a major Vedānta address is the same Graduate Philosophical Society to which Lovejoy belonged and in which he later served as secretary;
- Everett, one of Lovejoy’s confirmed comparative-religion teachers, introduced the published address;
- Lanman, Lovejoy’s confirmed Sanskrit/Buddhist-text teacher, assisted with the text’s revision.

### Evidence limit

**Lovejoy’s personal attendance at the 25 March 1896 lecture is not yet proved.** Wilson does not specify the date at which Lovejoy first joined the Society, only that he was a member and secretary during his second Harvard year. We should therefore treat this as a high-value institutional/network lead, not as a witnessed encounter with Vivekananda.

Priority archival test: inspect the Graduate Philosophical Society minutes around March 1896 and, if membership/attendance lists survive, determine whether Lovejoy was present. Search Lovejoy’s letters immediately around 25–26 March 1896.

## 3. Cambridge Conferences: Lovejoy’s extracurricular comparative-religion milieu can now be specified

Wilson says Lovejoy attended/enjoyed the **Cambridge Conferences** organized by Mrs. Ole Bull, where a deliberately heterogeneous range of speakers — including Hindus — lectured on ethics, history, and philosophy.

Harvard Crimson confirms the Conferences were explicitly offered to Harvard students/professors for comparative study and held at 168 Brattle Street. History Cambridge’s Bull/Curtis finding aid preserves a dedicated Cambridge Conferences series with letters from 1895 onward and describes the program as philosophical, religious, and social lectures.

A period series, *The Evolution of Ethics* (1896–97), states that some of its lectures were also delivered at the Cambridge Conferences and lists a striking comparative program:

- Swami Saradananda — **“Ethical Ideas of the Hindus”**
- Jehanghile Dossabhoy Cola — **“Ethics of Zoroaster and the Parsis”**
- Anagarika H. Dharmapala — **“Ethics of Buddhism”**
- F. Huberty James — **“Ethics of the Chinese Sages”**
- Crawford H. Toy — **“Ethics of the New Testament”**
- plus Greek, Hebrew, Islamic, German/evolutionary ethical topics.

This is unusually close to Everett’s documented comparative-religion syllabus and to the religious range appearing in Lovejoy’s early notebooks.

### Evidence limit

The surviving description establishes that these talks belonged to the 1896–97 Evolution of Ethics/Cambridge orbit, but it does **not yet establish which specific lectures Lovejoy personally attended**. The Bull/Curtis Cambridge Conferences papers (Series II, letters 1895–1900 and articles/programs) are a primary archive target for exact dates and membership/attendance.

## 4. Wider Harvard/Cambridge Indic public culture

Vivekananda’s Harvard address and the Cambridge Conferences show that Lovejoy’s formal Lanman/Everett training occurred inside a dense public culture of Asian religious speakers and comparative-religion events, not in isolation.

Additional confirmed context:

- Harvard Religious Union had hosted Vivekananda publicly in May 1894, before Lovejoy arrived; this demonstrates that the Union itself — which Lovejoy later joined — already had a precedent of direct engagement with an Indian religious lecturer.
- The Cambridge Conferences were designed to be accessible to students and faculty and explicitly advertised comparative study of ethics, philosophy, sociology, and religion.
- Sara Bull’s network hosted Vivekananda and later other Vedānta figures; archival papers survive at History Cambridge.

These facts are context, not proof of personal influence. The research payoff will come from tying individual events to Lovejoy’s own attendance, letters, notebooks, or course chronology.

## 5. Updated network hypothesis

The early Orientalist formation now has three overlapping layers:

1. **Formal pedagogy:** Everett (comparative religion), Toy (Hebrew religion), Lanman (Sanskrit and sacred Buddhist texts).
2. **Specialist textual infrastructure:** Harvard Oriental Series, Warren’s *Buddhism in Translations*, Pāli/Sanskrit editions, Lanman’s Indo-Iranian program.
3. **Public comparative-religion encounter:** Graduate Philosophical Society, Cambridge Conferences, Religious Union, visiting South Asian lecturers.

This layered model is stronger than a simple “Lovejoy studied Sanskrit” claim. It asks how university courses, Orientalist print infrastructure, and live comparative-religion encounters jointly produced the environment in which notebooks 004/005 and the 1898/1906 publications became possible.
