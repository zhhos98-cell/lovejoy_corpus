# Batch 25 — Paris 1898–99 exact course matrix: Lovejoy inside a dense Orientalist / history-of-religions curriculum

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced

## Research move

This batch returns to the unfinished **French / Paris side of notebook 005**, while keeping the main historical object **Lovejoy as Orientalist**. The key task is to replace general statements such as “Lovejoy studied comparative religion in Paris” with the exact courses and research problems operating in the institutions around him in 1898–99.

The result is unusually strong. Independent EPHE annual reports corroborate the three strands Wilson reconstructs from Lovejoy’s 1898 letters — Marillier, Vernes, Jean Réville — and reveal a fourth, directly relevant Indian-philosophy ecology running in parallel: **Foucher on Nyāya, Stickney on Sāṃkhya from Garbe, and Sylvain Lévi on the Bhagavad-Gītā and Indian literature.**

This does not prove Lovejoy attended every course listed below. It does show that the Paris environment in which 005 was produced simultaneously contained the exact comparative-religion and Indian-philosophy problems that frame Lovejoy’s early Orientalist work.

## 1. Marillier: Lovejoy is directly documented as an active participant

The EPHE Section des sciences religieuses report for 1898–99 names Lovejoy among the auditors who **“ont pris une part active aux travaux.”**

Marillier’s two conferences were:

- survivance / future life: explicit defence of the thesis that early eschatology is continuation rather than moral compensation, reparation or punishment, including a named refutation of Steinmetz and new evidence from the Americas;
- human sacrifice / ritual anthropophagy: Bantu southern Africa, Guinea, North America, non-Aryan India, expiatory/funerary/anthropophagic/magical sacrifice, blood, agrarian and fecundative rites.

This remains the strongest direct institutional anchor for notebook 005.

## 2. Maurice Vernes: the exact Psalms course is independently confirmed

Wilson, using Lovejoy’s 1898 letters, says Lovejoy studied the Psalms with Maurice Vernes. The EPHE programme for **1898–99** independently gives:

> **Maurice Vernes — Explication du livre des Psaumes**, Mondays at 2:30; and **Caractères de l’ancienne religion d’Israël**, Fridays at 2:30.

The annual report for the year confirms that the Israel/Semitic-religions teaching continued as a historically critical reconstruction of ancient Israelite religion.

This matters for the later Hebrew/Jewish layer in 005 and for Lovejoy’s 1907 “Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought.” It also independently corroborates Wilson’s letter-based reconstruction at the level of the actual Paris timetable.

Evidence limit: Lovejoy is not named in the surviving regular-attendee list for this course; attendance rests on Wilson’s letter-based evidence, while the EPHE programme confirms that the exact course existed.

## 3. Jean Réville: Wilson’s “Fourth Gospel” lecture is exactly the 1898–99 EPHE course

The 1898–99 EPHE report for **Jean Réville, Histoire de l’Église chrétienne**, gives two conferences.

The Saturday conference was explicitly:

> **Étude critique de l’authenticité du quatrième évangile et des traditions relatives à l’apôtre Jean.**

The report specifies:

- critical analysis of the Fourth Gospel;
- comparison with the Synoptics;
- criticism of the historical testimonies for Johannine authorship;
- Apocalypse and Johannine Epistles;
- hypotheses on origin;
- conclusion that the Fourth Gospel does not itself claim apostolic authorship, while retaining high religious value.

This is a remarkably exact primary corroboration of Wilson’s statement that Lovejoy “frequently heard Jean Réville lecture on the Fourth Gospel.” The lecture series later became Réville’s published *Le Quatrième Évangile: son origine et sa valeur historique*.

The other Réville conference treated the formation of the early Church through Jewish diaspora, Hellenization, Paul, primitive Christian varieties, Gnosticism, tradition and institutional formation. Thus the Paris year supplied Lovejoy not only comparative ethnology but a second model of **historical decomposition of a religious corpus and tradition**.

## 4. Foucher’s 1898–99 Indian-philosophy conference: Nyāya, causation, knowledge

The strongest new contextual finding is the exact content of the EPHE `Religions de l’Inde` conference in the same year.

Alfred Foucher’s Tuesday conference gave an **exposé of Indian logical doctrines according to Nyāya**. The report lists:

- definition by specific/non-common character;
- **theory of cause** as constant and necessary antecedent;
- three kinds of cause: coessential/material, non-coessential/formal, instrumental;
- theory of knowledge;
- perception, inference, analogy/induction, testimony/language;
- syllogism and fallacies;
- doubt, error, absurdity, regress, circularity.

Regular core participants named in the report were Lacôte, Mauss and Joseph Trumbull Stickney.

Lovejoy is **not** named as a regular participant in this course, so no attendance claim is made. But the fact that his documented Marillier participation occurred in the same section and year as a detailed Indian course on **causation, inference, language and classification** is highly relevant context for the broader `Lovejoy as Orientalist` reconstruction.

## 5. Stickney gives a Sāṃkhya series from Garbe in the same 1898–99 course

The same Foucher report contains an especially important sentence:

> Stickney had charge of a **series of conferences on the Sāṃkhya system according to the work of Professor Garbe**.

This intersects directly with the specialist problem-field already reconstructed around Lovejoy’s notebook 004 and the 1898 JAOS paper. In 1896 Richard Garbe and Hermann Jacobi were central to the live Sāṃkhya–Buddhism priority/genealogy debate; 004 is saturated with Sāṃkhya/Buddhism chronology and borrowing questions.

Thus, in Paris 1898–99, while Lovejoy is securely active in Marillier’s seminar, another EPHE Indian-religions course is explicitly revisiting **Sāṃkhya through Garbe**.

Evidence discipline:

- secure: the Sāṃkhya/Garbe series occurred;
- secure: Lovejoy was active elsewhere in the same EPHE religious-sciences section that year;
- unproved: Lovejoy attended Stickney’s Sāṃkhya sessions or used them in 004/1898;
- historiographically important: the Sāṃkhya–Buddhism problem did not disappear when Lovejoy arrived in Paris; it was literally being taught in the same institutional ecology.

## 6. Joseph Trumbull Stickney is an American specialist node in the same Paris milieu

The `Stickney` in the reports is **Joseph Trumbull Stickney (1874–1904)**, an American student in Paris. BnF records identify him as author of a Paris doctoral dissertation in 1903. BnF’s France–South Asia heritage portal confirms that Sylvain Lévi later worked from Stickney’s translation of the **Bhagavad-Gītā**, revising and enriching it; the translation appeared posthumously in 1938.

This matters because Stickney appears repeatedly in the 1898–99 Indianist teaching reports:

- active in Foucher’s Nyāya / Manu work;
- responsible for the Sāṃkhya series based on Garbe;
- strongly trained in Sanskrit and Indian philosophy;
- central participant in Lévi’s Bhagavad-Gītā reading.

No direct Lovejoy–Stickney personal relation has yet been found. Stickney is therefore a **same-place/same-year American Indianist control**, not a documented collaborator of Lovejoy.

## 7. Sylvain Lévi’s own 1898–99 Sanskrit conferences are now identified

The EPHE Section des sciences historiques et philologiques annual report for the same academic year gives two Sylvain Lévi conferences:

1. **Bhagavad-Gītā explanation**, with Stickney taking a constant and very active role;
2. a new continuous **survey of Indian literature**, intended to remedy the absence of elementary manuals, in which Lévi analyzed characteristic works, explained passages, and reviewed the current state of scholarship.

This sharply clarifies Wilson’s statement that Lovejoy had hoped to take **Pāli Buddhism with Sylvain Lévi**, but the desired course was unavailable.

The institutional picture is now precise:

- there was **no Pāli-Buddhism course by Lévi** in the 1898–99 EPHE programmes recovered here;
- Lévi was teaching Sanskrit/Indian literature and Bhagavad-Gītā;
- Foucher was teaching Nyāya/Manu in the religious-sciences section;
- therefore Lovejoy’s reported **private sacred-text reading with Lévi** took place alongside, but outside, these documented regular offerings.

This strengthens rather than weakens the private-reading evidence: Wilson’s claim fits the actual course record unusually well.

## 8. Paris 1898–99 is now a multi-track Orientalist formation, not a generic “year abroad”

The working matrix is:

| Track | 1898–99 Paris evidence | Lovejoy status |
|---|---|---|
| comparative religion | Marillier: survivance + sacrifice | **active participation primary-confirmed** |
| Hebrew religion | Vernes: Psalms + ancient Israel | letter-based attendance; exact course independently confirmed |
| early Christianity | Jean Réville: critical Fourth Gospel | letter-based attendance; exact course independently confirmed |
| Indian philosophy | Foucher: Nyāya + Manu | course context only; no attendance proof |
| Sāṃkhya | Stickney series from Garbe | course context only; no attendance proof |
| Sanskrit / Indian literature | Sylvain Lévi: Bhagavad-Gītā + Indian literature | regular-course attendance unproved |
| Buddhist textual study | hoped-for Pāli Buddhism with Lévi; private sacred-text reading | Wilson from Lovejoy letters; exact texts/languages unresolved |

The key point is historical density. Lovejoy’s 004 Buddhist/Sāṃkhya work and 005 comparative-religion work no longer look like two unrelated curiosities. The Paris environment connected **Indian philosophy, textual philology, comparative religion, Hebrew criticism and historical criticism of Christian origins** within overlapping institutions and personnel.
