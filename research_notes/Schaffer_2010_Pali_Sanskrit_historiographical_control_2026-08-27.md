# Simon Schaffer 2010 — *Lovejoy's Series*: direct extraction and historiographical control

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: **DIRECT SECONDARY CONTROL / PAGE-LEVEL EXTRACTION / HISTORIOGRAPHICAL POSITIONING**

Primary object checked:

- Simon Schaffer, `Lovejoy's Series`, *History of Science* 48 (2010), 483–494.
- Direct PDF supplied and checked on 2026-08-27: `007327531004800309.pdf`.
- Twelve-page PDF; printed article pagination 483–494.

This note records what Schaffer actually does with Lovejoy's early Pāli/Sanskrit study and how that biographical fact sits inside Schaffer's larger reconstruction of Lovejoy's historiography. It is not a general summary of the article and does not infer anything from Schaffer that the text does not support.

---

## 1. Article-level argument

Schaffer is not writing a biography of Lovejoy and not writing about Buddhism. His subject is the reflexive relation between **seriality as an historical object** and **seriality as a way of writing intellectual history**.

At printed pp.483–484 he places the traditional history of ideas beside forms of expertise and asks how historians organize intellectuals, doctrines and natural forms into sequences. *The Great Chain of Being* is his main case because Lovejoy's book is simultaneously a history of a graded series and an explicit statement about the province and method of the history of ideas.

Schaffer's larger interests include:

- seriality and sequence;
- evolution and emergence;
- hierarchy and discontinuity;
- the relation between intellectual doctrine and the climate or sensibility of an epoch;
- the autonomy and political responsibility claimed for the intellectual;
- the political consequences Lovejoy attached to intellectual formations.

Thus this is a particularly strong historiographical control: Schaffer is directly reconstructing Lovejoy's historical practice and intellectual role, not merely listing youthful interests.

---

## 2. The Pāli/Sanskrit passage: printed p.484

In the compact biographical transition after introducing the 1933 Harvard lectures, Schaffer summarizes Lovejoy's Harvard graduate study in 1895–98 and writes that William James discouraged him from taking a doctorate and encouraged him to go to Paris, **`where he studied Pali and Sanskrit`**.

This is the only substantive Pāli/Sanskrit statement in the article.

Immediate narrative sequence:

1. Harvard philosophy study;
2. James's advice;
3. Paris Pāli/Sanskrit;
4. later Harvard chair offers and presidential veto;
5. Lovejoy's public and political career.

Schaffer does not pause to identify Lanman, Sylvain Lévi, Buddhist texts, the 1898 JAOS article, the 1901 Buddhism syllabus, or Lovejoy's notebook work.

The evidentiary consequence is precise:

> By 2010 Lovejoy's Pāli/Sanskrit study was plainly biographically visible inside serious historiography of Lovejoy, but it could remain a one-sentence biographical fact rather than a source for reconstructing his historical practice.

## 4. Page-by-page extraction relevant to the JHI project

### pp.483–484 — history of ideas, seriality and intellectual role

Schaffer opens by connecting forms of intellectual authority to different kinds of historical inquiry. Traditional history of ideas is characterized by succession, sequence and the arrangement of ideas into series. He uses Foucault's contrast between knowing a series in advance and constituting or relating series as a reflexive frame.

**Use for JHI:** Schaffer represents a later attempt to historicize Lovejoy's own historical form rather than merely rehearse unit-idea doctrine.

### p.484 — *Great Chain* as self-reflexive historiography

Schaffer treats *The Great Chain of Being* as both a history of a graded series and a statement of historical method. He stresses Lovejoy's simultaneous attention to intellectuals as products of an epoch's climate/sensibility and as actors able to redirect it.

**Use for JHI:** this confirms that Schaffer is operating exactly in the historiographical territory where one might have expected early scholarly formation to matter. The Pāli/Sanskrit sentence nevertheless remains analytically undeveloped.

### pp.484–485 — Pāli and Sanskrit are biographically present

The Paris sentence records Pāli/Sanskrit study but does not name Buddhist scholarship as a historical workshop for Lovejoy.

**Use for JHI:** strongest direct evidence for `known but differently classified`, not `forgotten`.

### pp.485–486 — public intellectual, primitivism, series

Schaffer moves quickly to Lovejoy's public intellectual role, social discipline, academic freedom and primitivism. Seriality is treated as simultaneously an intellectual, natural-historical and political problem.

**Use for JHI:** explains what replaces Pāli/Sanskrit in Schaffer's genealogy: social/intellectual series, evolution, public expertise and the mature history-of-ideas programme.

### p.486 — doctrine-series and natural-series

Schaffer distinguishes two principal uses of series in Lovejoy: series of widely shared doctrines arranged by the historian, and series of continuously distributed entities classified by the naturalist. He also stresses Lovejoy's search for ideas outside formally philosophical texts.

**Use for JHI:** useful control against reducing later Lovejoy to a caricature of isolated unit-ideas. It does not connect these later practices to Buddhist philology.

### pp.487–488 — earlier evolution work reused and selectively omitted

Schaffer shows how pre-war work on eighteenth-century evolution and natural history fed into the later Harvard lectures, while some earlier material, such as Buffon, was reduced or omitted depending on the later argument.

**Use for JHI:** this is an important formal parallel to the notebook/article question. Schaffer himself is attentive to how earlier research is selected and rewritten into later publication. The JHI piece can therefore place its 004→1898 comparison inside an existing historiographical concern with Lovejoy's reuse and selection, without claiming the cases are identical.

### pp.489–490 — continuity, novelty, Romanticism and historical context

Schaffer emphasizes a tension in Lovejoy's programme: history of ideas searches for recurring ingredients and continuity while Lovejoy's own history of Romanticism makes novelty, diversity and discontinuity historically consequential. Lovejoy also describes Romantic historical imagination as reconstructing peoples remote in time, space or cultural condition.

**Use for JHI:** useful late control for the user's observation that the canonical Lovejoy/context opposition is less tidy in practice. Do not turn this into a claim that young Lovejoy was proto-Skinnerian.

### p.490 — resistance to presentism versus contextual explanation

Schaffer notes that Lovejoy resisted claims that current conditions should set historical agendas and objected to sociological inference about intellectuals, even while his own historical explanations repeatedly invoked sensibility, temperament, passions and interests.

**Use for JHI:** Schaffer's Lovejoy is already internally non-simple. This makes the notebook contribution a reconstruction of an earlier working practice, not another generic rehabilitation of contextual sensitivity.

### pp.491–492 — politics and the role of the intellectual

Schaffer follows Lovejoy's links between Romantic ideas, nationalism/racism, German fascism and the public responsibility of intellectual experts. His conclusion connects historical mastery of developmental sequences with Lovejoy's ideal of informed intellectual intervention.

**Use for JHI:** confirms that later historiography can connect historical form and political stakes without needing the Buddhist material. This makes the JHI article's 1906 `Democracy` turn complementary rather than redundant.

## 5. Schaffer's dependence on Wilson

Schaffer's references repeatedly use Daniel J. Wilson for Lovejoy's biography and career:

- Wilson on *The Great Chain of Being* after fifty years;
- Wilson on the moral of *Great Chain*;
- Wilson, *Arthur O. Lovejoy and the Quest for Intelligibility*;
- Wilson on Lovejoy's public and political career;
- Wilson on emergence and later intellectual development.

Do not infer that Schaffer independently reconstructed Lovejoy's Pāli/Sanskrit training from archival sources. The safe description is that Schaffer preserves, inside a later historiographical argument, a biographical fact already available through the Wilson tradition.

## 6. Relation to Wilson: a two-generation control

Wilson remains the detailed biographical control for:

- comparative religion at Harvard;
- Everett and Toy;
- Lanman, Sanskrit and Buddhist sacred texts;
- Paris comparative religion;
- hoped-for Pāli/Buddhist study and occasional private reading with Sylvain Lévi.

Wilson's 1982 bibliography also preserves the 1898 Buddhist article and the 1901 `Syllabus: The Philosophy of Buddhism` as explicit Buddhist nodes.

Schaffer demonstrates the later afterlife of only part of this knowledge: Pāli/Sanskrit remain visible, but the technical Buddhist work is not integrated into his history of Lovejoy's historiographical practice.

Safe two-generation formulation:

> **The early Asian formation was not lost between Wilson and later Lovejoy historiography. What remained thin was the connection between that known formation and accounts of how Lovejoy actually learned to make historical judgments.**

---

## 7. Relation to the current JHI argument

Schaffer supports a three-part historiographical distinction:

1. **biographical visibility** — Wilson and Schaffer show that Lovejoy's Asian-language formation was known;
2. **technical practice** — notebook 004 plus the 1898 article allow reconstruction of what he did with Buddhist texts, scholarly authorities and disputed historical claims;
3. **later historiographical organization** — Schaffer's reconstruction of Lovejoy's significance proceeds through seriality, evolution, hierarchy, public intellectual action and *Great Chain*, not through the Buddhist/Indological work.

The intervention should therefore not be framed as rediscovering a forgotten Buddhist Lovejoy. It changes the historiographical status of a known episode by reconstructing the scholarly work inside it.

## 8. Publication-facing wording

Safe short version:

> **Simon Schaffer could still note in 2010 that Lovejoy studied Pāli and Sanskrit in Paris, while reconstructing Lovejoy's historiography through seriality, evolution and the *Great Chain of Being*.**

Follow immediately with the article's own delta:

> **The notebooks make it possible to ask a different question: what historical work was being done inside that already-known episode?**

Do not make Schaffer a straw man. His article has a different problem, and it is analytically sophisticated about Lovejoy's practice in its own domain.

---

## 9. Guardrails

Do not claim:

- Schaffer discusses Lovejoy's Buddhism in detail;
- Schaffer connects Pāli/Sanskrit to unit-ideas or to the later history-of-ideas method;
- Schaffer proves a causal line from Paris to *Great Chain*;
- absence of `Buddhism`, `Lanman`, `Lévi` or `1898` proves he did not know those facts;
- Schaffer's seriality analysis is a disguised version of the notebook argument.

The direct point is narrower and stronger: **Pāli/Sanskrit are biographically present but analytically undeveloped in a major later reconstruction of Lovejoy's historiography.**
