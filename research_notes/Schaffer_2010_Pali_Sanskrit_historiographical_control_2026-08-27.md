# Simon Schaffer 2010 — Pāli/Sanskrit biographical visibility and historiographical non-integration

Date: 2026-08-27
Status: **DIRECT SECONDARY CONTROL / HISTORIOGRAPHICAL POSITIONING**

Primary object for this historiographical control:

- Simon Schaffer, `Lovejoy's Series`, *History of Science* 48 (2010), 483–494.
- Direct PDF supplied and checked on 2026-08-27.

## Exact result

Schaffer explicitly includes Lovejoy's Asian-language study in a compact biographical passage. After summarizing Lovejoy's Harvard graduate study in 1895–98, he writes that William James discouraged him from taking a doctorate and encouraged him to go to Paris, `where he studied Pali and Sanskrit` (printed p.484; PDF page 2, continuing into p.485).

This is direct evidence that by 2010 the fact of Lovejoy's Pāli/Sanskrit study was not confined to Daniel J. Wilson's biography or bibliography. It had entered a major later historiographical treatment of Lovejoy.

## What Schaffer does with the fact

The sentence is biographical, not analytical. Schaffer's article immediately moves to Lovejoy's later Harvard appointments, public intellectual role, social reform, primitivism, evolution, seriality, and *The Great Chain of Being*. The article's central argument concerns the relation between seriality, Lovejoy's historiographical practice, evolution, hierarchy, and the role of the modern intellectual.

A full-text check produced:

- `Pali`: one substantive biographical occurrence;
- `Sanskrit`: the same single occurrence;
- `Buddh...`: no occurrence;
- `Oriental`: no occurrence;
- `religion`: one unrelated discussion of Christianity as a category in *Great Chain* methodology.

Therefore Schaffer does **not** develop Lovejoy's Pāli/Sanskrit study into a discussion of the 1898 Buddhist article, the 1901 Buddhism syllabus, Lanman, Lévi, notebook practice, or a Buddhist/Indological contribution to Lovejoy's historical method.

## Historiographical consequence

Schaffer sharpens the current novelty claim.

The safe formulation is not:

> `historians did not know that Lovejoy studied Buddhism / Pāli / Sanskrit`.

Instead:

> **The Asian-language formation was biographically visible even in later methodological historiography, but it could remain a one-sentence fact while the genealogy of Lovejoy's historical practice was reconstructed through other materials — especially seriality, evolution, public intellectual work, and the mature *Great Chain of Being*.**

This is a stronger control than Wilson alone because it shows that the pattern persists after the 1980s: the information survives, yet it is not made into a problem about scholarly practice.

## Relation to Wilson

Wilson remains the detailed biographical source for Harvard comparative religion, Lanman, Sanskrit/Buddhist study, Paris, and Lévi. Schaffer shows the afterlife of part of that biographical knowledge in later Lovejoy historiography.

Do not infer that Schaffer independently reconstructed Lovejoy's Pāli/Sanskrit training from archival records. His article repeatedly cites Wilson for Lovejoy's biography and later career; the Pāli/Sanskrit sentence itself is not separately unpacked at source level.

## Relation to the current JHI argument

This source is especially useful for one precise historiographical contrast:

1. **biographical visibility:** Wilson and Schaffer show that Lovejoy's Pāli/Sanskrit formation was known;
2. **technical practice:** notebook 004 plus the 1898 article allow reconstruction of what Lovejoy actually did with Buddhist sources and live scholarly disputes;
3. **methodological genealogy:** Schaffer's own reconstruction of Lovejoy's historiographical significance proceeds through series, evolution, hierarchy, public intellectual action, and *Great Chain*, not through the Buddhist/Indological work.

Thus the article should not present itself as discovering a forgotten Asian episode. Its stronger intervention is to change the **historiographical status** of a known episode by reconstructing the working practice behind it.

## Publication-facing wording

Safe short version:

> **Simon Schaffer could still summarize Lovejoy's Paris years in 2010 by noting that he studied Pāli and Sanskrit there; the fact was visible, but it did not become part of Schaffer's reconstruction of Lovejoy's historiographical practice.**

Safer if space is tight:

> **Lovejoy's Pāli and Sanskrit study was not forgotten; what remained largely unreconstructed was the technical historical work he did with those materials.**

## Guardrails

Do not claim:

- Schaffer discusses Lovejoy's Buddhism in detail;
- Schaffer connects Pāli/Sanskrit to unit-ideas or to the later history-of-ideas method;
- Schaffer proves a causal line from Paris to *Great Chain*;
- absence of the word `Buddhism` proves he was unaware of the 1898 article.

The direct point is narrower: Pāli/Sanskrit are biographically present but analytically undeveloped.
