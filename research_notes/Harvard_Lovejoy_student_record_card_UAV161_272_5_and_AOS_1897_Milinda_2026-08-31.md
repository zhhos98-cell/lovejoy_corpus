On 31 August 2026, Harvard University Archives Reference Archivist Edward Copenhagen replied to the reproduction inquiry concerning Arthur O. Lovejoy's Harvard course record.

Harvard identified and supplied a scan described as:

> **Arthur O. Lovejoy (AM 1897) record card [transcript]. IN: Graduate School of Arts and Sciences - Student Record Cards. UAV 161.272.5 File I Box 9. Harvard University Archives.**

This is the first precise archival locator recovered for the individual Lovejoy student record that had previously been known only indirectly through Daniel J. Wilson's citation to a "List of courses, supplied by Harvard University Archives."

The Harvard response therefore upgrades the former Wilson-mediated actor-level enrollment bridge to a recoverable individual primary record.

## 3. New adjacent primary discovery: Lovejoy had an announced Milinda-pañha paper in April 1897

While controlling the Harvard chronology, a separate direct primary source was recovered in the University of Chicago Library scan of American Oriental Society circulars.

The **American Oriental Society, One Hundred and Eighth Meeting — 1897, Final Circular, dated 10 April 1897** announces the annual meeting at Johns Hopkins University, Baltimore, on 22-24 April 1897. Under `LIST OF PAPERS ANNOUNCED TO DATE`, item 24 reads:

> **Mr. ARTHUR O. LOVEJOY, Harvard University; Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha.**

Direct scan:
`https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/ead/pdf/ofcpreshjb-0007-013.pdf`

Relevant scan pages in that PDF:
- PDF p.9: final circular heading/date and meeting information;
- PDF p.10: list of announced papers, including Lovejoy as item 24.

### What this proves

By **10 April 1897**, while still identified institutionally as `Harvard University`, Lovejoy had an **announced scholarly communication on the argument of the Pāli Buddhist text Milinda-pañha** for the American Oriental Society.

This materially revises the chronology. The 1898 JAOS article on `upādāna` and `upādisesa` is no longer the earliest known Lovejoy Buddhist research product. There is now a documented 1897 scholarly communication, at least at the announced-paper stage, centered on a Pāli Buddhist text.

The phrasing is itself significant: this was not merely a general talk on "Buddhism" but a **"Critical summary of the argument"** of a specific Buddhist textual work. That places Lovejoy's pre-1898 activity squarely in argument reconstruction / textual analysis, not only comparative-religion survey.

## 4. Delivery firewall: announced paper != demonstrated presentation

The 10 April 1897 circular is a final programme/circular listing papers `ANNOUNCED TO DATE`. It establishes the paper title, author, affiliation, and intended meeting context.

It does **not by itself prove** that Lovejoy personally attended or delivered the paper.

The same circular's subsequent list of members/friends expected to be present does not visibly include Lovejoy. The published JAOS proceedings for the Baltimore meeting (JAOS 18 [1897], 375-390) must therefore be checked item-by-item before upgrading:

`ANNOUNCED COMMUNICATION -> ACTUALLY READ / DISCUSSED COMMUNICATION`.

Current status:

- 1897 AOS announced Lovejoy Milinda-pañha paper: **DIRECT PRIMARY CONFIRMED**;
- actual oral delivery at Baltimore meeting: **UNRESOLVED**;
- surviving manuscript/text of the 1897 communication: **UNRESOLVED**;
- relation of the 1897 Milinda paper to the 1898 `upādāna / upādisesa` article: **STRONG RESEARCH QUESTION, NOT YET A PROVEN VERSION CHAIN**.

The earlier safe sequence was:

`Everett comparative religion -> Toy Hebrew religion -> Lanman Sanskrit/Buddhist-text environment -> notebook 004 -> 1898 JAOS technical Buddhist paper`.

The new sequence is finer-grained:

`Harvard GSAS individual course record (now located; contents pending)`

`-> notebook 004 / further Buddhist technical work`

`-> 1898 JAOS, "The Buddhistic technical terms upādāna and upādisesa"`.

This is a much stronger research trajectory than an isolated 1898 curiosity. It shows that Lovejoy was already producing specialist Buddhist argument-analysis while a Harvard graduate student.

The remaining high-value question is now extremely narrow and archival:

> **What exact Lanman/Sanskrit/Pāli course numbers and grades appear on Lovejoy's individual card, and in which academic year?**

If the card places Lovejoy in one of Lanman's Pāli courses before or during 1896-97, the actor-level link between formal Pāli instruction and the 1897 Milinda communication becomes direct. If it lists Sanskrit/Buddhist work but no Pāli course, that negative result is equally important and would sharpen the distinction between formal language enrollment and research practice.
