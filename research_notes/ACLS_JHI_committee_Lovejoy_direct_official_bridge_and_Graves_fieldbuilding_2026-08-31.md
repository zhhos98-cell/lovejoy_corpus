Official source:
American Council of Learned Societies, centennial history, *The First Century*.
https://www.acls.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/The-First-Century-1.pdf

This closes the general institutional bridge:

`ACLS RESEARCH-PLANNING COMMITTEE`
-> `LOVEJOY-CHAIRED INITIATIVE`

## II. Mortimer Graves — mediator hypothesis materially strengthened

Official ACLS biography states that Mortimer Graves:

- served as ACLS secretary in the early 1940s;
- later became executive director (1953–57);
- played a pivotal role in the wartime Intensive Language Program;
- secured Rockefeller support;
- coordinated Spoken Language materials across Russian, German, Spanish, Japanese, Chinese, Thai, Burmese and other languages.

Official source:
https://www.acls.org/mortimer-graves/

Pauline Yu's 2021 JAOS history directly reconstructs Graves's earlier role in Chinese Studies:

- from 1928, newly appointed Assistant Secretary of ACLS;
- worked with members of the American Oriental Society;
- conducted planning conferences, surveys and reports;
- secured funding for faculty posts, fellowships, curricula, bibliographies, journals, a new scholarly society and language training;
- his campaign materially helped build the field of Chinese studies in the United States.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.7817/jaos.141.4.2021.ar034

A 1930 direct public article by Graves, `The Need and the Plans for Chinese Studies in the United States`, appeared in *The Annals* 152, 370–377 and is now open on JSTOR.

Source:
https://www.jstor.org/stable/1016570

Thus Graves is no longer merely a speculative connector name. He was an actor specializing in **field-building infrastructure** across multiple scholarly jurisdictions.

## IV. Why this matters historiographically

Standard histories of intellectual history often organize change through named methodological actors: Lovejoy, Skinner, Pocock, Begriffsgeschichte, cultural history, global intellectual history.

ACLS history exposes a different unit:

`FIELD-BUILDING ORGANIZATION`.

A method or object can become durable because an organization creates:

- stable publication channels;
- trained readers/researchers;
- bibliographic tools;
- language capacity;
- conferences;
- posts/fellowships;
- cross-disciplinary liaison.

Therefore:

`METHOD HISTORY`
!= only `ARGUMENT HISTORY`.

The repo's formula remains:

`METHOD CHANGE = ARGUMENT + CARRIER + CURRICULUM + NETWORK + CANON`.

ACLS now supplies direct institutional evidence for the carrier/network side of that formula.
