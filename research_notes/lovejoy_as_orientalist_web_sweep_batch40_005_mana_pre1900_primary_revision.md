## Research move

Batch 38 used Nicolas Meylan’s history of the `mana` category to localize Marillier’s first `mana` **within the 1900 Grande Encyclopédie article `Religion`** to printed p.349. Direct primary-text collation of Marillier’s 1897 *RHR* article now requires an important chronological refinement:

> **Marillier demonstrably used and glossed `mana` in print by 1897, before Lovejoy’s 1898–99 Paris year.**

Therefore 1900 p.349 is a synthesis-stage occurrence, not the beginning of Marillier’s engagement with the category.

## 1. Primary 1897 wording

In `La place du totémisme dans l’évolution religieuse`, *RHR* 36 (1897), second installment, Marillier argues that taboos differ in the degree to which they may be broken. He writes that their sacred character is, in a sense, proportional to:

> `au mana, à la force surnaturelle et magique de ceux qui les ont imposés`

and adds that a being endowed with superior magical power or superior `mana` may often violate them with impunity.

This is direct primary evidence, not a later retrospective description.

## 2. Marillier links the 1897 statement back to his Melanesian-taboo research

Immediately after this discussion Marillier refers the reader to his own earlier study:

> `Sur le caractère religieux du tabou mélanésien`

in *Études de critique et d’histoire*, 2e série.

The 1897 use of `mana` therefore belongs inside an already established Melanesian taboo programme rather than appearing as an isolated borrowing of Codringtonian vocabulary.

## 3. Revised source chronology

### 1891 — Codrington

R. H. Codrington publishes *The Melanesians*, the central named source for Melanesian `mana`.

### 1892 — Marillier engages Codrington/Frazer

Bibliographically secure:

- Marillier reviews Codrington, *The Melanesians*, *RHR* 25 (1892), pp.231–232;
- Marillier publishes `M. Frazer et la Diane de Nemi`, *RHR* 25 (1892), pp.71–99.

Nicolas Meylan reports that Marillier already mentions Melanesian `mana` in the latter. This remains **secondary-supported but not yet primary-collated**. The exact page is on the manual retrieval queue.

### 1896 — Melanesian taboo dossier

Marillier publishes `Sur le caractère religieux du tabou mélanésien`. Mauss’s contemporary 1896 discussion directs readers to pp.53–56 as a place where many Melanesian texts were assembled.

### 1897 — direct primary `mana`

Now secure: Marillier explicitly glosses `mana` as `force surnaturelle et magique` and uses differential mana to explain differential force of taboo.

### 1898–99 — Lovejoy in the workshop

The EPHE report names Lovejoy among auditors taking an active part in Marillier’s work. The two conferences concern survivance/justice and human sacrifice/ritual anthropophagy.

### 005 — efficacy decomposition

The later notebook pages distinguish:

- personal/spirit-mediated efficacy;
- efficacy of blood/body parts;
- transferable qualities;
- charms/plants/stones credited with efficacy in themselves;
- fetish efficacy through an indwelling/present spirit;
- magical sacrifice acting directly on forces of nature;
- quasi-mechanical causal operation.

The vocabulary in the notebook is broader than `mana`; the important continuity is the classificatory problem of **where efficacy resides and how it passes**.

### 1900 — Marillier synthesis

Meylan reports that `Religion` uses `mana` six times and first introduces it within that article at printed p.349. The article treats a dispersed/common magical-divine force and mechanical or constraining efficacy in sacrifice/magic.

This is best interpreted as consolidation of an already active category.

### 1906 — Lovejoy abstraction

`Primitive Energetics` isolates impersonal, diffused, transmissible/communicable efficacy as a general comparative category.

## 4. Why 1897 matters more than moving a date backward three years

The 1897 occurrence is embedded in Marillier’s methodological attack on a single explanatory system. In the same pages he argues:

- one theory may interpret part of the facts without explaining the whole class;
- a heterogeneous group should not be created merely from external resemblances;
- a partial explanation should not be rejected simply because it is not sufficient everywhere;
- taboo can depend on differential supernatural/magical power rather than one uniform categorical imperative.

Thus `mana` is already functioning inside the precise **anti-unilateral operator** that becomes visible in 005.

This is more useful than a bare word genealogy.

## 5. Direct notebook connection

### 005 PDF p.64 / MS p.129

Lovejoy describes magical sacrifice `in a sort of machine` and distinguishes constraint of divine will from direct action upon natural forces.

### 005 PDF pp.84–88 / MS pp.171–179

Blood, bodily remains and objects may work through independent protective or magical efficacy. The notebook then separates:

- fetish: efficacy because a spirit inhabits/is present in an object;
- charm/amulet/plant/stone: efficacy may be intrinsic to the object itself.

### 005 PDF pp.106–119

Eating selected organs is separated from communion and read through transmissible qualities / sympathetic efficacy: head/brain → intelligence, heart → courage/life, sexual organs → sexual power.

The notebook therefore converts the broad question of supernatural efficacy into a finer mechanism taxonomy.

## 6. Strongest safe formulation

> **Marillier was using `mana` as a category of supernatural and magical force in print by 1897, immediately before Lovejoy’s documented participation in his EPHE research programme. Lovejoy’s 005 notebook does not simply repeat the term; it repeatedly distinguishes the locations and modes of efficacy — spirit-mediated, intrinsic, transferable and direct — that later become a generalized problem in `Primitive Energetics`.**

This avoids claiming lexical borrowing where the stronger evidence is methodological transformation.
