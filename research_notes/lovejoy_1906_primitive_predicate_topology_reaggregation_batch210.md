# Lovejoy 1906 — predicate topology of `primitive`: from relational decomposition to collective reaggregation (Batch 210)

Date: 2026-08-26  
Status: synced  
Scope: Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`, *The Monist* 16.3 (1906), 357–382, read against notebooks 004/005 and Batches 157/208/209.  
Chronological rule: ordinary forward tracing stops at **1910**; later material enters only for explicit Buddhism / India / Sanskrit nodes.

## Core result

Batch 157 already established that Lovejoy’s 1906 article combines historical/genealogical pluralism with **mechanism universalism**: independently derived rites can instantiate the same impersonal, transferable, quasi-mechanical causal architecture without sharing one institutional origin.

Batch 210 isolates a different issue: **what happens to the predicate `primitive` when Lovejoy moves from notebook decomposition to published comparative theory?**

The result is a significant reaggregation.

In notebook 005, `primitive` can function as a mobile address for a relation-specific historical stratum: `Greek primitive`, a primitive plant-soul representation, an older non-moral mechanism beneath later ethical eschatology, or a more primitive naturalism surviving inside a later cult complex. The historical object is internally stratified.

In 1906, Lovejoy extracts one recurring causal relation from heterogeneous cases and gives it three increasingly broad addresses:

1. **mechanism/category** — impersonal, communicable, quasi-mechanical efficacy;
2. **belief/philosophy** — `Primitive Energetics`, the `fundamental concept of the primitive philosophy`;
3. **collective cognitive subject** — `savage thought`, `most savages`, `many or most races` at the relevant stage of culture.

Thus the publication does not merely preserve the mobility of `primitive` as an internal stratum. It **re-totalizes** one cross-cutting stratum into a generalized actor-level philosophy.

The strongest formulation is:

> **Lovejoy’s 1906 operation is decomposition followed by reaggregation: local rites and observer labels are broken apart until a recurrent causal mechanism can be isolated; independent regional instances of that mechanism are then correlated; finally the resulting abstraction is attributed back to a collective subject as the fundamental concept of `primitive philosophy`.**

This is not genealogical monism. It is **epistemic re-totalization after genealogical decomposition**.

`predicate topology`, `reaggregation`, and `epistemic re-totalization` are project analytical terms, not Lovejoy’s vocabulary.

---

## 1. Anti-duplication: how this differs from Batch 157

Batch 157 asked:

> Does 1906 abandon 005’s local pluralism by making one master theory?

Its answer was:

> historical pluralism can coexist with mechanism universalism.

That result remains secure. A shared causal mechanism does not imply a shared institutional ancestor.

Batch 210 asks something else:

> **After Lovejoy abstracts the recurrent mechanism, to whom or to what does he predicate it?**

This matters because a comparative category can remain analyst-owned, or it can be reattributed as the actual philosophy/thought of the populations compared.

The 1906 paper clearly makes the second move.

The analytical chain is therefore:

`heterogeneous local data`  
→ `mechanism abstraction`  
→ `comparative convergence`  
→ **`primitive philosophy` as collective actor-level category**.

That last arrow is the new object of Batch 210.

---

## 2. Predicate level 1: `primitive` attaches to a causal category

At printed p.359 Lovejoy rejects personality as the most fundamental category and proposes a conception of causation that is:

- impersonal;
- quasi-mechanical;
- diffused through persons and things;
- unequally localized;
- communicable / transferable;
- manipulable through contact, insulation and ritual procedure.

At this level the historical object is narrow. Lovejoy is defining a **causal architecture**.

The object does not need to be a whole religion or a whole culture. The same causal relation can appear in:

- body power;
- magical objects;
- rites;
- agriculture;
- taboo/contact;
- sacred terminology;
- unusual natural efficacy.

This continues 005’s mechanism-first classification.

At the end of the article Lovejoy proposes `Primitive Energetics` as a name for this **group of beliefs**.

The word `primitive` therefore first functions as a predicate of a mechanism/belief family.

---

## 3. Predicate level 2: the mechanism becomes `primitive philosophy`

The title already performs a major scalar move:

> `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`.

The article is not framed merely as:

> a recurring magical mechanism found in several places.

It is framed as recovery of a **fundamental concept** belonging to a broader `primitive philosophy`.

Lovejoy explicitly says the heterogeneous practices possess a recoverable `logic`, and that the paper aims to bring greater `system` and `connectedness` to familiar ethnographic facts.

This means the cross-cutting causal relation is elevated from one mechanism among others to an organizing principle of a reconstructed worldview.

Batch 157 showed that Lovejoy ranks the category as:

- `more fundamental than personality`;
- `most nearly ubiquitous`;
- `most all-pervasive`;
- highly influential;
- perhaps earlier than the personal/anthropomorphic conception.

The move is therefore not simply abstraction. It is:

> **abstraction → hierarchy → philosophical centrality.**

The relation-specific stratum is being made architectonic.

---

## 4. Predicate level 3: the reconstructed philosophy is attributed to `savage thought`

The article then shifts from the analyst’s comparative category to the cognition of the people studied.

Its evidentiary ambition is explicitly actor-level. The linguistic sections ask whether native vocabularies themselves express the proposed universal, impersonal and communicable power. Lovejoy is unwilling to treat the category as merely a useful modern typology.

The reconstructed force is said to operate in **`savage thought`** according to regular and intelligible laws; practical life is organized around possession, transfer, control and insulation of this power.

Batch 106 already identified this crucial epistemic address:

> analyst decomposes ethnographic reports → reconstructs an abstract causal category → attributes that abstraction to primitive/native thought.

Batch 210 places that address in the `primitive` topology. A causal mechanism has now become a predicate of a **collective style of thought**.

This is precisely the step later critics such as Jung would challenge: the abstraction may be an analyst’s conceptual construction even if grounded in indigenous terms and practices.

Within the 1910 cutoff, the important point is simply that Lovejoy himself treats it as actor-level philosophy, not merely analyst shorthand.

---

## 5. Predicate level 4: `primitive` is reattached to broad populations

The article’s strongest universalizing sentences move beyond `a category found in several source fields`.

Lovejoy speaks of:

- `many or most races` at the relevant stage of culture;
- the `dominant preoccupation, probably, of most savages`;
- independent observers of `three wholly distinct races` whose descriptions display near-verbal identity.

At printed p.376, the independence of North-American, Oceanic and African fields is part of the evidence. Convergence is not explained genealogically; it is used to support a broadly recurring causal-semantic type.

The crucial political/epistemic transformation is:

> **historically independent populations are disaggregated at the level of institutions but reaggregated at the level of philosophy/cognition.**

Lovejoy does not say their rites share one origin. He says, in effect, that their heterogeneous practices disclose the same fundamental category.

The universal object is therefore no longer a historical institution. It is a **collective causal Weltanschauung**.

---

## 6. 005 → 1906 is therefore not a simple movement from totalization to pluralism

A tempting developmental story would be:

`evolutionist whole-people categories`  
→ `005 decomposition / layered chronology`  
→ `more plural modern analysis`.

The 1906 publication blocks that trajectory.

The actual sequence is more complicated:

### 005

- outward forms are decomposed;
- local mechanisms are separated;
- genealogies remain plural;
- historical layers coexist;
- `primitive` can identify a relation-specific stratum.

### 1906

- one recurrent mechanism is abstracted across those plural histories;
- the mechanism becomes a `fundamental category`;
- the category is claimed to be pervasive or dominant;
- it is attributed to `primitive philosophy` and `savage thought` across broad populations.

Therefore:

> **decomposition can generate a new kind of totalization.**

This is one of the strongest current corrections to any narrative in which finer analytical discrimination automatically reduces political or civilizational generalization.

---

## 7. Why the re-totalization is not the old whole-people chronology

The new totalization differs structurally from a simple unilineal ladder.

Lovejoy is not saying:

> all these peoples occupy the same point because their institutions are historically identical.

Nor is he deriving the diverse practices from a single ancestral rite.

Instead:

1. institutions and rites can have different histories;
2. the same causal mechanism can recur independently;
3. independent recurrence licenses a comparative category;
4. the category is then generalized as a common feature of `primitive philosophy`.

Thus the homogenizing operation shifts **up one analytical level**:

- diversity is preserved at the level of institutional genealogy;
- homogeneity is reconstructed at the level of causal cognition.

This is exactly why mechanism universalism and historical pluralism can coexist.

The political effect of `primitive` is consequently more subtle than simple staging. It can totalize heterogeneous peoples by assigning them a shared **fundamental causal category** rather than a shared historical institution.

---

## 8. Internal temporalization and collective reaggregation are complementary, not contradictory

Batches 208–209 showed that `primitive` becomes portable once historical time penetrates below the level of whole traditions:

- primitive Greek layer;
- primitive naturalistic layer;
- primitive plant-soul representation;
- older non-moral mechanism under later morality.

That mobility creates the conditions for the 1906 comparative abstraction.

Because a `primitive` feature can be isolated **inside** different historically heterogeneous objects, Lovejoy can compare those features without requiring the whole objects to be contemporaneous, genealogically related or institutionally identical.

The operation is:

`A contains primitive relation M`  
`B contains primitive relation M`  
`C contains primitive relation M`

while:

`A, B, C` may have different institutional histories.

Then:

`M recurs independently`  
→ `M is fundamental to primitive philosophy`.

Thus **internal stratification enables cross-cultural reaggregation**.

This may be the key structural relation between the 005 notebook and the 1906 paper.

---

## 9. The evidentiary brakes remain real

The re-totalization should not be described as completely unqualified.

Batch 157 established three important self-limitations in the article:

- Lovejoy says he initially states the thesis `somewhat dogmatically, and somewhat too categorically`;
- the linguistic demonstration is only the `first stage` of the inquiry;
- the testimonies assembled are explicitly incomplete and are offered partly to elicit further evidence.

Batch 77 also showed that he does not treat source convergence crudely. Witnesses are decomposed proposition by proposition: an observer may be trusted for a custom or native lexical usage while rejected for theological translation or semantic abstraction.

Therefore the 1906 re-totalization is not methodologically careless in a simple sense.

A more exact formulation is:

> **Lovejoy builds a highly granular evidentiary apparatus in order to support a very large-scale collective abstraction.**

The micro-level proof discipline and macro-level universalizing ambition coexist.

That asymmetry is historically more interesting than describing him either as a crude evolutionist or as a proto-pluralist.

## 10. A predicate topology of `primitive` in 005 / 1906

| Predicate address | 005 | 1906 | Historical effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| specific representation / relation | primitive plant-soul form; older non-moral structures | impersonal / quasi-mechanical causal relation | isolates an earlier or more fundamental mechanism |
| intratraditional layer | `Greek primitive`; older naturalism under later cult | much less prominent as explicit intratraditional wording | detaches `primitive` from whole non-Western peoples |
| belief/mechanism family | ritual, magical, social determinants separated case-by-case | `Primitive Energetics` | cross-case comparative abstraction |
| philosophy / worldview | not stabilized as one whole philosophy | `primitive philosophy`, recoverable `logic` | reaggregates mechanisms into a systematic cognitive object |
| collective thought | heterogeneous actor fields remain highly local in notebook | `savage thought` | attributes analyst’s abstraction back to actors |
| populations / races | `primitive peoples`, `higher stage`, etc. coexist with internal layering | `most savages`, `many or most races`, `three wholly distinct races` | gives the category broad demographic/civilizational scope |

This matrix shows that 1906 does not merely repeat the notebook’s use of `primitive`. It **moves the predicate upward in scale**.

## 11. Political consequence: stratification can widen rather than weaken the reach of `primitive`

This is the central political result.

Internal stratification initially looks like a retreat from whole-people hierarchy because it allows:

- a canonical Greek tradition to have a primitive layer;
- a later religion to preserve an earlier mechanism;
- a historically complex society to contain heterogeneous strata.

But precisely because the category has become detachable from whole societies, it can travel across them.

Once isolated, the same `primitive` relation can be found in multiple traditions and then assembled into a cross-cultural comparative type.

The epistemic sequence is:

> **internal temporalization → portability → cross-cultural equivalence → collective reattribution.**

Thus fragmentation of the historical object does not necessarily reduce hierarchy. It can make the primitive category more **granular and extensible**.

Instead of saying:

> `this entire people is primitive`,

one can say:

> `this mechanism is primitive`,

find that mechanism across many peoples, and then reconstruct:

> `this is the fundamental concept of primitive philosophy`.

That is a different, arguably more technically powerful, form of temporal/civilizational classification.

## 12. Connection to the larger post-Enlightenment chronology thesis

Batch 209 proposed that after homogeneous whole-object chronology breaks down, historical ordering can move inside the object through relation-specific strata.

Batch 210 adds the second half of the mechanism:

> **strata can subsequently be compared and reassembled across objects.**

This yields a two-movement model:

### Movement I — decomposition / internal stratification

A religion, text or rite is separated into components with different:

- origins;
- logical positions;
- causal functions;
- historical ages;
- imported / local statuses.

### Movement II — transverse reaggregation

Components assigned the same relational type are grouped across otherwise heterogeneous traditions.

`Primitive` can serve as the index that makes this transverse grouping possible.

The resulting chronology is neither Gatterer-like table nor simple people-by-people evolution. It is closer to a **stratigraphic comparative grid**:

- vertical axis: layers inside each tradition/object;
- horizontal axis: equivalence among similarly classified layers across traditions.

This is a project-level analytical model, not Lovejoy’s diagram or terminology.

It may provide a particularly strong technical explanation for how `primitive` reorganizes world-historical chronology after a unified homogeneous temporal sequence becomes difficult to sustain.

## 14. Strongest current paper formulation

> **Notebook 005 and the 1906 `Primitive Philosophy` essay reveal two complementary operations. In the notebook, Lovejoy repeatedly fragments present religious complexes into asynchronous relations: inherited and borrowed elements, non-moral and moral strata, naturalistic and ancestral cults, magical and sacramental mechanisms. This decomposition allows `primitive` to become a mobile predicate of a relation rather than only of an entire people. The 1906 essay then reverses direction. Lovejoy isolates one such relation — impersonal, transferable, quasi-mechanical efficacy — across historically independent North-American, Oceanic and African materials, promotes it to a fundamental comparative category, and attributes the abstraction back to `savage thought` as the central concept of `primitive philosophy`. The result is not a return to single-origin evolutionism. It is a new totalization constructed out of decomposed strata: heterogeneous histories are reunited at the level of causal cognition.**

Compact version:

> **Lovejoy breaks cultures apart historically, then puts them back together comparatively. `Primitive` is one of the operators that makes both moves possible.**

That sentence is project analytical language and should not be quoted as Lovejoy’s own formulation.
