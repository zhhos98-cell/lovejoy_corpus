# Batch 93 — `Mechanical` is not one proposition: Hubert–Mauss → Lovejoy → Leuba → Saintyves → Malinowski and the spectrum from analogy to identity

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: correct a possible over-reading produced by Batches 91–92. The appearance of `mechanical` language downstream of Lovejoy is not by itself diagnostic of Lovejoy reception. Before and around 1906, mana/magical-force discourse already used mechanical physics as an analogy, contrast class, causal model, and eventually even genealogy. This batch distinguishes those operations propositionally.

## Core result

The phrase-family `mechanical force / mechanical power / mechanical factor` belongs to a **distributed comparative-religion vocabulary**, not uniquely to Lovejoy's `Primitive Energetics`.

The crucial distinction is that different writers use the mechanical analogy at different logical strengths:

1. **Hubert–Mauss 1902–03:** magical force is explicitly **non-mechanical**, yet its concept is comparable to mechanical force because both name the cause of a class of effects. Mana is spiritual and does not act mechanically, though it can corroborate ordinary mechanical efficacy. This is **functional analogy under ontological distinction**.
2. **Lovejoy 1906:** impersonal efficacy is characterized as **quasi-mechanical** and as operating according to `quite regular and intelligible laws`, available for study/control. This is a stronger **causal-law assimilation**.
3. **Leuba 1912:** rejects Lovejoy's stronger mechanical reading because no definite stable quantitative means–effects relation is shown. This is **mechanicality as an empirical proof burden**.
4. **Saintyves 1914:** pushes the comparison farthest, treating dynamistic notions of force/energy/mana as a universal form of thought and, in Meylan's controlled synopsis, arguing that modern physical unification of forces ultimately depends historically/conceptually on the mana hypothesis. This is **genealogical/identity escalation**; direct text is bibliographically available at Warburg/Gallica, but proposition-level wording here remains secondary-controlled until page images are collated.
5. **Malinowski 1915:** rejects sharply abstract primitive concepts/laws while calling manitu `in essence a mechanical factor`. This is **analyst-level causal description combined with anti-abstraction**.

The reception consequence is important:

> **mechanical vocabulary is not a Lovejoy fingerprint.**

What is more distinctive about Lovejoy is the **conjunction**:

`impersonal/communicable efficacy`
+
`native-language/grammar reproof`
+
`regular and intelligible causal laws`
+
`general philosophical category`
+
`Primitive Energetics`.

A downstream `mechanical` phrase without that conjunction must not be coded as hidden Lovejoy reception.

## 1. Hubert–Mauss already distinguish `comparable to mechanical force` from `mechanical force`

Henri Hubert and Marcel Mauss's *Esquisse d'une théorie générale de la magie*, originally published in *L'Année sociologique* VII (1902–03), supplies a direct pre-Lovejoy control.

Their argument is unusually explicit. At the general level of magical power they say:

- the magician, rite and spirit express different forms of one force;
- this force is **not mechanical but magical**;
- nevertheless the concept of magical force is **entirely comparable to our concept of mechanical force**;
- just as mechanical `force` names the cause of apparent movements, magical force names the cause of magical effects such as illness/death or happiness/health.

This is a logical analogy between **explanatory roles**, not an assertion that magical causation is ordinary mechanics.

They reinforce the distinction in the mana section:

- mana is a spiritual force;
- it **does not act mechanically** and works at a distance;
- it is the force of magician and rite;
- it is a distributed, non-fixed efficacy;
- as `efficacité véritable des choses`, it **corroborates ordinary mechanical action without annihilating it**.

Thus the pre-1906 vocabulary already contains all three terms:

`magical force`
`
mechanical force`
`
comparison between them`.

But the comparison is carefully bounded.

### Code

`Hubert–Mauss mechanicality`
→ `FUNCTIONAL ANALOGY`
→ `ONTOLOGICAL/CAUSAL DISTINCTION RETAINED`.

This matters because later historians can easily flatten `comparable to mechanical force` into `mechanical force`. The primary text does not permit that collapse.

---

## 2. Hubert–Mauss also show why `mechanical efficacy` is polysemous

The same essay occasionally uses mechanical vocabulary in another sense. In discussing collective rites that are not yet fully organized cults, Hubert–Mauss mention `contrainte et l'efficacité mécanique directe, sans intermédiaires spirituels différenciés`.

That phrase does not cancel their earlier statement that mana as spiritual force `n'agit pas mécaniquement`. It shows that `mechanical` can name different analytical dimensions:

- direct/automatic ritual efficacy without differentiated spirit intermediaries;
- ordinary physical mechanical action;
- modern scientific concept of force;
- analogy between the explanatory role of magical and mechanical forces.

Hence even inside one foundational text, `mechanical` is **not a unitary ontology word**.

### Historiographic rule

> **mechanical vocabulary must be coded by proposition and function, not keyword.**

This is the same source-function lesson developed for Lovejoy and Leuba, now applied to a single technical adjective.

---

## 3. Lovejoy's move is stronger than Hubert–Mauss's analogy

Lovejoy 1906 also speaks of impersonal, communicable, quasi-mechanical force/energy, but the argumentative burden is different.

His strongest claim is not simply that primitive magical causation has an explanatory role analogous to modern mechanics. He says the force is understood as working according to **quite regular and intelligible laws**, can be studied/controlled, and therefore warrants the label `Primitive Energetics`.

Batch77–78 showed how Lovejoy attempts to support this via high-resolution native linguistic/grammatical evidence, especially Jones on manitou and Hetherwick on mulungu, after explicitly limiting lower-resolution missionary glosses.

So relative to Hubert–Mauss:

### Hubert–Mauss

`magical force : mechanical force`
→ analogy of causal/explanatory function
→ magic remains a distinctive social/spiritual medium.

### Lovejoy

`impersonal native efficacy`
→ regular/intelligible causal law
→ comparative primitive philosophy / energetics.

Lovejoy's novelty therefore cannot be reduced to importing physics vocabulary. The stronger move is **turning the analogy into a proposition about the regularity and intelligibility of the primitive causal system itself**.

---

## 4. Leuba turns `mechanical` into a proof threshold

Leuba 1912 is best understood against this prehistory.

He does not object merely to the word `energy` or to physical analogy. He tests the proposition at the point where Lovejoy has strengthened it into regular causal law. His objection is relational and quasi-quantitative: ritual means used to invoke Wakonda / Manitou / Mana do not display a definite and stable quantitative relation to the effects produced.

Hence:

`Lovejoy: mechanical/regular law`
→ requires `stable means–effects relation`.

Leuba:
→ evidence does not meet threshold
→ call it magical force rather than mechanical power.

That distinction actually returns Leuba toward Hubert–Mauss's earlier conceptual separation between `force magique` and `force mécanique`, even though Leuba's immediate argument is his own empirical audit.

Do not infer a Hubert–Mauss → Leuba causal route from this structural resemblance without direct citation analysis.

## 6. Saintyves 1914 demonstrates how far the analogy could be escalated without Lovejoy

Pierre Saintyves's *La force magique: du mana des primitifs au dynamisme scientifique* (Paris: É. Nourry, 1914) is directly bibliographically controlled through the Warburg Institute Digital Library and BnF catalogue/Gallica notices. Warburg describes a 136-page book explicitly organized from `La force magique` through `L'Hypothèse dynamique` to a conclusion, with subjects including mana and force/energy.

Meylan's page-specific secondary reading reports a much stronger thesis than Hubert–Mauss or Lovejoy:

- Saintyves groups force, energy and mana among dynamistic notions;
- he argues modern physical laws such as gravitation and thermodynamics are ultimately deductive developments from a universal impersonal-force hypothesis;
- at p.110 he claims the modern idea that gravitation, heat, light, electricity and magnetism are forms of one fundamental energy depends, through Renaissance natural philosophy, on the theory of mana.

This is a radical **genealogical identity/escalation** of the mana–physics relation.

The important control for the Lovejoy project is negative: web searches of the currently indexed Saintyves record/textual discussions have not surfaced Lovejoy or `Primitive Energetics`. That is not proof Saintyves did not read Lovejoy. But it shows that very strong mana↔physics claims existed in 1914 within a broader dynamist field and must not automatically be attributed to Lovejoy.

## 7. Malinowski's `mechanical factor` becomes less diagnostic after Hubert–Mauss

Batch91–92 treated Malinowski 1915 cautiously as a citation-shadow relay because:

- he cites Jones, King and Leuba in the manitu/mana dossier;
- he also names Hubert–Mauss, Marett, Preuss and Durkheim;
- he does not name Lovejoy;
- he calls manitu `in essence a mechanical factor` while rejecting sharply abstract primitive concepts and laws.

The Hubert–Mauss primary control now lowers the diagnostic value of that phrase still further.

Malinowski had a directly cited pre-Lovejoy source in which:

- magical force is explicitly compared to mechanical force;
- mana is analyzed as generalized efficacy;
- mechanical action and mana-efficacy are distinguished and related;
- some ritual efficacy is described as directly mechanical in another analytical sense.

Therefore the phrase `mechanical factor` cannot carry a Lovejoy relay inference by itself.

The safe coding is:

`Malinowski mechanical factor`
→ `DISTRIBUTED VOCABULARY / MULTIPLE LIVE ROUTES`
→ `LOVEJOY-SPECIFIC DEPENDENCE UNIDENTIFIED`.

Batch91's citation-shadow claim remains valid at the level of **availability through Leuba**, but not at the level of lexical fingerprinting.

## 8. A spectrum replaces the binary `mechanical / non-mechanical`

The historical field can now be coded as a spectrum of logical operations:

| author | mechanical relation | logical strength |
|---|---|---|
| Hubert–Mauss 1902–03 | magical force comparable to mechanical force; mana itself non-mechanical/spiritual | ANALOGY / FUNCTIONAL HOMOLOGY |
| Jones 1905 | impersonal efficacy/transfer but interpretation not governed by regular rule | CAUSAL EFFICACY WITHOUT REGULAR MECHANICS |
| Lovejoy 1906 | quasi-mechanical, regular/intelligible laws, study/control | CAUSAL-LAW ASSIMILATION |
| King 1910 | mechanical-force comparison likely at p.139 | SECONDARY-CONTROLLED; PRIMARY STRENGTH HOLD |
| Leuba 1912 | rejects mechanical label absent stable quantitative means–effects relation | EMPIRICAL THRESHOLD / ROLLBACK |
| Saintyves 1914 | mana/dynamism genealogically continuous with scientific universal force | GENEALOGICAL/IDENTITY ESCALATION |
| Malinowski 1915 | vague/felt native force described analytically as mechanical factor | ANALYST-LEVEL MECHANICAL DESCRIPTION + ANTI-ABSTRACTION |

This spectrum is more useful than asking who `believed mana was mechanical`.

## 9. What is actually diagnostic of Lovejoy?

After this control, the mechanical vocabulary alone should be removed from the project's list of strong Lovejoy fingerprints.

### Weak fingerprints

- `force`;
- `energy`;
- `mechanical`;
- electricity analogy;
- impersonal power;
- mana/manitou/orenda comparative grouping.

All circulated broadly.

### Stronger Lovejoy packet

A downstream text becomes more plausibly Lovejoy-dependent when several of the following co-occur:

1. explicit Lovejoy name / Monist XVI locator;
2. `Primitive Energetics` or `manitouism` terminology;
3. `quite regular and intelligible laws` proposition;
4. contrast with Marett's `supernormal/anomalous` emphasis;
5. efficacy/power/productivity as semantic core rather than extraordinariness;
6. native grammar/language used as proof of abstraction/impersonality;
7. the specific North America → Oceania → Africa comparative architecture;
8. evidence dossier reused with Lovejoy's citation grouping/order.

This gives the reception census a much better false-positive filter.

## 10. Historiographic payoff

The early mana field did not borrow a single metaphor from physics and then transmit it unchanged. `Mechanical` acted as an **analogy operator** whose strength had to be specified each time.

Hubert–Mauss used mechanics to clarify the causal function of magical force while insisting on its non-mechanical spiritual mode. Lovejoy converted impersonal efficacy into a more strongly law-governed primitive causal philosophy. Leuba made that strengthening answer to a measurable proof burden. Saintyves could escalate mana into a remote genealogy of modern physical energetics. Malinowski could restore a mechanical descriptor at the analyst's level while denying that primitive actors possessed sharply abstract laws.

A compact formula:

> **the vocabulary travels more easily than the logical strength of the analogy.**

And for reception method:

> **lexical recurrence is weaker evidence than recurrence of an inferential configuration.**

## Working formulation

> **Lovejoy's `Primitive Energetics` did not introduce physical language into the mana debate. Hubert and Mauss had already compared magical force to mechanical force while explicitly denying that mana acted mechanically. Lovejoy's distinctive move was to strengthen impersonal efficacy into a system governed by regular and intelligible causal laws and to re-prove that abstraction through native linguistic evidence. The subsequent field repeatedly altered the strength of the comparison: Leuba rejected the mechanical inference, Saintyves radicalized the mana–physics relation into a genealogy of scientific energetics, and Malinowski combined primitive conceptual vagueness with an analyst's description of manitu as a mechanical factor. Mechanical vocabulary therefore cannot serve as a Lovejoy fingerprint; inferential configuration must replace keyword recurrence as the unit of reception evidence.**
