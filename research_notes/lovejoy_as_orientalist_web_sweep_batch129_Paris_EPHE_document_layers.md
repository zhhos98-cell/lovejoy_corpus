# Batch 129 — Paris EPHE document layers: programme, actual teaching, participant status, and the register beneath them

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: continue the Paris 1898–99 line while dynamic-viewer manuscripts are being retrieved separately. This pass asks what different EPHE documentary genres actually prove and uses that distinction to tighten Lovejoy notebook 005.

## Core result

The Paris evidence can now be organized into **four non-equivalent institutional layers**:

1. **advance programme** — what the Section intended to teach, with advertised days/times;
2. **annual retrospective report** — what the director says was actually taught and who materially participated;
3. **participant/enrolment layer** — named élèves/auditeurs and, beneath the published report, administrative registers;
4. **individualized instruction** — work that can fall outside or cut across a formal course title, documented independently in the Lévi–Mauss teaching relationship.

This distinction resolves several apparent tensions in the current Paris reconstruction. It also produces a new archival payoff: the contemporary Vᵉ Section rules say people admitted to conferences were required to **sign/register on the register before each lesson** and that prolonged absences had to be justified. Therefore a lesson-level attendance documentary layer existed. If the relevant physical register survives in the Archives nationales digitized EPHE corpus, it could potentially constrain Lovejoy's Paris arrival and actual sequence of attendance much more tightly than the annual report.

Caution: this rule does **not** prove that the `registres d'inscription` named in the 2025 digitization project are the identical lesson-by-lesson register. That equivalence must be established at cote/component level.

## 1. Notebook 005's two dominant themes are the exact advertised Marillier programme for Lovejoy's year

The official Vᵉ Section programme for **1898–99** advertises:

- `Les croyances relatives à la survivance de l'âme` — Wednesday, 5 p.m.;
- `L'anthropophagie rituelle et les sacrifices humains` — Friday, 9:30 a.m.

Primary programme:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1897_num_11_7_19495

The retrospective annual report then says Marillier actually pursued:

- the survivance/eschatology problem, explicitly testing whether a moral element enters the earliest afterlife conceptions and defending continuation rather than compensation/reward/punishment;
- continued research on human sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy, comparing sacrifice types and mechanisms across southern Africa, Guinea, North America, non-Aryan India, animal sacrifice and agrarian/fecundity rites.

The same report names:

`MM. Lovejoy, W. S. Andrews et Lorette`

as **`Auditeurs ayant pris une part active aux travaux`**.

Primary report:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1898_num_12_8_19501

Thus the notebook 005 thematic bridge is no longer merely:

`005 resembles Marillier's interests`.

It is:

`exact advertised themes`

→ `retrospective report confirms those themes were actually worked`

→ `same report explicitly places Lovejoy among active participants`.

This is currently the strongest direct institutional evidence for the Paris provenance/context of 005.

## 2. Human sacrifice / ritual anthropophagy was a multi-year Marillier research line, not a one-year topic invented when Lovejoy arrived

The advance programme for **1897–98**, one academic year before Lovejoy's documented participation, already advertises:

`L'anthropophagie rituelle et les sacrifices humains`

for Wednesdays at 9:30.

Primary programme:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1896_num_10_6_20489

The retrospective report for 1897–98 confirms that this conference actually ran and studied funerary, expiatory, anthropophagic and magical sacrifices across western/African material.

Primary report:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1897_num_11_7_19478

Then the 1898–99 annual report explicitly calls the Lovejoy-year sacrifice conference a **continuation** — `Suite des recherches sur les Sacrifices humains et l'anthropophagie rituelle`.

This matters for notebook 005. Its long sacrifice/cannibalism blocks should not be modeled as evidence that Lovejoy necessarily witnessed the genesis of Marillier's argument in 1898–99. The safer model is:

> **Lovejoy entered an already-running multi-year seminar research programme and participated during a documented continuation phase.**

That raises a useful transmission question for 005: which notebook strata preserve material presented in 1898–99, and which may summarize or inherit arguments whose seminar development began before Lovejoy arrived?

## 3. Advance programme and actual schedule are not identical

An unexpected documentary discrepancy appears inside the same year.

The advance 1898–99 programme advertises Marillier's two conferences on **Wednesday and Friday**. The retrospective report labels the survivance conference `Conférence du lundi` and the sacrifice conference `Conférence du mardi`.

Sources:

- advance programme: https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1897_num_11_7_19495
- annual report: https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1898_num_12_8_19501

At present the reason is unresolved. Possibilities include a real timetable change after publication or an editorial/reporting irregularity. No choice should be made without further evidence.

The consequence is methodological: **do not reconstruct Lovejoy's weekly movements from the advance programme alone.** The actual register/attendance layer now becomes more valuable precisely because it can adjudicate the live timetable.

## 4. The Section's own rules reveal a deeper attendance record beneath the published reports

The Vᵉ Section's published internal regulations state that:

- no condition of age, degree or nationality was required to register;
- students and auditors chose the conferences they wished to attend;
- directors could exclude the insufficiently prepared;
- those admitted were required to **register/sign on the register before each lesson**;
- prolonged absence had to be justified;
- the academic year ran from 1 November to 15 July.

Primary rules, printed with the 1897–98 report:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1897_num_11_7_20490

This changes the retrieval target. We are not merely looking for a generic student register proving that Lovejoy enrolled at some point during the year. The institutional rules imply a potentially much finer-grained documentary practice recording lesson attendance.

### Evidence caution

The EPHE/Archives nationales digitization project says it digitized selected `registres d'inscription` and `listes de présence`. The contemporary rules prove lesson-level registration existed. They do **not** yet prove that the surviving/digitized objects are the same register or that Lovejoy's conference survives at that granularity.

The correct next test in `FRAN_IR_061975` is therefore:

`cote / title / date / record type`

→ distinguish general enrollment register from lesson attendance/sign-in list

→ only then search Lovejoy and dates.

## 5. A hard opening-date control now exists for the IVᵉ Section

The official IVᵉ Section programme for 1898–99 states that conferences would begin on **3 November 1898** at the first floor of the Nouvelle-Sorbonne, 46 rue Saint-Jacques.

Primary programme:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1899_num_1_1_2222

This is a useful institutional boundary for the Sanskrit/Indianist side of Lovejoy's Paris year. It does not prove Lovejoy was in the room on 3 November. It does, however, give us a precise date against which the 17 October, 1 December and 5 December Lovejoy letters can eventually be aligned.

The Vᵉ Section regulations separately define the formal academic year as beginning 1 November. Thus the early-November window is now institutionally precise even though Lovejoy's own first attendance date remains unknown.

## 6. Formal programme, actual instruction and individualized teaching must stay separate on the Lévi side

Batch128 established Mauss's retrospective testimony that after initial Sanskrit training, Lévi and Finot initiated their group into **Pāli and Buddhist translation**; Mauss also said Lévi's 1896–97 Brāhmaṇa course was personally directed to him.

The contemporary institutional record independently anchors Mauss in this ecology:

- 1895–96 Indian-religions report: Mauss took an active part in Finot's work;
- 1896–97 Indian-religions report: Mauss is an `élève titulaire` under Finot's substitution for Foucher;
- 1896–97 Israel Lévi report: Mauss is also an `élève titulaire` in a seminar whose second-semester problem was **the fate of the soul after death**, with emphasis on the diversity of beliefs.

Primary sources:

- https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1895_num_9_5_19435
- https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1896_num_10_6_19458
- https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1896_num_10_6_19462

The Mauss case is therefore a powerful control for documentary modality. A single student could simultaneously move through Sanskrit/Indian religion, comparative religion and afterlife problems, while personalized instruction was not necessarily reducible to a prospectus title.

That is precisely why Wilson's report of Lovejoy's occasional private sacred-text reading with Sylvain Lévi cannot be disproved by the absence of a formal `Pāli Buddhism` course title in the 1898–99 programme.

## 7. A later primary control proves that home-based Lévi teaching was a real institutional practice

A much later EPHE report, for 1918–19, explicitly says that wartime/documentation duties prevented Lévi from maintaining fixed-hour conferences, but that he **frequently convened former auditors at his home** and worked with them on current research; the report then names participants and a Buddhist text project.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1919_num_1_1_2815

This is not evidence that Lovejoy's 1898 private reading definitely occurred in the same way. It is a modality control showing that `Lévi + students/auditors + scholarly textual work at home` is independently documented in Lévi's later teaching practice and is not an inherently implausible biographical motif.

The 1898 claim still rests on Lovejoy's letters as reconstructed by Wilson until the MS-0873 transcriptions are recovered.

## 8. New comparative control: Mauss had already crossed the same afterlife/India boundary before Lovejoy

The Israel Lévi report for 1896–97 is unexpectedly valuable for the notebook 005 problem. In the second semester the seminar treated:

`Le sort de l'âme après la mort selon les écrits talmudiques et midraschiques`

and the report says the teaching emphasized **the variety of beliefs** on the subject. Mauss is explicitly among the élèves titulaires.

At the same time, Mauss is also documented in the Indian-religions teaching network and later remembered Pāli/Buddhist-translation instruction with Sylvain Lévi and Finot.

This gives a pre-Lovejoy control student whose actual Paris formation crossed:

`Indian philology / Indian religion`

+

`afterlife / eschatological comparison`

+

`comparative-religion method`.

No Lovejoy–Mauss contact is inferred. The significance is institutional: the cross-domain formation reconstructed for Lovejoy was already structurally possible within the EPHE curriculum and student practice, rather than being an artificial combination imposed by our modern reconstruction.

## 9. Revised document hierarchy for Paris claims

### Level A — advertised possibility

Programme/course prospectus.

Can prove a course was planned and how it was publicly described. Cannot by itself prove it ran unchanged or that Lovejoy attended.

### Level B — realized institutional teaching

Annual retrospective course report.

Can prove what the director says was actually taught, and may name actual participants.

### Level C — participant trace

Named status in report, enrollment register, attendance list, lesson sign-in.

Can potentially prove presence at increasing temporal resolution.

### Level D — individualized transmission

Letters, diaries, private-reading notes, teacher/student memoirs.

Needed for claims such as the exact sacred texts/languages Lovejoy read privately with Lévi.

This hierarchy should now govern the Paris write-up. It prevents us from treating a programme title as attendance, or conversely treating the absence of a programme title as evidence that individualized instruction did not occur.
