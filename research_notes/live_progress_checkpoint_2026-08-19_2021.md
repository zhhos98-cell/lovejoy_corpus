## 1908–09 published extension

Lovejoy, "The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus," The Monist 18.4 (1908), pp. 597–609, has now been uploaded in full and closes the previous asymmetry with W. B. Smith's 1909 reply.

Main result from the primary pair:

- Lovejoy explicitly decomposes Smith's theory into definite contentions and tests the evidence for each.
- Lovejoy can grant broad contextual probability while refusing to let it automatically prove a stronger historical genealogy.
- At p. 609 he makes the proof burden prior-sensitive: a hypothesis strongly opposed by antecedent probabilities requires an overwhelming mass of specific historical evidence.
- Smith's reply is cumulative/configurational: the argument must be judged as a unit and individual clues can reinforce one another.
- The calibrated conclusion is not local proof versus cumulative proof. Lovejoy himself accepts cumulative/circumstantial inference elsewhere. The disagreement concerns which component propositions are sufficiently established to be accumulated into a stronger genealogical claim.

Batch167 and its symmetric matrix already record this closure.
