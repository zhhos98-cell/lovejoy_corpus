## Core result

Jacobi’s 1898 article contains two explicit backward references to his own review of Joseph Dahlmann’s 1896 _Nirvāṇa_:

- `Siehe meine Anzeige von Dahlmanns “Nirvāṇa” in Gött. gel. Anz. 1897, p. 272.`
- `Ebenda p. 268.`

The review is independently catalogued under Hermann Jacobi as:

**“Rezension: Nirvāṇa: Eine Studie zur Vorgeschichte des Buddhismus. von Dahlmann, J.” (1897).**

The full 1897 _Göttingische Gelehrte Anzeigen_ has now been located in both the Göttingen Academy repository and Internet Archive. The printed pages are therefore no longer an unidentified archival target: **pp.268–272 are the exact next primary slice.**

## 1. Why this review matters

Dahlmann 1896 is one of the authorities Lovejoy cites in his 1898 `upādi/upadhi` lexical discussion (`Nirvāṇa, p.14`). Jacobi 1898, meanwhile, explicitly uses his own 1897 review of Dahlmann while defending the Sāṁkhya-Yoga genealogy of Buddhist philosophy and before developing his nidāna interpretation.

The review therefore lies at a particularly dense point in the controversy:

`Dahlmann 1896 → Jacobi review 1897 → Jacobi nidāna article 1898`

This does **not** prove that Lovejoy read the review. Its value is different: it can reconstruct what the Sāṁkhya/Nirvāṇa genealogy looked like **in the immediate specialist field** that surrounded Lovejoy’s notebook and article.

## 4. Authorship is bibliographically secure

TITUS Bibliographia lists Hermann Jacobi entry 1424:

`Rezension: Nirvāṇa: Eine Studie zur Vorgeschichte des Buddhismus. von Dahlmann, J.` — 1897.

This independently supports the identification already embedded in Jacobi’s 1898 self-reference. We can therefore call the 1897 notice **Jacobi’s review** without relying only on the later article.

## 5. What must NOT be inferred before pp.268–272 are read

At present we should not claim that the review:

- endorses Dahlmann’s `upadhi/upādi` etymology;
- rejects it;
- already contains Jacobi’s 1898 `adṛṣṭa/karma/upādāna` argument;
- directly answers Senart;
- was read by Lovejoy;
- determined Lovejoy’s 1898 formulation.

All we securely know is that Jacobi 1898 regards pp.268 and 272 of his 1897 notice as relevant precedents for claims he is making in the later Sāṁkhya/nidāna discussion.

## 7. Secondary reception ecology located but deliberately downgraded

Two other 1897 notices of Dahlmann’s _Nirvāṇa_ are bibliographically identifiable:

- _Wiener Zeitschrift für die Kunde des Morgenlandes_ 11 (1897), pp.190–197;
- _Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society_ 29.2 (April 1897), pp.407–410, DOI `10.1017/S0035869X00145927`.

These may reconstruct how quickly Dahlmann’s thesis circulated across German/Austrian and British Orientalist venues. They are secondary to Jacobi’s notice because Jacobi himself explicitly carries his 1897 review forward into the 1898 controversy. Neither should be treated as evidence of Lovejoy access without independent documentation.

## 8. Implication for the 004 argument

This route reinforces the batch 42 revision. Lovejoy’s 004/1898 work sits in a field where scholars were already arguing not only over Buddhist doctrine but over the **evidentiary reach of genealogy**:

- Dahlmann: prehistory/genealogy of Nirvāṇa and Indian philosophical vocabulary;
- Jacobi: Sāṁkhya-Yoga genealogy, later defended against Oldenberg and Senart;
- Senart: filiation of terms and textual stratification;
- Oldenberg: textual-semantic control against overextended genealogical reconstructions;
- Lovejoy: grants much historical layering but repeatedly requires semantic/causal/usage evidence before genealogy is allowed to decide present function.

The review is therefore valuable not because it promises a missing “influence” link, but because it can make the **live standards of historical inference** around 1897 visible at one more point.
