# JHI 2012: Skinner re-enters the Journal as a canonical object — critic-set drift from method dispute to method/politics appraisal

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT JHI ISSUE / ARTICLE METADATA + MULTIPLE ABSTRACT/FULL-TEXT CONTROLS / BURKE INTRODUCTION ONE-PAGE METADATA CONTROL / EVENT-DATE CONFLICT PRESERVED**

## Core result

JHI 73.1 (January 2012) contains a special symposium, `On Quentin Skinner, from Method to Politics`, organized around the fortieth anniversary of Skinner's 1969 `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas`.

The section consists of:

- Martin J. Burke, `Introduction`, p.69;
- Melissa Lane, `Doing Our Own Thinking for Ourselves: On Quentin Skinner's Genealogical Turn`, pp.71–82;
- Bryan Garsten, `Liberalism and the Rhetorical Vision of Politics`, pp.83–93;
- Nadia Urbinati, `Republicanism after the French Revolution: The Case of Sismonde de Sismondi`, pp.95–109;
- Philip Pettit, `Freedom in Hobbes's Ontology and Semantics: A Comment on Quentin Skinner`, pp.111–126;
- Quentin Skinner, `On the Liberty of the Ancients and the Moderns: A Reply to My Critics`, pp.127–146.

This materially extends the institutional loop already recovered in the repository:

`WIENER 1961 IN JHI`
-> `SKINNER 1969 CRITIQUE, explicitly acknowledging Wiener`
-> `PAREKH/BERKI 1973 CRITIQUE IN JHI`
-> `SKINNER 1974 REPLY`
-> `BERKI/PAREKH 1976 REJOINDER`
-> `TULLY 1988 CANON PACKAGE`
-> `JHI LOVEJOY REASSESSMENT 1987 / KELLEY 1990`
-> `JHI 2012 SKINNER ANNIVERSARY SYMPOSIUM + SKINNER REPLY`.

The strongest interpretation is not simply that JHI later `accepted` Skinner. It is:

> **By 2012 JHI had converted Skinner from a critic of history-of-ideas practice into an object of its own retrospective and argumentative canon. The journal did not merely cite the 1969 rupture; it hosted a new critic set and gave Skinner the final reply.**

This is a major case of **recanonization through venue return**.

## II. The occasion: 1969 is now an anniversary object

Skinner's own 2012 reply abstract says the CUNY Graduate Center symposium marked the **fortieth anniversary** of `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas`.

A CUNY public-program announcement likewise states that the event celebrated and critically evaluated Skinner's work on the fortieth anniversary and names Skinner, Lane, Garsten, Pettit and Urbinati, moderated by Helena Rosenblatt.

This is historiographically important.

A text once functioning as a polemical intervention against inherited history-of-ideas practice has become:

`1969 POLEMIC`
-> `ANNIVERSARY LANDMARK`
-> `SYMPOSIUM OBJECT`.

That is a different canonical status from merely being heavily cited.

# IV. The 2012 critic set is structurally different from the 1973 and 1988 critic sets

This is the highest-value result.

### Parekh/Berki 1973

Their critique targets Skinner's methodology directly:

- intention;
- sufficiency of intention recovery;
- necessity of context;
- transhistorical audience;
- paradigms/traditions;
- relation between ideas and action.

### Tully 1988 selected critic roster

Hollis, Graham, Femia, Tarcov, Minogue, Keane, Taylor.

The package asks what the strongest critical perspectives on Skinner's methodological programme are.

### JHI 2012 critic roster

Lane, Garsten, Urbinati, Pettit.

Their questions are largely about the **mature consequences and extensions** of Skinner's method and substantive political thought.

This suggests:

`CRITIC SET`
changes as
`CANONICAL STATUS OF THE OBJECT`
changes.

New concept:

### `CRITIC-SET DRIFT`

The historically changing selection of which objections, interlocutors and problem-types count as representative criticism of a canonical figure or method.

`HISTORICALLY IMPORTANT CRITIC`
!=
`LATER REPRESENTATIVE CRITIC`.

This can explain Parekh/Berki's declining anthology visibility without calling them forgotten or suppressed.

# V. Lane 2012: from method/political philosophy separation to genealogy

Melissa Lane begins from a famous conclusion of Skinner 1969: when seeking answers to philosophical/political-philosophical questions, historians should learn to do their own thinking rather than treat the canon as supplying timeless answers.

She emphasizes that the original manifesto sharply separated:

`WORK OF THE HISTORIAN`
from
`WORK OF THE POLITICAL PHILOSOPHER`.

Her 2012 target is Skinner's later **genealogical turn**, in which historical reconstruction can equip present political reflection by exposing contingency and alternative prior uses of concepts.

Lane argues that Skinner's genealogy is:

- less novel than his own characterization may suggest;
- more modest;
- more productive.

This is a critique of **self-description and political payoff**, not a return to the 1970s question `does context matter?`.

The method has become sufficiently canonical that its later evolution can itself have a history.

---

# VI. Garsten 2012: the political implications of the rhetorical method

Bryan Garsten begins from Skinner's *Visions of Politics* and identifies what he calls Skinner's `rhetorical` vision of politics.

He does not reject the centrality of rhetoric and contestation. Instead he asks about its political consequences, especially Skinner's relation to liberalism.

Garsten's key move is to argue that one can affirm rhetorical contestation without accepting a simple view of liberalism as an unhealthy Hobbesian/neo-Kantian attempt to halt political flux.

Thus the criticism is:

`METHOD / HISTORIOGRAPHY`
-> `IMPLICIT POLITICAL VISION`
-> `POLITICAL CONSEQUENCES`.

This is exactly the symposium title's shift:

`FROM METHOD TO POLITICS`.

---

# VII. Urbinati 2012: historical counter-case against a mature neo-Roman narrative

Nadia Urbinati's article works differently.

Rather than offering an abstract methodological critique, she reconstructs Sismonde de Sismondi after the French Revolution and uses that case against aspects of contemporary neo-Roman republicanism.

Her abstract argues that:

- republicanism survived the French Revolution and capitalism by changing;
- the priority of individual interests/liberty was not necessarily the central problem for modern republicans;
- democratic equality, electoral consent, changing ideas of virtue and honor altered the republican problem-space.

This is a **historical counter-case** to a mature substantive genealogy of liberty/republicanism.

Methodological implication:

`CONTEXTUAL METHOD`
does not immunize
`SUBSTANTIVE GENEALOGY`
from revision by new historical cases.

Thus one of Skinner's critics in 2012 is criticizing by **doing more history**, rather than by opposing contextualism as a theory.

---

# VIII. Pettit 2012: conceptual change requires semantic/ontological typing

Philip Pettit's article is especially important for the repo's relation-specific thesis.

He accepts the importance of contextualist analysis and Skinner's account of Hobbes on liberty but argues that conceptual shifts may have to be typed more carefully.

Pettit's distinction:

`SEMANTIC CHANGE`
!=
`ONTOLOGICAL CHANGE`.

A thinker can change:

- how a word or concept is applied to the social/political world;

without necessarily changing:

- the underlying conception of the constituents and relationships of that world.

Pettit says Skinner's Hobbes work can be revised in a limited way to illustrate this distinction.

This is strikingly continuous with the project's broader jurisdictional problem:

`OBSERVED CONCEPTUAL SHIFT`
requires
`RELATION-TYPE DISCRIMINATION`.

The descendant methodological issue is no longer `ideas vs context`; it is **what kind of conceptual change has occurred**.

# IX. Skinner 2012 replies inside JHI

Skinner's `On the Liberty of the Ancients and the Moderns: A Reply to My Critics` addresses all four symposium papers.

This is institutionally significant independent of the exact substantive replies.

The venue sequence becomes:

`1969 SKINNER CRITICIZES HISTORY-OF-IDEAS PRACTICE FROM HISTORY AND THEORY`

then

`1973 JHI PUBLISHES CRITIQUE OF SKINNER`

then

`2012 JHI HOSTS A SKINNER ANNIVERSARY SYMPOSIUM AND PUBLISHES SKINNER'S OWN REPLY`.

JHI is therefore not merely an object left behind by contextualist innovation. It becomes one of the venues through which Skinner's later canon is debated and renewed.

Strong formula:

`CRITIC OF FIELD`
-> `INTERLOCUTOR OF FIELD`
-> `CANONICAL OBJECT WITHIN FIELD`.

# X. Critic-set drift across 1973 / 1988 / 2012

The three snapshots can now be compared.

## 1973 — methodological challenge

Representative problem:
`IS SKINNER'S CONTEXT/INTENTION THEORY VALID?`

## 1988 — canonization of the controversy

Representative problem:
`WHICH EXISTING CRITICISMS BEST DEFINE THE SKINNER DEBATE?`

Contemporaneous reviewer Jane Arscott describes Tully's selections as surveying the spectrum of the debate rather than merely a few themes.

This makes Parekh/Berki's omission a question about **representativeness by 1988**, not presumptive bias.

## 2012 — appraisal of mature programme

Representative problems include:

- genealogy and present critical use;
- rhetoric and political vision;
- republican/liberal historical genealogy;
- semantic vs ontological conceptual change.

Therefore:

`REPRESENTATIVE CRITICISM`
is itself a historical variable.

This is a powerful mechanism of canonical compression.

# XI. Relation to anthology portability

The 2012 symposium also demonstrates that anthology canonization does not freeze the debate permanently.

Tully 1988 made one critic set highly portable.

But JHI 2012 constructs a new set without reproducing that earlier roster.

Thus:

`PORTABLE CANON`
!=
`PERMANENT CRITIC SET`.

Later institutions can reselect the problem and reconstruct the interlocutors.

This reinforces `CANONIZATION BY PROBLEM SELECTION`.
