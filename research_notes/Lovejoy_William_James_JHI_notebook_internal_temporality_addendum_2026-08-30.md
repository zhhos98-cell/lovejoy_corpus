## New direct notebook evidence

A re-read of the corrected transcription of notebook 004 adds a stronger internal bridge than the parent dossier initially recorded.

### MS p. 51 — Buddhist flux as an ultimate time-process

In the notebook's `Flux` section, after summarizing a *Milindapañha* objection that absolute flux would destroy time unless there were static units, Lovejoy characterizes Nāgasena's answer as simply insisting on the fact that time is of this sort. His own gloss then says, in substance, that the **time process, with all its inconceivabilities, is treated as an ultimate and inexplicable fact**.

Source: `archive_transcriptions/MS38_004_001_061_004_p001-018_clean.json`, PDF p. 11 / MS p. 51.

This matters because the notebook itself, not merely Lovejoy's 1911 retrospective on James, already contains an anti-reductive temporal formulation. Time is not being dissolved into a static logical or substantial substrate simply because flux produces conceptual difficulty.

### MS p. 67 — the object is the complicated interrelation of the twelve notions

At the opening of the long nidāna analysis, Lovejoy says that understanding Buddhism requires understanding the technical working-out of its general position in dependent origination and the **very complicated interrelation** of the twelve notions. He describes the present effort as an approach through careful analysis of the texts.

Source: `archive_transcriptions/MS38_004_001_061_004_p019-036_clean.json`, PDF p. 20 / MS p. 67.

This is useful because it shows that the notebook is organized around relations among terms rather than merely extraction of isolated doctrinal definitions.

### MS pp. 121–141 — temporal order and classificatory order repeatedly cross

The notebook then makes the problem increasingly explicit:

- MS p. 121 diagrams `nāmarūpa` against the five khandhas.
- MS p. 123 states that `viññāṇa` is temporally (?) antecedent to `nāmarūpa` while logically a subdivision of it.
- MS p. 125 treats `vedanā` simultaneously as a subdivision of `nāma` and as a link in the causal chain.
- MS p. 137 concedes that the nidāna sequence is not homogeneous in strict temporal relation but rejects the inference that it is therefore unintelligible or accidental.
- MS p. 139 makes the difficulty the relation among terms that precede one another **temporally as well as logically**.

Source: `archive_transcriptions/MS38_004_001_061_004_p037-054_clean.json`, especially PDF pp. 41–50 / MS pp. 121–139.

Thus MS p. 123 is not a one-off wording accident. It sits inside a local run in which Lovejoy repeatedly compares classificatory inclusion, causal succession, temporal priority, collective designation, and historical composition.

## Revised James comparison

This notebook-internal evidence materially improves the James hypothesis while preserving the causal firewall.

William James's *Principles of Psychology* (1890) contains two strikingly congruent formulations available before Lovejoy's Harvard years:

1. `relation` is an unusually ambiguous abstract word; its precise meaning must be fixed within the particular sphere of application at issue;
2. the empirical order of coexistence/succession in time and space differs from the rational order of comparison and classification.

Lovejoy's own 1911 `William James as Philosopher` later identifies the irreducibility and primacy of the temporal quality of experience as one of James's durable philosophical contributions. His 1930 autobiographical `A Temporalistic Realism` makes temporality an early touchstone of his own philosophical development.

The notebook now supplies an earlier internal middle term:

`Jamesian temporal / relation problem-space available before Harvard`

→ `Lovejoy notebook 004: time process treated as ultimate; complex relations analyzed without forcing all terms onto one axis`

→ `Lovejoy 1911: James retrospectively identified with temporal irreducibility`

→ `Lovejoy 1930: temporalism retrospectively identified as an early personal touchstone`.

The chain is historically suggestive but remains **problem genealogy rather than demonstrated textual transmission** because no contemporary Lovejoy note currently cites James or the relevant *Principles* chapters.

## Adversarial reminder

Do not convert this into `James → Lovejoy's partition method`.

The 1908 pragmatism controversy remains essential because Lovejoy later treats some Jamesian distinctions as logically independent where James and later interpreters understand them as functionally connected. The genealogy may therefore be transformative:

`Jamesian anti-reduction / temporal irreducibility`
→ `Lovejoyian relation-specific partition`
→ sometimes `overpartition`.

This makes James both an antecedent and a control on Lovejoy's later analytical style.
