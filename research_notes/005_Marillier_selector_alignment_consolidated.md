## Core result

The complete user-returned OCR of Léon Marillier, `La survivance de l’âme et l’idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés` (1893/94), together with the already recovered direct Söderblom 1901 control, closes the main originality questions left around the early 005 eschatology block.

The old sequence of progressively narrower claims was:

1. Marillier supplies continuation vs retribution; Lovejoy invents an intermediate determinant taxonomy.
2. Marillier supplies distributed determinant ingredients; Lovejoy serializes them and adds `desert` / `half-moral`.
3. The conceptual threshold is shared, but perhaps serial ordering itself remains Lovejoy-specific.

The current direct-primary state removes all three priority claims.

Marillier himself already provides:

- an explicit moral/non-moral selector criterion;
- a multi-selector field including rank, manner of death, ritual fidelity, journey skill, courage/cowardice, divine arbitrariness and chance;
- mixed cases in which `good/bad` classification is partly moral but above all ritual;
- contact/borrowing and historical superposition controls;
- a transitional condition in which moral reward/punishment is recognizable but not yet disentangled from contrary conceptions or sharply defined;
- the warning that formally recurring myths can have different `signification` and `portée`.

Söderblom, another participant in Marillier's 1898–99 seminar environment, then directly shows that **student-level serial redivision** was itself already an actor-recognized workshop practice. He says he takes up Marillier's study `avec d’autres divisions et à un point de vue un peu différent` and lays out successive selector classes: chance/no relation to present life; terrestrial condition; kinds of death; funeral provision/rites; then the transition toward genuinely religious/moral qualification. His text also uses `principes moraux ou quasi-moraux` and explicitly historicizes the boundary between `good` as capacity/fitness and `good` as moral/religious value.

Therefore neither the constituent taxonomy, the moral threshold, the quasi/half-moral transition, nor **serial ordering as such** can safely bear a Lovejoy priority claim.

The strongest remaining Lovejoy delta is narrower and more documentary:

> **Lovejoy gives the shared selector field his own compact redivision, then uses one ambiguous case repeatedly as an active test: hold the visible outcome fixed, switch the candidate operative relation, reallocate the case, and carry the same diagnostic discipline into new domains.**

The later 1906 transformation remains stronger still: one recurrent causal family — impersonal/direct/transferable efficacy — is abstracted from heterogeneous local cases into a cross-cutting comparative category.

This is not a new Batch227 and does not create a new 004↔005 continuity claim. Batch156 already established the diagnostic-property → causal-bearer → comparative-category sequence. The present dossier changes the **attribution and granularity of the 005 delta**, not the existence of that previously controlled developmental pattern.

## 1. Direct Marillier criterion: what makes an afterlife allocation moral?

Marillier states the criterion directly. Happiness, misery, separate destinations, survival or annihilation are not moral when they are determined **not by the good or bad quality of conduct during earthly life**, but by selectors such as chance, divine whim, social rank, kind of death, skill or strength.

Operationally:

`post-mortem difference`

is not enough. The question is:

`what variable selects the outcome?`

If the selector is rank, manner of death, ritual qualification, journey competence, physical strength, chance or arbitrary divine action, differentiated reward-like or punishment-like outcomes need not be moral. If allocation turns on the **moral value / good or bad quality of earthly conduct**, the relation is moral.

### Lovejoy 005

PDF p.20 / ms p.39 says future life does not become moral until fate/punishment/reward depends on `desert`; p.23 likewise treats universal happiness as naturalistic because `desert` is absent.

Final allocation:

- `desert` = **Lovejoy actor-local compact wording / working test**;
- moral-selector criterion = **direct Marillier precedent, not Lovejoy conceptual invention**.

The Lovejoy wording is concise and repeatedly usable, but the inferential rule is upstream.

## 2. The determinant field is directly Marillier

Earlier Batch205 needed Mauss's page-addressed relays to Marillier pp.37, 42 and 44. The returned full primary now replaces those relays at the proposition level.

Marillier directly distributes souls according to multiple reasons including:

- `rang`;
- `genre de mort`;
- fidelity to rites;
- ability to overcome journey trials;
- courage/cowardice;
- arbitrary divine choice;
- chance.

Elsewhere he distinguishes ceremonies, rites and often magical practices from acts judged morally good or bad.

Thus the early 005 sequence

`manner of death → ritual/manual qualification → social/clan relation → later moral interpretation → strict moral allocation`

cannot be described as a Lovejoy-invented middle taxonomy emerging from a Marillier binary. The selector architecture is already explicit in Marillier's memoir.

## 3. Mixed cases are already a Marillier problem

Marillier does not simply oppose pure non-moral systems to pure moral systems. In the Haida discussion he asks what distinguishes `good` from `bad`. His answer is mixed: partly moral conduct, but also — and especially — fidelity to ritual practice. A `good Indian` is defined through worship, sacrifice, ritual dances, contribution to feasts and obedience to the inspired medicine-man.

Thus:

`native/observer label = good/bad`

 does not settle:

`operative relation = moral merit`.

The inferential caution that a moral-looking label may conceal heterogeneous selectors is already direct-primary Marillier.

## 4. `half-moral` has both direct Marillier and same-workshop conceptual controls

Marillier's conclusion says moral reward/punishment can already be recognizable in earlier social/religious forms while **not yet disengaged from opposed conceptions and not yet very precise**.

Söderblom makes the same workshop territory even more explicit. In his discussion of Marillier he refers to the `principes moraux ou quasi-moraux des peuples étudiés`, and he historicizes the point where `good` meaning capable/fit separates from `good` meaning morally/religiously good.

Final allocation:

- exact English phrase `half-moral` = potentially Lovejoy's own lexical compression;
- transitional/quasi-moral concept = **directly upstream/workshop-shared**;
- no conceptual priority claim should rest on the phrase.

## 5. Historical superposition is directly Marillier

Earlier Batch204 used Mauss 1896 as the strongest direct printed control for moral beliefs superposed on older eschatological machinery. The returned Marillier memoir independently supplies the same operation.

Marillier describes newer Christian/Buddhist/Muslim ideas as borrowed, added or superposed upon older Indigenous images of the afterlife rather than simply replacing them. In the Andaman discussion an older common country of the dead is transformed when heaven, hell/purgatory and the sorting of good/bad are juxtaposed with it; the older structure remains as one layer inside the composite arrangement.

Therefore:

`older non-moral afterlife structure`
+
`later moral reward/punishment layer`
→ `composite transformed system`

is a direct Marillier operation before Lovejoy's Paris notebook.

Mauss remains a valuable controversy witness and pre-Paris circulation control, but he is no longer needed to supply the only direct formulation of moral superposition.

## 6. Söderblom proves that serial selector ordering and redivision were workshop-shared

The direct Söderblom 1901 control changes the last remaining systematization claim.

Söderblom explicitly says he attaches his inquiry to Marillier's `Survivance` study but will proceed:

> `avec d’autres divisions et à un point de vue un peu différent`.

He then offers successive solutions to why the dead do not all receive the same fate:

1. no connection between differentiated survivance and present life / chance;
2. different future destinies because of different terrestrial conditions;
3. different future destinies because of different kinds of death.

His table and exposition further divide the field into:

- tribe;
- wealth and social position;
- natural gifts/powers;
- manner of death;
- funeral provision;
- rites observed by survivors;
- then the transition toward religious/moral qualification.

Therefore:

> **serial selector ordering itself is not a secure Lovejoy innovation.**

The historically appropriate category is **redivision**: students could inherit the same problem field and source dossier while cutting and ordering it differently.

This actor-near control is stronger than describing 005 merely as either passive influence or private invention.

## 7. Söderblom also shares the observer-category correction and borderline-case logic

Söderblom's direct text supplies near-exact controls for several 005 operations.

- Strength, dexterity, wealth and natural gifts can determine superior future destiny without entering the religious/moral sphere.
- Women dying in childbirth, suicides and warriors killed in battle should not be redescribed as morally `best` merely to rescue a retribution theory.
- A poor funeral should not be transformed into punishment of the deceased for relatives' avarice when the simpler selector is the actual provision placed in the grave.
- In a Nicaragua example, an observer's `good/bad` wording is checked against the underlying report and resolves instead into battle-death versus natural death.

This means the inference syntax

`apparently good/bad outcome`
→ `inspect source and operative selector`
→ `withhold moral classification if the selector is death-type/status/provision/etc.`

is workshop-shared, not Lovejoy-specific.

What remains distinctive is not discovering this syntax but the **density and longitudinal reuse** with which Lovejoy deploys it in one working notebook.

## 8. Same-looking forms can have different significance

Marillier explicitly says analogous mythic incidents recur across Oceania, Africa, Brazil and the Indian archipelago **with different significance and scope**.

This joins the eschatology problem to the mechanism problem later visible throughout 005:

`same visible form`
≠
`same relation / significance / historical role`.

The Paris comparative-religion environment already contains this inferential firewall. Lovejoy's stronger documentary behavior is to carry it across a wider internal range of the notebook and later isolate recurrent causal mechanisms.

## 9. Same-workshop stages are not one mechanical civilization ladder

Söderblom's direct text also matters for the larger `primitive` problem. Although he uses `successive solutions`, `epoch` and developmental language, he explicitly blocks a simple universal mechanical ladder:

- his classificatory divisions arose from prolonged analysis rather than an a priori system;
- he distinguishes multiple parallel evolutions that can coexist within one religion;
- he states that retribution beyond the grave does not appear at one fixed degree of civilization for all peoples;
- Israel serves as a counterexample to an evolution proceeding everywhere equally and `pour ainsi dire mécanique`.

This does not prove Lovejoy copied Söderblom's model. It shows that within the same Marillier environment, developmental classification could already be **relation-specific, internally coexisting and non-synchronized**.

That is an immediate-period control for reading 005's mixture of `primitive`, intermediate and moral categories without flattening them into a single world ladder.

## 11. What is no longer safe to claim as Lovejoy-specific

Do not use the following as originality claims merely because they are explicit in 005:

- differentiated afterlife ≠ moral reward/punishment;
- rank/status, rites, manner of death, funeral provision, natural gifts or journey competence ≠ moral merit;
- `good/bad` labels can conceal non-moral selectors;
- contact can import a moral layer;
- later moral beliefs can coexist with and transform older non-moral structures;
- a transitional/quasi-moral layer can be identifiable while still mixed with contrary conceptions;
- full moralization requires allocation according to moral/religious value rather than fitness/status/etc.;
- serial selector ordering as such;
- use of borderline death/provision cases to challenge a forced retribution theory;
- developmental stages need not map mechanically onto one global civilization level.

All now have direct upstream or same-workshop control.

## 12. What remains strong in Lovejoy 005

### 12.1 Particular redivision and notebook compression

The safest actor-near category is Söderblom's own `d’autres divisions`: Lovejoy gives a shared field **his own cut and adjacency**, in compressed working-note form.

Marillier's memoir is an extended empirical argument; Söderblom publishes a systematic but differently cut typology; Lovejoy produces another redivision optimized for active case testing.

### 12.2 Repeated reallocation of the same ambiguous case

Suicide remains especially important. One visible punitive outcome is successively tested under:

- kind of death;
- taboo/custom;
- ritual qualification;
- clan/social injury;
- exclusion;
- blood/contact consequence;
- later imported/higher-stage moralization;
- strict moral desert.

The notebook's active operation is therefore **switching the proposed selector while holding the case constant**. This exact dense serial migration has not been located as an upstream duplicate.

### 12.3 Cross-domain migration inside 005

The same burden-of-proof discipline later migrates from afterlife allocation to sacrifice, anthropophagy, blood, charms, foundation rites and agriculture. Outward morphology repeatedly loses the right to determine the class by itself; the operative relation/mechanism must be isolated.

This is already controlled in Batches149 and 156 and should not be presented as newly discovered here.

### 12.4 1906 abstraction

The strongest publication-level transformation remains the abstraction of impersonal, diffusive, transferable efficacy into a category more fundamental than personality. One causal architecture can recur across historically heterogeneous rites without implying one universal origin.
