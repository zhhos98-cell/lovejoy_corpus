## 1. Direct Marillier criterion: what makes an afterlife allocation moral?

Marillier states the criterion with unusual clarity. After reviewing differentiated destinies, he says that happiness, misery, separate destinations, survival or annihilation are not moral when they are determined **not by the good or bad quality of conduct during earthly life**, but by other selectors such as chance, divine whim, social rank, kind of death, skill or strength.

Operationally:

`post-mortem difference`

is not enough.

The question is:

`what variable selects the outcome?`

If the selector is:

- earthly/social rank;
- manner of death;
- ritual qualification;
- journey competence;
- physical force/skill;
- chance;
- arbitrary divine action;

then differentiated reward-like or punishment-like outcomes need not be moral.

If allocation turns on the **moral value / good or bad quality of earthly conduct**, then the relation is moral.

This is already the conceptual equivalent of the distinction Lovejoy later compresses through `desert`.

### Lovejoy 005

PDF p.20 / ms p.39 says the future life does not become moral until fate/punishment/reward depends on `desert`; p.23 similarly treats universal happiness as naturalistic because `desert` is absent.

The safest allocation is therefore:

- `desert` = **Lovejoy actor-local compact wording / notebook test**;
- moral-selector criterion = **direct Marillier precedent, not Lovejoy conceptual invention**.

Lovejoy's wording may be sharper and more portable, but the inferential rule is already upstream.

## 2. The determinant field is directly present in Marillier, not merely reconstructed through Mauss

Batch205 relied partly on Mauss's page-addressed relays to Marillier pp.37, 42 and 44. The returned full primary now replaces those relays at the proposition level.

Marillier directly distributes souls between favorable/unfavorable destinies according to multiple reasons, including:

- `rang`;
- `genre de mort`;
- fidelity to rites;
- ability to overcome journey trials;
- courage/cowardice;
- arbitrary divine choice;
- chance.

Elsewhere he explicitly distinguishes ceremonies, rites and often magical practices from acts judged morally good or bad.

This means that the early 005 series

`manner of death → ritual/manual qualification → social/clan relation → later moral interpretation → strict moral allocation`

should no longer be described as Lovejoy inventing a stable middle taxonomy out of a Marillier binary. The constituent selector architecture is already explicit in Marillier's memoir.

What 005 does differently is place the alternatives adjacently and repeatedly use them as a working decision procedure.

## 3. Mixed cases are already a Marillier problem

Marillier does not simply oppose pure non-moral systems to pure moral systems.

In the Haida discussion he asks what distinguishes `good` from `bad`. His answer is mixed: partly moral conduct, but also — and especially — fidelity to ritual practice. A `good Indian` is defined through worship, sacrifice, ritual dances, contribution to feasts and obedience to the inspired medicine-man.

This matters because it removes another possible originality story. A category can be verbally `good/bad` and still require decomposition because the selector underneath the label is heterogeneous.

Thus:

`native/observer label = good/bad`

does not settle:

`operative relation = moral merit`.

That inferential caution is already direct-primary Marillier.

Lovejoy's `half-moral` notation remains valuable as an economical notebook label, but not as evidence that he first discovered the conceptual middle zone.

## 4. `half-moral` has a direct conceptual precursor

Marillier's conclusion states that, in social/religious forms corresponding to an earlier period, reward and punishment resulting from the moral value of conduct can already be recognizable while **not yet disengaged from opposed conceptions and not yet very precise**.

This is functionally very close to Lovejoy's `half-moral character`.

The evidence allocation is therefore:

- exact phrase `half-moral` = potentially Lovejoy's own compact lexical choice;
- transitional moralization / mixed moral-nonmoral structure = **direct Marillier precedent**;
- no priority claim should rest on Lovejoy's label alone.

This directly resolves Batch204's former HOLD on whether Marillier himself reached the transitional moralization problem at comparable resolution.

## 5. Historical superposition is directly Marillier, not only a Mauss field precedent

Batch204 used Mauss 1896 as the strongest direct printed control for moral beliefs superposed on older eschatological machinery. The returned Marillier memoir now independently supplies the same historical operation.

Marillier repeatedly describes newer Christian/Buddhist/Muslim ideas as borrowed, added or `superposed` upon older Indigenous images of the afterlife rather than simply replacing them. In the Andaman discussion, an older common country of the dead is transformed when heaven, hell/purgatory and the sorting of good/bad are juxtaposed with it; the older structure remains as one layer inside the composite arrangement.

Therefore:

`older non-moral afterlife structure`

+

`later moral reward/punishment layer`

→ `composite transformed system`

is a direct Marillier operation before Lovejoy's Paris notebook.

Mauss remains a valuable controversy witness and pre-Paris circulation control, but he is no longer needed to supply the only direct formulation of moral superposition.

## 6. Same-looking forms can have different significance

Marillier explicitly says that analogous mythic incidents recur across Oceania, Africa, Brazil and the Indian archipelago **with different significance and scope**.

This is important for the larger project because it joins the eschatology problem to the mechanism problem later visible throughout 005:

`same visible form`

≠

`same relation / significance / historical role`.

Again, this is not a uniquely Lovejoyan rule introduced in the notebook. The Paris comparative-religion environment already contains the inferential firewall. Lovejoy's distinctive documentary behavior is to make the rule portable across many additional cases and domains.

## 8. What is no longer safe to claim as Lovejoy-specific

Do not use the following as originality claims merely because they are explicit in 005:

- differentiated afterlife ≠ moral reward/punishment;
- rank/status ≠ moral merit;
- rites ≠ moral merit;
- manner of death ≠ moral merit;
- journey ordeal ≠ moral trial;
- `good/bad` labels can conceal ritual rather than moral selectors;
- contact can import a moral layer;
- later moral beliefs can coexist with and transform older non-moral structures;
- a transitional moral layer can be recognizable while still mixed with contrary conceptions;
- the criterion for full moralization is that allocation track the moral quality/value of conduct.

All now have direct Marillier-side control.

## 9. What remains strong in Lovejoy 005

### 9.1 Compression into a working notebook apparatus

Marillier's memoir is an extended empirical argument. Lovejoy turns the field into adjacent short tests and classificatory alternatives. The difference is not ownership of the ingredients but **arrangement, compression and operational portability**.

### 9.2 Repeated reallocation of the same case

Suicide is especially important because one visible outcome is successively tested under:

- kind of death;
- taboo/custom;
- ritual qualification;
- clan/social injury;
- exclusion;
- blood/contact consequence;
- later imported/higher-stage moralization;
- strict moral desert.

The notebook's active operation is therefore not merely listing categories; it is **switching the proposed selector while holding the case constant**.

### 9.3 Cross-domain migration

The same burden-of-proof structure later migrates from afterlife allocation to sacrifice, anthropophagy, blood, charms, foundation rites and agriculture. Outward morphology repeatedly loses the right to determine the class by itself; the operative relation/mechanism must be isolated.

This is already well controlled in Batches149 and 156 and should not be presented as a newly discovered continuity here.

### 9.4 1906 abstraction

The strongest publication-level transformation remains the abstraction of impersonal, diffusive, transferable efficacy into a category more fundamental than personality. That category can recur across historically heterogeneous rites without implying one universal origin.
