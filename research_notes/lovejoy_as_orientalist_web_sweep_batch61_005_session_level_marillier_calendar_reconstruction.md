The decisive control is Léon Marillier’s retrospective EPHE report for **1898–1899**, the year in which Lovejoy is explicitly listed among the auditors who “took an active part in the work.” The report states that Marillier’s actual conferences that year were:

- **Tuesday**: continuation of research on human sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy; special attention to southern African Bantu material, and discrimination among expiatory, funerary, anthropophagic, magical, animal, agrarian, and fecundative sacrifices.

## Why the timetable discrepancy matters

The **preliminary programme** for 1898–1899, printed before the year, had announced:

- survivance: Wednesday, 5 p.m.;
- ritual anthropophagy / human sacrifice: Friday, 9:30 a.m.

Source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1897_num_11_7_19495

But the **retrospective report after the year** says the conferences actually ran Monday and Tuesday. The notebook’s independently dated entries line up with the retrospective Monday/Tuesday schedule:

- 29 May 1899 = Monday, future-life material;
- 6 June 1899 = Tuesday, `Sac.` and sacrifice/Africa material;
- 12 June 1899 = Monday, explicit `Marillier — Survivance`.

This is not a nuisance discrepancy. It is useful microhistorical evidence that the working timetable changed after the preliminary programme was printed, and 005 helps recover the timetable actually experienced by Lovejoy.

## Mauss control: Marillier’s method as an institutional tradition

The connection becomes substantially stronger when the notebook calendar is read beside Marcel Mauss’s succession to the same EPHE conference.

In his 1902 opening lesson, Mauss asks three linked questions: what should be studied, how facts should be observed, and how they should be explained. His methodological sequence is explicit:

- first **record and criticize** ethnographic facts;
- assess author, circumstances, locality, date, and conditions of observation;
- undertake bibliographic research “exhaustive autant que possible” because facts are dispersed and the source base is poorly mapped;
- exercise critical faculties collectively;
- devote one conference to close analysis of documents from determined social groups;
- conduct this as **seminar work**, reading texts together and testing critical hypotheses in order to recover the “véritable fait” behind the report.

Most important for transmission, Mauss states that, **“en ce qui concerne la critique des faits,” he need only follow Marillier’s example**. He then sharply diverges from Marillier at the explanatory level: rather than appeal to universal psychological motives, Mauss proposes to attach religious facts to other objectively established social facts.

Accessible electronic reproduction of the RHR text, original RHR pp. 42–54: https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/mauss_marcel/oeuvres_3/oeuvres_3_09/metier_ethnographe.html

This is independently reinforced by Mauss’s EPHE report for 1901–1902. His Tuesday conference was an “Étude critique de documents concernant la magie chez les Mélanésiens”; he says he tried to maintain the traditions that Marillier had implanted in this kind of study; students completed `travaux de séminaire scientifique` and learned the main procedures of ethnographic bibliography.

Primary institutional source: https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1901_num_15_11_19570

## Strongest historical formulation after Batch 61

The evidence now supports a narrower and stronger claim than generic “Marillier influence”:

> Lovejoy’s Paris notebook preserves dated work produced inside the actual calendar of Marillier’s comparative-religion conferences. The 1898–1899 EPHE report names Lovejoy as an active participant and describes a Monday survivance seminar and a Tuesday sacrifice/ritual-anthropophagy seminar. 005 independently preserves a Monday May 29 future-life sequence, an explicit Monday June 12 slip headed “Marillier — Survivance,” and a Tuesday June 6 entry headed “Sac.” that opens the sacrifice/African sequence. Mauss’s subsequent description of the same chair identifies source criticism, exhaustive ethnographic bibliography, collective document analysis, and seminar work as a tradition implanted by Marillier. The most defensible transmission claim is therefore methodological and documentary before it is doctrinal.

## Relation to the 005 causal-reclassification argument

This session-level finding does **not** prove that Lovejoy’s later mechanism categories were dictated by Marillier. It does, however, change the evidentiary setting in which their emergence should be studied.

The working sequence visible across 005 can now be described at three levels:

1. **Institutional/session level:** Marillier’s actual Monday/Tuesday research conferences, with Lovejoy an active participant.
2. **Documentary level:** bibliography slips, source extraction, exact-page reading, and source criticism.
3. **Analytic level:** refusal to infer meaning from outward form alone; decomposition by motive, causal bearer, social relation, historical layer, and later syncretism/reinterpretation.

The Trumbull and Kingsley exact-source transformations show level 2 → level 3 directly. Batch 61 supplies unusually strong evidence for level 1 → level 2 adjacency.

## Continuity before and after Lovejoy’s year

The method/problem complex was not invented ad hoc for Lovejoy.

- In 1897–1898 Marillier had already insisted on distinguishing marriage rites of union from magical purification/preservation and fecundative rites, while separately analyzing funerary, expiatory, anthropophagic, and magical sacrifice in West Africa.
- In 1899–1900 he continued with agrarian sacrifice, the magical/fecundative power of blood, expiatory sacrifice, and critical examination of Frazer’s *Golden Bough*.
- After Marillier’s death, Mauss explicitly described critical document study and ethnographic bibliography as traditions implanted by his teacher.

Controls:

This makes the safest developmental language **participation in an ongoing research programme**, not sudden conversion to a doctrine.
