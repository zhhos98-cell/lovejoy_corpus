# Batch 186 — Lovejoy before 1905: Paris, Buddhist philology, Indian scholarship, and documented international circulation

Research checkpoint: 2026-08-25. Historical cutoff: **31 December 1904**. This file is intentionally saved incrementally while source verification continues.

## Research boundaries and evidence rules

This batch concerns Lovejoy's academic formation, Sanskrit/Pali studies, Buddhist texts, Indian scholars, and the Paris research environment. It excludes later developments, race, exhibitions, political controversies, and Lovejoy's Stanford resignation.

An institutional course record establishes that a course existed, not that Lovejoy attended it. A bibliographic entry establishes contemporary discoverability and disciplinary classification, not that any particular scholar read, cited, or endorsed the article. Shared use of the same Buddhist text establishes object-level overlap, not direct interpersonal influence.

## 1. Lovejoy's 1898 Buddhist article was internationally indexed by September 1899

Lucian Scherman's *Orientalische Bibliographie*, volume XII, is designated **for bibliography year 1898**, but its title page gives **1899** as the publication year and its preface is signed at Munich in **September 1899**. Its Pali-and-Prakrit section lists entry 1492:

> Lovejoy, Arthur Oncken. — The Buddhistic technical terms upādāna and upādisesa: JAOS XIX, 2, S. 126–36.

This is direct contemporary evidence that Lovejoy's first Buddhist-philological publication entered an international specialist bibliography within approximately a year. It corrects the earlier working assumption that no pre-1905 documentary trace of the article's contemporary disciplinary circulation had yet been located.

The same volume's preface reports support awarded in spring 1899 by the Royal Bavarian Academy of Sciences and the American Oriental Society. Scherman specifically thanks E. Kuhn, A. V. W. Jackson, and **Charles R. Lanman** for their initiative in securing the reported result. The title page also identifies London, Paris, and New York booksellers.

These are unusually concrete institutional conditions for transatlantic bibliographic circulation. They do **not** establish that Lanman personally selected Lovejoy's entry or that Sylvain Lévi, Louis de La Vallée Poussin, or any other named scholar read the article. In a different section, entries 1418 and 1419 list works by Lanman and Lévi consecutively: useful evidence of a shared bibliographic field, not a demonstrated personal transmission chain.

The items adjacent to Lovejoy's entry also include a Pali-text installment edited by **Dharmārāma Sthavira** and printed at Pāliyagoda, with **D. L. Rūpāsiṃha** and **M. I. Perera** named in the imprint. This recovers named South Asian editorial and printing labor from the same documented Buddhist-philological circuit. It does not prove that Lovejoy used that edition.

Primary source: [*Orientalische Bibliographie*, XII (for 1898; published 1899), title page, preface, entries 1418–1419 and 1491–1496](https://archive.org/stream/orientalischebibliographie12.1898/Orientalische%20Bibliographie%2012.1898_djvu.txt).

## 2. Newly recovered 1900 and 1901 membership rolls give Lovejoy's Paris address and tighten his AOS chronology

The American Oriental Society's corporate-member list explicitly headed **“REVISED, DECEMBER, 1900”** contains:

> ARTHUR ONCKEN LOVEJOY, 5 Rue Rollin, Paris, France. 1897.

The number following the address is the year of election, not the year of residence. The list therefore documents Lovejoy as an AOS member through **December 1900**, with **5 Rue Rollin, Paris** recorded as his correspondence address.

This improves two existing findings simultaneously. First, the repository's last previously recovered positive membership entry was December **1899**, when the AOS still gave Lovejoy's Cambridge address. The new document extends his confirmed corporate membership by one year. Second, it supplies an independent contemporary institutional record of a precise Paris address connected with the society in which his Buddhist-philological paper had appeared.

A second newly recovered corporate-member list, explicitly headed **“REVISED, DECEMBER, 1901,”** again retains Lovejoy:

> ARTHUR ONCKEN LOVEJOY, [address desired.] 1897.

He therefore remained a corporate member through **December 1901**. This directly closes another open question in batches 69–70: Lovejoy still held AOS membership during the same calendar year as his separately documented **1901 four-lecture course, *The Philosophy of Buddhism***. The printed syllabus itself has not yet been recovered; its course description remains mediated by Wilson's bibliography, while the society membership is independently confirmed by a contemporary primary record.

The change from **5 Rue Rollin** in the December 1900 list to **“[address desired.]”** in December 1901 shows an actual break in the society's recorded correspondence information. It does not prove nonpayment, a resignation, a requested removal, or the reason his membership subsequently ended.

The January **1903** corporate roll omits Lovejoy, with its alphabetized entries proceeding directly from Jacob W. Loch to Percival Lowell. The best supported institutional-exit interval is therefore now:

> **After the membership roll revised December 1901 and before the membership roll revised January 1903.**

This revises batches 69–70, which placed the lower bound at December 1899, by **two full years**. It also replaces their tentative suggestion that Lovejoy's 1901 Buddhism teaching might have followed his AOS departure: the 1901 roster now establishes the coexistence of dedicated Buddhist teaching and formal AOS affiliation during the same year.

Publication-date control is essential. The directory is **revised December 1900**, while the printed index states that it was **issued April 1902**; JSTOR separately labels the associated volume **1900**. The evidentiary date of the membership list is its explicit revision date, not JSTOR's volume label or the later issue date. The Paris address can reflect an older correspondence record and does **not** establish that Lovejoy was physically in Paris in December 1900 or April 1902.

Primary sources: [AOS front matter and member list, revised December 1900, issued April 1902](https://www.jstor.org/stable/592505); [AOS front matter and member list, revised December 1901](https://www.jstor.org/stable/592404); [AOS front matter and member list, revised January 1903](https://www.jstor.org/stable/592370).

## 3. Buddhist teaching existed during Lovejoy's actual 1898–99 Paris year

Two independent contemporary EPHE documents now recover a Buddhist course previously obscured by first-page-only extraction:

1. The official **1898** programme for **1898–99** announces that **Édouard Specht** would explain chapters of the Chinese *Fo-tsou-tong-ki* concerning the history of Buddhism in China, on **Mondays at 3:15 p.m.**
2. The retrospective Sanskrit-chair report **published in 1899** confirms that Specht actually continued a weekly **Sanskrit–Chinese** conference and explained the *Fo-tsou-tong-ki* chapters concerning Buddhist history.

The prospective announcement and retrospective performance report therefore corroborate each other for **Lovejoy's exact Paris year**. This revises the earlier reconstruction substantially: Sylvain Lévi himself did not offer the particular **Pali-Buddhism course** Lovejoy hoped to find, but it is incorrect to infer that Buddhist teaching was absent from Lévi's wider Sanskrit-chair environment. A specifically documented Chinese-Buddhist historical-text course existed and was taught during that very year.

This is a correction about the available academic environment, not proof that Lovejoy knew Chinese, attended Specht, met Specht, or used the Chinese historical work. The attendance list for this conference remains unrecovered.

Primary sources: [EPHE programme for 1898–99, published 1898, pp. 137–143](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1899_num_1_1_2222); [EPHE, *Langue sanscrite*, retrospective 1898–99 report published 1899, pp. 65–67](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1900_num_1_1_2251). The relevant passages are discoverable in the indexed full text even though Persée's displayed HTML transcribes only the first page of each document.

Lovejoy's surviving notebook independently places him in Marillier's **Monday** survivance conferences on **12 and 19 June 1899**. Specht's course likewise met on Mondays, at **3:15 p.m.** The weekday overlap is secure, but Marillier's precise start time has not been recovered, so neither a timetable conflict nor compatibility can yet be established.

## 4. Later Paris course reports identify the *Milindapañha* and a named Buddhist specialist

The EPHE report published in **1902**, retrospectively describing the preceding academic work, identifies **Édouard Specht** teaching the **Chinese recension of the *Milindapañha***. The named attendees include **Louis de La Vallée Poussin**. The same report also places La Vallée Poussin in Alfred Foucher's elementary Sanskrit conference.

This matters because Lovejoy's 1898 article cites the *Milinda Pañha*, and his notebook 004 discusses the same work's palace-window analogy against a substantial self. The result is an independently documented shared Buddhist textual object between Lovejoy's early research and a later Paris specialist course attended by La Vallée Poussin.

Evidence grading: the common text and named attendance are secure; Lovejoy's attendance at this later course, any direct Lovejoy–La Vallée Poussin connection, and any La Vallée Poussin reading of Lovejoy remain unproved.

Primary sources: [EPHE, *Langue sanscrite*, report published 1902, pp. 80–81](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1903_num_1_1_2409); [complete bound-volume OCR including the otherwise omitted second page](https://archive.org/stream/annuaire20frangoog/annuaire20frangoog_djvu.txt); [Lovejoy, “The Buddhistic Technical Terms Upādāna and Upādisesa” (1898)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/592475); repository transcription `archive_transcriptions/MS38_004_001_061_004_p037-054_clean.json`, manuscript p. 131, PDF p. 46.

## 5. Two distinct *Sūtrālaṃkāra* projects must not be conflated

An **1898** Académie report identifies a manuscript attributed to **Asaṅga**, obtained during Lévi's Nepal research. A separate EPHE report **published in 1903** says that Lévi's former student **Édouard Huber**, by then at Hanoi, had completed a translation of a *Sūtrālaṃkāra*. An independently preserved letter from Lévi to Paul Pelliot, dated **1 October 1903**, identifies Huber's project as the *Sūtrālaṃkāra* attributed to **Aśvaghoṣa**.

Thus the similar titles designate **two distinct scholarly objects**: the Asaṅga-related Nepal manuscript and Huber's Aśvaghoṣa-related translation. They should not be merged into one supposed manuscript-to-translation pipeline. The 1903 letter also provides a contemporary named Paris–Hanoi scholarly connection among Lévi, Huber, Pelliot, and Foucher; it does not establish any Lovejoy connection.

Sources: [Académie report on Lévi's Nepal manuscript, 1898](https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_1898_num_42_2_71154); [EPHE, *Langue sanscrite*, report published 1903, pp. 89–91](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1904_num_1_1_2463); [Musée Guimet research edition quoting Lévi's letter to Pelliot dated 1 October 1903](https://guimet-photo-pelliot.fr/essais/essai_8.php). Only the contemporary 1903 letter is used here; the translation's later publication lies outside this batch's cutoff and is intentionally excluded.

## 6. A named Indian author and additional Buddhist philology appear in the 1904 Paris record

The EPHE Sanskrit report **published in 1904**, covering the **1903–04** academic year, identifies **Pandit Ganapati Śāstrī**, principal of the Maharaja of Travancore's college, as the author of a Sanskrit reader intended for schools in India and also for Sanskrit instruction in Europe. Its printing had already begun under Lévi's supervision. The report documents an Indian intellectual as an author and curriculum-maker, while also making the asymmetrical editorial supervision explicit.

The same report records Lévi's comparison of a Chinese translation attributed to **Paramārtha** with the Sanskrit *Sāṃkhya-kārikā*; identifies a student, **Cuny**, who had learned Sanskrit and Pali and intended to study the Kashgar *Dhammapada*; and describes Specht's teaching from a Chinese Buddhist *Amṛtaśāstra* and from the Chinese Buddhist canon.

Specht's syllabus covers topics that substantially overlap Lovejoy's established Buddhist research interests, including the aggregates, conditioned formations, links of causality, rebirth, and action. These are course-content parallels, not evidence that Lovejoy participated in the course or derived his arguments from Specht.

Chronological control: although the annual's cover title is *Annuaire 1905*, Persée dates its publication to **1904**, and the reported academic activity is **1903–04**. Both the publication and the events therefore remain inside the agreed cutoff.

Primary sources: [EPHE, *Langue sanscrite*, report published 1904, pp. 76–78](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1905_num_1_1_2518); [complete bound-volume OCR, including the omitted second and third pages](https://archive.org/stream/annuaire20frangoog/annuaire20frangoog_djvu.txt).
