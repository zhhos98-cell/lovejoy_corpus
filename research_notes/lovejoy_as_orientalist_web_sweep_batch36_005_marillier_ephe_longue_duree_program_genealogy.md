## Research move

Batches 30–35 reconstructed individual bridges into notebook 005: Marillier’s 1900 `Religion`, the taboo/contagion line, Mauss’s 1896 Steinmetz intervention, shared ethnographic authorities, and Lovejoy’s exact bibliography entry `Endokannibalismus — Steinmetz.` The EPHE annual reports now make it possible to step back and reconstruct a longer institutional genealogy.

The important result is that the two apparently different halves of 005 — **future life / justice** and **sacrifice / ritual efficacy** — reproduce two research programmes that Marillier had been developing, revising and joining across roughly a decade before and during Lovejoy’s Paris year.

This is stronger than saying that Lovejoy attended one year of comparative-religion lectures. The better model is:

> **Marillier’s decade-long EPHE research architecture → 1898–99 workshop in which Lovejoy is documented as an active auditor → 005 as a material reworking of that architecture → 1906 abstraction of cross-cutting causal efficacy.**

The claim is programmatic continuity and transformation, not line-by-line lecture transcription.

## 1. 1889–93: taboo was already being separated from morality

The EPHE report covering Marillier’s conferences of 1889–90, 1890–91 and 1891–93 records a sustained sequence on death, funerary rites, sorcery and Oceanian taboo.

For 1890–91 taboo is defined as a set of **protective means against supernatural dangers**. The provisional conclusions for 1891–93 are unusually explicit:

1. the sacred and the impure are originally confused;
2. religious interdictions originate in the idea of a supernatural danger against which one must guard;
3. none of these interdictions appears originally to possess a moral meaning.

Primary control:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1889_num_5_1_19340

This is an exceptionally close prehistory to 005 PDF pp.17–18, where Lovejoy separates violation of custom / taboo / ritual interdict from violation of moral order and then tests social-clan exclusion, blood/contact and suicide without automatically converting them into moral guilt.

Notebook control: 005 PDF pp.17–18, manuscript pp.33–35.

## 2. 1892: institutions and rites can outlive the beliefs that produced them

The 1892–93 EPHE report gives an important methodological statement from Marillier’s Melanesian work. He says the religions were already observed in a period of dissolution and that, in many cases,

> institutions, rites and legends **survived the beliefs that had given birth to them**.

Primary control:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1892_num_6_2_19363

This is high-value for the later half of 005. Lovejoy repeatedly treats a visible ritual complex as historically composite:

- PDF p.65 / MS p.131: several motives can be mingled; delicate analysis is required; syncretism often follows a period in which sacrifices were distinct in character and later combined;
- PDF p.73 / MS p.147: a funerary practice can acquire a new explanatory rationale after the practice itself already exists.

Thus the notebook’s insistence that **present form does not fix historical origin or mechanism** already has a clear institutional antecedent in Marillier’s programme.

## 3. 1893: afterlife is continuation/status, not moral compensation

The 1893–94 EPHE report on the origin of death and condition of souls gives nearly the same analytical baseline that opens 005:

- the abode of the dead resembles the world of the living;
- ranks in the afterlife are marked by the rank held during life, **not merit or moral value**;
- the other life is conceived as a **continuation** of this one rather than a repair of earthly injustice;
- survivance is not founded on moral conceptions.

Primary control:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1893_num_7_3_19386

Marillier’s full memoir, `La survivance de l’âme et l’idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés`, occupies pp.1–46 of the same report:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1893_num_7_3_19382

The report also names **Codrington** and **Haddon** as source authorities in the Melanesian comparative work. This independently reinforces the source-ecology result of batch 33: Lovejoy’s 005 opening bibliography and notes visibly use Codrington, while Marillier’s programme had already made Codrington a working comparative source years earlier.

### Direct 005 concordance

The early notebook explicitly develops a staged distinction among:

- naturalistic continuation / status;
- ritual determinant;
- social/community determinant;
- strictly moral guilt/desert.

PDF pp.16–20 then applies this distinction case by case to manner of death, suicide, ritual violation, clan solidarity, contact and missionary contamination.

The relationship is therefore not just thematic (`future life`). It is an **identical problem of causal/normative classification**.

## 4. 1894: missionary contamination is already an explicit source-critical problem

The 1894–95 EPHE report says Marillier conducted a **critical examination** of flood myths across the Americas and explicitly identified the influence of biblical traditions imported by missionaries.

Primary control:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1894_num_8_4_19409

This gives a direct institutional prehistory to 005 PDF p.19 / MS p.37. Lovejoy’s Pawnee/Murray note asks whether an apparently moralized future-life account can be trusted given its date and missionary contact, and insists that provenance/chronology be kept in mind.

This makes `missionary influence` in 005 part of a longer **text-critical procedure**, not an isolated caution.

## 5. 1896–97: Marillier explicitly decomposes one ceremony into distinct mechanisms

The 1896–97 report on marriage is methodologically important even though marriage is not the principal subject of 005. Marillier says rites that establish religious union must be carefully distinguished from:

- magical rites of purification and preservation;
- fecundative rites.

He also reconstructs a transition in which an originally economic/civil relation can later acquire social and religious value.

Primary control:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1896_num_10_6_19455

This is the same analytical operation later applied to sacrifice in 005: a single ceremony can contain elements whose **functions, causes and histories are different**.

## 6. 1897–98: sacrifice becomes an explicit taxonomy of mechanisms

For 1897–98, Marillier’s second conference is `L’anthropophagie rituelle et les sacrifices humains`. The report says he studied distinct sacrifice types on the West African coast:

- funerary;
- expiatory;
- anthropophagic;
- magical.

Primary control:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1897_num_11_7_19478

The official programme had already announced ritual anthropophagy and human sacrifice:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1896_num_10_6_20489

This is the immediate prehistory to the sacrifice bibliography and typological analysis beginning around 005 PDF pp.61–65.

## 7. 1898–99: Lovejoy is documented inside both problem families at once

The 1898–99 EPHE report is the central institutional document.

### Monday conference: survivance and justice

Marillier’s essential task is described as proving that **no moral element intervenes at the origin** of non-civilized eschatology. The conference explicitly refutes Steinmetz’s objections to Marillier’s memoir and restates the thesis that the afterlife is **continuation**, not compensation, reparation or punishment. New evidence is added especially from the two Americas.

### Tuesday conference: human sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy

The report then describes the second conference as continuing research on human sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy, especially among Bantu populations of southern Africa, and determining the reasons for using human victims in distinct types:

- expiatory;
- funerary;
- anthropophagic;
- magical;

with comparison to Guinea, North-American Indigenous peoples and non-Aryan India; animal bloody sacrifice, agrarian rites and fecundative sacrifice are studied in parallel.

### Lovejoy’s status

The same report lists:

> `Auditeurs ayant pris une part active aux travaux : MM. Lovejoy, W. S. Andrews et Lorette.`

Primary control:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1898_num_12_8_19501

This is the strongest institutional anchor yet for the architecture of 005. The notebook’s two major research blocks correspond to **the two Marillier conferences in which Lovejoy is explicitly said to have taken an active part**.

These pages do not merely echo Marillier’s conclusion. They show Lovejoy **operationalizing** it as a classification and source-critical procedure.

## 10. 1899–1900 confirms that the programme continued after Lovejoy left Paris

The next EPHE report is a useful chronological control, not evidence of Lovejoy’s attendance. Marillier continued:

- agrarian sacrifices among non-Aryan peoples of India, especially the Khonds of Orissa;
- `la puissance magique et fécondatrice du sang`;
- expiatory sacrifice and expulsion of evils;
- critical study of Frazer’s *Golden Bough*;
- condition of the soul after death, origin/nature of death and disease;
- the **amoral** character of eschatology;
- Christian interpolations in American eschatological myths.

Primary control:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1899_num_13_9_19524

This is striking because it shows the exact categories that later become prominent in 005 — blood efficacy, fecundative sacrifice, Frazer criticism, non-moral eschatology, contamination/interpolation — as one continuing Marillier programme.

The evidentiary limit is important: Lovejoy is **not** listed among the 1899–1900 active auditors, so this report demonstrates programme continuity, not direct attendance.

The strongest current sequence is now:

> **1889–93:** death / funerary rites / taboo; supernatural danger separated from moral meaning
>
> **1892:** rites/institutions can survive the beliefs that generated them

> **1893:** afterlife as continuation/status rather than moral compensation

## 12. What this changes at paper level

The paper no longer needs to frame 005 as merely a notebook `influenced by Marillier` or as the residue of one Paris course.

A stronger defensible formulation is:

> **Notebook 005 materializes a research architecture that Marillier had been developing through the EPHE across the 1890s: separating ritual danger from morality, distinguishing continuation from retributive justice, criticizing contaminated ethnographic testimony, and decomposing ritual complexes by causal function and historical layer. Lovejoy encountered the two main branches of that programme directly in 1898–99 and reorganized them in his notebook into a progressively mechanism-centered comparative method.**

The novelty is not simply source inheritance. It is the **transformation of a workshop architecture into Lovejoy’s own analytic procedure**.

This formulation remains safely below the stronger and currently unproved claims that every 005 note derives from Marillier, that Lovejoy copied specific lecture sheets, or that the later history-of-ideas method was already fully formed in Paris.

## 13. Evidence limits

1. EPHE reports summarize courses/programmes and do not preserve every seminar exchange.
2. `active auditor` proves participation, not the exact content of Lovejoy’s oral interventions.
3. Similarity between earlier/later Marillier reports and 005 is strongest when combined with the direct 1898–99 attendance record, exact source overlaps and notebook slips; similarity alone is not treated as proof of borrowing.
4. The 1899–1900 report is programme continuity only; Lovejoy is not listed there.
5. The `Endokannibalismus — Steinmetz` reading is based on direct visual recollation of 005 p.62 and corrects the older uncertain OCR/clean transcription.
6. Marillier’s 1896 review of `Endokannibalismus` still requires direct facsimile transcription before any substantive statement about what Marillier praised/rejected in the review.
