# Lovejoy as Orientalist / comparative religion — Batch 155

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: re-read the established notebook 004 → 1898 publication transformation after Batches 152–154. The question is no longer only what material passes from manuscript to article, but **what kind of claim survives publication and what kind is pruned**. This batch treats publication selection as an evidentiary pattern, not as proof of Lovejoy's private intention.

## Core result

Notebook 004 contains a substantial **manuscript surplus** that does not enter `The Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa` (1898): broad Sāṁkhya/Buddhism chronology, system-identification tests, large psychological taxonomies, an original/derived-elements model, and stronger Nirvāṇa conclusions.

The 1898 article does not simply shorten this material evenly. The surviving claims have a distinctive evidentiary profile.

What is retained and sharpened is mostly:

- page/text-controlled lexical dispute;
- semantic discrimination by usage;
- causal-role differentiation;
- compositeness without loss of configuration;
- explicit acknowledgment of contradictory passages and a provisional ending.

What is muted or removed is mostly:

- broad priority/genealogy of Sāṁkhya and Buddhism;
- the notebook's explicit diagnostic test for identifying genuinely Sāṁkhyan doctrine;
- large-scale reconstruction of Buddhist psychology;
- an explicit original-versus-derived historical anatomy;
- stronger global claims about Nirvāṇa and extinction.

The observable transformation is therefore best described as **evidentiary compression**:

> **the publication narrows the object until the surviving propositions can be supported by more local textual, semantic, and causal controls.**

This does **not** prove that Lovejoy consciously followed a formal rule of `publish only what can be locally proved`. No draft, referee report, or correspondence presently documents such an intention. The secure claim is narrower: the manuscript/article pair displays a non-random asymmetry between the breadth of a proposition and the kind of evidence retained in print.

A second result follows from Batch 153. The notebook's explicit Sāṁkhya **diagnostic-specificity** operation disappears as a topic, but the operator itself does not vanish. It is **relocated** into the article's semantic and causal discrimination. The content is pruned; the proof discipline survives in a different object.

No new GLA/GAL/COV identifier is created in this batch.

## 1. The broad Sāṁkhya/Buddhism priority problem is manuscript surplus

Notebook 004 begins with much more than the two technical terms eventually published.

It works through:

- chronology and priority of Sāṁkhya and Buddhism;
- late surviving Sāṁkhya texts versus possible earlier doctrines;
- Brahmajāla heresies as possible witnesses;
- the diagnostic value of eternity, dualism, soul/body distinctions, and related propositions;
- source criticism of Rhys Davids and the broader Garbe controversy.

The 1898 article largely removes this field. Sāṁkhya remains only locally relevant, especially in lexical/genealogical comparisons around `upādhi/upādi` and Indian philosophical background.

Thus the notebook's broad historical question:

> `How far can early Buddhist witnesses establish a pre-Buddhist Sāṁkhya system?`

is not the published paper's burden.

The article's object is much narrower:

> `What do upādāna and upādisesa mean and what work do they perform in Buddhist doctrine?`

This is not mere shortening. It changes the proposition type from a broad historical genealogy to a local semantic/causal reconstruction.

---

## 2. The explicit diagnostic-specificity move is pruned as content

Batch 153 isolated the strongest authorial intervention in the early Sāṁkhya section.

After copying Rhys Davids, Lovejoy writes:

`To R.D.'s remark it should be added...`

He argues that generic eternity of Matter and Self is not the most discriminating marker; the `separateness of Self and World` / `thorough-going dualism` is more system-defining, and he proposes another Buddhist textual witness accordingly.

This is a clear manuscript operation.

But the 1898 article does not publish a developed argument around this specific diagnostic test. The broad Sāṁkhya identification problem has been removed with the surrounding chronology.

The important archival point is therefore negative:

> **one of the clearest identifiable Lovejoy interventions in the notebook does not survive as a published thesis in 1898.**

That makes it especially valuable as evidence of research practice rather than reception history. A historian using only the published article would miss it.

---

## 3. Yet the operator survives by relocation: from system diagnosticity to semantic discrimination

Although the Sāṁkhya diagnostic content is absent, the article repeatedly performs the same higher-order operation on different evidence.

### In the notebook

Competing doctrinal parallels share a generic feature (`eternity`). Lovejoy moves to a more discriminating feature (`dualistic separateness`).

### In the `upādāna` article

Competing identifications share lexical/classificatory overlap. Lovejoy moves to **causal role**:

- skandhas are not identical with `upādāna` merely because `upādānakkhandha` exists;
- karma and `upādāna` both bear on rebirth but perform different causal jobs.

### In the `upādi/upadhi` problem

Competing genealogies remain etymologically possible. Because etymology cannot discriminate, Lovejoy moves to **textual distribution and doctrinal usage**.

The operator has therefore changed object but not basic form:

`shared / ambiguous evidence`  
→ `insufficient discrimination`  
→ `replace with a feature/test carrying more functional information`.

The manuscript-to-publication transformation is consequently not `method discarded with unused material`.

It is:

> **topic deletion + operator retention.**

---

## 4. Khandha/nidāna mapping is compressed rather than abandoned

A second transformation confirms the pattern.

The notebook spends many pages mapping the twelve nidānas onto the five khandhas and asking whether the sequence is temporal, logical, psychological, karmic, or composite.

The article does not reproduce the full diagrams/taxonomy. Instead it condenses them into a more sharply testable causal architecture:

- the formula is not a strict simple temporal chain;
- past/present/future relations can be distinguished;
- `karma` explains the particular condition/form of rebirth;
- `upādāna` explains renewed existence as such.

The large classificatory apparatus is therefore pruned, while a specific causal distinction is retained.

This is another instance of evidentiary compression:

`large exploratory map`  
→ `small publishable causal proposition`.

---

## 5. Historical stratification is muted while configuration remains

Notebook 004 ends with an explicit historical-structural anatomy:

- original elements: flux / Three Characteristics + dependent origination;
- derived elements: sense-perception theory, psychology, khandhas.

The article does **not** publish this original/derived scheme as a firm historical reconstruction.

Yet it preserves a related but less genealogically demanding proposition. Lovejoy allows that the terminology and perhaps even ideas are largely borrowed, while insisting that their **arrangement and application are original and characteristic**.

This is a crucial shift in proof burden.

The notebook asks:

> which elements are historically original and which derived?

The article can establish more securely:

> whatever their provenance, the elements perform a distinctive role in the Buddhist configuration.

Thus provenance is not denied; it is **bracketed where the historical anatomy is harder to prove**, while configurational intelligibility is retained.

This is exactly the distinction that later becomes important in the 1902 proof-level architecture.

---

## 6. Stronger Nirvāṇa conclusions are softened

The notebook at points explores a stronger conclusion about absolute cessation of existence as the Buddhist goal.

The published article narrows the issue to the technical semantics of `upādisesa/anupādisesa` and resists equating the religious goal simply with physical death or disappearance of the aggregates.

Crucially, the article ends by acknowledging passages favorable to the conventional interpretation and does not pretend the whole dispute is closed.

This is another asymmetry:

- broader metaphysical conclusion: muted;
- narrower usage claim: argued extensively but left provisional where textual contradiction remains.

Again, this does not prove an intentional editorial philosophy. It shows that uncertainty is **carried into print rather than erased** when the narrow claim itself encounters resistant evidence.

## 7. The publication gradient

The manuscript/article pair can now be mapped by claim breadth.

| Manuscript problem | 1898 publication treatment | Transformation |
|---|---|---|
| Sāṁkhya/Buddhism priority and chronology | largely omitted | broad genealogy pruned |
| `thorough-going dualism` as diagnostic marker | omitted as explicit Sāṁkhya thesis | content pruned; discrimination operator relocates |
| full khandha/nidāna diagrams and psychology | heavily compressed | exploratory taxonomy → causal proposition |
| original vs derived Buddhist elements | not asserted as full historical anatomy | genealogy muted; configuration retained |
| Senart `upādāna = upādānakkhandha` problem | central published target | local semantic/function test sharpened |
| karma vs `upādāna` | sharpened | causal non-equivalence published |
| `upādi/upadhi` competing etymologies | retained but etymology bracketed | usage becomes deciding evidence |
| stronger Nirvāṇa/metaphysical conclusion | softened | narrower semantic thesis retained |
| contradictory passages | retained | conclusion explicitly provisional |

The visible gradient is:

> **the broader the genealogical/metaphysical claim, the more likely it is to be suppressed or softened; the more locally controllable the semantic/causal claim, the more likely it is to be sharpened.**

This is an observed publication-selection pattern, not a causal explanation of editorial intention.

## 8. Why this matters for the archive argument

The archive now contributes something the publication cannot.

If one reads only the 1898 article, Lovejoy appears as a narrowly technical Buddhist philologist intervening in two lexical problems.

Notebook 004 reveals that the article was extracted from a much broader research process involving:

- competing historical genealogies;
- source criticism;
- system-identification criteria;
- psychological diagrams;
- stratification hypotheses;
- unresolved metaphysical questions.

The published article therefore records the **result of selection**, while the notebook records the **space of rejected, suspended, or compressed alternatives**.

This makes the archive historiographically useful not because every discarded idea later became important, but because it exposes the transformations by which a broad comparative dossier became a narrow claim.

A compact formulation is:

> **the notebook preserves the proof problems that publication removes.**

## 9. Relation to Batch 154 and 1902

Batch 154 distinguished two gates:

1. diagnostic specificity;
2. independent genealogical warrant / explanatory non-necessity.

Batch 155 adds a publication-level observation.

The 1898 article preferentially publishes claims that can be adjudicated **before** the hardest genealogical gate:

- semantic role;
- causal role;
- textual usage;
- configurational arrangement.

It largely avoids turning the notebook's broad Sāṁkhya genealogy into a firm published conclusion.

By 1902 Lovejoy can return to large-scale genealogy because he makes the proof partition itself explicit: an affinity can be admitted while transmission remains separately unproved; an internal genealogy can be reconstructed; historical systems can be decomposed into combinations and survivals.

The developmental sequence can therefore be restated:

`004 exploratory comparative surplus`  
→ `1898 evidentiary compression into local semantic/causal claims`  
→ `1902 re-expansion to comparative philosophy with explicit separation of affinity, logical function, and genealogy`.

This is more precise than saying simply that Lovejoy moved from philology to history of ideas.

## 10. Strongest paper formulation

> **Lovejoy's 1898 Buddhist article was not a miniature of notebook 004. It was a selective extraction from it. The notebook's broad Sāṁkhya chronology, explicit test for a diagnostically Sāṁkhyan doctrine, large psychological taxonomy, and original-versus-derived model were mostly suppressed. What survived were claims that could be controlled more locally: the semantic difference between attachment and its objects, the different causal roles of karma and upādāna, and the use of textual distribution when competing etymologies could not decide meaning. The archive therefore reveals a significant asymmetry between research and publication. Broad genealogical hypotheses remain in the working dossier, while the published intervention concentrates on narrower semantic and causal propositions and even leaves those provisional where contrary passages persist. The result is not evidence for a formal editorial doctrine, but it is evidence that a diagnostic method could survive the removal of the particular historical thesis on which it had first been exercised.**

## Evidence discipline

- `manuscript surplus`, `evidentiary compression`, `topic deletion + operator retention`, and `publication gradient` are analytical labels, not Lovejoy's terminology.
- The notebook/article relationship is extremely close, but exact dating of every notebook leaf relative to submission/publication remains unresolved.
- Do not infer that Lovejoy consciously rejected every omitted hypothesis; omission can reflect scope, space, venue, timing, uncertainty, or other unknown editorial factors.
- Do not infer a lost larger publication solely from the surplus material.
- The strongest secure observation is comparative: which claims and operations are present in the notebook and which are present in the published article.
- The 1898 article's provisional ending is evidence of published uncertainty, not proof of a general lifelong methodological rule.
