## Core result

The exact appearance of Rudolf S. Steinmetz's `Endokannibalismus` in Lovejoy's 005 sacrifice bibliography is a secure bibliographic-object bridge into a live Paris controversy. The primary texts now permit a more exact reconstruction than the early batch sequence did.

The decisive historical contrast is **not** `Steinmetz = one cause` versus `Lovejoy = many causes`.

Steinmetz himself explicitly attacks `Simplomanie`, distinguishes motives, tests contrary/negative conditions, evaluates evidentiary reliability, and allows animistic, magical, ceremonial and communion-like meanings to become later motives or refunctionalizations. What remains architectonic in his argument is a strong **origin hierarchy**: ordinary/nutritional corpse-eating is reconstructed as historically primary, while many religious interpretations are treated as secondary additions to an older custom.

Mauss changes the relevant explanatory level by redescribing `endocannibalisme` as `l'anthropophagie rituelle des parents`, foregrounding kin relation, ritual setting, solidarity and transfer of the dead person's virtues.

Marillier's now directly recovered 1896 review goes further than the earlier project reconstruction expected. He explicitly argues that practices grouped by outward resemblance can be generated by disparate reasons and therefore are **not susceptible to one interpretation**. He accepts physiological/economic causes for some cases but rejects Steinmetz's claim to a unique origin; animistic or magical conceptions may independently explain other cases.

Lovejoy 005 then works at still finer resolution. Kinship, ceremoniality, ingestion, sacrifice-language and bodily incorporation are repeatedly treated as insufficient by themselves to settle classification. Cases are redistributed among famine, revenge, affection, funerary relation, propitiation, sympathetic/transferable efficacy, sacramental communion and other operative mechanisms.

The strongest sequence is therefore:

> **Steinmetz: local motive plurality + strong origin hierarchy**
> → **Mauss: relational/ritual reclassification of the surviving practice**
> → **Marillier: external similarity does not license one explanatory class or one origin**
> → **Lovejoy 005: mechanism-level redistribution of individual cases without allowing one origin narrative to exhaust later classification**.

This is a controversy-level genealogy and a shared problem field. It does not prove that Lovejoy read Mauss's article or copied Marillier's review.

## II. Steinmetz primary text: anti-`Simplomanie` within a strong origin hierarchy

Primary source: complete 1896 `Endokannibalismus`, printed pp.1–60, recovered from the bound Vienna Anthropological Society volume.

### 1. Methodological anti-simplification

Steinmetz explicitly says motives must be distinguished and warns against selecting one plausible motive and letting it explain the whole phenomenon. He calls such one-sided explanatory reduction `Simplomanie`.

His origin analysis is built through a sequence of enabling or negative conditions rather than one crude cause:

- omnivory / demand for meat;
- absence or historical variability of disgust;
- absence of fear that the dead spirit will retaliate;
- absence of sympathy with the corpse;
- absence of a strong desecration taboo;
- hunger and economic pressure as one factor among others.

Printed p.46 is especially important: episodic hunger does not by itself explain continuing endocannibalism, since groups practising it are not simply the economically most desperate.

Therefore the project must not caricature Steinmetz as incapable of motive plurality or counterexample testing.

### 2. p.45: corpse manipulation, danger and transfer

The p.44–45 cluster includes corpse substances used to acquire strength, magical use of corpse matter, and mutilation/binding/disabling of corpses as protection against the dead person's return or vengeance.

This page supports a field in which corpse relation, danger, transfer and prophylaxis are already differentiated. It should not be reduced to `selected-part eating = communion`.

### 3. p.52 §26: vital-force transfer as secondary refunctionalization

Section 26 asks whether animism caused endocannibalism. Steinmetz denies that animism is the original cause but allows it to:

- furnish new motives;
- preserve an older practice;
- reinterpret nutritional corpse use as appropriation of soul / vital force;
- generate reduced or ceremonial forms;
- provide later sentimental or honorific rationalizations.

Thus transfer of soul/vital force is already in the source field, but Steinmetz ranks it historically as a **later motive layered onto an older custom**.

### 4. pp.59–60: the architectonic commitment

The conclusion permits later religious meanings, including communion-like interpretations, but retains a strong universal/phylogenetic priority claim: nutritional corpse-eating under reconstructed primitive conditions is treated as historically prior to the later religious motives.

The correct characterization is therefore:

> **local explanatory pluralism + global genealogical priority**.

## III. Mauss: reclassification by social relation and ritual mechanism

In `La religion et les origines du droit pénal d'après un livre récent`, Mauss does not merely repeat Steinmetz's category. He says `endocannibalisme` can be named more exactly:

> `l'anthropophagie rituelle des parents`.

This changes the classificatory unit. The relevant features become:

- the eaten person is a relative;
- the act is embedded in funerary/ritual practice;
- the family/clan relation is central;
- the dead person's `vertus` pass to survivors;
- the common meal can realize solidarity or communion.

Mauss also uses Marillier's `Tabou mélanésien`, pp.53–56, as an assembled Melanesian evidence packet and criticizes overly broad comparative rubrics. His point is not that Steinmetz lacks counterexamples; it is that comparison can remain invalid if heterogeneous relations are counted under one heading.

Thus:

> **classification controls what comparison can prove**.

Mauss is a contemporary controversy control, not proof of direct Lovejoy reading.

## IV. Marillier 1896 review: the anti-single-interpretation operator is directly upstream

Primary source: Léon Marillier, review of Rudolf S. Steinmetz, `Endokannibalismus`, `Revue de l'histoire des religions` 34 (1896), **pp.113–115**. Page 116 fixes the review boundary.

### p.113

Marillier recognizes the strong religious significance of surviving endocannibal practices and accurately represents Steinmetz's thesis that religious motives may be superadded to practices whose origin lies elsewhere. He nevertheless insists that many observed anthropophagic customs are inseparable from animistic beliefs as actually practised.

### p.114 — decisive methodological statement

Marillier emphasizes the **variety and disparate character of the reasons** given for cannibal practices. His conclusion is that the cases are phenomena grouped together only through **external resemblances** and are **not susceptible to one interpretation**.

This directly removes a major possible Lovejoy-priority claim. Before Lovejoy's Paris notebook, his teacher had already published the rule:

> **same outward form ≠ one explanatory class**.

### p.115 — rejection of unique origin

Marillier concedes that physiological and economic causes may be decisive in many cases, but says Steinmetz has not established a **unique origin**. In some cases animistic or magical conceptions are sufficient explanations. The chronological priority of one global form cannot therefore simply be inferred.

He closes by praising Steinmetz's information while criticizing his `théories exclusives et parfois hasardées`.

The most accurate contrast is consequently:

> **Steinmetz: plurality of local motives subordinated to one origin hierarchy**
> versus
> **Marillier: external similarity is insufficient for explanatory unification, and origin itself may be plural**.

Lovejoy's later notebook work belongs inside this pre-existing methodological environment.

## V. Lovejoy 005: mechanism-level case redistribution

Primary loci: corrected 005 transcription, especially PDF pp.62–75 and 106–119.

### 1. Ritual form does not decide the class

Lovejoy repeatedly refuses to infer sacrificial or sacramental meaning from visible morphology alone:

- ritualized killing can be punishment/accountability rather than sacrifice;
- public repetition or ceremonial regulation does not make anthropophagy sacramental;
- human/animal substitution is not accepted as a universal developmental law;
- funerary companion-killing can be provision/service or communication rather than communion;
- casting a victim to a dangerous water power can be propitiation rather than sacramental union.

### 2. Anthropophagy is redistributed by operative relation

The final block separates live explanatory candidates including:

- famine / nutritional necessity;
- revenge / hatred;
- affection or kin relation;
- acquisition of qualities;
- sympathetic / transferable efficacy;
- propitiation;
- sacramental union.

Kinship and ceremoniality remain evidence, but not sufficient diagnostics.

### 3. Selected body parts: transfer is one mechanism, not the genus

Lovejoy's p.119 sequence associates:

- head/brain with intelligence;
- heart with courage/breath/life;
- sexual organs with sexual power.

He treats these mainly through transferable/sympathetic efficacy. Historical derivation from whole-body eating can remain possible without making the present mechanism identical to communion.

This is the key distinction:

> **genealogy and operative mechanism can be analytically separable**.

### 4. Relation to 1906

The 005 notebook repeatedly extracts causal relations across morphologically different rites. By 1906 one recurrent relation, impersonal/direct/transferable efficacy, is abstracted upward as a comparative category.

This should not be described as a universal common-origin claim. The stronger developmental point is:

> **recurring causal mechanism ≠ single institutional origin**.

## VIII. Final attribution

### No longer safe as Lovejoy-specific

- recognition that one broad practice can have multiple motives;
- rejection of form alone as sufficient explanation;
- distinction between nutritional, animistic, magical and ritual motives;
- awareness of later refunctionalization;
- the general rule that externally similar practices may require different explanations;
- rejection of one unique origin for all cases.

Steinmetz, Mauss and especially Marillier already occupy this methodological terrain before Lovejoy's documented Paris participation.

### Strong Lovejoy manuscript delta

- unusually dense redistribution of borderline cases within one working notebook;
- separation of historical derivation from present causal function;
- portability of the same burden-of-proof discipline across survivance, sacrifice, blood, charms/fetishes, foundation rites, agriculture and anthropophagy;
- later abstraction of one recurrent causal architecture into the 1906 comparative category.

The strongest formulation is therefore not `Lovejoy invents mechanism analysis`, but:

> **005 shows Lovejoy intensively operationalizing and transporting a controversy-level discipline of explanatory redivision across heterogeneous domains.**
