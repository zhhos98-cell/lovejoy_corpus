## Direct evaluative claims

The review attacks Dahlmann's framing at the level of the question itself.

1. Dahlmann's Nirvāṇa alternative—annihilation versus everlasting bliss—is rejected as a wrongly stated problem.
2. His second alternative—Buddhism derived either from Sāṃkhya dualism or Vedānta monism—is likewise rejected. The reviewer emphasizes the chronological distortion involved in treating later system labels as if they could straightforwardly function as antecedent sources for early Buddhism.
3. The reviewer explicitly says the proper response is to reject both horns of the dilemma rather than choose between them.
4. Dahlmann is criticized for not actually providing a detailed account of early Indian Buddhism or a detailed comparison with the later systems from which it is supposedly derived.
5. The review nevertheless praises the collection and organization of Mahābhārata philosophical passages as useful and suggestive.
6. The central reservation is methodological: the reviewer wants the facts disentangled from the author's wide-reaching theory, questions whether the passages constitute a single system, and questions the extension of the name `Sāṃkhya` to the whole philosophical material of the epic.
