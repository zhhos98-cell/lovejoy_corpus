# Batch 19 — Cross-student control: Söderblom 1901 ↔ Lovejoy notebook 005 ↔ Marillier’s survivance seminar

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced

## Research move

The direct plaintext capture of Nathan Söderblom’s 1901 *La vie future d’après le mazdéisme* makes possible the first **same-teacher, same-seminar, cross-student concordance** with Lovejoy notebook `MS38_004_001_061_005`.

The result is stronger than a thematic resemblance. Söderblom explicitly says he profited from Marillier’s 1898–99 conferences and from Marillier’s *Survivance* study; 005 has the inserted heading `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`; and the two students work through several of the **same source cases, books, page-level problems and causal distinctions**. Yet they organize them differently. That difference is the most useful evidence.

## 1. Shared conceptual starting point: continuation is not retribution

### Söderblom, printed pp.17–18

Söderblom adopts Tylor’s distinction between `continuation theory` and `retribution theory`, credits Marillier with translating the former as `survivance`, and defines the relevant class by the **absence of specifically moral and religious considerations**. Life after death is a prolongation of life before death, not reward or punishment.

### Lovejoy 005, PDF pp.3–6 and pp.14–15

Lovejoy begins with the same analytical problem. Rank, wealth, magical power, ritual competence and worldly status can continue beyond death without constituting moral recompense. He then develops an explicit staged outline in which naturalistic continuity, ritual determinants and social/community determinants must be separated before strictly moral guilt appears.

### Judgment

This is a shared Marillier problem-field, not proof of textual dependence between the two students. The importance lies in the way each turns Marillier’s continuation/retribution distinction into an analytical architecture.

## 2. Same source: C. A. Murray on the Pawnees

### Söderblom, printed p.19

In the section illustrating continued earthly occupations, Söderblom cites:

> C. A. Murray, *Travels in North America*, I, p.365

for Pawnees hunting peacefully and smoking their pipes in the future life.

### Lovejoy 005, PDF p.19 / MS p.37

Lovejoy also cites **Murray, *Travels in North America*, vol. I**, in connection with Pawnees and a happy hunting ground. But the notebook immediately turns the citation into a **source-critical problem**: an 1819-era or early nineteenth-century statement may be affected by missionary influence, so provenance/contact chronology must be checked before treating the belief as indigenous evidence.

### Difference

Söderblom uses Murray mainly as an example inside a comparative typology. Lovejoy foregrounds **contamination/contact criticism**. The shared source therefore reveals divergence of method, not merely shared reading.

## 3. Same Greenland/Eskimo problem: two destinations without reward/punishment

### Söderblom, printed pp.29–30

In `e) Deux endroits`, Söderblom cites **Hans Egede** on Greenland. Women who die in childbirth and people drowned while whaling reach a pleasant realm, while another post-mortem region is worse. Söderblom stresses the missionary’s difficulty with the combination: **two destinations, but no reward and no punishment**.

### Lovejoy 005, PDF p.11 / MS p.19, dated `Dec. 14`

Lovejoy records essentially the same Greenland/Eskimo test: two abodes; the favorable one associated with drowning and women dying in childbirth; the distinction is not moral punishment/reward and is better understood through ritual/magical conditions. He then adds Rink and other Eskimo material.

### Difference

Söderblom places the case in a morphology of one/two/multiple abodes of the dead. Lovejoy treats it as a diagnostic test for **whether a differential future destiny is actually moral**.

## 4. Same Madagascar cluster: Abinal & Vaissière, Hova, second death

This is the strongest cross-student source cluster so far.

### Söderblom, printed pp.26–27

Söderblom cites the Révérends Pères **Abinal et Vaissière, *Vingt ans à Madagascar***. He discusses:

- Hova beliefs about the kingdom/mountain of the dead, Ambondrombé;
- Betsileo material;
- Vazimba prestige and supposed secret/sorcerous power;
- the claim that Hova souls pass through regions and undergo a **second death** after a limited period;
- different post-mortem status continuing earthly rank;
- contradictory testimonies and the possibility that European observers have **systematized beliefs that indigenous actors did not themselves make fully consistent**.

### Lovejoy 005, PDF pp.29–30 / inserted slip on MS p.55

The notebook has an inserted bibliographic slip explicitly headed:

> `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`

It then turns to **Madagascar**, distinguishes population/layer questions, cites material including **Le P. Vaissière / *Vingt ans à Madagascar* [?]**, and continues with Hova material, the abode/mountain/forest of the dead, continued occupations, and a **second death / annihilation after a limited future life**.

### Judgment

This is no longer a loose contextual parallel. We have:

- same Marillier seminar/problem label;
- same regional cluster;
- same named source/book;
- same Hova case;
- same second-death problem;
- two students from the same 1898–99 seminar orbit.

The safest inference is a **shared Marillier source apparatus / seminar dossier field**, not that one student copied the other. Söderblom’s direct testimony that Marillier circulated his own notes makes dossier-mediated convergence materially plausible.

## 5. Same Cheyenne / status-selection problem

### Söderblom, printed pp.25–26

In `Survivance des plus aptes`, Söderblom cites Cheyenne material in which only certain deaths/status conditions give access to the happy hunting grounds. The surrounding section treats survival or superior afterlife as a function of status, rank, prowess or non-moral qualification.

### Lovejoy 005, PDF pp.21–23

Lovejoy discusses Cheyenne powers/gods and access to a happy hunting ground, emphasizing that bodily/ritual conditions such as being scalped, warrior status or other non-ethical conditions cannot simply be translated into moral merit.

### Difference

Again, Söderblom’s grouping is morphological/status-based; Lovejoy asks which **causal determinant** is doing the sorting and whether the analyst is illicitly moralizing it.

## 6. Same mode-of-death problem

### Söderblom

His section `c) D’après les différents genres de mort` groups future destinies determined by manner of death and burial rather than piety. Women dead in childbirth, drowned persons, warriors and suicides repeatedly appear.

### Lovejoy

005 repeatedly uses suicide, drowning, childbirth and violent death as tests. Lovejoy pushes further into the social logic of suicide: apparent punishment may arise from clan solidarity, taboo, exclusion or status rather than individual moral guilt. Later moralization can result from cultural contact or diffusion.

### Difference

Lovejoy’s notebook is more aggressively **mechanism-separating and source-critical**. It does not merely classify kinds of death; it asks what social, ritual or moral logic makes that death consequential.

## 7. The two students explicitly differ in classificatory architecture

Söderblom himself supplies the key methodological clue. Around printed p.33 he says he is attaching himself to Marillier’s remarkable study but proceeding **“avec d’autres divisions et à un point de vue un peu différent.”**

His sequence is broadly:

1. define continuation/survivance;
2. classify the **abode/form of survival**: one place, survival of the most fit, animal forms, fixed/wandering souls, two/multiple places, divided components;
3. classify **separation among the dead** by chance, terrestrial condition/status, manner of death;
4. compare Marillier’s moral criterion;
5. move toward transition into retribution.

Lovejoy’s 005 produces a different abstraction. The clearest notebook outline (PDF pp.14–15) sorts determinants as:

1. naturalistic continuation/status;
2. ritual/religious performance;
3. social/community membership and obligation;
4. other social allocation mechanisms;
5. strictly moral guilt/desert.

This is analytically important. **Lovejoy is not simply reproducing Marillier’s or Söderblom’s taxonomy.** He is increasingly classifying by the *kind of mechanism or norm that produces the result*.

That orientation becomes even more visible in the sacrifice half of 005 and is exactly the operation later generalized in the 1906 `Primitive Philosophy` paper as a theory of quasi-mechanical/transferable causal efficacy.

## 8. New historiographical formulation

The cross-student comparison gives a more precise way to describe Marillier’s influence:

> **Marillier transmitted a problem-field, source apparatus and discipline of `critique des faits`; different students then reorganized that shared material according to distinct analytical interests.**

Söderblom’s 1901 book turns the shared survivance dossier into comparative eschatology centered on forms/stages of post-mortem existence and their transition toward retribution.

Lovejoy’s 005 turns much of the same field into a taxonomy of **determinants and causal mechanisms**: naturalistic continuation, status, ritual, magic, social membership, contact/diffusion, and moral desert.

This is a stronger and more historically grounded claim than saying that Marillier simply “influenced” Lovejoy. The seminar appears as a **generative research environment in which shared documents could yield different conceptual abstractions**.

## 9. Evidence limits

Secure:

- same 1898–99 Marillier orbit;
- Söderblom explicitly benefited from Marillier’s conferences and notes;
- 005 explicitly marks `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`;
- multiple exact/near-exact source and case convergences: Murray/Pawnee, Greenland/Egede-type problem, Madagascar/Abinal-Vaissière/Hova/second death, manner-of-death classes.

Strong inference:

- both students drew from a shared Marillier source apparatus, whether via published *Survivance*, seminar discussion, working notes, or some mixture of all three.

Still unproved:

- that Lovejoy physically consulted the same Marillier sheets used by Söderblom;
- that any specific 005 passage is copied from a Marillier note rather than from Marillier’s publication or a common source;
- exact chronology of all 005 leaves beyond the internally dated anchors.
