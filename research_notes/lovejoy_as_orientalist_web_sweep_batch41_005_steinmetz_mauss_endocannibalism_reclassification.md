A fresh visual check of 005 PDF p.62 / MS p.125 resolves an important bibliographic uncertainty in the corrected transcription. Lovejoy writes, clearly enough to treat the identification as secure:

> **`Endokannibalismus — Steinmetz.`**

The archive transcription has been corrected accordingly from the earlier uncertain `Ritualkannibalismus — Steinmetz [?]` reading.

This exact title opens a tighter contextual reconstruction than a generic `cannibalism` genealogy. In 1896 Rudolf Steinmetz published *Endokannibalismus* in *Mittheilungen der Anthropologischen Gesellschaft in Wien*, vol. XXVI. In the same year Léon Marillier reviewed that exact work in *Revue de l’histoire des religions* 34, pp.113–115. Later in the same RHR volume Marcel Mauss began his extended critique of Steinmetz; the critical continuation appeared in vol.35. Mauss there explicitly reclassified what Steinmetz called `endocannibalisme` as, more precisely, **ritual anthropophagy of relatives**, and discussed the transfer of the dead person’s virtues to those who consume the body.

This gives notebook 005 a highly specific live controversy behind its later anthropophagy pages. The important continuity is again not a borrowed doctrine but a **problem of how to divide one outward practice into distinct motives and mechanisms**.

## 1. Steinmetz 1896 is now a directly identified notebook source

Bibliographic control:

- Rudolf S. Steinmetz, `Endokannibalismus`, *Mittheilungen der Anthropologischen Gesellschaft in Wien* XXVI (1896), 60 pp. / article occupying the relevant vol.26 fascicle;
- contemporary reviews identify it explicitly as a reprint from vol. XXVI;
- Lovejoy 005 PDF p.62 / MS p.125 writes the exact title and author.

A contemporary American review by W. I. Thomas (*American Journal of Sociology* 2 [1897], pp.610–611) is useful as an independent control on the contents of Steinmetz’s work. Thomas says the study gives a comparative description of cannibalism within the tribe together with a table recording:

- peoples said to practise it;
- motives assigned to the practice;
- reliability of the information.

The motives summarized by Thomas include food scarcity/hunger, appetite or taste for human flesh, and animistic belief. Thomas also emphasizes Steinmetz’s attempt to expose contrary as well as supporting evidence and praises the article as a model of ethnological source management.

This matters because Steinmetz should not be reduced to a crude morphological classifier. He was already sorting motives and source reliability. The later controversy concerns **how those motives should be weighted, related, and generalized**.

## 3. Mauss performs a useful reclassification of Steinmetz’s category

In the critical part of `La religion et les origines du droit pénal`, Mauss discusses the broad distribution of what Steinmetz called `endocannibalisme`, but immediately says it could be named more exactly as:

> `l’anthropophagie rituelle des parents`

The analytical change is significant. The classificatory object is no longer simply `cannibalism within the group`; it becomes a relational and ritual configuration involving kin, funerary practice and clan solidarity.

Mauss then reports a recurrent indigenous/ethnographic explanation: eating dead relatives permits the **virtues of the dead** to pass to the survivors. Whether or not one accepts Mauss’s wider theory of clan solidarity, the operation is clear:

> visible act of eating a human body → specify relation to victim → specify ritual setting → specify transmitted quality/effect.

That operation is exceptionally close to the one Lovejoy later performs in 005 pp.106–119.

## 4. Mauss’s Steinmetz critique also explicitly routes through Marillier’s 1896 Melanesian dossier

The same Mauss essay contains a direct reference to:

> L. Marillier, `Tabou mélanésien`, pp.53–56

and identifies those pages as a place where most of the relevant Melanesian texts were assembled. This independently confirms that Marillier’s 1896 work was functioning as a **source packet** for a contemporary critique of Steinmetz, not simply as a detached publication.

The Mauss discussion of taboo also uses differential magical efficacy: a person may survive a taboo violation because, in Melanesian terms, he has a superior `mana`/magical power. This gives a same-controversy control for two problems already independently visible in the Marillier genealogy behind 005:

1. **classification by mechanism rather than surface form**;
2. **differential supernatural efficacy as an explanatory variable**.

This does not prove a linear `Mauss → Lovejoy` transmission. It does show that Steinmetz, Marillier’s assembled Melanesian evidence, taboo sanction, `mana`, and ritual anthropophagy were already being made to interact in one Paris comparative-religion argument before Lovejoy’s seminar year.

### pp.106–108 — refuse the category as self-explanatory

The later notebook asks whether eating human flesh is in itself religious. Lovejoy separates:

- famine;
- revenge/hatred;
- affection for relatives;
- acquisition of qualities;
- sympathetic magic;
- propitiation;
- sacramental union.

He also says that publicity, solemnity, repetition and fixed ceremonial form do not by themselves establish a sacramental origin.

### p.108 — efficacy rather than communion

Selected organs may be eaten because they are credited with transmitting strength, courage, intelligence or another quality. Lovejoy explicitly allows this to belong to sympathetic magic rather than sacrifice/communion.

### p.117 — distributional conclusion

The notebook summarizes the larger sacrifice field by treating alimentary and expiatory/propitiatory sacrifice as common while sacramental union is comparatively exceptional. This blocks a universal communion model.

### p.119 — selected-organ transfer

The final sustained anthropophagy passage makes the mechanism extremely concrete:

- head/brain → intelligence;
- heart → courage/breath/life;
- sexual organs → sexual power.

The outward form `eating part of a human body` is therefore reclassified through **what property is believed to reside in the part and what effect its ingestion is supposed to produce**.

This is one of the cleanest documentary sequences in the notebook:

> **named recent source on p.62 → comparative decomposition of the source-domain on pp.106–119.**

## 6. The strongest historical comparison is not `Steinmetz wrong, Lovejoy right`

The newly recovered controls make a more precise sequence possible.

### Steinmetz

Already builds a comparative table of cases, motives and source reliability, but advances broad claims about the primitive prevalence and causal priority of hunger/food conditions.

### Mauss

Reweights and subdivides the field. `Endocannibalism` becomes more exactly ritual anthropophagy of relatives in a particular relational/religious setting; transfer of the dead person’s virtues becomes one mechanism requiring explanation.

### Marillier

Directly reviews *Endokannibalismus* in 1896; his exact review intervention is still blocked. Independently, his 1896 Melanesian taboo dossier is cited inside Mauss’s critique, and his 1898–99 EPHE programme explicitly returns to Steinmetz-related problems.

### Lovejoy 005

Carries the exact Steinmetz title as a working source, then refuses to let cannibalism, ceremoniality or body-eating define one explanatory class. It redistributes cases among hunger, vengeance, funerary relation, transferable qualities, sympathetic efficacy, propitiation and sacramental union.

The genealogy is therefore best described as a **controversy-driven refinement of comparative classification**, not a replacement of an unsophisticated predecessor by a sophisticated successor.
