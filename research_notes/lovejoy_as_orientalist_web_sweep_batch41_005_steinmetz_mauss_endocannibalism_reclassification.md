## 2. Mauss narrows Steinmetz’s category by relation and ritual setting

In the critical part of `La religion et les origines du droit pénal d’après un livre récent`, Mauss discusses what Steinmetz called `endocannibalisme` and immediately says that it could be named more exactly:

> **`l’anthropophagie rituelle des parents`**

This is a small lexical move with a large classificatory consequence. `Endocannibalism` names a spatial/social inclusion relation: eating people from within one’s own group. Mauss’s phrase specifies instead:

- **who** is eaten: relatives;
- **what setting** frames the act: ritual/funerary practice;
- **what social relation** matters: clan/family solidarity.

The comparison therefore moves from a broad behavioural class toward a historically and relationally specified rite.

That operation is directly useful for 005 because Lovejoy’s later anthropophagy pages repeatedly insist that the visible fact `human flesh is eaten` does not yet tell us what sort of religious act, if any, has occurred.

## 3. Mauss gives an exact transfer-of-quality mechanism

Immediately in this discussion Mauss reports a recurrent explanation from observers: by eating their dead relatives, people believe that the **virtues of the dead pass to them**.

The relevant analytical sequence is:

> body/person → property or virtue attributed to the dead → ingestion/contact → transmission to survivor.

That sequence is a much closer contemporary control for 005 p.119 than the generic statement that both authors discuss cannibalism.

005’s final sustained anthropophagy passage separates body parts according to the quality believed to reside in them:

- head/brain → intelligence;
- heart → courage/breath/life;
- sexual organs → sexual power.

Lovejoy then classifies this primarily as sympathetic/transferable efficacy rather than sufficient evidence for sacramental communion.

The historical point should remain modest but precise:

> **the Steinmetz controversy already contained a move from the broad category `endocannibalism` to a transfer-of-qualities mechanism; 005 later makes that mechanism one member of a still finer taxonomy of anthropophagic motives.**

## 4. Mauss explicitly routes the same controversy through Marillier’s 1896 source packet

Elsewhere in the same critical essay, when turning to Melanesian taboo sanctions, Mauss cites:

> **L. Marillier, `Tabou mélanésien`, pp.53–56**

and says that these pages bring together most of the relevant Melanesian texts.

This is a stronger documentary fact than a thematic resemblance. It shows Marillier’s 1896 publication functioning contemporaneously as an **assembled evidence packet** inside the Steinmetz critique.

The same Mauss passage then explains exceptional survival after taboo violation by differential magical power: a violator may possess a superior `mana`. Thus within one controversy complex we can observe:

- Steinmetz’s comparative problem;
- Marillier’s assembled Melanesian evidence;
- Mauss’s reclassification of ritual anthropophagy;
- transfer of qualities/virtues;
- differential supernatural efficacy (`mana`).

These are exactly the domains that later become separated and recombined across 005.

This remains a **contextual network**, not proof that Lovejoy read Mauss’s article or copied Marillier’s printed pages.

## 5. The 005 concordance becomes sharper

The older comparison was:

> Steinmetz category → Lovejoy splits motives.

The stronger comparison is now:

### Steinmetz

A broad comparative class, already internally coded by motives and source reliability.

### Mauss

A relational redivision inside that field: `endocannibalisme` → `anthropophagie rituelle des parents`, with transmission of the dead person’s virtues as a specific mechanism.

### 005

A further decomposition of anthropophagy into:

- famine;
- revenge/hatred;
- affection/kin relation;
- acquisition of qualities;
- sympathetic efficacy;
- propitiation;
- sacramental union;
- and, in selected-organ cases, location-specific transferable qualities.

Thus the most interesting continuity is not the category name. It is the repeated **lowering of the level of generalization until an operative mechanism and historical relation can be specified**.

## 7. Strongest safe formulation

> **Within the Steinmetz controversy, Mauss did not merely dispute a theory of cannibalism; he narrowed `endocannibalisme` to ritual anthropophagy of relatives and treated the transfer of the dead person’s virtues as one specific mechanism. In the same critical argument he explicitly used Marillier’s 1896 Melanesian taboo dossier as an assembled source packet. Lovejoy’s 005 notebook, which records Steinmetz’s exact title, later decomposes anthropophagy still further, treating transfer of qualities as only one mechanism among hunger, revenge, kin relation, propitiation and sacramental union.**
