## New canonical control

Schaffer explicitly notes in his biographical transition that Lovejoy went to Paris and studied Pāli and Sanskrit. This occurs inside an article whose central task is to reconstruct Lovejoy's historiography through seriality, evolution, hierarchy, public intellectual authority and *The Great Chain of Being*.

The article does not develop that Pāli/Sanskrit fact into a discussion of:

- Buddhism;
- the 1898 JAOS article;
- the 1901 Buddhism syllabus;
- Lanman;
- Sylvain Lévi;
- Lovejoy's Buddhist notebooks;
- a Buddhist/Indological contribution to Lovejoy's historical practice.

Exact full-text checks on the supplied PDF returned one substantive occurrence each for `Pali` and `Sanskrit`, and no occurrences for `Buddhism`, `Lanman`, `Lévi` or `1898`.

## Historiographical consequence

Wilson alone already blocked any claim that Lovejoy's Buddhist/comparative-religion formation was unknown. Schaffer strengthens the point across generations:

> **The Asian-language formation remained biographically visible inside later historiography specifically concerned with Lovejoy's historical practice, while the technical work performed inside that formation remained analytically undeveloped.**

This refines the novelty claim.

Do not write:

> `Lovejoy's Buddhist formation was forgotten.`

Prefer:

> **The formation was known but differently classified. Notebook 004 changes its historiographical status by making the scholarly work inside that known episode reconstructible.**

## Relation to existing canonical novelty claim

The existing canonical distinction remains valid:

1. biography — early Indian/Buddhist work was visible;
2. practice — notebook 004 reconstructs what Lovejoy actually did with sources and disputed claims;
3. disciplinary genealogy — later accounts of Lovejoy's historical practice generally developed through other materials.

Schaffer now provides a direct positive control for proposition 3.

## Editorial relevance

The JHI editor's request to reconstruct what Lovejoy read, how the material reached him, how he reconfigured it in print, and how the early work changes the later disciplinary story is therefore well calibrated to the historiographical gap.

The intervention is not the addition of an unknown youthful interest. It is the recovery of scholarly practice behind a known biographical fact.

## Guardrails

Do not claim:

- Schaffer was unaware of Lovejoy's Buddhist article;
- Schaffer should have written a different article;
- Schaffer establishes a causal relation between Pāli/Sanskrit study and *Great Chain*;
- the absence of Buddhist discussion in Schaffer proves field-wide neglect.

The source supports a narrower claim about **biographical visibility without analytical integration in this major later historiographical treatment**.
