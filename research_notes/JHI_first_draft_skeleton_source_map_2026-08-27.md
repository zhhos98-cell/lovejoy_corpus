## Paragraph 3 — Warren gives the problem; Lovejoy contests one answer

### Job in the article

Show that distinguishing different relations was already part of the Buddhist scholarly field, then isolate Lovejoy's own local intervention.

### Historical point

Henry Clarke Warren already argued that the links of dependent origination did not all stand in the same sort of causal or temporal relation. Lovejoy did not invent that question. What is visible in the notebook is that he uses the question to challenge Warren's particular identification of desire and attachment.

### Main materials

**Warren primary**
- Henry Clarke Warren, `On the so-called Chain of Causation of the Buddhists`, JAOS / AOS Proceedings XVI (1893), especially the discussion beginning around p. xxvii.
- Warren: `paccaya` expresses different kinds of dependence; each link must be examined separately; Time is not uniformly controlling.
- Warren nevertheless treats the relation between `taṇhā` and `upādāna` as one of identity.

**Lovejoy notebook**
- 004 PDF p.22 / manuscript p.75.
- Lovejoy copies the Warren judgment and writes: `But not so if we are to follow [the] present text.`
- he checks Buddhist classifications of the four `upādānas` against the six `taṇhās`.

**Archive text**
- `archive_transcriptions/MS38_004_001_061_004_p019-036_clean.json`

### Repo routes

- `research_notes/JHI_blog_004_relation_type_inventory_Warren_method_2026-08-20.md`

## Paragraph 4 — Senart: origins of parts do not by themselves settle the character of their arrangement

### Job in the article

Increase the scale from a pair of terms to the twelve-nidāna arrangement without changing the article's basic question: what exactly has the evidence established?

### Historical point

Lovejoy could concede historical mixture, borrowing, repetition, and non-uniform sequence while resisting the further conclusion that the received arrangement was therefore accidental or unintelligible.

### Main materials

**Senart primary**
- Émile Senart, `À propos de la théorie bouddhique des douze nidânas`, in *Mélanges Charles de Harlez* (1896), 281–297.
- p.284: possibility of a later construction made from originally independent categories.
- pp.284–285: `upādāna` treated as shortened `upādāna-skandhas`, producing the duplication problem.
- pp.286–288: proposed historical layers and Sāṃkhya-Yoga background.
- pp.288, 293, 295: Senart himself is analysing filiation and composition; do not caricature him as simply declaring the formula meaningless.

**Lovejoy notebook**
- 004 PDF pp.49–50 / manuscript pp.137–139 are the strongest local response area.
- Lovejoy grants heterogeneity and rejects the stronger inference to accidental or unintelligible order.

**Lovejoy 1898**
- *JAOS* 19 (1898), especially pp.127 and 132.
- published formulation: terms, `perhaps even the ideas`, may be borrowed while their `arrangement and application` remain original and characteristic.

## Paragraph 11 — `Democracy`: `Man` divided `longitudinally and cross-wise`

### Job in the article

Make `Democracy in the Twentieth Century` the main later turn.

### Historical point

Lovejoy describes nineteenth-century thought as undoing the Enlightenment's homogeneous `Man` through two different historical distinctions.

### Main materials

**Lovejoy primary**
- Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Democracy in the Twentieth Century`, *The Bulletin of the Washington University Association* 4 (1906), 81–102.
- delivered 19 February 1906; published April 1906.
- direct source returned from HathiTrust, Harvard-origin scan, handle recorded in the canonical dossier.

Core language:
- eighteenth-century `Nature` and `man as man` versus nineteenth-century `Evolution`.
- `generic Man` divided `longitudinally and cross-wise`.
- longitudinal distinction: stages of social evolution / stage of culture.
- cross-wise distinction: race and nationality / racial or national genius.

### Repo routes

- `research_notes/Lovejoy_1906_Democracy_direct_primary_stratification_control_2026-08-27.md`

## Paragraph 15 — historiographical close

### Job in the article

Close on the history of Lovejoy's reception and the disciplinary memory of intellectual history, not on a claim of non-Western origin.

### Historical point

Lovejoy's comparative-religion and Buddhist formation was not wholly unknown. What the notebook changes is the level at which the early scholarly practice can now be reconstructed.

### Main materials

**Daniel J. Wilson**
- documents comparative religion at Harvard, Sanskrit/Buddhist study with Lanman, and Paris Buddhist work / comparative-religion context.
- therefore blocks the claim that biography simply forgot the Asian strand.

**History-of-ideas historiography**
- 1948 JHI memorial/method cluster; Wiener, Feuer, Duffin and later debates made philosophy, mature method, unit-ideas, *Great Chain*, and the Skinner controversy the dominant routes to Lovejoy.
- recent scholarship has already complicated a simple Skinnerian caricature; do not claim novelty merely by saying Lovejoy was subtler than `unit-ideas without context`.

**Mature Chinese control**
- `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism` prevents any claim that mature Lovejoy never worked outside European material.
- the early Indian/Buddhist case is different because it places Lovejoy inside technical specialist controversy before the mature disciplinary programme had stabilized.

**Reception split — optional final sentence if space permits**
- specialist Buddhist scholarship continued to cite the 1898 paper for its substantive argument, including Soonil Hwang (2006) and Amrita Nanda (2017/2024).
- general Lovejoy reception more often retained the canonical historian of ideas and the fact of an early Buddhist article without reconstructing the same technical trajectory.
