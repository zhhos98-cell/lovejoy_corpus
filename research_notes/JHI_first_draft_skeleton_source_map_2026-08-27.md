## 0. Narrative rule: the notebooks carry the article

Except for the *Great Chain of Being* close, every substantive paragraph should begin from, return to, or be explicitly motivated by material in notebook 004 or notebook 005.

External material has a limited role:

- Warren, Senart, Oldenberg and other specialists identify the controversy in which a notebook entry was made;
- Harvard course material explains the institutional setting of work already visible in notebook 004;
- Marillier and Söderblom identify what was already shared in the Paris field around notebook 005;
- the 1898 and 1902 publications show what Lovejoy carried from or narrowed out of notebook work;
- the 1906 publications are read back against questions already visible in 004/005, without claiming direct transmission;
- *The Great Chain of Being* is the deliberate point at which the article leaves the notebooks and asks how the mature work places the earlier Asian materials.

This is a narrative rule, not a causal claim. The article should not read as a sequence of separate mini-essays on Warren, Harvard, Paris, 1902 and 1906.

Practical test while drafting: if a paragraph could be removed from the article without changing what the reader understands about the notebooks, it probably belongs in an endnote, a shorter sentence, or outside the first draft.
