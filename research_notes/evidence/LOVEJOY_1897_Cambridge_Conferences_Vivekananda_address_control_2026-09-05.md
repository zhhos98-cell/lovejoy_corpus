# Lovejoy 1897 Cambridge Conferences -> Vivekananda address — exact witness control

Date of control: 2026-09-05  
Status: **ACTIVE ACTOR-BEARING FORMATION / PRE-1898 COMMUNICATION WITNESS**

## 1. Object

A previously unpromoted contemporary printed witness places A. O. Lovejoy inside a collective transatlantic communication about Vedānta before his 1898 JAOS article.

The immediate printed carrier is *Prabuddha Bhārata, or Awakened India*, vol. I, no. 9 (Madras, March 1897), in the report `Swāmi Vivekānanda in Madras`. The report says that an address had been received during Vivekananda's stay in Madras and prints it under the heading `TO SWAMI VIVEKANANDA—INDIA` / `DEAR FRIEND AND BROTHER`.

Online complete-issue witness:

- https://www.vivekananda.net/PDFBooks/PrabuddhaBharata.pdf
- current search/index route: https://vivekananda.net/Library.html (`Prabuddha Bharata - Complete Edition, March 1897`)

## 2. Lovejoy-bearing printed state

The address speaks in the first-person plural as members of the Cambridge Conferences devoted to comparative study in `Ethics, Philosophy, and Religion`. It praises Vivekananda's and Saradananda's expositions of Vedānta as having more than speculative interest, assigns them ethical value in connecting distant peoples, and asks whether Vivekananda may return as a teacher.

The printed signatory block includes:

- Lewis Janes, D.D., Director;
- C. C. Everett;
- William James;
- John H. Wright;
- Josiah Royce;
- J. E. Lough, identified as president of the Harvard Graduate Philosophical Society, 1895–96;
- **A. O. Lovejoy, identified as `Secretary, do 1896-97`;**
- Rachel Kent Taylor, president of the Radcliffe Philosophical Club, 1896–97;
- Sara C. Bull;
- John P. Fox.

The same collective address includes the phrase `our distant brothers of the great Aryan Family`.

### Dating control

The Cambridge Conferences address is not assigned an exact composition date in the recoverable March 1897 printing. The secure state is:

> **undated address -> represented as received during Vivekananda's Madras stay -> printed March 1897.**

Do not import `December 31st, 1896` from the separate Brooklyn Ethical Association letter printed immediately afterward. That date belongs to the Brooklyn letter, not automatically to the Cambridge Conferences address.

## 3. Contemporary institutional context

A second contemporary witness is Sara Chapman Bull, `The Cambridge Conferences`, *The Outlook*, 7 Aug. 1897, pp. 845–849:

- https://www.unz.com/PDF/PERIODICAL/Outlook-1897aug07/27-31/

Bull states that the Conferences ran from November 1896 to May 1897 for comparative study of ethics, religion, and philosophy, with roughly three hundred acceptances from professors, clergy, and Harvard/Radcliffe graduate and undergraduate students. The published programme includes, among other items:

- 29 Nov. 1896 — Swami Saradananda, `Ethical Ideas of the Hindus`;
- 10 Mar. 1897 — Saradananda, `Social Conditions and Needs of Modern India`;
- 4 Apr. 1897 — Anagarika H. Dharmapala, `Ethics of Buddhism`;
- 7 Apr. 1897 — Charles R. Lanman, `The Practical Teachings of Buddha: Their Method and Their Contents`;
- ten lectures on `Indian Literature` by M. A. Potter of Harvard;
- a number of lectures on Vedānta by Saradananda;
- four lectures on Buddhism by Dharmapala.

This programme establishes the institutional problem-field around the address. It does **not** establish Lovejoy's attendance at any individual lecture.

## 4. What the witness changes

### Formation

This upgrades one part of the Harvard/Boston formation picture from generic proximity to **documented actor-bearing participation in a comparative-religion institution**. Lovejoy is represented in a contemporary Indian periodical both as a signatory/member of the Cambridge Conferences collective communication and as secretary of the Harvard Graduate Philosophical Society for 1896–97.

It does not establish that Vivekananda, Saradananda, Dharmapala, Lanman, or any particular Cambridge Conferences lecture supplied a proposition or method found in Lovejoy's notebooks.

### Pre-1898 output / communication discovery

The repository's live question asks whether Buddhist/Indic communications, reports, addresses, exercises, abstracts, or meeting papers existed before the 1898 JAOS article in intermediate carriers. This witness gives one positive answer at the **collective institutional communication** level. It is not a newly recovered Lovejoy scholarly paper and should not be entered into the 004 -> 1898 manuscript/title chain.

### Political / relational language

The address is also a direct pre-1898 Lovejoy-bearing carrier for public `Aryan family` language joining comparative religion, ethical universalism, and transnational fraternity. The safe actor-level formulation is:

> Lovejoy's name and Harvard society office appear among the signatories to a collective Cambridge Conferences address that used `great Aryan Family` language in framing relations with Vivekananda and Indian interlocutors.

This licenses analysis of the **collective act to which his signature was attached**. It does not license claims that Lovejoy coined the phrase, drafted the address, independently held a racial doctrine encoded by it, or supplied the address's conceptual vocabulary.
