Source returned by user: Northcote Whitridge Thomas, `La survivance du culte totémique des animaux et les rites agraires dans le Pays de Galles`, *Revue de l'histoire des religions* 38 (1898), 295–347; JSTOR stable 23660286.

## Direct results

Thomas does not treat outward resemblance as a self-executing genealogy. The article repeatedly separates categories by diagnostic form and admissible inference.

Most importantly:

- he distinguishes `superstitions catégorisées` from a second class whose `forme n'est pas exclusivement totémique`;
- he excludes several phenomena from proof of prior totemism when their form is too indeterminate;
- he says divination is not specifically totemic: totemism may be one point of origin but is not the only origin;
- he repeatedly asks what additional conditions would license a totemic-origin claim rather than treating animal association itself as sufficient;
- for Shrove-Tuesday cock rites, he explicitly tests competing classifications—totemic sacred animal versus representative of the corn/vegetation spirit—and concludes for the agrarian interpretation only after further comparison;
- he warns that calendrical relocation does not by itself prove that a ceremony belongs to the Christian feast at which it is now performed.

This yields a genuine local operator:

`present form / present occurrence`
≠ `historical origin`
≠ `ritual function`
≠ `chronological placement`.

The conclusion is equally revealing. Thomas says current ethnological knowledge scarcely licenses hypotheses about the race that generated the totemic/agricultural superstitions. Yet he immediately entertains a broad developmental reconstruction in which more `advanced` Aryanized populations are contrasted with earlier tribes and in which totemic versus agricultural rites are assigned to different civilizational strata. He proposes systematic folklore classification by a `geological` character: the stage of `civilisation or savagery` that produced a belief, together with a map of survivals.

## Interpretive payoff

Thomas is therefore an unusually useful same-workshop second-student control because the same article contains both:

1. **local inferential restraint** — form does not by itself settle origin; ambiguous forms are excluded or reclassified; competing mechanisms are tested;
2. **large-scale developmental reaggregation** — the conclusion moves toward racial/civilizational strata and a mapped historical geology of survivals.

This is a close field-level parallel to the repo's central point that decomposition and totalization can be contemporaneous rather than sequential.

It also narrows what can responsibly be said about Marillier's workshop. The Thomas product shows that under the same teacher apparatus one could learn to distinguish local forms/functions/origins without thereby abandoning broad evolutionary reconstruction.
