# *Political Theory*, 1973–1975: embedded differentiation and editorial classification pluralism

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT EDITORIAL STATISTICS + CSPT INSTITUTIONAL SELF-DESCRIPTION + RECENT ARCHIVAL HISTORY CONTROL**

## Core result

The early institutional history of *Political Theory* and CSPT does not support a simple choice between:

`STAY INSIDE POLITICAL SCIENCE`
vs
`BUILD AN AUTONOMOUS RIVAL DISCIPLINE`.

Instead, the infrastructure exhibits **embedded differentiation**:

- CSPT remained a confederated interdisciplinary network that repeatedly operated inside/alongside APSA, AHA, Canadian PSA and other established associations;
- it built its own newsletter, conferences, paper circulation, regional chapters, conference books and journal ties;
- *Political Theory*, which emerged from a Columbia CSPT chapter meeting, could nonetheless carry sharply adversarial claims about mainstream political science;
- the journal's own editors treated `historical`, `modern`, `methodological` and `conceptual` work as overlapping, difficult-to-fix classifications.

Therefore:

> **institutional embeddedness did not require epistemic accommodation, and methodological differentiation did not require disciplinary secession.**

This is much closer to Pocock's December 1967 `relatedness` position than to Wood's imagined independent discipline at the level of organizational outcome, but this should be described only as **outcome congruence**, not as proof that Pocock's proposal prevailed causally.

---

## I. CSPT's mature organizational form is embedded rather than separatist

CSPT's institutional self-description says it became a confederation of regional, urban and national groups whose members communicate through meetings and a newsletter.

It also describes regular programs at:

- American Historical Association meetings;
- American Political Science Association meetings;
- Canadian Political Science Association meetings;
- regional professional meetings.

This is important in relation to the 1967 founding dispute.

Neal Wood had imagined a new constellation of financial, departmental and professional organizations potentially capable of separating political theory from political science.

Pocock instead argued against a general war or departmental split and urged practitioners to do their work while maintaining its relatedness to behavioralist political science.

The later CSPT structure is organizationally compatible with the second strategy:

`OWN INFRASTRUCTURE`
+
`CONTINUED INTERFACE WITH EXISTING DISCIPLINES`.

But no direct founding minutes yet establish that Pocock's view caused this outcome.

---

## II. CSPT built communications infrastructure rather than one doctrinal center

Its retrospective institutional account identifies two especially important communication mechanisms:

1. international meetings;
2. a twice-yearly Newsletter.

It also:

- distributed annual-meeting papers to members;
- published selected proceedings as books;
- fostered regional chapters;
- maintained close ties to several journals, whose editors became ex-officio members of its Executive Committee;
- repeatedly sought foundation support because it lacked permanent funding.

The institutional product is therefore not a single school manifesto.

It is a **communications ecology** capable of sustaining actors with divergent methodologies.

This is highly comparable to the earlier repo finding that JHI/ACLS infrastructures made heterogeneous history-of-ideas practices recurrent without imposing one method.

Again, comparison is structural, not genealogical.

---

## III. *Political Theory* could be more adversarial than the society's organizational form

Recent disciplinary history says that *Political Theory* was established in 1970 at a graduate-student meeting of the Columbia CSPT chapter to `build bridges`.

Yet the same study notes that many of the journal's earliest articles argued that mainstream political science had taken a wrong, value-free direction and presented political theory as a distinct/preferable alternative.

Thus:

`BRIDGE-BUILDING VENUE`
can carry
`ADVERSARIAL CONTENT`.

Do not infer journal corporate doctrine from every published article; nor should CSPT's continued APSA/AHA embeddedness be taken to imply intellectual reconciliation.

This is another application of:

`INFRASTRUCTURE != CONTENT POSITION`.

---

## IV. 1975 editors explicitly report classification ambiguity

A 1975 `From the Editors` article provides unusually useful direct execution evidence.

The editors report approximately:

- 185 submissions from March 1974 to January 1975;
- 18 accepted in that period;
- about 30 additional accepted/published earlier;
- overall acceptance near one in ten for the profiled period.

More important for the present project, they explicitly caution that classification is difficult:

- individual essays may be viewed as `historical or modern`;
- they may also be `methodological or conceptual`;
- the editorial categories are therefore tentative.

They classify roughly:

- 100 of 185 submissions as `historical`, because they treat individual theorists from Plato onward;
- 85 as residual `modern`, encompassing methodology, critical political theory and conceptual analysis.

This is direct actor-level evidence that the journal's own editorial practice did not recognize neat mutually exclusive method/object classes.

Compact rule:

`EDITORIAL CATEGORY != NATURAL ONTOLOGY OF ARTICLE`.

An article can be multi-role/multi-unit even before our retrospective coding.

---

## V. This validates multi-label coding in the current project

The repo's JHI census and UNIT×RELATION matrix deliberately allow multiple role/unit codes for one article.

The 1975 *Political Theory* editors provide contemporaneous warrant for this general analytic caution: historical, methodological, conceptual and contemporary work could overlap enough that the editors themselves classified only tentatively.

This does not validate every code in our matrix automatically.

It validates the **anti-exclusive-classification principle**:

`ONE ARTICLE != ONE NECESSARY METHOD ROLE`.

## VI. Organizational outcome versus founding intentions

The CSPT case now needs four levels:

1. `FOUNDING THREAT PERCEPTION` — behavioralism, APSR publication access, professional isolation;
2. `COMPETING INSTITUTIONAL STRATEGIES` — Wood's possible secession vs Pocock/Richter embedded relatedness;
3. `ORGANIZATIONAL OUTCOME` — chapters, meetings, newsletter, publications, society ties within/alongside existing disciplines;
4. `VENUE CONTENT` — sometimes strongly adversarial articles in *Political Theory*.

These levels must not be collapsed.

This is a useful general model for intellectual-institutional history:

`FOUNDERS' PREFERENCES`
!= `INSTITUTIONAL FORM`
!= `PUBLISHED CONTENT`
!= `LATER DISCIPLINARY IDENTITY`.
