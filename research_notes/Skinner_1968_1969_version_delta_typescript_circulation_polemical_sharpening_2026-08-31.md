## Core result

The 1968 Political Studies Association conference paper `Political Theory: the Unimportance of the Great Texts` must now be treated as a concrete document state in the textual history of `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas`, not merely as an anecdotal earlier title.

Adrian Blau's 2026 work directly cites the typescript by page/note, and other researchers explicitly thank Blau for sharing a copy of it. This establishes:

`1968 TYPESCRIPT EXISTS AS A RESEARCHABLE DOCUMENT`
+
`BLAU POSSESSES / CONTROLS ACCESS TO A COPY`
+
`BLAU HAS SHARED IT WITH OTHER RESEARCHERS`.

What remains open is the typescript's **original archival/source provenance**: whether Blau received it from Skinner, from a private correspondent, from PSA records, or from another archive.

The version evidence already shows that 1968 -> 1969 involved more than a title change. At least two directly controlled transformations are visible:

1. **polemical sharpening** in the attack on artificially coherent histories;
2. **example/evidentiary pruning** in the treatment of historians' mentality and perceptual expectations.

This suggests a version-history research programme:

`S1 WRITING 1966–67`
-> `S2a COLLEGIAL DRAFT CIRCULATION`
-> `S3 PSA PERFORMANCE VERSION 1968`
-> `S4 PUBLISHED VERSION 1969`

in which changes must be typed clause by clause rather than attributed wholesale to one reader, editor, or event.

## II. Direct delta 1: the anti-coherence polemic becomes substantially sharper

Blau's 2026 `Racist Political Theory and the Mythology of Coherence` gives a direct clause comparison.

### 1968 conference typescript, p.4

Skinner describes the result of forced coherence as, in Blau's quotation, **`histories of nothing that was ever actually said or done`**.

### 1969 publication

The published article gives a much more extended and categorical formulation: the procedure ceases to be an historical account of thoughts actually thought and becomes a history of abstractions at a level of coherence no one attained.

The important historical result is not the longer quotation itself but the change in argumentative force:

`1968 — HISTORICAL EMPTINESS / NOTHING ACTUALLY SAID OR DONE`
->
`1969 — ONTOLOGICAL-POLITICAL SHARPENING: NOT A HISTORY OF ACTUAL THOUGHT AT ALL`.

This can be coded as:

`SAME TARGET`
+
`STRONGER PUBLISHED CLAIM`.

Do not infer why it sharpened.

Possible causes include authorial revision, audience reaction, collegial comments, editorial pressure, or simple rewriting. No causal edge is yet demonstrated.

## III. Direct delta 2: the 1968 version has a concrete Gombrich perception example missing from the published argument

Blau also shows that the 1968 draft makes Skinner's discussion of mentality especially concrete.

The typescript uses Ernst Gombrich's example of a nineteenth-century artist drawing Chartres Cathedral with expected Gothic pointed windows rather than its actual Romanesque curved windows. Skinner uses the example to show how expectation/preconception can alter what an observer sees or remembers.

Blau cites this to Skinner's 1968 `Political Theory`, note 31, and contrasts it with the published article's more general treatment of mentality.

Thus:

`1968 — PERCEPTUAL/PSYCHOLOGICAL EXEMPLAR`
->
`1969 — MORE ABSTRACT METHODOLOGICAL FORMULATION`.

This is significant because the published 1969 essay is often canonically remembered as a methodological text about context and linguistic action. The earlier performance version exposes a more visible **psychology-of-historiographical-error** layer.

Possible description:

`EXAMPLE PRUNING / ABSTRACTION`.

Again, no causal reason is yet known.

## V. Austin / speech-act delta — currently secondary control

A publicly accessible reproduction of an Adrian Blau methodological article states that Skinner's 1968 conference paper **does not cite J. L. Austin**, whereas the 1969 published version does; it also notes Dunn had recognized Austin's importance before Skinner.

The underlying claim is highly plausible and consistent with:

- Dunn 1968's direct use of Austin;
- Skinner's later acknowledgment of Dunn's role;
- Skinner's subsequent 1970–71 speech-act essays.

However, the currently retrieved carrier for this exact 1968-vs-1969 Austin statement is lower-grade than the direct Blau/SAGE typescript quotations above.

Therefore status:

`1968 NO AUSTIN -> 1969 AUSTIN`
= **SECONDARY CONTROL / DIRECT TYPESCRIPT CHECK DESIRABLE**.

Do not yet use this as the decisive causal story of the 1969 article's formation.

If directly confirmed, it would be exceptionally important because it would show that one of the later canonical philosophical markers of Skinnerian contextualism entered between the conference and publication states.
