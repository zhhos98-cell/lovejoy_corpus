# Archive-side Batch 110 — JHU Lovejoy authority record as a cross-collection map: three new positive components, derivative transcription custody, and authority-link controls

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: after Batch109's Science Service preservation work, return to Johns Hopkins with a repository-wide question rather than another surname search inside MS-0038. The current JHU ArchivesSpace authority record for Arthur O. Lovejoy states that the person record is found in **14 Collections and/or Records**. This batch audits all fourteen links and separates exact Lovejoy material from cataloging/authority associations that do not yet prove a Lovejoy document.

## Core result

The authority page yields three previously unmapped positive Lovejoy archival components outside the main MS-0038 creator fonds:

1. **History of Ideas Club records, RG-15-120** — official scope explicitly identifies a special correspondence file concerning publication of a volume of Lovejoy's collected papers and, separately, **a number of letters handwritten by Professor Lovejoy**. This becomes `GLA0053`.
2. **Daniel J. Wilson transcriptions of Arthur O. Lovejoy letters, MS-0873, Box 1** — a separate collection consisting of transcriptions, mostly typewritten and some handwritten, of Lovejoy letters dated 1872-1962. This becomes `GLA0054`, strictly classified as a **derivative transcription collection**, not original-letter custody.
3. **Richard Macksey papers, MS-0907 / Author Letters / Hopkins Festival** — an exact archival file titled `Arthur O. (Oncken) Lovejoy, 1948-06-16`. This becomes `GLA0055`. The exact date and hierarchy are positive, but material form, item count, sender/recipient and container remain unresolved because the direct child page was not retrievable in the current session.

The rest of the authority links are retained as resolution targets rather than automatically promoted.

4. Department of Romance Languages records, RG-04-080;

8. Hermann Collitz papers, MS-0014;

11. Margaret Donaldson Boehm papers, MS-0044-a;
12. Philosophy Department records, RG-04-120;
13. Raymond Dexter Havens papers, MS-0024;
14. Victor Lowe papers, MS-0284.

The critical control is the Bergson file. Its item page identifies **Henri Bergson as Creator** and lists **Lovejoy under Related Names: Source**. Therefore the mere fact that an archival object appears on Lovejoy's authority page cannot be converted into `Lovejoy letter`, `Lovejoy correspondent`, or even `Lovejoy subject` without reading the relationship role and object description.

This becomes the governing rule for the JHU authority sweep:

> **authority-record membership is a discovery relation, not a document-event relation.**

`jhu_lovejoy_authority_record_crosscollection_batch110.csv` records all fourteen links with their evidence tier.

## 2. RG-15-120 is a direct organizational Lovejoy archive component

The History of Ideas Club finding aid is unusually explicit. Its scope states that the records contain:

- correspondence relating to the club's functions;
- a **special file of correspondence** concerning a volume of Lovejoy's collected papers published by the club;
- and **a number of letters handwritten by Professor Lovejoy**.

These are not necessarily the same objects and should not be merged. At minimum the collection contains two documentary classes:

`Lovejoy-authored handwritten letters`

and

`organizational / potentially third-party correspondence about publishing Lovejoy's collected papers`.

The public interface currently exposes no requestable containers and does not provide the exact folders, dates, recipients, directions or Lovejoy item count. Therefore `GLA0053` remains collection-level positive evidence, with the next task being folder/item recovery.

This component is especially valuable because it sits between the personal fonds and an institution Lovejoy helped create. It offers a direct test of how documents generated by or about Lovejoy were retained in an organizational record group rather than in MS-0038.

## 3. MS-0873 creates a new derivative textual domain

MS-0873 is not a subsection of MS-0038. JHU catalogs it separately as:

`Daniel J. Wilson transcriptions of Arthur O. Lovejoy letters`

with Box 1 and an abstract specifying transcriptions, mostly typewritten but some handwritten, of Lovejoy letters from 1872-1962.

This is strong positive evidence for surviving **texts of letters**, but the manifestation is derivative. The project therefore distinguishes:

`historical letter event`

from

`original / retained physical manifestation`

from

`later transcription witness`.

A transcription may be extremely valuable when an original is remote, missing, inaccessible or differently cataloged, but it cannot be silently substituted for the original object.

The next operation is to inventory MS-0873 at item level and capture any heading, sender, recipient, date, repository/source note, editorial bracket or annotation. Only then can individual transcriptions be crosswalked to MS-0038 or to external archives.

## 4. Wilson's two JHU roles must remain separate until provenance proves a connection

The MS-0038 processing note states that Daniel J. Wilson prepared a detailed index of the Lovejoy papers during 1974-1975, listing the subject/title of each item and producing a complete correspondence index. It also records earlier and later descriptive layers:

- 1963 — Marjorie Nicolson examined and arranged some material into labeled envelopes;
- 1974-75 — Wilson produced the detailed item/correspondence index;
- 1979 — papers were reboxed while the order corresponding to Wilson's index was retained;
- 1993 — a container list was prepared to align material with current box numbers.

MS-0873 separately bears Wilson's name as the collector/creator of Lovejoy-letter transcriptions.

The obvious hypothesis is that the transcription collection may intersect Wilson's work on MS-0038. But the current catalog does not say that MS-0873 was transcribed solely from MS-0038, nor that every transcription has an original in the Lovejoy papers.

Therefore the correct rule is:

> **shared processor/editor identity is not provenance identity.**

Any MS-0873/MS-0038 relation must be established letter by letter.

## 5. The Macksey 1948 file is exact but semantically underdetermined

The Lovejoy authority page gives an exact archival object:

`Arthur O. (Oncken) Lovejoy, 1948-06-16`

under:

`Richard Macksey papers / Author Letters / Hopkins Festival`.

That is enough to establish a positive component and exact date, hence `GLA0055`.

It is not enough to say that Lovejoy wrote Macksey, that Macksey received the original, or that the item concerns the Hopkins Festival. The folder hierarchy supplies archival context, but the direct object page/container was not retrievable in the current session and the authority result does not expose direction or physical form.

This should therefore be requested as an object-level manifestation test and compared against MS-0038 and MS-0873 by date only after the literal sender/recipient and form are recovered.

## 7. JHU now has at least four distinct Lovejoy manifestation domains

The new crosswalk separates:

`MS-0038` — creator personal fonds, containing direct, outgoing-form-unknown, and third-party retained documents;

`MS-0873` — derivative transcriptions of Lovejoy letters;

`RG-15-120` — organizational History of Ideas Club records containing Lovejoy holograph letters and publication correspondence;

`MS-0907` — a later collected Author Letters file with an exact 1948 Lovejoy object.

The fact that all four are housed at JHU does not collapse them into one provenance chain. Repository co-location is not custody identity, and shared personal-name authority control is not manifestation identity.

The proper reconstruction order is:

`authority link -> exact archival component -> document/material form -> sender/recipient -> historical document event -> cross-collection manifestation relationship`.
