# Batch 59 — Nicolas Meylan as historiographic precedent: from genealogy of mana to documentary microhistory

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: incorporate the newly supplied Chapter 4 of Nicolas Meylan, *Mana: A History of a Western Category* (2017), and recalibrate the novelty claim of the Lovejoy/Marillier/Mauss paper.

## Core result

The historiographic claim must be corrected in one important respect.

Nicolas Meylan **already places Lovejoy in direct comparative relation to Hubert and Mauss** within the genealogy of mana. In his discussion of Lovejoy’s 1906 *The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy*, Meylan notes that Lovejoy equates `mana` with `orenda`, `manitou`, `ngai`, etc., but not with the terms drawn from more prestigious textual civilizations that Hubert and Mauss had aligned with mana. Meylan explicitly marks this as **“contra Hubert and Mauss 1904, 116–18.”**

Meylan also correctly identifies the conceptual center of Lovejoy’s intervention: Lovejoy opposes Marett’s supernatural/supernormal interpretation and defines the relevant category through efficacy, productivity, communicability, quasi-mechanical operation, and regular/intelligible causation. Meylan therefore treats Lovejoy’s `Primitive Energetics` as a primitive theory and praxis of causation rather than simply a variant of dynamism.

Therefore the paper must **not** claim that Lovejoy’s proximity to Hubert–Mauss, or his distinctive causal reading of mana, has gone unnoticed.

The novelty lies elsewhere and is stronger when stated narrowly:

> **Meylan reconstructs a genealogy of published second-order categories; the archive permits a microhistory of the research operations by which those categories were made.**

Or:

> **The novelty is not to connect Lovejoy to Mauss abstractly, but to reconstruct the documentary machinery through which adjacent Marillier-centered investigations turned overlapping evidence into divergent mechanisms.**

---

## 1. What Meylan has already established

In Chapter 4, printed pp.79–80, Meylan places Lovejoy in the `Supernatural?` section of the post-1905 mana debate.

His main propositions are:

1. Lovejoy attacks Marett’s treatment of mana as the supernormal/supernatural and therefore as specifically religious.
2. Lovejoy instead defines the category as **“the efficacious, the powerful, the productive.”**
3. Lovejoy correlates mana with `orenda`, `manitou`, `ngai`, etc.
4. Unlike Hubert and Mauss, he does **not** extend the same equivalence to notions from prestigious textual cultures; Meylan explicitly writes `contra Hubert and Mauss 1904, 116–18`.
5. Lovejoy names the universal primitive conception `primitive energetics` / `manitouism`.
6. Meylan characterizes it as impersonal, quasi-mechanical, communicable and governed by regular/intelligible laws.
7. Most importantly, Meylan reads this as a primitive **theory and praxis of causation**, lying behind domains that later differentiate into religion and science.
8. Meylan situates Lovejoy within a broadly Tylorian/intellectualist paradigm and contrasts him with Marett’s emotionalist/supernaturalist formulation.

This is a substantive precedent, not a passing citation. Any literature review must acknowledge it.

---

## 2. Meylan’s comparison with Hubert–Mauss is real but operates at a different historical level

Meylan’s comparison is conceptual and genealogical. Its objects are **published theoretical constructions**:

- Hubert–Mauss 1904 and their cross-cultural analogues of mana;
- Marett’s pre-animism/dynamism;
- Lovejoy 1906 and Primitive Energetics;
- later transformations by King, Read, Saintyves, Durkheim, etc.

The question is essentially:

> What did `mana` become as a Western scholarly category, and how did one second-order formulation differ from another?

That is not the same question as the one now opened by the Lovejoy archive:

> By what paper practices, source selections, lecture environments, bibliographies, excerpt sequences, and reclassificatory operations did Lovejoy arrive at the causal distinctions later compressed into the published category?

The archival paper should therefore position itself as **complementary to Meylan, not corrective of him on the existence of the Lovejoy–Hubert–Mauss contrast**.

---

## 3. What the archive adds beyond Meylan

The archive gives at least five layers unavailable from a published-text genealogy alone.

### A. A pre-publication chronology

Lovejoy’s 1906 article retrospectively says its conclusions were reached in 1900 and strongly influenced by studies under Léon Marillier. Notebook 005 supplies working material from the Paris/Marillier research environment before the published category is stabilized.

This changes the historical unit from:

`1906 theory compared with 1904/1900 theories`

to:

`1898–1900 research operations → 1906 theoretical abstraction`.

### B. Marillier’s documentary infrastructure

Independent evidence now shows that Marillier circulated bibliography and notes from his different courses to Mauss. Lovejoy’s notebook contains Marillier-dated inserted notes and a back-leaf Marillier memorandum. Söderblom’s student work independently transmits a Marillier-generalized `mana` formulation.

This does not establish that all three students/readers received an identical packet. It does establish that Marillier’s intellectual role included a material/documentary dimension that a genealogy of published concepts does not capture.

### C. Exact-page source uptake

The archive lets us move below book-title citation.

- Trumbull: Lovejoy explicitly cites *The Blood Covenant* p.118 and p.129 and appears to move through the intervening sequence in order.
- Kingsley: Lovejoy explicitly cites *Travels in West Africa* p.511 and p.525.

These are direct textual contacts, not inferred intellectual affinities.

### D. Source transformation before theory

The strongest archival result is not that Lovejoy read these books, but that we can observe what he does to them.

- Trumbull’s blood/life/covenant sequence becomes curative, protective, sympathetic and transferable efficacy.
- Kingsley’s spirit-inhabited suhman at p.511 becomes the positive boundary case against which Lovejoy defines an amulet with **no necessary spiritualistic idea**.
- Kingsley’s blood material at p.525 is reopened as a problem of whether spirit-action, sympathetic relation, or intrinsic blood potency is the relevant causal bearer.

This is the **reclassification stage** hidden by the later publication.

### E. Controlled divergence inside a shared documentary field

Hubert–Mauss and Lovejoy share sources and problem nodes, but the archive lets us demonstrate that the overlap does not collapse into identical extraction.

For Kingsley:

- Hubert–Mauss cite p.454: spirit residence, sacrifice, blood at fetish hut/gateways.
- Lovejoy cites p.511 and p.525: spirit-inhabited suhman as contrast class; blood as independent causal problem.

For Trumbull, both are interested in blood/foundation material, but Lovejoy’s exact sequential extraction and resulting causal taxonomy are independently demonstrable.

Thus the archive makes possible a history of **divergent research operations inside an overlapping source ecology**.

## 4. The strongest novelty claim after Meylan

The novelty should be formulated at the level of method and archive, not at the level of first noticing a theoretical resemblance.

### Too strong / now incorrect

> No one has connected Lovejoy’s Primitive Energetics with Hubert and Mauss.

Meylan disproves this.

### Still too broad

> The paper is the first to show that Lovejoy was part of the mana debate.

The 1906 article itself and Meylan make that untenable.

### Strong and supportable

> **Existing histories, most clearly Nicolas Meylan’s genealogy of mana, have situated Lovejoy’s 1906 “Primitive Energetics” among rival second-order theories of mana and have noted its contrast with Hubert and Mauss. The surviving Paris notebook makes it possible to shift the scale of analysis. Rather than comparing finished categories alone, it reveals the documentary work by which Lovejoy separated causal bearers across sacrifice, blood, fetish, amulet, cannibalism and agricultural ritual, often through exact-page extraction from the same ethnographies circulating in the Marillier-centered field. The result is a microhistory of classificatory operations preceding the published concept.**

Even tighter:

> **From genealogy of concepts to microhistory of research operations.**

## 5. Meylan actually strengthens, rather than weakens, the 005 → 1906 argument

Meylan’s reading of Lovejoy independently converges on what the notebook evidence has been revealing.

He emphasizes that Lovejoy’s 1906 category is about:

- efficacy;
- causality;
- quasi-mechanical operation;
- regular/intelligible laws;
- practical applications in custom and rite;
- a conceptual layer prior to the later differentiation of religion and science.

Notebook 005’s strongest recurring operation is precisely to ask **what makes an act efficacious and through what bearer/mechanism**:

- personal spirit or god;
- dead ancestor;
- sacrificial intermediary;
- blood/body matter;
- sympathetic relation;
- intrinsic charm efficacy;
- quasi-mechanical action on natural forces;
- social incorporation;
- sacramental union.

Meylan therefore supplies a useful historiographic vocabulary for the endpoint while the archive supplies the working process.

A particularly useful division of labor is:

> **Meylan tells us what kind of category Lovejoy published; 005 shows how heterogeneous evidence was made commensurable enough for such a category to be thinkable.**

## 6. Söderblom: Meylan independently corroborates the Marillier transmission node

The newly supplied chapter also adds an important secondary control on the Söderblom line.

Meylan notes that Söderblom’s 1901 dissertation on representations of life after death contains a single occurrence of `mana` in a Melanesian discussion, explicitly routed through Léon Marillier:

> `en un mot et pour employer l’expression typique dont se sert M. Marillier d’après les Mélanésiens, le mana de l’homme`

Meylan cites Söderblom 1901, p.37.

This is important because it independently confirms that a **Marillier-generalized mana vocabulary entered a student product** very close to the Lovejoy chronology.

The safe inference is:

> Marillier was not merely a name attached retrospectively to Lovejoy’s 1906 footnote; his formulations can be observed moving into student work in the same intellectual environment.

The unsafe inference remains:

> Söderblom and Lovejoy received the same notes, wording, or source packet.

There is still no proof of that.

## 7. A revised historiographic architecture for the paper

The literature review can now be organized in three layers.

### Layer 1 — histories of the mana category

Use Meylan as the principal modern control. He already reconstructs the Western second-order career of mana and accurately positions Lovejoy against Marett and, at one point, Hubert–Mauss.

### Layer 2 — histories of comparative religion / Durkheimian and Marillier networks

Use work on Mauss, Hubert, EPHE, Durkheim and Marillier to establish institutions, controversies and intellectual relations.

### Layer 3 — the archival intervention

Present the Lovejoy notebook not as a missing “theory” but as evidence for **how a theory was made**:

- bibliographies;
- course-linked inserted notes;
- exact-page excerpts;
- sequential reading;
- comparison tables/typologies;
- negative classifications;
- causal-bearer distinctions;
- source re-use and reclassification.

The paper’s distinctive unit of analysis becomes the **research operation**, not the doctrine.

---

## 8. Consequence for the broader Lovejoy/intellectual-history argument

This historiographic correction also improves the larger payoff.

A conventional Lovejoy paper might ask whether `Primitive Energetics` anticipates later unit-ideas or where it sits in the history of anthropology. Meylan already gives us a substantial theoretical genealogy. The archive offers another historiographic route: it lets us study **intellectual history before concepts become clean textual objects**.

Notebook 005 shows that the eventual theoretical object is assembled through:

- discarded equivalences;
- rival causal bearers;
- excerpts taken from ethnographies;
- distinctions produced against those excerpts;
- lecture-linked paper fragments;
- cases retained while source interpretations are rejected.

That is potentially the paper’s strongest contribution beyond the local mana debate:

> **the archive allows the history of an idea to be written as a history of classificatory labor.**

This formulation should remain attached to the actual documentary evidence rather than expanded into a universal claim about Lovejoy’s entire later method.

## 9. Draft-ready historiographic paragraph

> **Nicolas Meylan has already located Lovejoy’s 1906 “Primitive Energetics” within the Western genealogy of mana. He reads Lovejoy’s rejection of Marett’s supernaturalism as a shift toward causation—the efficacious, productive and communicable power that Lovejoy aligned with mana, manitou, orenda and related terms—and explicitly contrasts Lovejoy’s selection of comparanda with Hubert and Mauss’s wider analogical field. The point of returning to Lovejoy’s Paris notebook is therefore not to establish a previously unnoticed theoretical proximity. It is to move beneath the level of finished second-order categories. The notebook preserves the documentary labor that the published article effaces: page-specific extraction from ethnographies, distinctions between spirit-mediated and intrinsic efficacy, the redistribution of blood and bodily transfer away from automatic communion, and repeated tests of whether visible ritual similarity licenses a common causal explanation. In this sense the archive permits a shift from a genealogy of mana to a microhistory of the operations by which “Primitive Energetics” became thinkable.**

## Evidence cautions

1. Meylan is a genuine historiographic precedent and must be cited as such.
2. Do not claim Meylan reconstructs the Paris notebook or Marillier paper circulation; the supplied chapter does not do so.
3. Do not imply Meylan proves direct Lovejoy ↔ Hubert–Mauss reading/transmission. His comparison is conceptual/published-text based.
4. The Söderblom/Marillier quotation is reported by Meylan with a primary citation to Söderblom 1901 p.37; retrieve/verify the primary dissertation page for final publication if possible.
5. The archive-based novelty claim should remain at the level of documentary infrastructure, exact source use and reclassification operations.
6. `From genealogy of concepts to microhistory of research operations` is a historiographic framing, not a claim that published concepts are unimportant.
