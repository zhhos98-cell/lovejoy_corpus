# MS38_004_001_061_005 pp. 31–60 ↔ Marillier's 1897–1898 totemism/sacrifice critique

## Status

This note re-examines the apparent transition in notebook 005 from totemism / clan theory (PDF pp. 37–45) into the sacrifice dossier (pp. 46–60). The transition is less abrupt than it first appeared. Léon Marillier's long 1897–1898 article, **“La place du totémisme dans l'évolution religieuse. À propos d'un livre récent: F. B. Jevons, *An Introduction to the History of Religion*,”** was published immediately before and during Lovejoy's documented 1898–1899 participation in Marillier's EPHE conferences, and it directly attacks the same Robertson Smith/Jevons attempt to make totemism and communion-sacrifice a general evolutionary key.

**Current hypothesis:** notebook pp. 37–60 may preserve a connected Marillier-defined problem sequence — totemic clan/kinship → alleged Semitic totemism → Robertson Smith's communion theory → plural functional types of sacrifice — rather than two unrelated blocks of notes.

The full four-part Marillier article has not yet been ingested into the repository, so this is not yet a phrase-by-phrase manuscript collation.

---

## 1. Marillier's 1897–1898 article is precisely about overextension of totemism

The article appeared in four installments in the *Revue de l'histoire des religions*, vols. 36–37 (1897–1898), as a sustained critique of F. B. Jevons's *Introduction to the History of Religion*.

The central methodological target, as preserved in later discussion and citations, is the attempt to treat diverse animal/plant cults, gods and sacrificial institutions as genealogically derived from one totemic source. Marillier insists instead that apparently related customs can arise from notions that develop in divergent directions and generate practices/institutions that are not truly genealogically related.

He also rejects the assumption that a clan-bound totemic cult can simply expand into tribal or national religion. Once an animal object becomes a deity of a larger group, its specifically totemic structure may have disappeared; animal and plant gods can have multiple origins and functions.

This is the same anti-unification pressure visible throughout 005.

---

## 2. Notebook pp. 37–45 sit directly inside this controversy

### p. 37: what counts as a totem, and is the totem an ancestor/kindred?

Lovejoy asks:

- what classes of objects function as totems;
- whether the clan-totem is regarded as an ancestor;
- whether clan members and the totemic animal/being are regarded as kindred;
- whether metamorphosed culture-hero stories actually prove ancestral descent.

This is a definitional policing exercise. The outward presence of an animal/myth is not sufficient to establish a totemic genealogy.

### p. 38: explicit rejection of the Semitic-totemism inference

The notebook contains a note that a reference article “pronounces agt Semitic totemism” and distinguishes three kinds of alleged kinship evidence, emphasizing that the crucial proof — kinship between groups of men and groups/classes of beasts — is not established merely by evidence of kinship between men and gods or gods and animals.

This aligns closely with the Marillier/Jevons/Robertson Smith controversy. Robertson Smith's theory had made alleged Semitic totemism foundational for sacrifice and religious kinship; Marillier's 1897–1898 article was one of the major contemporary critiques of this construction.

### p. 45: clan/gens definition

Lovejoy defines the clan/gens by common name/emblem, claimed common descent, shared customs/religious observances and consanguineal structure. This makes the social substrate of totemism explicit before the notebook moves into sacrifice.

The sequence therefore looks deliberate: establish what a clan/totem actually is before allowing totemic kinship to explain sacrificial communion.

## 3. The sacrifice section begins by testing precisely the theory Marillier had attacked

Notebook pp. 49–60 repeatedly names Robertson Smith and tests whether sacrifice is fundamentally communion or union:

- p. 49 warns against constructing an anthropological scheme beyond the evidence;
- p. 50 begins an explicit sacrifice typology, separating mystical/communion sacrifice from other forms;
- pp. 51–52 distinguish transfer of life/strength and expiation;
- p. 53 isolates magical sacrifice as direct/mechanical efficacy rather than personal communion;
- pp. 56–60 return repeatedly to Robertson Smith, funerary sacrifice, cannibalistic sacrifice, god-man sacrifice and the limited distribution of true union/communion forms;
- p. 59 treats Robertson Smith's theory as too narrow / insufficiently elastic;
- p. 60 again concludes that the complete communion form is not generally present.

This is not merely similar to Marillier's course taxonomy. Contemporary Hubert and Mauss explicitly identify Marillier as one of the strongest critics of Robertson Smith's theory of sacrifice.

The notebook's movement from totemism into sacrifice thus mirrors the live controversy in which Marillier was already intervening publicly.

## 4. Why the connection matters

Earlier readings of 005 treated pp. 31–45 (totemism/Hebrew eschatology) and pp. 46–119 (sacrifice) as separate thematic strata. The Marillier evidence suggests a more coherent intellectual transition at least for pp. 37–60:

> **define and restrict totemism**
> → test alleged kinship/ancestry
> → reject insecure Semitic-totemism inference
> → examine Robertson Smith's totemic theory of sacrificial communion
> → replace a single genealogy with a plural typology of sacrificial mechanisms.

That sequence is central to Marillier's published critique and to Lovejoy's notebook procedure.

The Hebrew eschatology leaves on pp. 39–44 remain a separate or overlapping teaching/research stratum and should not be forced into this sequence; the physical notebook is accretive.

---

## 5. A stronger methodological inheritance than previously recognized

Marillier's totemism critique strengthens the broader methodological correction already recorded for 005:

- one must not infer genealogical identity from analogy;
- customs can coexist without sharing an origin;
- animal/plant cults can have multiple origins/functions;
- a social form such as the clan places structural limits on what counts as a genuinely totemic institution;
- grand evolutionary theories must be broken apart when the evidence supports heterogeneous mechanisms.

Lovejoy's later notebook habit — “same outward form, different mechanism” — is therefore not merely compatible with Marillier; it belongs to a controversy in which Marillier had already formulated closely related methodological objections.

---

## 6. What remains potentially distinctive in Lovejoy

Even if the anti-Robertson-Smith decomposition is substantially inherited, Lovejoy appears to push the method further in two directions:

1. **mechanism taxonomy becomes increasingly causal rather than merely genealogical** — provision, propitiation, expiation, sympathetic transfer, incorporation, fecundation, protection, communion, intrinsic efficacy;
2. by 1906 one mechanism is abstracted out of the ritual dossier and made a general philosophical category: **impersonal, diffused, transferable, controllable, quasi-mechanical efficacy/energy**.

The key intellectual-development question is therefore no longer “Where did Lovejoy learn to decompose ritual complexes?” The evidence increasingly answers: **Marillier/EPHE**. The sharper question is: **how did Lovejoy transform Marillier's anti-unilinear comparative method into a general theory of causal categories?**
