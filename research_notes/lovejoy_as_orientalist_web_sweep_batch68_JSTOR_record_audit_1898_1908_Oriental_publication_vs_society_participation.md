# Batch 68 — JSTOR record audit, 1898–1908: Oriental publication is secure; AOS meeting/membership participation remains unproved

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: a JSTOR-centered audit prompted by the observation that Lovejoy’s early participation in Oriental / comparative-religion scholarship leaves multiple records in JSTOR. The purpose is to separate four different claims that can too easily be conflated: specialist Oriental publication, American Oriental Society institutional participation, continued Indic scholarship, and broader history-of-religions publication.

## Core result

The JSTOR record strengthens the project in a specific way.

Lovejoy’s relation to late-nineteenth-century Oriental studies is **not merely reconstructed from his notebooks or from later biography**. In 1898 he appears by full name and Harvard affiliation as the author of an eleven-page technical Buddhological article in the *Journal of the American Oriental Society*, the flagship journal of the American Oriental Society. The article works directly with Pāli canonical usage, Senart, Oldenberg, Warren, Rhys Davids, Childers, Sanskrit/Pāli etymological alternatives, and the relation of Buddhist technical vocabulary to Sāṃkhya/Nyāya.

The same JSTOR archive also makes visible the surrounding American Oriental scholarly field and its annual Society proceedings. Yet a systematic search of the 1896–99 proceedings has **not** so far produced a secure Lovejoy name-hit. Publication in JAOS therefore establishes **participation in Oriental scholarly publication**, not automatically Society membership, attendance at an AOS meeting, election to membership, or oral presentation.

The later JSTOR/publication trail then shows a change of venue rather than a clean disappearance of this formation:

> **1898 specialist Buddhist philology in JAOS → 1901 Buddhism teaching (artifact known bibliographically) → 1902 comparative history of religion/categories with explicit reuse of the 1898 Buddhist analysis → 1907 Jewish historical-religion work + same-year review of current Vedānta scholarship.**

The narrow proposition `Lovejoy did not become a career Buddhologist` remains true. The broader proposition `Oriental/Indic engagement ended in 1898` does not fit the surviving record.

## 1. Hard JSTOR object: Lovejoy as a published contributor to JAOS in 1898

Primary item:

Arthur Oncken Lovejoy, `The Buddhistic technical terms upādāna and upādisesa`, *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 19 (1898), 126–136.

JSTOR stable:
`https://www.jstor.org/stable/592475`

The first lines give:

> `ARTHUR ONCKEN LOVEJOY, Harvard University, Cambridge, Mass.`

This matters institutionally. Lovejoy is not appearing in a general philosophical periodical commenting loosely on Asian religion. He is publishing a technical intervention in the American Oriental Society’s own scholarly journal under a Harvard affiliation.

JSTOR’s description of JAOS identifies it as the regular publication of the AOS and describes its long-standing purpose as publishing advanced research on Asian languages, literatures, civilizations, philosophy and religion.

### The article’s practice is unmistakably specialist

The opening states that the meaning of `upādāna` and `upādi-` should be established by **examining their use in the Piṭakas**.

The article then:

- reconstructs and rejects Senart’s recent `upādāna = upādānakkhandha` interpretation;
- tests the claim against passages in the Sutta Piṭaka / Saṃyutta Nikāya;
- distinguishes the fact of semantic/causal relation from identity of terms;
- separates `karma` as cause of the particular condition of rebirth from `upādāna` as cause of rebirth as such;
- grants that many Buddhist terms/ideas may be borrowed while insisting that their arrangement/application can still be original and characteristic;
- distinguishes Buddhist psychology from Vedāntic metaphysical assimilation;
- discusses Sanskrit `upādhi` as a technical term in Nyāya and Sāṃkhya;
- explicitly says that because competing etymologies are possible, the meaning of `upādi` must be settled **by use rather than etymological arguments**.

This is direct publication-level evidence for the evidentiary-burden operations reconstructed from notebook 004.

### Publication-level claim now safe

> **By 1898 Lovejoy was not simply studying Buddhism at Harvard; he had entered the American Orientalist publication field with a technical Buddhological article.**

Do not silently upgrade this to AOS membership or meeting participation.

## 2. JSTOR exposes the AOS institutional record, but current search does not close Lovejoy’s status

The relevant annual proceedings are all identifiable on JSTOR:

- 1896 Andover: `Proceedings ... April 9th, 10th, and 11th, 1896`, JAOS 17, pp.145–188, stable `592502`;
- 1897 Baltimore: `Proceedings ... April 22d, 23d, and 24th, 1897`, JAOS 18, pp.375–390, stable `592308`;
- 1898 Hartford: `Proceedings ... Hartford, Connecticut, 1898`, stable `592480`;
- 1899 Cambridge: `Proceedings ... Cambridge, Mass., 1899`, JAOS 20, pp.361–371, stable `592344`.

The current web/JSTOR indexing exposes the issue records and XML/download routes, but the proceedings documents are not all being returned as searchable text through the present interface. Exact-name searches for `Lovejoy` / `Arthur Oncken Lovejoy` in combination with these proceedings have not yielded a secure hit.

### Therefore use three different statuses

**Secure:**
- Lovejoy published in JAOS in 1898 under Harvard affiliation.

**Plausible but unproved:**
- he may have known/participated in AOS networks beyond publication, given Harvard’s Lanman/Warren/Toy ecology and the journal venue.

**Not established:**
- AOS corporate/member status;
- attendance at the 1898 Hartford or 1899 Cambridge meeting;
- reading/presentation of a paper at an AOS meeting;
- election recorded in proceedings.

This distinction is especially important because the 1899 meeting was in Cambridge and therefore geographically tempting. Geography is not evidence of attendance.

## 3. JAOS 1897–99 shows the immediate field in which Lovejoy’s article sat

JSTOR issue pages make the local publication ecology unusually visible.

### JAOS 18 (1897)

The volume contains, among other things:

- Tibetan Buddhist birth stories;
- Buddhist art;
- Jāiminīya Brāhmaṇa material;
- historical grammar of the Rig and Atharva Vedas;
- Assyrian/Babylonian studies;
- the Baltimore AOS proceedings.

Lovejoy’s 1898 article therefore enters an established specialist journal space rather than a general comparative-religion supplement.

### JAOS 20 (1899)

The following cluster is especially relevant to the Lovejoy corpus:

- Crawford H. Toy, `Taboo and Morality`, pp.151–156;
- Edward Washburn Hopkins, `Economics of Primitive Religion`, pp.303–308;
- Charles Carroll Everett, `The Psychology of the Vedanta and Sankhya Philosophies`, pp.309–316;
- Morris Jastrow Jr., `The Historical Study of Religions in Universities and Colleges`, pp.317–325;
- Crawford H. Toy, `Relation between Magic and Religion`, pp.327–331;
- Charles R. Lanman, `Henry Clarke Warren: An Obituary Notice`, pp.332–337;
- AOS Cambridge proceedings, pp.361–371.

This volume appeared on Lovejoy’s Paris/return horizon and gathers almost every American-side problem family that now appears across 004/005: Sāṃkhya/Vedānta, primitive religion, taboo/morality, magic/religion, history-of-religions institutionalization, and Warren/Lanman’s Indic-Buddhist network.

Again, this is **field ecology**, not proof that any item was read by Lovejoy.

## 4. The 1902 JSTOR record demonstrates direct reuse, not merely thematic continuity

Primary item:

Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Religion and the Time-Process`, *American Journal of Theology* 6.3 (July 1902), 439–472.

JSTOR stable:
`https://www.jstor.org/stable/3153919`

The existing project close-reading established that this article makes Vedānta and Buddhism its first major comparative case and explicitly sends the reader back to Lovejoy’s own 1898 `Buddhistic Technical Terms` article.

The continuity is therefore authorial and documentary:

> **1898 specialist philology → 1902 reuse of that result inside a much larger historical argument about categories/presuppositions.**

The venue has changed from JAOS to a theology/religion journal, but the 1898 Orientalist work remains active argumentative infrastructure.

This is a stronger claim than saying Lovejoy retained a general interest in Buddhism.

## 5. 1907 gives two different continuities: Jewish historical scholarship and current Vedānta reviewing

### A. `The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought`

Arthur O. Lovejoy, *American Journal of Theology* 11.2 (April 1907), 228–249.

JSTOR stable:
`https://www.jstor.org/stable/3153710`

The project has already recovered the full primary text. Lovejoy explicitly says he accepts a relative sequence of Wisdom literature `in deference to the authority of Professor Toy`, preserving a direct published connection to Crawford H. Toy, his Harvard teacher in Hebrew religion.

The article also separates logical sequence from calendar chronology and traces the indirect/gradual development of a conceptual-ethical disposition across heterogeneous Jewish texts.

This is not `Orientalism` in the narrow Indological sense, but it is direct continuity in the historical study of Asian/Near Eastern religious traditions and in the Harvard specialist-source ecology.

The issue itself also contains `Influence of Max Müller’s Hibbert Lectures in India`, `Religion and Mythology in the Old Testament`, Egyptian religion, Hellenistic religion in Egypt, and missionary material. This is useful venue ecology, not a transmission claim.

### B. Deussen / Vedānta review

Secure bibliographic identity:

Arthur O. Lovejoy, review of Paul Deussen, *Outline of the Vedānta System of Philosophy, According to Shankara*, *The Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* 4.1 (1907), 23–24.

JSTOR preserves the full run of this journal and indexes Vol.4 No.1 as 3 January 1907, but the present search interface has not yet surfaced the exact stable ID for Lovejoy’s two-page review.

PhilPapers independently confirms the item. Wilson’s synopsis says Lovejoy treats the English outline as useful to teachers of Hindu philosophy and urges greater scholarly attention to Rāmānuja rather than continued concentration on Śaṅkara. The latter wording still requires direct two-page primary verification before quotation.

The important point at present is bibliographic rather than interpretive:

> **Nine years after the JAOS Buddhist article, Lovejoy was still reviewing current specialist scholarship on Vedānta.**

This makes `venue shift` a better description than `Oriental disengagement`.

## 6. Revised continuity statement after the JSTOR audit

A disciplined version is now:

> **Lovejoy did not pursue a continuous professional identity as a Buddhologist or Indologist, but the early Orientalist formation visible in Harvard notebook 004 was publicly instantiated in a technical JAOS Buddhological article in 1898 and remained active in changing scholarly forms through at least 1907: Buddhist teaching, comparative category-history that explicitly reused the 1898 analysis, Jewish historical-religion scholarship linked to Toy, and review of current Vedānta research.**

This formulation makes several distinctions that are historiographically useful:

- `Buddhist philology` is narrower than `Indic scholarship`;
- `Indic scholarship` is narrower than `history of religions`;
- movement from a specialist Oriental journal to theology/philosophy venues is a change of scholarly form, not evidence by itself of abandonment;
- publication in JAOS does not prove corporate participation in the AOS.

This also refines Karttunen’s statement that Lovejoy `Apparently never returned to Buddhist studies`. If it means `no second dedicated Pāli/Buddhist specialist paper has yet been located`, it remains a useful narrow description. It should not be expanded into `Lovejoy ceased engaging Buddhism/Indian philosophy after 1898`.

## 7. A methodological gain from reading the 1898 JSTOR text directly

The JSTOR PDF is especially valuable because it lets the paper distinguish the **finished public intervention** from notebook 004’s source-processing layer.

Three formulations are particularly important:

1. **usage over etymology:** competing derivations do not settle meaning; use does;
2. **borrowed material versus original configuration:** terms/ideas can be borrowed while arrangement/application remains original;
3. **relation versus identity:** a close relation between `upādāna` and the skandhas does not license Senart’s identity claim.

These are not retrospective abstractions imposed on the article. They are explicit argumentative moves in the 1898 publication.

The notebook remains indispensable because it shows how Lovejoy arrived at and tested those moves across Rhys Davids, Hardy, Senart, Oldenberg and other source objects.

## 8. What the JSTOR run does NOT yet give us

1. No secure JSTOR proceedings hit proving Lovejoy was an AOS member.
2. No secure meeting-attendance record for Hartford 1898 or Cambridge 1899.
3. No evidence that his 1898 JAOS paper was first presented orally to the Society.
4. No exact JSTOR stable ID yet recovered for the 1907 Deussen review.
5. No JSTOR-based contemporary 1898–1905 reception hit for the Buddhist article has yet been securely identified.
6. The JAOS issue ecology establishes availability/co-presence, not Lovejoy’s reading of every adjacent item.

## Bottom line

> **JSTOR changes the evidentiary posture of the project: Lovejoy’s Oriental engagement is not only visible retrospectively in notebooks. It entered a recognized specialist public venue in 1898, remained available to his own later arguments, and continued in altered forms of teaching, comparative religion and Indic reviewing. What JSTOR has not yet given us is a corporate biography inside the American Oriental Society. Publication is secure; membership and meeting participation remain separate questions.**
