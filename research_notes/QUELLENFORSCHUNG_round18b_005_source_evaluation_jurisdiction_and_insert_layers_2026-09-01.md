## 1. p.49 — explicit source-evaluation criterion

A physically inserted memorandum on PDF p.49 evaluates contemporary authorities. The key Tylor passage is directly legible:

`Tylor, the master of t. school Anthropolog'y, + t. only one wh. does not construct theories beyond his evidence.`

Lovejoy immediately adds that Tylor's books:

`shld always be kept at hand in study of subject.`

The surrounding slip also mentions Robertson Smith, Frazer / *Golden Bough*, Jevons, and Marillier, though some exact title/reference details remain uncertain.

This is unusually valuable because it converts a methodological inference into an explicit notebook norm. Lovejoy is not simply using multiple authorities; he ranks them by evidential discipline, and specifically praises refusal to construct theory beyond evidence.

## 2. p.53 — one sacrificial form, a non-divine-will mechanism

The direct image permits a stronger reading than the old low-medium summary. Lovejoy describes the sacrifice as:

`merely a means of magical compulsion`

and:

`as it were purely mechanical`.

He compares it to a charm which `directly controls natural phenomena`, then says that often the god is `not concerned in it at all`: the god's will is not the operative relation.

Thus the visible category `sacrifice` does not determine the explanatory mechanism. At least locally, Lovejoy separates:

`SACRIFICE AS TRANSACTION/WILL-RELATION`

from

`SACRIFICE AS DIRECT QUASI-MECHANICAL EFFICACY`.

This controls later temptation to flatten every sacrifice into propitiation, communion, or offering to a personal deity.

## 3. p.66 — type indexed by social bearer / competent performer

The facing page to the funerary-sacrifice discussion adds another relation not captured well in the first pass. Lovejoy says expiatory sacrifice is `very generally performed` by the whole community; the community thereby disembarrasses itself of responsibility for a crime/offence. Magical sacrifice, by contrast, is performed by priests, and specifically by priests possessing magical skill in charms.

Safe operator:

`RITUAL TYPE -> DIFFERENT SOCIAL JURISDICTION / COMPETENT AGENT`.

This is not just classification by victim, object, or theological purpose. Who may or must perform the rite is itself evidence about its mechanism.

## 4. pp.92–95 — first-pass material conflation corrected

PDF p.92 had been summarized too continuously. Direct inspection shows two physically and intellectually distinct layers:

- underlying notebook prose continues the first-fruit / new-fruit problem from p.91;
- a separate inserted leaf lies over it and is directly headed:

`Marillier — June 13 — Sacrifice`.

The inserted leaf begins a funerary/divinity comparison. It cannot be treated as if it continued the underlying first-fruit paragraph.

PDF p.94 supplies a second direct anchor in the same insertion run:

`Marillier — 13 June — Sac.`

and a clearly marked classificatory heading:

`C. Naturalistic divinities`.

The p.92–95 run therefore represents an inserted June-13 Marillier layer involving funerary sacrifice and classes/genealogies of divinities. This is followed shortly by the already controlled p.96 insertion headed `Marillier ... June 20`.

Material consequence:

`HOST-PAGE CONTINUITY != INSERTED-TEXT CONTINUITY`.

A scan page can contain two different chronological/argumentative witnesses. Flattening them creates false transitions that Lovejoy never wrote.

## 5. Combined consequence

The new controls sharpen the broader working pattern already visible across 004 and 005:

1. source authorities are ranked by how far theory exceeds evidence;
2. a named ritual form is decomposed by operative mechanism;
3. mechanism can be diagnosed partly through social jurisdiction and competent performer;
4. material layers must be separated before argumentative chronology is reconstructed;
5. developmental arrows are then tested and can be reversed, as p.104 already demonstrated.

This is still an operational continuity claim, not a genealogy of the mature unit-idea method. The archive now supports something more precise: Lovejoy repeatedly treats **classification, mechanism, source authority, material witness, and historical succession as separable evidential problems**.
