### Consequence

The strongest current provenance model is now **longitudinal and stratified**:

1. the notebook was acquired/used in Paris and contains an extensive **1898–99 Marillier/EPHE core** (Paris stationery; `Dec. 14`, `May 29`, `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`; official EPHE report naming Lovejoy as an active auditor);
2. at least part of the notebook was **reused or extended in 1905**, when Lovejoy was teaching at Washington University;
3. the exact dating of individual later sections, especially pp. 46–119, still has to be mapped rather than assigned wholesale to 1898–99 or 1905.

This supersedes the simpler formulation that 005 is simply a Paris 1898–99 notebook that later happened to resemble the 1906 paper.

## 2. Revised 005 → 1906 model

The 1905 date makes the relation to **“The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy” (1906)** more interesting again.

The current model is:

> **Marillier/EPHE comparative-religion formation and source collection (1898–99) → continued/reused History of Religion notebook work documented in 1905 → theoretical condensation around efficacy/power in 1906.**

This preserves the Marillier institutional genealogy while allowing a much more immediate pre-publication phase. It also fits the notebook's movement from classification of future-life beliefs and sacrifice toward distinctions between personal/spiritual agency and intrinsic or quasi-mechanical efficacy.

The exact course behind the heading `Hist. Relig.` is **not yet independently identified**. Washington University catalogues for 1904–05/1905 are digitized, and Lovejoy was on the Washington University faculty in this period, but the present web sweep has not yet recovered a catalogue entry explicitly naming a Lovejoy course called `History of Religion`. The manuscript heading is primary evidence for Lovejoy's own `Hist. Relig.` outline; institutional/course attribution remains a separate problem.

## 4. New strict-core evidence: Lovejoy was still reviewing Vedānta scholarship in 1907

A further finding materially extends the Indian-philosophy strand beyond the 1898 article and 1901 Buddhism syllabus.

**Arthur O. Lovejoy reviewed Paul Deussen, _Outline of the Vedānta System of Philosophy, According to Shankara_, in _Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods_ 4.1 (1907), pp. 23–24.**

PhilPapers independently confirms the review metadata. Daniel J. Wilson's annotated bibliography summarizes Lovejoy's judgment:

- the English outline extracted from Deussen's larger _System des Vedānta_ would be a useful **“convenience” for those teaching Hindu philosophy**;
- Lovejoy urged more study of **Rāmānuja's Vedānta**, rather than continuing to concentrate scholarly attention on Śaṅkara.

This is important evidence against a simple narrative in which Lovejoy's direct Indian-philosophy engagement ended with the 1898 Buddhist paper or even the 1901 Buddhism lectures. By 1907 he was still reading and evaluating specialist Vedānta literature, and he framed the review partly in terms of the needs of **teachers of Hindu philosophy**.

Primary/metadata routes:
- PhilPapers record for Lovejoy review, 1907, JPP&SM 4(1):23–24.
- Wilson bibliography item 176 for the evaluative synopsis.

A clean primary scan of the two-page review is still needed before quoting its wording beyond Wilson's synopsis.

## 5. The religion strand also remains active in 1907–08

Batch 09 established a sequence that should now be treated alongside the Vedānta review:

- 1907, **“The Entangling Alliance of Religion and History,”** _Hibbert Journal_ 5, 258–276;
- 1907, **“The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought,”** _American Journal of Theology_ 11, 228–249;
- 1908, **“Pragmatism and Theology,”** _American Journal of Theology_ 12, 116–143;
- 1908, **“Religious Transition and Ethical Awakening in America,”** _Hibbert Journal_ 6, 500–514;
- 1908, **“The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus,”** _The Monist_ 18, 597–609.

Wilson explicitly characterizes Lovejoy's first fifteen years of twentieth-century publishing as ranging widely across the **history of religion and history of philosophy**, and calls these early historical papers early essays in the method later known as the history of ideas.

The chronology therefore looks less like `Orientalism → abandonment → history of ideas` and more like overlapping transformations: direct Sanskrit/Buddhist/Indic work, comparative religion, Jewish/Christian historical work, and philosophical history coexist for a substantial period.

## 6. A methodological lead from 1908

Wilson's synopsis of the 1908 essay-review of W. Benjamin Smith is especially suggestive. Smith proposed primitive Christianity as the product of a **“vast and slow syncretism”** with distinctive elements derived largely from Gnosticism. Lovejoy tests the proposed genealogy against the evidence and concludes that the major theory is not sustained and is in places contradicted by its evidence.

At minimum, this belongs to the same family of operations already visible in notebooks 004 and 005:

- do not accept a named system or ritual complex as an explanatory unit;
- discriminate constituent elements and mechanisms;
- test borrowing/syncretism historically rather than assuming it from resemblance;
- ask whether the proposed arrangement actually fits the evidence.

Whether this constitutes one continuous method must be demonstrated by close reading, but the comparison is now worth pursuing directly.
