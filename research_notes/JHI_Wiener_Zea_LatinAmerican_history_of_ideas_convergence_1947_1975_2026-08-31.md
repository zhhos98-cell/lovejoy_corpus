# Wiener, Zea, and the convergence of JHI with Latin American `historia de las ideas`, 1947–1975

Date: 2026-08-31  
Status: **INSTITUTIONAL NETWORK DOSSIER / STRONG PRIMARY-RETROSPECTIVE + SECONDARY CONTROL / CONFERENCE ARCHIVE OPEN**

## Core result

The internationalization of JHI/history of ideas after Lovejoy was not simply the export of an American programme.

By the late 1940s Latin America had an independently organized **Comité de Historia de las Ideas en América**, centered on Leopoldo Zea and the Pan-American Institute of Geography and History (IPGH). Philip P. Wiener and the JHI network made contact with this movement very early; in 1962 the International Society for the History of Ideas held a Mexico City conference with the participation/support of Zea's committee and UNAM; by 1973 Zea was a JHI Corresponding Editor; in 1975 JHI published a review-article treating Zea's *Positivism in Mexico* as a question about the importance of the history of ideas in Latin America.

This is better represented as **convergence between institutionalized traditions**:

`JHI / WIENER / INTERNATIONAL SOCIETY`
×
`ZEA / IPGH / LATIN-AMERICAN HISTORIA DE LAS IDEAS`.

---

# I. Formation of the Latin American committee

A contemporary 1947/48 report later reproduced by filosofia.org and modern scholarship records the proposal and formation of the `Comité de Historia de las Ideas en América` through the History Commission of the Pan-American Institute of Geography and History.

Leopoldo Zea proposed the committee, supported by Luis Recaséns Siches. The programme aimed to:

- stimulate study of ideas, thought and philosophical influences across the American continent;
- develop national histories of thought/ideas/philosophy;
- use these national histories as the basis for a general history of American thought and ideas.

Modern institutional history locates formal creation in 1948 while the project/proposal was circulating in 1947. Keep the date distinction visible rather than forcing one foundation date.

---

# II. Wiener/JHI contact is present at the movement's beginning

The early committee report records responses to Zea's organizing initiative.

Particularly important:

- Patrick Romanell conveyed **Philip P. Wiener's approval**, identifying Wiener as Managing Editor of the *Journal of the History of Ideas*;
- Wiener expressed willingness/desire to contribute to the successful development of the enterprise.

Other responses included scholars such as F. S. C. Northrop, Arthur Whitaker, Hanke, Weinberg/Francisco Romero, Arturo Ardao and Edmundo O'Gorman.

Thus JHI contact with the Latin American movement predates the 1960 International Society.

Safe claim:

`EARLY MUTUAL CONTACT / SUPPORT` = secure.

Do not yet claim formal organizational affiliation between JHI and the Committee in 1947/48.

---

# III. The Latin American movement has its own institutional logic

The Committee should not be described as a regional branch of Lovejoy's school.

Its programme is embedded in:

- Pan-American institutional cooperation;
- national philosophical/intellectual self-histories;
- the `Idea of America` problem;
- Mexican historicism / Zea / Gaos networks;
- continental publication schemes;
- regional philosophical debates about cultural consciousness, dependence, modernity and national thought.

The committee's core architecture is:

`NATIONAL HISTORIES OF IDEAS`
→ `CONTINENTAL GENERAL HISTORY OF AMERICAN THOUGHT`.

This differs materially from Lovejoy's unit-idea / cross-disciplinary long-range tracking programme even though both use the label `history of ideas`.

The convergence is therefore historiographically interesting precisely because the traditions are non-identical.

---

# IV. Rockefeller / FCE / IPGH publication infrastructure

Modern research on the project reconstructs substantial institutional support.

The history-of-ideas programme emerged from Zea's continental scholarly networking and was supported by Rockefeller-funded travel/research contexts.

The IPGH project developed a publication series with Fondo de Cultura Económica. By the mid-1950s works appeared on contemporary ideas/philosophy in Uruguay, Bolivia, Brazil, Argentina, Central America, Mexico and other regions.

Zea's later retrospective describes the central task as preparation/publication of histories of ideas for the nations of the Americas, with general continental synthesis as the longer objective.

This is a real parallel **research-program + publishing infrastructure**, not only a philosophical label.

---

# V. 1956 seminar = disciplinary consolidation within Latin America

Scholarship on the movement treats the 1956 Seminar on the History of Ideas in America as a major maturity/consolidation event.

It:

- linked researchers across Latin American countries;
- discussed objects and methods of regional history of ideas;
- contributed to a dedicated journal/project ecology;
- helped make history of ideas not only a scholarly technique but, for some participants, a reflective intellectual current tied to social/national reality.

This supplies a major control against treating the later 1962 International Society meeting as the first moment of regional organization.

---

# VI. Mexico City 1962: International Society meets an existing Latin-American movement

A first-person/near-contemporary Uruguayan account records participation in a **1962 Conference on the History of Ideas in Mexico**, organized by the `International Society for the History of Ideas`, headquartered in New York, with the participation/support of:

- Zea's Committee on the History of Ideas in America;
- the University of Mexico / UNAM;
- Rector Ignacio Chávez, who opened the sessions.

This follows:

- Cambridge 1960 — first International Society meeting;
- Mexico City 1962 — second meeting/conference, also attested in Paolo Rossi archival records.

This means the Society's internationalization should be described as **interface with pre-existing regional formations**, not merely expansion from an American/European center.

---

# VII. Zea enters JHI governance

A 1973 JHI editorial letterhead under Philip Wiener lists **Leopoldo Zea** among the journal's Corresponding Editors, alongside:

- Isaiah Berlin;
- Herbert Butterfield;
- Alvar Ellegård;
- Hajime Nakamura;
- Franco Venturi.

This is direct evidence that the mature Wiener-era JHI governance structure incorporated an established Latin American historian/philosopher of ideas rather than only publishing occasional Latin American subjects.

The same masthead makes clear that corresponding editorship was part of a deliberately international network.

---

# VIII. 1975: Latin American history of ideas becomes an explicit JHI historiographical object

JHI 36.1 (1975) includes Jorge J. E. Gracia's review-article:

`Importance of the History of Ideas in Latin America: Zea's Positivism in Mexico`, pp.177–184.

This is historiographically significant because the object is no longer simply `Mexican positivism`.

The review title makes the **importance/status of history of ideas in Latin America** itself the problem.

Thus the institutional sequence can be represented:

`EARLY CONTACT WITH REGIONAL COMMITTEE`
→ `INTERNATIONAL SOCIETY CONFERENCE CONVERGENCE`
→ `REGIONAL LEADER ENTERS JHI GOVERNANCE`
→ `LATIN-AMERICAN HISTORY-OF-IDEAS TRADITION BECOMES JHI SELF-REFLECTIVE OBJECT`.

---

# IX. Relation to the global JHI master map

The Latin American line gives a crucial correction to a simple `West -> non-West` expansion story.

By the 1960s–70s JHI/Wiener is interacting with at least three differently constituted networks:

1. **China/Asian Studies** — Committee on Chinese Thought, AAS, ACLS, Wright/Fairbank/Nivison etc.;
2. **East-West comparative thought** — Nakamura/Wiener/Wright/East-West Center;
3. **Latin American historia de las ideas** — Zea/IPGH/FCE/national histories.

These networks have different units, institutions and political projects.

The mature JHI becomes an **interface among traditions** rather than simply one tradition gaining broader regional coverage.

---

# X. Important methodological non-equivalence

Do not collapse Zea's history of ideas into Lovejoy's.

Lovejoyian/JHI technical questions often emphasize:

- discriminable ideas/components;
- recurrence/continuity;
- influence/source proof;
- cross-disciplinary migration;
- historical combination.

Zea's Latin American programme is more strongly structured around:

- national/continental historical consciousness;
- philosophy as response to historical circumstance;
- reception/transformation of imported doctrines;
- American cultural identity;
- intellectual dependence/autonomy and modernity.

The historical payoff is precisely to study what happens when the label `history of ideas` coordinates non-equivalent programmes.

---

# XI. New large-scale hypothesis

The post-Lovejoy history of JHI may be better described as a shift from:

`ONE JOURNAL / ONE PROGRAMME / BROAD SUBJECT MATTER`

toward:

`A JOURNAL AS INTERNATIONAL INTERFACE AMONG MULTIPLE HISTORIES-OF-IDEAS TRADITIONS`.

Evidence currently includes:

- International Society Cambridge 1960;
- Mexico City 1962 convergence with Zea committee;
- Wiener/Nakamura East-West Center collaboration 1962–64;
- JHI international/corresponding editorial network by 1973;
- Zea/Nakamura/Wright in governance/collaboration;
- Dictionary-era international comparative ambitions.

This hypothesis should be tested against editorial correspondence and International Society records rather than asserted from mastheads alone.
