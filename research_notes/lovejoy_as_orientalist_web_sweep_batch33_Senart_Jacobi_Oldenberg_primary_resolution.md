# Lovejoy as Orientalist / comparative religion — web sweep batch 33

Date: 2026-08-18  
Focus: manual primary-source recovery for the Senart–Jacobi–Oldenberg controversy around notebook 004 and Lovejoy 1898. This batch supersedes the unresolved priority fork in batch 32 where the newly recovered primary evidence permits a decision.

## Core advance

The manual uploads materially narrow the originality claim for Lovejoy 1898. Three primary texts are now in hand: Émile Senart 1896, Hermann Jacobi 1898, and Hermann Oldenberg 1898. Together they show that the key philological problem around `upādāna`, the relation between `upādāna` and `upādānakkhandha`, and the functional sequencing of the nidānas were already an active, tightly coupled controversy before and during Lovejoy’s article.

The safest revised history is therefore not “Lovejoy discovers the functional coherence of a formula that Senart declared composite.” It is: **Lovejoy enters a live European Indological controversy, absorbs its textual controls, recomposes them in a compact technical article, and later generalizes the same kind of inferential discipline into a history of categories.**

## 1. Senart 1896: the compositional thesis is now first-hand

Recovered primary:

Émile Senart, “À propos de la théorie bouddhique des douze nidânas,” in *Mélanges Charles de Harlez* (Leiden: Brill, 1896), pp.281–297.

The article explicitly asks whether the twelve-nidāna nomenclature directly represents an original, coherent Buddhist theory or is a later construction amalgamating originally independent categories without strict logical order. Senart answers that the second alternative alone is admissible.

The crucial philological move is equally explicit: `upādāna` is said to be only a shortened form of `upādāna-skandhas`, more fully `pañca upādānaskandhas`, i.e. the five skandhas. This makes the formula appear to contain the skandhas once collectively in `upādāna` and then again in more detailed form. Senart’s historical reconstruction therefore turns apparent semantic duplication into evidence for composition.

His later summary sharpens the genealogy: the formula contains (1) a Sāṁkhya-derived complex around avidyā / saṃskāra and (2) a detailed repetition of the skandhas already contained en bloc in `upādāna`.

This matters because we no longer need to reconstruct “Senart’s position” from Lovejoy’s quotations. The target Lovejoy was attacking is exact and primary-supported.

## 2. Jacobi 1898: the 1897 Oldenberg control is effectively identified

Recovered primary:

Hermann Jacobi, “Über das Verhältnis der buddhistischen Philosophie zu Sāṁkhya-Yoga und die Bedeutung der Nidānas” (1898), pp.1–15.

Jacobi opens by saying that his 1896 argument for a Sāṁkhya-Yoga origin of Buddhist philosophy had drawn objections from Oldenberg and Senart. He explicitly points to Oldenberg’s *Buddha*, **3rd edition**, p.448ff., and to Senart, *Mélanges Charles de Harlez*, p.281ff.

More decisive for the immediate priority problem is Jacobi’s footnote in the `upādāna` discussion: regarding Senart’s view that `upādāna = upādānakkhandha`, he directs the reader to **Oldenberg, *Buddha*³, p.273 note**.

This establishes at minimum that the exact Senart `upādāna = upādānakkhandha` problem had already been treated by Oldenberg in the 1897 third edition. It therefore rules out presenting Lovejoy 1898 as the first scholar to isolate that problem. The exact wording and full argumentative direction of Oldenberg’s 1897 note still require recovery of the real third-edition p.273, but the priority control is no longer a blank.

Jacobi himself offers a different interpretation: he tries to align `upādāna` functionally with `adṛṣṭa` / karma and construes it as passions or sinful dispositions that permit karmic effects and the production of new karma. His argument is precisely that simple “grasping” risks collapsing `upādāna` into either `sparśa` or `tṛṣṇā`.

## 3. Oldenberg 1898 pp.690–694: the closest parallel is now primary

Recovered primary:

Hermann Oldenberg, “Buddhistische Studien,” *ZDMG* 52 (1898), section VII, especially pp.690–694.

Oldenberg rejects Jacobi’s construction from Sāṁkhya `adṛṣṭa` / karma and insists that the Buddhist use of `upādāna` and `upādiyati` be established from the Buddhist textual tradition itself.

On p.691 he begins with a negative control: Buddhist `upādāna` has nothing to do with philosophical `upādāna` as *causa materialis*. He then moves through the ordinary semantic field of Sanskrit `upādatte` / Pāli `upādiyati`: taking hold, seizing, appropriating.

On p.692 the functional chain is explicit. `phassa → vedanā → taṇhā → upādāna` is read as successive stages: contact of the senses with an external object; feeling; desire; then grasping or appropriation of the object. Oldenberg directly answers Jacobi’s objection that this sense would be indistinguishable from `phassa`: contact and grasping are different stages. He also notes that if `upādāna` is interpreted subjectively as `nandi` / `chandarāga`, the boundary with `taṇhā` becomes closer, but the textual categories remain distinguishable.

This is important for our own reconstruction: **the operation we had provisionally labelled Lovejoy’s “functional non-equivalence” is already very strongly present in Oldenberg 1898.** Oldenberg is not merely saying that the vocabulary differs; he distinguishes adjacent links by the different work they do in the causal sequence.

Oldenberg then uses this semantic-functional reading to reject Jacobi’s stronger Sāṁkhya genealogy. He permits limited historical contact or older conceptual antecedents, but refuses to infer wholesale dependence merely from parallel terms.

A text search across the recovered pp.681–694 section finds no occurrence of `Lovejoy`. This is negative OCR evidence only, but it makes the earlier “immediate Oldenberg uptake of Lovejoy” hypothesis substantially less plausible. There is at present no direct sign in this section that Oldenberg was responding to Lovejoy.

## 6. What is no longer safe to claim for Lovejoy

Three earlier provisional formulations should now be retired or narrowed.

1. **“Lovejoy first rejects Senart’s `upādāna = upādānakkhandha`.”** No. Jacobi 1898 explicitly points to Oldenberg’s 1897 third-edition p.273 note for that exact Senart view.
2. **“Lovejoy’s distinctive move is functional non-equivalence.”** Too broad. Oldenberg 1898 performs the same kind of control very explicitly in the `phassa → vedanā → taṇhā → upādāna` sequence.
3. **“Oldenberg may have immediately absorbed Lovejoy’s article.”** Currently weak. The recovered Oldenberg section has no Lovejoy hit, while the relevant dispute already points backward to Oldenberg’s 1897 third edition.

## 7. What may still be specifically Lovejoy’s increment

The narrower Lovejoy delta now needs to be tested in three places rather than assumed.

### A. Karma / upādāna causal allocation

Lovejoy appears to sharpen a distinction between the cause determining **whether rebirth continues** and the cause determining **the character or form of rebirth**. This may be more explicit than Oldenberg’s treatment, but the full Lovejoy–Oldenberg collation must be made before calling it original.

### B. Extension from `upādāna` to `upādisesa`

Lovejoy’s article joins the `upādāna` dispute to an extended treatment of `upādisesa / anupādisesa`, where he tests residue, attachment, skandha persistence, and ethical condition. That coupling may be the most publication-specific part of the article. Oldenberg is himself a major source here, so the question is one of synthesis and argumentative recombination rather than simple novelty.

### C. Reuse as method after 1898

The strongest historical payoff may lie downstream. Even if the 1898 philological controls are shared with Oldenberg and the wider controversy, Lovejoy’s later work can still be studied for how he transports the same discipline of **distinguishing provenance from logical function, and resemblance from explanatory identity** into 1902 category-history. That is a reception-and-transformation argument, not a priority claim.

## 8. Revised thesis for the 004 route

The evidence now supports a stronger and less vulnerable formulation:

> Notebook 004 is not best read as the private origin of an isolated Lovejoy innovation. It is a working dossier inside the Senart–Jacobi–Oldenberg controversy of 1896–98. Lovejoy’s significance lies in how he learned to police genealogy with semantic, causal, and configurational controls, how he recomposed those controls in his own 1898 article, and how the same inferential habit survives when the object shifts from Buddhist technical terminology to the history of philosophical categories.

This formulation preserves the historically important continuity `004 → 1898 → 1902` while removing a priority claim the recovered European Indological sources do not support.
