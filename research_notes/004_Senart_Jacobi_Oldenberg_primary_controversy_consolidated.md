## 1. Senart 1896 — stratigraphic genealogy and the duplication problem

Primary object: Émile Senart, `À propos de la théorie bouddhique des douze nidânas`, in *Mélanges Charles de Harlez* (1896), pp.281–297.

Direct primary controls:

- p.284 frames the historiographical alternative: the twelve-nidāna nomenclature may not be the direct expression of an original, fully conscious Buddhist theory but a later construction amalgamating originally independent categories without strict logical order.
- pp.284–285 identify `upādāna` as a shortened designation for `upādāna-skandhas` / `pañca upādānaskandhas`; on this reading the five skandhas occur once collectively and again in distributed form.
- pp.286–288 separate historical layers: `taṇhā/upādāna` and the following terms form one nucleus; `avidyā/saṃskāra` are linked to a Sāṃkhya-Yoga background; other links repeat material already contained in the supposed collective `upādāna`.
- pp.288, 293, 295 make clear that Senart is himself doing analysis, lexical filiation and stratification. He must not be reduced to a foil who simply declares the formula meaningless.

The operative Senart inference is:

`repetition + heterogeneous inherited categories + term filiation → compositional stratification → reduced confidence in an originally autonomous systematic whole`.

The strong Lovejoy/Senart contrast is therefore not `analysis versus no analysis`. Lovejoy grants historical mixture and repetition but refuses to let those facts alone settle the present semantic/causal relation among the terms or prove that the received arrangement is unintelligible or merely accidental.

## 2. Oldenberg before Lovejoy — 1890 baseline

Oldenberg's 1890 second edition of *Buddha* already supplies a pre-controversy evidentiary baseline:

- p.100 n.1 admits real Sāṃkhya/Buddhist `Anklänge` but treats them as no more than the `Familienähnlichkeit` of Indian philosophical systems unless stronger evidence is supplied;
- p.226 n.2 notes a fourfold Sāṃkhya/Yoga liberation schema but says the direction of borrowing cannot be established;
- pp.262–266 compare `avidyā/aviveka`, beginningless causal chains and `saṃskāra/vāsanā`, including the possibility that Sāṃkhya preserved an older configuration;
- p.268 already contains substantially the `saṅkhāra` explanation Jacobi later attacks in the third-edition pagination.

Thus the 1897 third edition is an expansion and polemical consolidation of an already cautious inferential regime, not a reversal from simple anti-Sāṃkhya skepticism.

## 3. Oldenberg 1897 third edition — true edition now directly controlled

Primary object: Hermann Oldenberg, *Buddha: Sein Leben, seine Lehre, seine Gemeinde*, `DRITTE VERMEHRTE AUFLAGE` (Berlin: Wilhelm Hertz, 1897).

The older local witness with `...03...` in its filename is in fact the **1890 second edition**. Edition identification must follow title page/preface, not filename.

### pp.272–273 — inherited vocabulary and Senart

Oldenberg accepts that Buddhism can use inherited `Schlagworte` while combining them so that recognizable thought remains. On p.273 he directly rejects Senart's equation of `upādāna` with the five `upādānakkhandha`: canonical passages distinguish attachment from the aggregates to which attachment is directed, and `upādāna` is connected with `chandarāga` toward the five khandhas. The alleged simple duplication therefore disappears.

This is an exact pre-Lovejoy control for the anti-Senart semantic move.

### pp.443–455 — Sāṃkhya/Buddhism excursus

The edition-specific excursus supplies multiple field-level proof controls:

- p.443: recent Garbe, Jacobi, Senart and Dahlmann work leads Oldenberg to place Buddhism closer to Sāṃkhya while resisting maximal dependency claims.
- p.444: old keywords, keyword-groups and whole category-series can be put to work under new fundamental ideas; gradual formations may later acquire a strict systematic shape and appear `aus einem Gusse`.
- p.445: comparison must seek genuinely characteristic `Hauptgedanken`, while leaving open whether earlier thought-circles were already identical with communities bearing the later Sāṃkhya name.
- pp.445–446: generic parallels may be common slogans or may conflict with conspicuous Sāṃkhya doctrine.
- p.447: similar sequences can answer **different questions**; Buddhism reconstructs the arising of suffering through psychic processes, while Sāṃkhya explains cosmic evolution.
- p.448: related concepts and neighboring positions do not by themselves establish the precise genealogical construction claimed.
- p.449: agreement in generic categories proves little; comparison must reach genuinely characteristic conceptions.
- p.450: absence of the guṇas in Buddhism cannot bear the weight assigned to it as negative genealogical evidence.
- pp.451–453: source chronology and literary genre constrain historical use of Aśvaghoṣa's Arāḍa report; negative coincidence is weak evidence.
- pp.454–455: positive canonical usage of `sakkāya/satkāya` defeats Senart's speculative `satkārya` etymology.

The field repertoire visible here before Lovejoy's 1898 print therefore already includes:

`composite inheritance ≠ incoherence`; `generic resemblance ≠ characteristic system identity`; `formal sequence ≠ same question/function`; `negative coincidence = weak genealogy`; `source chronology/genre constrain reconstruction`; `usage may defeat speculative etymology`.

## 4. Jacobi 1897 — diagnostic difference-points

Primary object: Hermann Jacobi's review of Joseph Dahlmann, *Nirvāṇa*, *Göttingische Gelehrte Anzeigen* (1897), pp.265–279; core pp.268–272.

Direct controls:

- p.268 asks how the epic material relates to classical Sāṃkhya and Yoga and emphasizes differentiating doctrines rather than generic affinity.
- p.271 warns against identifying a broad speculative/religious formation with one philosophical school merely because it absorbs multiple authorities.
- p.272 stresses several nontrivial difference-points: Īśvara and the differentiation of `buddhi`, `ahaṅkāra`, and `manas`, plus Yoga's systematized ascetic practice.

This is a direct pre-004 control for the general operation project language calls `diagnostic specificity`. Lovejoy's own `To R.D.'s remark it should be added...` intervention remains a real manuscript operation, but the operator itself was already live in the controversy.

No direct Lovejoy use of Jacobi's 1897 review is established.

## 5. Jacobi 1898 — genealogy plus constructive system reconstruction

Primary object: Hermann Jacobi, `Über das Verhältnis der buddhistischen Philosophie zu Sāṁkhya-Yoga und die Bedeutung der Nidānas` (1898), pp.1–15.

Jacobi clarifies that Buddhism is not a mere `Abklatsch` of a finished Sāṃkhya system. Sāṃkhya ideas are treated as a conceptual background transformed by Buddha. In the `upādāna` discussion he retracts a simple equation with `adṛṣṭa` and instead construes the term as passions/dispositions through which karma becomes effective and new karma arises.

Crucially, Jacobi explicitly directs Senart's `upādāna = upādānakkhandha` view to Oldenberg, *Buddha*³ p.273 n. This independently fixes Oldenberg's 1897 priority for that semantic objection.

## 6. Oldenberg 1898 — local semantic/causal function against genealogy

Primary object: Hermann Oldenberg, `Buddhistische Studien`, *ZDMG* 52 (1898), section VII, especially pp.690–694.

Direct controls:

- p.691 rejects importing the philosophical Sanskrit sense `upādāna = causa materialis` into Buddhist usage and starts from `upādatte/upādiyati`: seizing, appropriating, taking hold.
- p.692 reconstructs `phassa → vedanā → taṇhā → upādāna` as contact → feeling → desire → grasping/appropriation. He directly rejects Jacobi's claim that this collapses `upādāna` into `phassa`; the terms occupy different causal stages.
- where `upādāna` is rendered through `nandi/chandarāga`, Oldenberg acknowledges a closer boundary with `taṇhā` while maintaining textual distinction.
- he cross-refers the canonical `nandi/chandarāga` evidence back to *Buddha*³ p.273 n., independently corroborating Jacobi's pointer.
- the semantic-functional reading is used to resist Jacobi's stronger `adṛṣṭa/karma` genealogy.
- a search of the recovered section produced no `Lovejoy` hit. This is bounded negative evidence, not proof of non-reading.

Thus functional non-equivalence among adjacent nidānas is not a Lovejoy-specific invention.

## 7. Lovejoy 004 / 1898 after the primary controls

The notebook still provides unusually fine documentary evidence of Lovejoy's own source transformation:

- it works through the same khandha/nidāna, Senart, Oldenberg and karma problems;
- it grants historical mixture while seeking a technical ordering;
- it separates source propositions from Lovejoy additions and tests;
- it develops a `fact` versus `manner` causal question around Hardy;
- it carries broad exploratory genealogy, taxonomy and diagrams that are later pruned from print.

Lovejoy's 1898 article:

- directly rejects Senart's `upādāna = upādānakkhandha` interpretation, but Oldenberg 1897 has priority for the basic semantic objection;
- distinguishes `karma` as determining the **particular condition/form** of rebirth from `upādāna` as explaining renewed existence **as such**;
- joins the `upādāna` dispute to the extended `upādisesa/anupādisesa` problem;
- explicitly allows borrowed terms, `perhaps even the ideas`, while retaining an `original and characteristic` `arrangement and application`;
- brackets competing etymologies when they cannot decide contextual meaning and turns to usage;
- retains contrary passages and ends provisionally.

The remaining Lovejoy delta is therefore **selection, reweighting, recombination and publication transformation**, not priority for the constituent semantic/source-critical operators.
