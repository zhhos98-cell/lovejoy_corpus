## 2. Warren on taṇhā -> upādāna: correct the notebook characterization

Notebook 004 PDF p.22 / MS p.75 says, in substance, that Warren regards the relation of attachment to desire as one of `identity`, then Lovejoy objects that the present text does not support this.

Direct Warren §34 does **not** simply assert identity.

Warren's text says:

- attachment can be described as `tenacity of desire`;
- a prior desire can become tenacious;
- another reported explanation distinguishes desire as quest before possession from attachment as seizing an object once within reach;
- the four attachments are separately enumerated.

Therefore source coding must be:

`WARREN TEXT = CLOSE SEMANTIC CONTINUITY / TENACITY + ALTERNATIVE STAGE DISTINCTION`

whereas

`WARREN = IDENTITY`

is **LOVEJOY'S COMPRESSED CHARACTERIZATION OF WARREN**, not Warren's literal proposition.

This matters because Lovejoy's rejection is stronger than a simple author-vs-author contradiction. He sharpens Warren into an identity thesis and then tests that sharpened thesis against the fourfold attachment / sixfold desire architecture.

Use:

> `Lovejoy construes Warren's close semantic treatment as an identity problem and rejects that stronger construction.`

Do not quote Warren as saying `identity` unless a separate Warren passage with that word is recovered.

## 3. Childers ↔ Müller on Dhammapada 1: the controversy is partly pre-packaged by Müller

Notebook 004 PDF p.29 / MS p.89:

- directly invokes Childers pp.576–577 for a strong `viññāṇa` claim;
- then says `But v. Childers s.v. dhamma & Müller's Dhammapada for a diff. interpretation of Dhammapada 1.`

Max Müller's SBE translation note to Dhammapada 1 itself reproduces the history of Childers's change of mind:

1. Childers, dictionary p.120 / `dhamma`, originally approves Müller's simpler rendering and calls the commentarial technical reading forced;
2. Childers p.577 later argues that the first verse strongly asserts the superiority of `viññāṇa`, with the other mental khandhas dominated/governed by Mind;
3. Müller concedes that the technical reading may represent a traditional/Buddhaghosa interpretation but retains the simpler moral-contextual reading.

Thus Lovejoy's p.29 node is not simply:

`CHILDERS INDEPENDENT SOURCE vs MÜLLER INDEPENDENT SOURCE`.

It is at least partly:

`CHILDERS DICTIONARY (directly page-checked by Lovejoy)`
+
`MÜLLER NOTE THAT ALREADY COLLATES CHILDERS'S TWO POSITIONS`
-> `LOVEJOY KEEPS Dhammapada 1 FROM SETTLING THE VIÑÑĀṆA PRIMACY CLAIM`.

Source-role coding:

- Childers pp.576–577 = `DIRECT LEXICAL/DOCTRINAL AUTHORITY`;
- Childers s.v. dhamma = `DIRECT COUNTER-CONTROL`;
- Müller Dhammapada note = `SOURCE ROUTER + ARGUMENTATIVE COUNTER-READING`;
- Lovejoy's decision to keep the proposition open and continue through nidāna/khandha evidence = `LOVEJOY DOSSIER MANAGEMENT / RECOMPOSITION`.

Do not award Lovejoy priority for discovering the Childers contradiction. Müller had already published it.

What remains Lovejoy-specific is its placement inside his larger `viññāṇa` dossier and its subsequent relation to the temporal/logical problem at MS p.123.

## 6. upādisesa: Oldenberg supplies semantic direction and much of the evidence; Lovejoy changes the tribunal

Existing direct-primary dossier establishes:

- Oldenberg 1882 English p.433 is Lovejoy's directly named positive source;
- Oldenberg already supplies the ethical-residue reading, contrary physical-residue evidence, Anāgāmin/Arahat contrast, deceased-monk case, and poison/remnant analogy;
- Oldenberg resolves the contradiction by a strong `upādi = upadhi` orthographic/text-history hypothesis.

The nineteenth-century lexical fork used by Lovejoy also precedes him:

- Childers / Rhys Davids: `upādi` linked to five skandhas / grasping line;
- Edward Müller / Dahlmann: `upadhi ↔ upādi` line;
- Müller citation in Lovejoy appears to contain a p.30/p.36 slip unless another edition pagination is recovered.

Sentence-level Lovejoy delta:

1. juxtapose live incompatible derivations;
2. judge **both etymologically possible**;
3. conclude derivation lacks discriminating power;
4. shift adjudication to patterned usage and doctrinal contrast;
5. add/reweight further Sutta-Nipāta evidence;
6. preserve contrary passages;
7. end preferred but explicitly provisional.

Thus the distinctive operation is not `discover ethical meaning`, but:

`ETYMOLOGICAL UNDERDETERMINATION -> CHANGE OF TRIBUNAL -> USAGE-BASED RECOMPOSITION -> PROVISIONAL ENDPOINT`.
