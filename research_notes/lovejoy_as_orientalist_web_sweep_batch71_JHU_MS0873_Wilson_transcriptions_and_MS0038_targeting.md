# Batch 71 — Johns Hopkins archive targeting: Wilson’s Lovejoy-letter transcriptions and limits of MS-0038 search

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: identify exact Johns Hopkins containers that can advance the 1895–1907 Harvard/Paris/Orientalist chronology without asking repository staff to perform a speculative subject search.

## Core result

Johns Hopkins has one unusually clean, compact derivative archive that should move up the retrieval queue:

> **Daniel J. Wilson transcriptions of Arthur O. Lovejoy letters**  
> **Identifier: MS-0873**  
> **Box 1**  
> **Barcode: 31151034446595**  
> Dates represented: **1872–1962**

The collection abstract states that it consists of transcriptions, mostly typewritten with some handwritten, of Lovejoy letters.

This matters because Wilson’s biography is already the source that reconstructs Lovejoy’s 1898–99 Paris year from specific correspondence. MS-0873 may preserve readily searchable transcriptions of precisely the letters that underlie that reconstruction, potentially including the October/December 1898 and March 1899 letters already flagged in the project.

The finding aid has not exposed folder/date subdivisions inside Box 1, so the present locator is **collection + exact single box**, not letter-level.

## 1. Why MS-0873 may be higher-yield than starting with the full Lovejoy papers

Lovejoy’s main papers are large:

> **Arthur Oncken Lovejoy papers, MS-0038**

Johns Hopkins describes them as 39.4 cubic feet and including correspondence, manuscript lecture notes, notebooks, diaries, reports, speeches, drafts, typescripts, galley proofs, photographs and annotated/owned books.

The main collection is indispensable in the long run but currently remains difficult to target from web-search alone at the level needed for the Orientalist project.

By contrast, MS-0873 is a single-box research derivative assembled by the biographer who already used Lovejoy’s correspondence to reconstruct the very chronology we are testing.

A reasonable retrieval priority is therefore:

> **MS-0873 Box 1 first → use transcription dates/correspondents as locators → then target originals in MS-0038 or other repositories.**

This does not substitute transcriptions for originals in final publication where manuscript wording/materiality matters; it is an efficient locator layer.

## 2. High-value 1898–99 search terms if MS-0873 is obtained

Search the transcription box for the known Paris chronology and these terms:

- dates around **17 Oct 1898, 20 Oct 1898, 1 Dec 1898, 5 Dec 1898, 26 Mar 1899**;
- `Paris`, `Sorbonne`, `École pratique`, `Hautes Études`;
- `Marillier`;
- `Maurice Vernes`;
- `Réville`;
- `Lévi` / `Sylvain Levi`;
- `Pali`, `Buddhism`, `comparative religion`, `history of religion`;
- `Bibliothèque Nationale`;
- `dissertation`, `fellowship`, `Walker`;
- possible references to Harvard teachers or the foreign-study authorization.

These are research instructions after acquisition, not a request for Johns Hopkins staff to search the box.

## 3. MS-0038: what is secure and what is not yet precisely targetable

Johns Hopkins confirms that MS-0038 contains the categories most likely to hold early Orientalist continuity:

- lecture notes;
- notebooks;
- correspondence;
- drafts/typescripts/galley proofs;
- annotated books.

A surfaced item such as `Consciousness and Knowledge`, Box 9, Folder 5, item 2, shows that item-level records do exist in the finding aid. But current exact-name/topic searches have not exposed an equivalent item for:

- Buddhism;
- Vedānta;
- Sāṃkhya;
- `Primitive Philosophy`;
- history of religion;
- the 1901 Buddhism syllabus;
- the 1906 *Monist* article.

Therefore the full MS-0038 should not yet generate broad scan requests based only on series names.

## 5. Relationship to Harvard scan strategy

The Harvard constraint remains stricter because Harvard does not provide the kind of lookup service needed to reverse-engineer broad references. The present JHU result offers a workaround for chronology rather than a substitute for Harvard records:

- Harvard public source already proves the 1898 James Walker Fellowship, classified as Philosophy with leave abroad.
- MS-0873 may show how Lovejoy himself described what he actually did with that leave in Paris.
- EPHE records independently prove active participation in Marillier’s 1898–99 conference work.

If the transcriptions contain explicit Marillier/EPHE language, the administrative-to-intellectual sequence would become unusually tight:

> **Harvard funding category → Lovejoy’s own contemporary account → Paris institutional record → notebook 005.**

## Bottom line

> **The most efficient next Johns Hopkins retrieval is not a broad trawl through 39.4 cubic feet of MS-0038. It is MS-0873, Box 1: Daniel J. Wilson’s transcriptions of Lovejoy letters. That single box may convert Wilson’s biographical citations into date/correspondent-level locators for the originals and directly test what Lovejoy said about his Paris research while it was happening.**
