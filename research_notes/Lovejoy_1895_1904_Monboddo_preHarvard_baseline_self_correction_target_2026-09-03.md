## II. 1904 — Lovejoy's direct primary radically increases resolution

In `Some Eighteenth Century Evolutionists`, Lovejoy returns to Monboddo in a direct signed primary.

The 1904 analysis explicitly separates:

- evolution of language;
- human–orangutan ancestry;
- strict identity of species;
- kinship/descent relations;
- general transformism / mutability of species.

Lovejoy says that Monboddo nowhere treats the general biological transformation of species and may not have believed transformism as such. Calling him a `virtual evolutionist` is therefore probably too much.

The orangutan argument is especially important. Monboddo can claim ancestry while insisting on strict species identity; that move can actually fail to establish the stronger proposition that one species transforms into another.

Thus:

`ANCESTRY CLAIM`
!=
`GENERAL SPECIES TRANSFORMISM`.

The `tails` material supplies a second direct control. Lovejoy says it may be suggestive but is not designed to bear the `weight of proof` for the main proposition.
