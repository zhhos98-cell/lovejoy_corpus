## Core delta

The logical-analysis branch now has evidence at three distinct levels that should not be collapsed:

1. **pre-Lovejoy pedagogical environment:** George Holmes Howison's Berkeley philosophy teaching explicitly included formal logic, and Howison published an elementary formal-logic text before Lovejoy became his student;
2. **contemporary classification of Lovejoy:** Christine Ladd-Franklin in 1912 polemically placed Lovejoy near the `Bertrand Russell school` in a live dispute over implication and existence;
3. **documented downstream pedagogy:** Lovejoy's own doctoral student Alvin Thalheimer in 1918 explicitly described `analysis` as a parts-before-whole method of dissecting concepts and credited Lovejoy with training him to be `keen and acute in analysis`.

This materially strengthens the claim that `analysis` was not merely a retrospective descriptor for Lovejoy's style. It was a historically situated, taught, contested philosophical practice.

The principal remaining gap is earlier and narrower:

> **we still do not have direct enrollment proof that Lovejoy took Howison's formal-logic component at Berkeley or Royce's logic seminar/course at Harvard.**

Therefore the current genealogy is an ecology + person-practice + downstream-pedagogy chain, not yet a closed teacher-course-student transmission chain.

## I. Berkeley before Harvard — Howison's formal-logic ecology

### A. Howison's Berkeley propaedeutic

A University of California / Berkeley historical account of the Psychology–Philosophy institutional genealogy states that, beginning in 1888, George Holmes Howison taught a course called **`Propaedeutic to Philosophy`** that included:

- empirical psychology;
- **formal logic**;
- inductive and deductive reasoning.

The same account notes that Howison had previously taught **logic and philosophy of science** at MIT (1871–1879) and later taught at Michigan before taking the Berkeley chair.

This matters because Lovejoy's Berkeley formation should not be described simply as exposure to `Howison's idealism`. The relevant pedagogical repertoire included explicit logical training.

Source control:
- UC Berkeley Psychology Department historical biographical entry for George Holmes Howison: `https://psychology.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/in_memoriam_alphabetized_-_aug_2022.pdf`

### B. Howison authored a formal-logic manual before Lovejoy's Berkeley years

Howison's **`A Synopsis of Elementary Formal Logic in Questions`** was first published in 1888. The text distinguishes `Real Logic` from `Formal Logic` at the outset and was designed as an elementary instructional synopsis.

Source controls:
- Open Library author/work record: `https://openlibrary.org/authors/OL2503662A/George_Holmes_Howison`
- bibliographic copies of *A Synopsis of Elementary Formal Logic in Questions* (1888).

### C. Person-level relation is secure; exact course enrollment remains open

Lovejoy was directly Howison's student. Johns Hopkins archival biography states that Howison shifted Lovejoy's principal interest from religion toward philosophy; Wilson likewise treats Howison as inculcating habits of skepticism and rigorous inquiry.

There is a secondary biographical claim that Lovejoy took his first philosophy course from Howison in 1894. At present this exact `1894 course` proposition is not controlled by a direct enrollment record, transcript, or contemporary course list tied specifically to Lovejoy.

Therefore:

**Secure:**

`Howison → Lovejoy` documented teacher/student relation  
`Howison → formal logic` documented teaching/publication relation

**Not yet secure:**

`Lovejoy enrolled in Howison's formal-logic course/component`

Do not convert the two secure edges into the third.

## II. Berkeley logical culture was broader than Howison alone

The University of California philosophy publication series gives an additional ecology control. Its first volume (1904), prepared in honor of Howison and containing Lovejoy's `The Dialectic of Bruno and Spinoza`, also included Knight Dunlap's `A Theory of the Syllogism`. The second volume later included William Ernest Hocking's `Two Extensions of the Use of Graphs in Elementary Logic` (1909), while Charles H. Rieber's *Footnotes to Formal Logic* appeared as volume 3 (1918).

This does not establish direct textual influence on Lovejoy. It does show that the Berkeley/Howison network in which Lovejoy remained publishably embedded contained active work on syllogism, logical graphs, and formal logic.

Useful public-domain publication register:
- `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/86/Footnotes_to_formal_logic_%28IA_footnotestoforma01rieb%29.pdf`

Interpretive ceiling:

> **Lovejoy's return to the Howison publication network in 1904 placed his historical-philosophical essay in an institutional series whose contributors were also actively revising elementary and formal logic.**

Do not infer that the series caused Lovejoy's 1907–1910 logic work.

## III. 1912 Ladd-Franklin — a contemporary logician classifies Lovejoy as Russell-adjacent

Christine Ladd-Franklin, `Implication and Existence in Logic`, *The Philosophical Review* 21.6 (1912), 641–665, directly engages Lovejoy's 1911 `Existence and Formal Logic`.

Her polemical diagnosis is unusually valuable because it is contemporaneous rather than retrospective. She says that Lovejoy has been **`hypnotized more or less by the Bertrand Russell school`** into treating the universal proposition as decisive.

Direct primary PDF:
- `https://www.unav.es/gep/ImplicationExistenceLogic1912.pdf`

This sentence must be handled as an opponent's classification, not as proof of a Russell→Lovejoy transmission chain.

Safe inference:

> **By 1912, a technically active American logician could perceive Lovejoy's position in a live dispute over implication/existence as Russell-adjacent.**

Unsafe inference:

> `Lovejoy learned his method from Russell`.

No such causal chain is established here.

### Ladd-Franklin's methodological vocabulary matters independently

The same article defends modern/symbolic logic as a discipline of explicitness. Its value lies in making premises, conclusions, equivalences and relations sharp and definite and in guarding against the unnoticed introduction of assumptions not explicitly enumerated.

This gives an actor-level contemporaneous vocabulary for a structural feature the project has often described analytically as `evidentiary leakage` or `proposition-sensitive warrant`.

Do not write that Lovejoy borrowed this vocabulary from Ladd-Franklin. The point is field-level:

> **the philosophical/logical environment already treated unauthorized transfer among premises, relations and conclusions as a technical problem of method.**

## IV. 1918 Thalheimer — direct pedagogical transmission from Lovejoy

Alvin Thalheimer, *The Meaning of the Terms: `Existence` and `Reality`* (Johns Hopkins dissertation, 1918; printed 1920), supplies the strongest person-level downstream evidence currently recovered.

Direct public-domain PDF:
- `https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ad/The_meaning_of_the_terms-_%27existence%27_and_%27reality%27_%28IA_meaningoftermsex00thalrich%29.pdf`

### A. Thalheimer defines the contemporary analytical turn

In his opening methodological discussion, Thalheimer contrasts an older tendency to treat the world as a whole with a newer tendency to investigate **parts before the whole**. He describes the newer method as:

- concentration on specific problems;
- reduced appetite for premature synthesis/generalization;
- increased demand for `analysis`;
- dissection of intricate concepts;
- avoidance of hidden or `lurking` confusions;
- precision in distinguishing senses of terms such as `existence` and `reality`.

He associates neo-realism with organized support for this tendency but explicitly refuses to make analysis the property of one epistemological doctrine.

This is extremely important for historical vocabulary. `Analysis` in this immediate Lovejoy teaching environment is not yet equivalent to a settled later disciplinary identity called `analytic philosophy`. It is a portable method of concept dissection, problem restriction and anti-confusion work.

### B. Thalheimer explicitly credits Lovejoy for analytical training

In his Vita, Thalheimer states that he was a Johns Hopkins graduate student in philosophy under Lovejoy from 1914 to 1916 and returned in 1917 to complete the doctorate. He then says:

> Lovejoy tried to make him `critical and circumspect` in dealing with philosophical problems and `keen and acute in analysis`.

This is direct student testimony for pedagogy, not merely similarity between writings.

The evidentiary chain is therefore:

`Lovejoy's 1907–1912 published logical/analytical practice`
→ `Lovejoy teaching at Johns Hopkins`
→ `student explicitly reports training in critical, circumspect analysis`
→ `student dissertation defines analysis as concept dissection / parts-before-whole / anti-confusion method`.

Do not infer that every feature of Thalheimer's dissertation was taught by Lovejoy; Thalheimer also studied with Dewey, Woodbridge, Montague, Bush, Costello and H. C. Brown at Columbia.

## V. Problem isolation was itself a contested philosophical proposition

The genealogy of `unit-idea` should now be widened from formal logic to a contemporaneous dispute over **isolation**.

### Norman Kemp Smith, 1912

`How Far Is Agreement Possible in Philosophy?`, *Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* 9 (1912), 701–711, argues that philosophy differs from sciences because its problems cannot yet be successfully isolated: philosophy deals with complexes for which no adequate method of isolation has been formulated.

This is a crucial field control. `Isolation` was already a methodological term with epistemic stakes before Lovejoy's mature history-of-ideas programme.

### Lovejoy, 1916–1917

Lovejoy's APA programme moved in the opposite direction. He sought more organized inquiry around defined philosophical problems, cooperative investigation, cautious/hypothetical handling of individual propositions or ideas, and objective/verifiable/communicable results.

### Contemporary resistance, 1917

The collective response to Lovejoy's presidential address — Ernest Albee, Charles M. Bakewell, Theodore de Laguna, William Ernest Hocking, Edmund H. Hollands, `Progress in Philosophical Inquiry and Mr. Lovejoy's Presidential Address`, *Philosophical Review* 26.3 (1917), 315–338 — directly contests aspects of this programme. Hollands especially questions whether philosophical problems can actually be isolated from one another.

Thus:

> **problem isolation was not a neutral technique silently available to everyone; it was itself a disputed philosophical wager about how inquiry could become cumulative.**

This makes the later historian's isolation of `unit-ideas` historically intelligible as one possible extension of a prior methodological problem, without proving a simple one-to-one derivation.

## VI. Revised genealogy: three intersecting repertoires

The evidence now supports a more precise model than `formal logic caused the history of ideas`.

### 1. Logical discrimination / isolation

Historical environment and later direct primary evidence include:

- Howison's formal-logic pedagogy;
- Kant-form classification audit (Lovejoy 1907);
- logical independence of propositions (1908);
- logical analysis of historical intellectual tendency (1909);
- Vennian relation enumeration and opposition of propositions (1910);
- explicit jurisdiction limits of formal logic (1911);
- contemporary Russell-adjacent classification (Ladd-Franklin 1912);
- organized problem isolation and analytical pedagogy (1916–1918).

### 2. Philological / historical warrant disciplines

The 004/005 notebooks and early publications supply:

- textual witness control;
- semantic/function distinction;
- chronology;
- logical subdivision;
- causal mechanism;
- genealogy/borrowing;
- source competence by proposition;
- historical configuration and reaggregation.

### 3. Controlled recombination

Lovejoy repeatedly refuses both undifferentiated wholes and permanently isolated atoms:

- 1898: borrowed terms/ideas can acquire an original arrangement/application;
- 1904: contradiction must be reconstructed both logically and historically;
- 1909: compatible motives can be recombined into a `new compound in intellectual chemistry`;
- later history of ideas: analytical units are traced across changing complexes and relation types.

The strongest current hypothesis is therefore:

> **Lovejoy's history-of-ideas method may be better reconstructed as controlled isolation plus historically warranted recombination than as a simple ontology of eternal idea-atoms.**

This does not dissolve the genuine later problem of trans-contextual identity. It relocates it:

> **under what warrant can an analytically isolated component be recognized as the `same` component after its linguistic, psychological, doctrinal and historical relations have changed?**

That question remains the strongest point of collision with Skinner and later contextualism.

## VII. Evidence ceilings and firewalls

### Secure

- Howison was Lovejoy's Berkeley philosophy teacher.
- Howison's Berkeley propaedeutic included formal logic.
- Howison authored an elementary formal-logic synopsis in 1888.
- Lovejoy explicitly used formal-logical scrutiny by 1907.
- Lovejoy directly published technical formal-logic work in 1910 and 1911.
- Ladd-Franklin directly classified Lovejoy as Russell-adjacent in 1912, polemically.
- Thalheimer was directly Lovejoy's graduate student and explicitly credits him with training in analysis.
- Thalheimer's dissertation directly defines analysis as parts-before-whole conceptual dissection and anti-confusion work.
- problem isolation was an explicit, contested philosophical-methodological issue in the 1910s.

### Strong but bounded

- Lovejoy's analytical history may have grown at the intersection of formal-logical and historical/philological proof repertoires.
- the later `unit-idea` can be investigated as an analytical unit before being treated as an ontological commitment.
- contemporaries perceived Lovejoy's logical practice as connected to modern/symbolic-logical debates.

### Open

- exact Lovejoy enrollment in Howison's formal-logic component/course at Berkeley;
- exact Lovejoy enrollment in Royce's logic course/seminar at Harvard;
- Lovejoy's direct reading date for Venn, Schröder, Russell, Peirce or other logical sources before the first explicit citations;
- whether notebook 004's 1898 relation-partition consciously drew on formal-logical training;
- whether Lovejoy retrospectively described the origin of his own analytical method in correspondence, lecture notes, or autobiographical material.

### Do not claim

- Howison directly taught Lovejoy the mature history-of-ideas method;
- Royce or Peirce transmitted a complete logical method to Lovejoy;
- Russell caused Lovejoy's analytical practice;
- 004 is `symbolic logic applied to Buddhism`;
- Lovejoy belongs straightforwardly to a later canonical analytic-philosophy camp;
- unit-ideas are merely formal-logical atoms.
