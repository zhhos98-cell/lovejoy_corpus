## Core result

The defensible novelty is narrower and stronger than either `Lovejoy studied Buddhism` or `global intellectual history should include non-Western objects`.

Daniel J. Wilson already documented Lovejoy's comparative-religion training, Sanskrit/Buddhist work with Charles R. Lanman, and Paris Buddhist reading with Sylvain Lévi. Recent Lovejoy scholarship has also complicated the standard Skinnerian caricature, and Lovejoy's mature Chinese work has not been ignored. The new archival contribution is therefore not biographical discovery or generic rehabilitation.

The strongest gap is:

> **Lovejoy's non-Western/comparative-religion formation has long been biographically visible, but his technical Indological/Buddhist scholarly practice has been weakly integrated into the methodological genealogy through which historians of ideas narrate Lovejoy and the discipline. Notebook 004 makes that practice reconstructible at proposition level.**

A complementary geographical formulation is:

> **The working prehistory of Lovejoy's historical practice was geographically wider than the later disciplinary genealogy through which intellectual historians usually encounter him.**

The essay should therefore distinguish three propositions:

1. **biography:** Lovejoy's Indian/Buddhist work was not wholly forgotten;
2. **practice:** the notebook lets us reconstruct what he actually did with sources, semantic relations, borrowing, configuration, and genealogy;
3. **disciplinary genealogy:** restoring that practice complicates an overwhelmingly intra-European/American self-history of intellectual history without claiming that Buddhism or India `originated` the discipline.

## I. Lovejoy scholarship: what is already occupied

### Daniel J. Wilson

Wilson remains the indispensable biographical control. He documents:

- comparative religion as a major secondary Harvard interest;
- Charles C. Everett, Crawford H. Toy, Lanman, Sanskrit and Buddhist sacred texts;
- Paris work with Maurice Vernes and Jean Réville;
- hoped-for Pāli/Buddhist study with Sylvain Lévi and occasional private reading with Lévi;
- the larger developmental relation between early religious/history-of-thought work and later philosophical concerns.

Wilson therefore blocks any claim that historians simply failed to know Lovejoy had studied Buddhism or comparative religion.

The project delta is that Wilson does not reconstruct notebook 004's Indological polemics and proof operations at proposition level.

### Classic Lovejoy historiography

The 1948 JHI memorial/method cluster, Philip Wiener, Lewis Feuer, Kathleen Duffin and related scholarship made philosophy, historiography, time, novelty and the mature history-of-ideas programme the dominant retrospective channels through which Lovejoy was read.

This explains the historiographical gravity but does not itself establish suppression of the Buddhist archive.

### Recent rehabilitation / anti-caricature literature

Do not compete by claiming merely that mature Lovejoy was subtler than `unit-ideas without context`.

Relevant controls include:

- Anthony Grafton on the history/institutional fortunes of the history-of-ideas project;
- Leo Catana on continuity between nineteenth-century history of philosophy and Lovejoy's actual practice, and on the rhetorical function of the mature programme;
- John Patrick Diggins on Lovejoy's broader intellectual-historical range, including Chinese material;
- Darrin McMahon on the return/recovery of history of ideas;
- Daniel Wickberg on Lovejoy as cultural history;
- Carl Knight on more exact readings of unit-ideas;
- Nico Mouton on long-range method, synchronic/diachronic analysis and the inadequacy of simple contextualist caricatures.

Therefore these are background conditions, not the intervention:

- `Skinner was wrong about Lovejoy`;
- `Lovejoy cared about context`;
- `unit-ideas were misunderstood`;
- `Lovejoy sometimes worked outside Europe`.

## II. Mature Chinese material is a necessary novelty control

Lovejoy's `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism` is a real mature non-Western node and appears in modern Lovejoy scholarship, including Diggins and Mouton.

The early Indian/Buddhist case differs in kind:

- mature Chinese work traces a European formation through Chinese sources/transmission inside an already formed history-of-ideas practice;
- the 1890s Indian case places Lovejoy **inside a technical specialist controversy over Indian philosophy before the mature disciplinary programme had stabilized**.

Safe distinction:

> **The early Indian case is not merely another example of Lovejoy looking beyond Europe; it reconstructs one working environment in which the later historian of ideas was still learning and contesting the terms of historical inference.**

---

## III. Two disciplinary afterlives of the 1898 article

Lovejoy's 1898 Buddhist article was not lost. Specialist Buddhist/Indological scholarship continued to cite it in debates over `upādi/upadhi/upādisesa`, while intellectual-history scholarship remembered Lovejoy primarily through unit-ideas, `The Great Chain of Being`, pragmatism, Romanticism and historiographical method.

Notebook 004 reconnects these separated afterlives.

Useful formulation:

> **Buddhist studies retained the article; intellectual history retained the founder. The notebook allows the two Lovejoys to be read together.**

The archival novelty is thus not recovery of an unknown publication but recovery of the **scholarly practice behind a publication whose later disciplinary lives diverged**.

---

## IV. The first-order thesis remains historical, not methodological self-celebration

The Blog's substantive thesis should stay close to Lovejoy's own 1898 position:

> **A philosophical formation could be historically composite without being merely derivative: its terms, `perhaps even the ideas`, might be borrowed while their `arrangement and application` remained `original and characteristic`.**

Against Senart's `ordre plus ou moins accidentel`, notebook 004 and the 1898 article separate:

- provenance of components;
- semantic/causal function;
- configuration;
- historical identity of the resulting system.

This is what the piece proves. The disciplinary/geographical payoff follows from where that argument was being made.

## V. Global / non-Western intellectual history: adjacent fields already exist

The essay should not claim novelty merely by moving intellectual history beyond European objects.

### Global intellectual history

The Moyn/Sartori volume and subsequent work made circulation, translation, comparison, transregional connection and multiple intellectual worlds central problems. Andrew Sartori's synthetic account, Janaki Bakhle's critique of one-way European-origin models, Shruti Kapila's claim that India can be methodologically instructive, and Sanjay Subrahmanyam's criticisms of Western-theoretical overdetermination all make `add India` or `reverse the flow` inadequate interventions.

The distinct chronological question here is:

> **Was the prehistory of the discipline's own canonical practice already geographically wider than its later self-genealogy suggests?**

### Omnia El Shakry

El Shakry's formulation that the non-West often supplies `exemplars but rarely epistemologies` is especially useful. It asks how non-Western intellectual histories might alter the contours of intellectual-history debates themselves.

The Lovejoy archive supplies a different kind of intervention: rather than recovering a non-Western analogue of a later method, it restores a non-Western scholarly field **inside the early archive of a canonical Western founder**.

### Humeira Iqtidar

Iqtidar's `Provincialising Skinnerian Contextualism` directly provincializes a methodological chronology by recovering long-standing Islamic practices of context/intention.

Our move must remain different:

> **do not claim Buddhist philology had Skinnerian contextualism first.**

Instead, the archive provincializes a **disciplinary genealogy** by showing Lovejoy's own early practice embedded in Indian philosophical controversy.

### Jonardon Ganeri

Ganeri tests contextualist methods against Sanskrit intellectual cultures and uses points of breakdown to refine method. The direction is broadly `method → Indian field → methodological adjustment`.

Our chronological arrangement is different: `pre-history-of-ideas Lovejoy → Indian controversy → later disciplinary codification`.

### Leigh Jenco

Jenco's work is useful for the stronger epistemic question of whether non-Western thought merely enlarges the archive or alters what counts as intellectual knowledge. It supports the final disciplinary question without implying direct transmission to Lovejoy.

## VI. History of philology / humanities supplies the structural bridge

The history-of-humanities literature makes the Lovejoy case much more plausible historically.

### James Turner

Turner reconstructs modern humanities disciplines as differentiating from a porous nineteenth-century philological matrix. Sanskrit, classics, comparative Indo-European grammar, biblical philology, history and literature did not begin as sealed domains.

### Devin Griffiths

Griffiths identifies the comparative method as a major cross-humanities formation and tracks tensions between genetic/historical and functional/contextual explanation across philology, anthropology, sociology, mythology, folklore, history and comparative literature.

### India/Sanskrit are constitutive, not incidental

Thomas Trautmann and related scholarship on India/Sanskrit in comparative philology and ethnology, Ian Stewart's reconstruction of the British line after Sir William Jones, and comparative histories of philology in Asia all make a sealed Europe-origin model historically inadequate.

The useful gap is therefore between two disciplinary genealogies:

> `comparative philology / Oriental scholarship / Sanskrit → formative for modern humanities`

versus

> `history of philosophy → Lovejoy → history of ideas → Skinner/new intellectual history`.

Notebook 004 provides an archival bridge through Lovejoy himself.

## VII. Classical / Oriental binary: use as boundary-making, not factual ontology

Max Müller's retrospective 1901 description of his 1869 Académie election as a contest `between classical and Oriental scholarship` is useful because it makes disciplinary boundary-making visible.

It must be qualified by institutional evidence:

- official French records confirm Müller's election but do not independently prove every detail of his retrospective Mommsen-vs-Müller final matchup;
- the 1867 Bopp succession placed Mommsen, Müller and Lepsius together on a candidate list;
- EPHE's early publication programme circulated Müller and Georg Curtius together;
- Harvard's own institutional history shows James Bradstreet Greenough teaching Sanskrit/comparative philology as Latin electives before Lanman's later Indo-Iranian specialization.

The correct story is **differentiation inside a porous philological field**, not two sealed disciplines.

---

## VIII. Transregional first-order controls: Bankim and Rajendralal Mitra

Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay (1871) distinguishes similarity from identity and leaves the direction of borrowing as a further question.

Rajendralal Mitra distinguishes antecedent philosophical materials from formation of a complete system and elsewhere rejects genealogy from phonetic resemblance without evidence `worth the name`.

No Bankim/Mitra → Lovejoy transmission is proved.

Their evidentiary function is geographical and intellectual-field control:

> **the proof problem itself was transregional; Indian scholars were participants in arguments over similarity, system-formation and historical dependence, not merely sources of data for European method.**

For the v2 Blog these should remain compact and paraphrased unless original-edition quotation is page-locked.

---

## IX. Political guardrail: widening geography does not purify the politics

Lovejoy's 1906 `Primitive Philosophy` and the wider Orientalist/evolutionist environment prevent a celebratory reading.

Technical discrimination, source criticism and relation-sensitive analysis can coexist with civilizational hierarchy and universalizing claims about `primitive` thought.

Safe formulation:

> **Recovering non-Western material in Lovejoy's formation changes the geography of the story without purifying its politics.**

Do not turn the early archive into proto-decolonial contextualism.

## X. Strong novelty statement

Long version:

> **This essay does not recover a wholly forgotten Buddhist Lovejoy. His comparative-religion training was known to his biographer, and his later Chinese work has attracted attention. What notebook 004 makes newly reconstructible is the intellectual practice inside that early Indological strand: the sources, live scholarly disputes, and proposition-by-proposition judgments through which Lovejoy treated borrowing, originality, textual usage and philosophical system before the mature history-of-ideas programme acquired its canonical form. Restoring that practice shows that the working prehistory of one canonical founder was geographically wider than the predominantly Western disciplinary genealogy through which intellectual historians later came to know him.**

Compact Blog version:

> **Indian philosophy was not a late or incidental object for a pre-existing Lovejoyian method; it belonged to some of Lovejoy's earliest sustained historical scholarship.**

Guardrail immediately after:

> **That does not make Buddhism the origin of the history of ideas or prove a causal line from Indology to unit-ideas. It identifies a documented early workshop that later disciplinary memory largely separated from the figure of `Lovejoy`.**

## XI. Claims to avoid

- `Scholars forgot Lovejoy studied Buddhism.`
- `Lovejoy anticipated Skinner/contextualism.`
- `Skinner was simply wrong.`
- `The history of ideas has a non-Western origin.`
- `India/Buddhism caused the mature unit-idea method.`
- `Bankim or Mitra influenced Lovejoy` without direct evidence.
- `Oriental scholarship was outside Western scholarship.`
- `Global intellectual history has ignored non-Western actors.`
- `Lovejoy's use of non-Western evidence makes the politics decolonial or egalitarian.`

Preferred distinction:

> **causal origin ≠ documented geography of working prehistory**.
