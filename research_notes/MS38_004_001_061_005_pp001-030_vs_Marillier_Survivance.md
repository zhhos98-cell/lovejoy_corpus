## 1. Direct Marillier baseline now secure

Marillier's complete `La survivance de l’âme et l’idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés` directly establishes:

1. belief in survivance is not originally generated by a demand for justice;
2. the other world frequently continues the living world rather than repairing its inequalities;
3. differentiated destiny can depend on rank, manner of death, rites, journey skill/strength, courage/cowardice, divine arbitrariness or chance rather than moral conduct;
4. the decisive moral criterion is whether destiny tracks the good/bad quality or moral value of earthly conduct;
5. moral-looking `good/bad` classifications can in fact be principally ritual;
6. later Christian/Indic/Islamic moral conceptions can be added or superposed upon older structures;
7. moral reward/punishment can appear in an intermediate form not yet disengaged from contrary conceptions or sharply defined;
8. analogous forms can recur with different `signification` and `portée`.

The full primary therefore replaces the older need to reconstruct Marillier pp.37/42/44 mainly through Mauss.

## 3. What Lovejoy takes over or works inside

### 3.1 Selector before moral label

The recurring question is not whether an outcome looks favorable or punitive but what relation selects it. Marillier directly supplies that burden of proof; Söderblom shows the same logic in another same-workshop student product.

### 3.2 Continuation as baseline

Earthly rank, condition, power and material/ritual qualifications can continue after death. Differentiation alone does not establish moral retribution.

### 3.3 Provenance and layering

Missionary/contact and imported Indic categories can alter a living afterlife system. Marillier directly treats new moral ideas as added/superposed to older beliefs rather than simply replacing them.

### 3.4 Serial redivision is workshop-normal

Söderblom's explicit `d’autres divisions` is the best actor-near category. Student work can share sources/problem field while imposing a different ordering and point of view.
