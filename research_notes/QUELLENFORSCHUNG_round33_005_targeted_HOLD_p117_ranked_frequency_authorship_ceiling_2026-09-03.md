## 2. Steinmetz closes the quantitative/motive-classification environment, not the exact p.117 result

Rudolf Steinmetz's *Endokannibalismus* (1896) is an immediate pre-Paris comparative source organized around:

- classified motives for cannibalism;
- tabular/comparative evidence across peoples;
- explicit attention to the numerical distribution of cases;
- source reliability and the interpretation of ethnographic reports.

Contemporary review literature confirms that the book used a tabular exhibit of tribes, motives, and source reliability. Thus an argument of the form

`CLASSIFY CASES BY MOTIVE -> COMPARE FREQUENCIES -> RESTRICT A GENERAL THEORY`

is not methodologically novel in Lovejoy's notebook.

### Mauss's 1896 control is especially important

In his 1896 critique of Steinmetz, Marcel Mauss explicitly objects that statistics made under overly broad rubrics prove little and says that the same problem appears in *Endokannibalismus*. This establishes that **how one bins cases before counting them** was already an explicit methodological controversy in the exact field surrounding p.117.

Therefore p.117 cannot be promoted to Lovejoy originality merely because it performs comparative frequency ranking.

Calibration:

- quantitative comparative ranking as an operator: F/A0–A1;
- motive classification before frequency comparison: Steinmetz/Mauss field A0–A1;
- exact p.117 ordering: still unresolved.
