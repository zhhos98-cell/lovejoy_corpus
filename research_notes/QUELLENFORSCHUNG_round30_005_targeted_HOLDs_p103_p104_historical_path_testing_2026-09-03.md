## 2. The economic/ethnographic premise is upstream

The proposition that agriculture does not everywhere require prior pastoralism, developed animal domestication, or draught animals belongs to the late-nineteenth-century social-evolution / ethnographic field.

Herbert Spencer's 1898 *Principles of Sociology*, Part VIII, is a particularly close contemporary control. Spencer explicitly says social progress is not linear but `divergent and re-divergent` and surveys African cases in which cultivation proceeds without draught animals or plough agriculture. His examples include West-African / Coast-negro and Congo agriculture performed with hoes or simple soil preparation and no draught animals, alongside more complex mixed agricultural-pastoral cases.

Spencer therefore supplies precisely the sort of comparative economic fact Lovejoy invokes:

`AGRICULTURE != UNIVERSALLY DEPENDENT ON PRIOR DRAUGHT-ANIMAL DOMESTICATION`.

This fact is not a Lovejoy discovery and must remain A0–A1.

The same general issue appears throughout contemporary debates over hunting, pastoral, and agricultural stages. No priority claim is licensed for the economic premise.
