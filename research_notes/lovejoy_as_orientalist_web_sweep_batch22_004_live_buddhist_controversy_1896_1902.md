# Lovejoy as Orientalist / comparative religion — web sweep batch 22

Date: 2026-08-18  
Focus: move the `MS38_004_001_061_004` line forward through context rather than further manuscript reading: the live Sāṁkhya/Buddhism and dependent-origination controversy of 1896–97, then the contraction/re-expansion from notebook 004 to the 1898 JAOS article and the 1902 category-history paper.

## Core finding

Notebook 004 should no longer be contextualized only as a private Harvard research notebook that happened to yield a narrow 1898 philological article. Its broad problems — Sāṁkhya/Buddhism priority, the historical status and internal logic of the twelve nidānas, borrowed terminology, the relation of dependent origination to Buddhist psychology, and the possibility of recovering coherence from a composite formula — sit inside a conspicuously live international scholarly controversy concentrated in **1896–97**.

The strongest contextual evidence is not retrospective historiography but contemporary scholarly publication. Richard Garbe and Hermann Jacobi were arguing for a strong Sāṁkhya-Yoga priority/source relation to Buddhism; Émile Senart was reopening the interpretation and historical composition of the twelve nidānas; Louis de La Vallée Poussin’s 1897 JRAS note explicitly describes `pratītyasamutpāda` as a difficult, primary Buddhist formula that had repeatedly attracted interpretation and places **Warren, Jacobi and Senart in the same current bibliography**. This constellation is unusually close to the source/problem field that 004 itself records.

The result is a better historical model:

> **live 1896–97 controversy over Sāṁkhya/Buddhism, dependent origination and doctrinal composition**  
> ↓  
> **004 as a broad analytical notebook testing chronology, borrowing, classification and coherence**  
> ↓  
> **1898 JAOS as a deliberately narrowed technical intervention on `upādāna/upādisesa`**  
> ↓  
> **1901 Buddhism syllabus as an intermediate teaching condensation**  
> ↓  
> **1902 “Religion and the Time-Process” as re-expansion into comparative history of a category, with an explicit authorial reference back to the 1898 paper**

This is more precise than a simple `004 → article` genealogy. The notebook belongs to a wider problem-field; the 1898 paper contracts that field around two terms; the 1902 paper re-expands the analytical result into a history of categories and logical motives.

## 1. Garbe and Jacobi: Sāṁkhya/Buddhism priority was a live problem, not an eccentric notebook tangent

Richard Garbe’s **_Sāṃkhya und Yoga_** (Strassburg, 1896) states in strong terms that Sāṁkhya belongs before Buddhism and treats it as a major source for the theoretical side of Buddhism. In the same discussion Garbe explicitly cites Hermann Jacobi’s newly published **“Der Ursprung des Buddhismus aus dem Sāṁkhya-Yoga”**.

Jacobi’s paper appeared in the Göttingen academy _Nachrichten_ in 1896, pp. 43–58. A full digitized volume is publicly available (Internet Archive identifier `nachrichtenvond09klasgoog`; HathiTrust record `000517694`).

This matters for 004 because the notebook’s extensive attention to priority, possible borrowing and the danger of projecting a later systematic Sāṁkhya backward is now best read against an active philological-historical dispute. The notebook is not simply collecting “Indian philosophy” background before getting to Buddhism. It is working inside a contested genealogy.

### Evidentiary limit

No direct evidence yet shows that Lovejoy read Jacobi’s paper itself rather than encountering the problem through Garbe, Senart, Lanman/Warren, or the wider debate. The claim at this stage is **problem-field contemporaneity**, not a demonstrated Jacobi→Lovejoy line.

## 2. Senart 1896: the twelve nidānas themselves were under historical reconstruction

Émile Senart’s **“À propos de la théorie bouddhique des douze nidânas”** appeared in the 1896 _Mélanges Charles de Harlez_ (Leiden: Brill). Bibliographic sources disagree by roughly a page on the starting pagination (280/281), so the exact first page should be verified from the scan before canonical citation.

Senart’s argument is already known to be the immediate polemical target of Lovejoy’s 1898 article. Contextually, however, it does more work: it places the notebook’s questions about duplication, sequence, heterogeneous categories and historical accretion inside a fresh controversy over whether the nidāna formula is a coherent Buddhist construction or a composite accumulation whose order lacks strict temporal/logical unity.

The notebook–article pair therefore contains two separable historical operations:

1. **source/genealogy problem** — where do terms and component ideas come from, and what is their relative chronology?;
2. **system/intelligibility problem** — even if terms or ideas are inherited and the formula is heterogeneous, does their arrangement perform a distinctive Buddhist explanatory function?

The second operation is what the 1898 article makes especially explicit when it allows borrowing while defending an original/characteristic arrangement and application.

## 3. La Vallée Poussin 1897 gives a contemporary map of the controversy

Louis de La Vallée Poussin, **“The Buddhist ‘Wheel of Life’ from a New Source,”** _Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society_ 29.3 (July 1897), 463–470, DOI `10.1017/S0035869X00024680`, is the most useful contextual control found in this batch.

At the opening of his discussion he treats `pratītyasamutpāda` as a difficult but primary formula of early Buddhism that had already generated repeated interpretation. His notes then place in one compact current bibliography:

- Burnouf;
- Kern;
- Oldenberg;
- Henry Clarke Warren;
- Hermann Jacobi’s 1896 Sāṁkhya-Yoga/Buddhism essay;
- Émile Senart’s 1896 article on the twelve nidānas.

That list closely reproduces the intellectual neighborhood of 004. It is therefore strong contemporary evidence that Lovejoy’s notebook source cluster was not idiosyncratic accumulation. He was working through the same current interpretive nodes visible in a specialist Buddhist publication of 1897.

### Historiographical payoff

This changes how the breadth of 004 should be described. The broad notebook is not merely “material omitted from the final paper.” Much of that breadth is the **controversy-space required to make the narrow paper possible**: priority, borrowing, compositional history, logical order, psychological classification and the meaning of the nidānas are mutually entangled in the 1896–97 literature.

## 4. Harvard makes this debate locally accessible

The external controversy fits Lovejoy’s documented Harvard formation unusually well:

- Charles R. Lanman taught him Sanskrit and sacred Buddhist texts;
- Charles C. Everett taught comparative religion, including Vedic religion, Hinduism and Buddhism;
- Henry Clarke Warren’s **_Buddhism in Translations_** appeared in the Harvard Oriental Series in 1896 and is heavily used in 004/1898;
- Warren’s volume was dedicated to Lanman;
- Harvard/Cambridge also possessed a wider public comparative-religion ecology in which Vedānta and Buddhism were being presented and debated.

The important contextual claim is therefore institutional and bibliographic, not biographical speculation: Lovejoy was training in a place where a new Harvard Buddhist source-book and specialist Sanskrit instruction met exactly the international controversies he records in 004.

Two high-value controls remain:

1. recover the exact 1896–97 Lanman course description and determine whether Warren’s new volume or the nidāna controversy entered assigned work;
2. map every Warren citation in 004/1898 against the structure of _Buddhism in Translations_ to see whether Warren functioned mainly as a translation reservoir or as an argumentative scaffold.

## 5. The 1898 publication is best understood as contraction, not culmination

The existing 004→1898 cross-read already establishes that the article is far narrower than the notebook. The contextual material found here suggests a stronger interpretation of that narrowing.

The 1898 article does not simply represent the notebook becoming more mature. It is a **genre transformation** from broad research within a live genealogy/composition controversy to a publishable technical intervention whose burden of proof is limited to two terms.

What survives the contraction is methodologically revealing:

- separate the object/condition of attachment from attachment itself;
- distinguish causal/logical function from strict temporal sequence;
- admit borrowed or inherited terminology without treating the resulting system as an accidental heap;
- resolve a local philological problem by reconstructing the explanatory function of the larger formula;
- preserve contrary passages and end provisionally where the textual evidence remains mixed.

What is pruned includes most of the open Sāṁkhya/Buddhism chronology, the large psychological taxonomy, and the notebook’s stronger original/derived historical stratification.

Thus 1898 should be read as **selective extraction from controversy-space**, not as an exhaustive statement of what Lovejoy had been doing in 004.

## 6. 1901 is an intermediate teaching node, though primary evidence remains missing

Wilson’s bibliography records **“Syllabus: The Philosophy of Buddhism,” Washington University Association, 1901**, organized as four lectures:

1. the place of Buddhism among the philosophies of India;
2. and 3. the two essential formulas of Buddhism;
4. Buddhist ethics and its goal.

This is highly suggestive for the 004 line because the structure resembles the notebook/article emphasis on the relation of Buddhism to Indian philosophy plus a theoretical core organized around a small number of formulas. It also lies exactly between the 1898 paper and the 1902 category-history essay.

The syllabus itself has still not been recovered. Therefore it should remain a **bibliographically confirmed continuity node**, not yet a source for detailed claims about lecture content beyond Wilson’s titles.

Priority: locate the Washington University Association syllabus or _Bulletin_ copy and test whether the “two essential formulas” are dependent origination plus the Three Characteristics, as the 004 closing outline and 1898 article would lead us to predict. That identification is currently a hypothesis.

## 7. 1902 is re-expansion, with an explicit authorial bridge

Lovejoy’s **“Religion and the Time-Process,”** _The American Journal of Theology_ 6 (1902), 439–472, changes the scale again. The full primary text has already been recovered.

The paper begins with changing **categories** in the history of philosophical and theological opinion. Vedānta and Buddhism form the first major analytic case. Lovejoy treats impermanence and dependent origination as central to the Buddhist negative valuation of temporal process and, at the relevant passage, explicitly refers readers back to his own 1898 `upādāna/upādisesa` article.

This creates a secure authorial chain:

> **1898 specialist philology was reused by Lovejoy himself as a component in a 1902 history of a category.**

The scale shift is important. In 1898 the question is what two Buddhist technical terms do inside the nidāna structure. In 1902 the object becomes a trans-traditional conceptual presupposition: the valuation of time/becoming, compared across Indian, Greek, Christian and modern philosophical formations.

The 1902 paper also sharpens a second operation already latent in 004. Lovejoy refuses to infer historical borrowing from conceptual resemblance between Aristotle and Vedānta where an internal Greek genealogy can explain the result. Thus **resemblance, historical transmission and logical recurrence become analytically distinct questions**.

## 8. Revised 004-route thesis

The strongest formulation now is not that notebook 004 already contains the mature history-of-ideas method. It is narrower and better evidenced:

**Between the live Buddhist/Sāṁkhya controversies of 1896–97, notebook 004, the 1898 JAOS article and the 1902 “Religion and the Time-Process,” Lovejoy repeatedly separates historical genealogy from logical function: borrowed or composite materials can acquire a distinctive arrangement; chronological priority does not by itself determine conceptual role; and structural resemblance does not by itself prove transmission. The 1902 history of categories makes explicit an analytical practice whose earlier laboratory can be observed in 004 and its 1898 extraction.**

This complements, rather than duplicates, the 005 line. The two notebooks appear to foreground different but related operations:

- **004:** genealogy / semantic discrimination / composition / logical intelligibility;
- **005:** source criticism / determinant / causal mechanism / reclassification.

The later history-of-ideas practice can eventually be tested as a redistribution of both sets of operations, without treating either notebook as a simple embryonic `unit-idea` method.

## 9. Evidence limits after batch 22

**Secure**
- 004’s close relation to the 1898 JAOS paper.
- Senart 1896 and Warren 1896 belong directly to the notebook/article source constellation.
- Garbe/Jacobi/Senart show a live 1896 controversy over Sāṁkhya/Buddhism and nidāna interpretation.
- La Vallée Poussin 1897 independently places Warren, Jacobi and Senart inside one current specialist discussion of dependent origination.
- Lovejoy’s 1902 paper explicitly refers back to the 1898 Buddhist article.

**Strong contextual inference**
- The broad opening and middle sections of 004 are best understood as participation in the controversy’s problem-field rather than irrelevant preliminary reading.
- The 1898 article is a selective contraction from that wider field.

**Still unproved**
- exact dates of individual 004 entries;
- direct Lovejoy reading of Jacobi 1896;
- exact role of Lanman in directing the specific 004 research problem;
- whether Warren’s 1896 volume was formally assigned to Lovejoy;
- primary text of the 1901 Washington University Buddhism syllabus;
- immediate 1898–1905 reception of Lovejoy’s JAOS argument.

Modern scholarship continues to cite Lovejoy in the `upādi/upādisesa` controversy, which establishes a disciplinary afterlife but should not be substituted for evidence of immediate reception.
