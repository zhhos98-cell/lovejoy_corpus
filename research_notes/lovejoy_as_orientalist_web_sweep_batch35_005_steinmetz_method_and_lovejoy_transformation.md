## Research move

Batch 34 established the exact-title bridge:

> Steinmetz, *Endokannibalismus* (1896) → Marillier review → Mauss use → Lovejoy 005 p.62 bibliography.

The next question is more important than provenance alone:

> **What sort of comparative operation did `Endokannibalismus` represent, and what does 005 do differently?**

A contemporary American review by W. I. Thomas supplies a concise synopsis of Steinmetz’s method and thesis. Read together with Mauss’s 1896 critique and the cleaned 005 pages, it reveals a specific transformation rather than simple influence.

The emerging result:

> **Lovejoy appears to retain the Steinmetz-era demand for exhaustive comparative evidence, explicit source reliability and rival motives, while moving against a single universal origin story. In 005 the unit of comparison becomes the mechanism that produces a rite, not the rite’s outward form.**

## 1. Steinmetz’s method as described by W. I. Thomas, 1897

W. I. Thomas reviewed *Endokannibalismus* in the *American Journal of Sociology* 2 (1897), pp.610–611.

Thomas says Steinmetz’s study contains:

- a comparative description of cannibalism within the tribe;
- a **tabular exhibit** of tribes practising endocannibalism;
- the **motives assigned** for the practice;
- the **reliability of the information in each case**.

He summarizes Steinmetz’s motive-list as including:

- lack of food;
- desire for meat / relish for human flesh;
- animistic belief.

The objects of the practice are grouped as women, children, invalids, the aged and criminals.

Thomas’s review is electronically reproduced here:
https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Thomas/Thomas_1897d.html

## 2. The crucial tension inside Steinmetz: plural motives, singular origin

Steinmetz’s apparatus is pluralistic at the level of cases and motives, but his developmental conclusion is much more unilateral.

Thomas reports the argument that primitive humans were necessarily omnivorous and lacked the aesthetic, religious and sympathetic inhibitions that later prevented eating the dead. The decisive historical claim is that **hunger precedes superstition**: superstition could not have generated cannibalism unless periodic hunger had produced the practice earlier.

Thus the structure is:

> **many observed motives / many case qualities**
> → **one primitive naturalistic origin**.

This is exactly the kind of structure Lovejoy 005 later strains against.

## 3. Thomas praises the source-critical apparatus even while withholding assent to conclusions

The final paragraph of Thomas’s review is unexpectedly relevant to the 005 paper. He says ethnological data are unusually difficult to manage because sources are unreliable and the material is vast. He contrasts Steinmetz with comparative writers who select only facts that support their own opinions.

Thomas explicitly allows that Steinmetz’s conclusions may not all be valid while praising him for presenting practically all the relevant facts and calls the article a model of ethnological research.

This gives us a useful separation:

- **methodological virtue:** expose evidence, counter-evidence, motives and source reliability;
- **theoretical vulnerability:** force the evidence into a universal developmental story.

That separation is very close to the operation visible in 005.

## 4. Mauss 1896 attacks exactly the vulnerable point

Mauss’s 1896 Steinmetz review repeatedly praises the scale of the factual work while attacking the explanatory generalization.

Relevant criticisms include:

- broad statistical rubrics prove little when distinct religious institutions are collapsed together;
- Steinmetz at times relies too heavily on indigenous or ethnographer interpretations;
- funerary customs and cults of the dead must be distinguished;
- the same apparent practice can arise through different social/religious relations;
- endocannibalism, funerary human sacrifice, substitution, magical efficacy and communion cannot simply be read as one developmental line.

Mauss explicitly says that the same problem of overconfidence in interpretations occurs in *Endokannibalismus*.

This means the exact book entered in Lovejoy’s p.62 bibliography was already embedded in a **live methodological dispute about how comparative evidence should be classified**.

## 5. 005 preserves Steinmetz’s evidentiary discipline but radicalizes mechanism separation

Lovejoy’s later cannibalism pages are striking when put beside Thomas’s synopsis of Steinmetz.

The notebook repeatedly refuses to infer religious meaning from outward form:

- repeated or customary anthropophagy need not be sacramental;
- public feast, ceremony or fixed ritual rule does not prove mystical communion;
- eating flesh may reflect famine, revenge, affection, medicine, acquisition of qualities, magical transfer, propitiation or communion;
- eating a particular organ can work through transferable efficacy rather than communion;
- companion-killing at burial can be provision of servants / communication with the dead;
- casting a victim to a water spirit can be propitiation of danger;
- ritual killing of a specialist can be punishment for failed performance, not sacrifice;
- circumcision is explicitly rejected as a reduced human sacrifice and analyzed through initiation, blood and incorporation;
- animal sacrifice cannot be universally derived from substitution for an earlier human victim.

Thus Lovejoy retains a **multiple-motive table**, in effect, but denies that the table licenses a single origin.

## 6. The key unit of analysis shifts from `practice` to `mechanism`

Steinmetz begins with a named practice: `endocannibalism`.

Lovejoy’s notebooks repeatedly ask what actually makes a case what it is:

- what relation connects act and effect?
- does efficacy lie in an indwelling spirit, an intrinsic property, contact, blood, similarity, direct action on nature, social incorporation, contractual exchange, propitiation or communion?
- is the visible rite inherited while its explanation changes?
- are several historically distinct motives now syncretically fused?

The object of comparison therefore shifts:

> **same rite across peoples**

becomes

> **same or different causal mechanisms beneath superficially similar rites**.

This is probably the strongest methodological bridge between 005 and the 1906 `Primitive Energetics` paper.

## 7. Source criticism and classification become one operation

The early 005 future-life section already performs this move before the sacrifice bibliography appears.

On pp.16–19 Lovejoy separates:

- manner of death from moral desert;
- taboo/custom from ethical guilt;
- clan sanction from personal punishment;
- missionary contamination from older ethnographic layers.

Later sacrifice/cannibalism pages apply the same procedure to ritual mechanism.

So the longitudinal coherence of 005 can be stated as:

> **the source must first be historically criticized, then the observed form must be decomposed by mechanism, and only then can a comparative/developmental claim be made.**

This is more precise than saying that Lovejoy was simply anti-evolutionist. He uses developmental arguments repeatedly; what he resists is development inferred from formal resemblance alone.

## 8. This also explains Lovejoy’s repeated interest in `syncretism`

005 p.65 explicitly says that several sacrificial motives can become mixed and that syncretism may follow a period in which the sacrifices were distinct in character.

That claim supplies the historical counterpart to mechanism analysis:

- a present rite may contain several mechanisms;
- those mechanisms need not have the same origin;
- ritual form can persist while explanation/function changes;
- therefore classification is simultaneously reconstruction of historical layers.

This is the same operation visible in the future-life pages when moral meanings infiltrate older non-moral eschatologies.

## 9. A more exact genealogy than `French sociology influenced Lovejoy`

The current chain can now be rendered as an argument about scholarly operations:

> **Steinmetz:** exhaustive comparative tables + source reliability + motive coding, but strong universal-origin conclusions
>
> **Marillier / Mauss controversy:** criticism of broad rubrics, source interpretation and unilateral causal histories; taboo, clan solidarity and ritual mechanism separated
>
> **Lovejoy 005:** same evidence-dense comparative workflow, but repeated decomposition of each practice into distinct mechanisms and historical layers
>
> **Lovejoy 1906:** one mechanism class, impersonal / intrinsic / transferable causal efficacy, becomes a portable comparative abstraction.

This is a materially recoverable intellectual history. It does not require us to project mature `history of ideas` terminology into the 1890s.

## 10. Paper-level formulation

A defensible paragraph-level claim:

> **The exact appearance of Steinmetz’s *Endokannibalismus* in Lovejoy’s sacrifice bibliography matters less as proof of doctrinal borrowing than as evidence of the comparative problem-space in which the notebook worked. Contemporary readers praised Steinmetz for tabulating cases, motives and the reliability of evidence, while critics such as Mauss objected to explanations built from overly broad rubrics. Lovejoy’s notes preserve the evidentiary discipline but repeatedly refuse the inference from common form to common origin: cannibalism, killing, blood-use and sacrifice are decomposed into funerary, magical, social, propitiatory and sacramental mechanisms whose histories may diverge or later become syncretically fused.**

## 11. Evidence limits

1. Thomas’s review is an external contemporary synopsis; direct Steinmetz collation remains necessary for exact wording and table structure.
2. Marillier’s own pp.113–115 review remains the most valuable missing text because it may reveal precisely which parts of Steinmetz he approved or rejected before Lovejoy’s Paris year.
3. We should not assume Lovejoy read Thomas.
4. We should not call Lovejoy anti-evolutionist; 005 repeatedly builds developmental hypotheses but insists on mechanism and source criticism.
5. Scope remains 005 only.
