## Core result

The strongest cross-document continuity is not generic `source criticism`, skepticism, or even comparison. It is a recurrent **partition of proof burdens**.

Across four objects the evidentiary nouns change, but the logical structure remains recognizable:

### 004 — philological / doctrinal object

`textual witness`
→ `semantic relation`
→ `doctrinal / causal function`
→ `historical dependence, composition, borrowing or textual genealogy`.

### 005 — ethnographic / comparative-religion object

`reporter / document / contact history`
→ `comparative class or formal relation`
→ `operative social / ritual / magical mechanism`
→ `origin, survival, borrowing, substitution or developmental genealogy`.

### 1902 — comparative intellectual-history object

`cross-cultural or doctrinal affinity`
→ `logical motive / presupposition`
→ `internal versus external genealogy`
→ `historical combination, survival, and conflict inside a later system`.

### 1906 — published comparative causal category

`lexical / grammatical / native-semantic existence control`
→ `statement-specific source competence`
→ `independent evidence for the proposed mechanism as a vera causa`
→ `return to customs as applications and reciprocal corroboration`
→ `cross-regional generalization under unequal evidentiary resolution`.

The stable rule is therefore:

> **A stronger proposition does not automatically inherit the evidence that established a weaker proposition.**

A report may be usable without proving an indigenous genealogy. A formal similarity may support comparison without proving common mechanism. A mechanism may be plausible without proving a common historical origin. An obvious affinity may remain real while an influence claim fails. A causal abstraction may organize rites only after its existence is independently evidenced. Even then, the resulting synthesis may outrun the evidentiary resolution available in each region.

This is the proof architecture that the older batch chain was progressively discovering.

## I. Why `source criticism` is too weak a description

005 certainly contains source criticism, but direct period controls show that source criticism itself was already widespread in the materials around Lovejoy.

### Keating

Lovejoy's Dakota future-life locus is William H. Keating, vol. I, pp.393–394, not the earlier misread p.343. Keating himself warns that Dakota evidence may be less contaminated because of limited missionary intercourse while immediately acknowledging that Christian doctrines may nevertheless have entered the reported beliefs. Elsewhere, pp.111–112, Keating explicitly says missionary and white contact can make original doctrine difficult to recover.

The important historical correction is therefore:

> missionary-contact criticism is not uniquely Lovejoyan and need not be attributed to Marillier.

Lovejoy's downstream operation is more specific: he refuses to let reported reward/punishment, heaven/hell, suicide, chastity or manner-of-death distinctions automatically prove one moral eschatology. The same dossier is redistributed among moral desert, taboo, social obligation, manner of death, and borrowed moralization.

### Jarvis

Samuel Farmar Jarvis's 1819/1820 discourse criticizes observer bias, speculative theories of American origins, and the coloring of reports by European modes of thought while still advancing very strong universal claims about retributive immortality.

This gives a clean period control:

`source-bias criticism`
≠
`restricted universal inference`.

Jarvis remains a chronological/problem-field control; the unresolved `1819` note in 005 must not be silently identified with him without manuscript proof.

### Brinton

Daniel G. Brinton explicitly treats comparative mythology as dependent on careful selection of authorities and assignment of relative evidentiary weight. Yet Brinton also constructs large comparative and developmental arguments. Again, source ranking itself does not determine the permitted scale of inference.

### Marshall / Breeks

005 p.26 visibly marks the Toda/Nilgiri authorities as poor or weak while still retaining them in the comparison. The cluster is securely associated with William E. Marshall and J. W. Breeks, even though the compressed handwriting around `poor authority/authorities` should not be quoted diplomatically without the page image.

The notebook therefore treats bibliography as a weighted evidence set rather than a binary accepted/rejected corpus.

The resulting historiographical shift is:

> **The distinctive question is not whether Lovejoy criticized sources, but how he allocated different inferential licenses to different claims drawn from those sources.**

## II. 005: question-relative evidentiary license

A four-level reconstruction works across the notebook.

### L1 — witness admissibility

Can the report enter the evidence set at all, and with what weight?

Relevant variables include:

- reporter competence;
- date;
- missionary/contact history;
- possibility of borrowing;
- translation;
- internal contradiction;
- explicit notes of weak authority.

### L2 — classificatory admissibility

Can the reported phenomenon legitimately be compared with other cases?

A differentiated afterlife, ritual killing, blood use, first-fruit rite, cannibal practice, bodily injury, sacred object, or sacrificial act can often remain inside a comparative class even when stronger conclusions fail.

### L3 — mechanism inference

What relation actually makes the phenomenon work?

Candidate mechanisms include:

- moral desert;
- taboo/interdict;
- social or clan obligation;
- manner of death;
- provision or message-bearing for the dead;
- propitiation;
- incorporation;
- sacramental union;
- indwelling spirit;
- intrinsic/direct efficacy;
- sympathetic transfer;
- purification;
- fertility/fecundation;
- punishment or status transition.

### L4 — genealogy / development

Does the case establish borrowing, survival, substitution, common origin, a universal primitive stage, or a necessary historical sequence?

This level bears the highest burden.

The recurrent state is asymmetric:

> `admissible at L1` → `useful at L2` → `plausible/contested at L3` → `insufficient at L4`.

The notebook does not solve overgeneralization by abandoning comparison. It repeatedly **keeps the comparison while withdrawing the stronger inference**.

## III. 005 notebook-wide controls

The four-level model is not local to one section.

### Future life, pp.16–28

Differentiated destinations are real comparative data, but destination is not identical with the criterion of destination. A heaven/hell, bridge, trial, special village or bad region may be determined by ritual, bodily, social or manner-of-death variables rather than moral desert. Contact/borrowing can further complicate genealogy.

### Sacrifice, pp.61–76

Present sacrifice can be syncretic. Funerary killing can serve provision, message-bearing, status continuity, appeasement or communion. A ritualized killing can be punishment rather than sacrifice. Victim species and ceremoniality have low diagnostic value by themselves.

### Blood, fetish, charm and body parts, pp.82–90

The same substance or object can cross mechanisms:

- incorporation;
- protection;
- curing/rejuvenation;
- transfer of intelligence/strength/power;
- spirit-mediated fetish efficacy;
- intrinsic charm/amulet efficacy.

`same material` therefore does not establish `same religious relation`.

### Agrarian rites, pp.91–105

Offering-like forms can function through purification, taboo, renewal, propitiation or quasi-mechanical fecundative transfer. Developmental claims are tested against independent chronology. The agriculture/domestication discussion explicitly exposes a circular developmental sequence when typological order is made to generate the historical order it is then supposed to prove.

### Cannibalism, pp.106–111

Publicity, solemnity and rule-bound ceremoniality do not establish sacrament. Rival mechanisms include famine, revenge, affection, transfer of qualities, magic and propitiation. Frequency of analogy is not frequency of demonstrated historical transition.

### Initiation, pp.112–117

Circumcision, cutting, whipping and scarification can remain comparable while a partial-human-sacrifice genealogy fails. Incorporation, purification, ordeal, fortification, puberty/status transition and group discipline remain distinct mechanisms.

### Final selected-body-part material, p.119

A transfer mechanism may be plausible while exact historical origin remains unrecoverable. The notebook can terminate in underdetermination rather than forcing a genealogy.

The practical rule is:

> **comparison is cheaper than mechanism; mechanism is cheaper than genealogy.**

This is analytical shorthand, not Lovejoy's terminology.

## IV. Paris controls: calibrated proof existed in the field

The Paris claim should not be framed as `Paris taught Lovejoy to be critical`.

### Marillier 1888 — document classes

Marillier's early EPHE programme distinguished travellers' narratives, missionary reports, official inquiries and related testimony classes and applied different critical rules to different document types. This supplies an institutional analogue to 005's reporter/contact weighting.

### Marillier 1897–98 — bounded inference

The period Marillier material reconstructed in the older proof-threshold batches supplies a more specific control than generic pluralism:

- a local survival can support a bounded local history without making totemism a necessary universal stage;
- a shared animistic/magical substrate does not establish real institutional or genealogical kinship;
- a sacred animal does not logically require former totemic status.

Where exact Marillier wording was originally recovered through later quotation or contemporary relay rather than direct facsimile, that evidentiary grade remains part of the claim. Later direct-primary returns strengthen Marillier's broader relation-specific approach but should not retroactively convert every 1897 page quotation into direct facsimile evidence.

### Hubert–Mauss 1899

Their methodological criticism is even more explicit: the error is to arrange facts **genealogically from perceived analogy**. They separate logical analysis from historical anteriority, reject apparent simplicity as proof of priority, and emphasize that similar ritual forms can have contrary functions.

Their later 1909 retrospective formalizes an even steeper ladder: compatibility does not prove primitiveness; former clan structure does not prove the sacrifice itself reaches back to a totemic period; ritual continuity must be independently demonstrated.

The 1909 formulation is a later continuity/control, not evidence Lovejoy encountered that later wording in 1898–99.

The strongest Paris-field statement is therefore:

> **The late-1890s comparative-religion environment calibrated what analogy could prove. Resemblance could license comparison before it licensed mechanism, priority, continuity, or common origin.**

This is methodological ecology, not one-to-one transmission.

## V. 004: the same architecture already operates on textual evidence

The continuity is not that 004 always rejects genealogy. It does not. Lovejoy sometimes advances compositional, borrowing or Sāṃkhya-related historical hypotheses.

The continuity is that the levels remain separable.

### Textual witness

Canonical passage, recension/version, commentary, translation and textual scope constrain what semantic and historical propositions can be drawn.

### Semantic relation

The `upādāna` material distinguishes attachment from the skandhas that are objects of attachment. Usage, not mere morphology or compound form, controls the relation.

### Doctrinal / causal function

Hardy's two-factor rebirth statement is transformed into a causal-allocation problem: `upādāna` can explain the fact of continued existence while karma explains the particular manner/condition of rebirth.

### Historical dependence / genealogy

The Nidāna/Sāṃkhya block can move into stronger compositional and external-tradition hypotheses, but those are additional propositions built after semantic and functional analysis rather than synonyms for resemblance.

### `upādisesa` as the cleanest asymmetry

Contextual usage can support an ethical/doctrinal interpretation while etymology, contradictory passages, scribal history and final textual genealogy remain unsettled. The 1898 article explicitly says etymological possibilities do not settle meaning and that relevant evidence need not be conclusive.

Thus:

> **L4 may be accepted or rejected, but it must be argued as L4.**

This is why the 004→005 continuity is better described as proof-level separation than genealogy skepticism.

## VI. Chronology: Harvard-to-Paris is an overlap zone, not a clean handoff

Chronology blocks a simplistic before/after influence story.

The 1898 Buddhist article appears in **JAOS 19, Second Half (1898)**, with serial metadata placing the half broadly in **July–December 1898**. Lovejoy is officially an active participant in Marillier's EPHE work in the **1898–99 academic year**.

The publication window and Paris institutional interval overlap too much to prove that every published Buddhist argument was finalized before Paris.

At the same time, notebook 004 preserves a real prepublication working apparatus feeding the article. Therefore the project does not need a false sequence:

`completed Harvard method` → `Paris influence`.

The safer chronology is:

`004 prepublication philological apparatus`
→ `JAOS article issued in the second half of 1898`
↔ `1898–99 Paris / Marillier active participation`
→ `005 comparative-religion application`
→ `1902 published scale expansion`.

Hubert–Mauss belongs to the same immediate Paris field, but ambiguity in the exact printing chronology of *L'Année sociologique* II and lack of a reading record prohibit a claim that Lovejoy necessarily used the printed essay while writing a particular 005 leaf.

Compact formulation:

> **The Harvard-to-Paris transition is an overlap zone, not a handoff.**

## VII. 1902: proof partition becomes published comparative intellectual history

`Religion and the Time-Process` (1902) is the decisive post-Paris bridge.

### Lovejoy self-links the Buddhist work

The Buddhist section explicitly directs readers back to his earlier technical paper on `upādāna` and `upādisesa`. The printed footnote gives the wrong Roman numeral (`XVIII` rather than actual volume 19), but the title makes the identity secure. The miscitation is in the printed article, not an OCR invention.

This is an authorial bridge from technical Buddhology to the larger 1902 argument.

### Buddhism and Platonism

Lovejoy identifies a related `logical essence` while explicitly preserving difference of form.

Therefore:

`logical comparability`
≠
`formal identity`.

### Aristotle and Vedānta

The affinity is called obvious, but an Oriental-influence hypothesis is rejected as gratuitous because Lovejoy believes the Greek position can be generated through an internal Platonic genealogy.

Therefore:

`strong affinity`
≠
`historical transmission`.

An internally sufficient genealogy reduces the explanatory need for diffusion unless independent evidence chooses it.

### Neoplatonism

Temperament, mystical tone and rhetoric are separated from the logical import of the ideas. The historian must specify **which feature** is being genealogized.

### Developed Christianity

Christianity is reconstructed as a conjunction of Judeo-Christian and Hellenic materials with partially opposed consequences for temporal history and perfection. A system is therefore a recombination, not an indivisible historical unit.

### Royce

Lovejoy describes an ancient perfectionist presupposition as surviving inside modern idealism and calls the resulting configuration one of `warring categories`.

The 1902 scale change is substantial:

> `affinity` → `logical motive` → `competing genealogy` → `historical combination / survival`.

This should not be redescribed as the mature unit-idea programme. It is direct evidence for layered historical decomposition before the later terminology.

## VIII. 1906 proof ideal: hypothesis → independent warrant → reciprocal corroboration

`The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` makes the proof architecture unusually explicit.

### p.359 — theory, verification, qualification

Lovejoy presents the proposed category broadly while acknowledging that evidence must verify it and that qualifications will be required. Conceptual coverage is not treated as self-validating proof.

### p.362 — a first evidentiary stage

He defines the first question as whether the peoples under study possess vocabulary expressing a universal, impersonal, communicable energy. The article explicitly presents this lexical/linguistic stage before the fuller analysis of customs and rites.

### p.377 — `vera causa`

This is the crucial locus. Lovejoy says that when he later connects many customs and beliefs to the conception, he will at least be dealing with a **vera causa** because the idea is not merely inferred from the customs it is then used to explain; its existence can be shown by independent evidence.

This gives an anti-circularity rule:

> **Do not construct an explanatory entity out of a morphology and then cite the same morphology as proof that the entity exists.**

### Reciprocal corroboration

Language and comparative custom are not simply ranked first/second. Lovejoy says the two evidentiary channels reciprocally corroborate and illuminate one another.

The intended architecture is:

`native lexical / grammatical evidence`
→ `existence and semantic structure of category`
→ `customs as applications and scope tests`
↔ `reciprocal corroboration`.

This is the published endpoint of the proof-partition problem.

## IX. 1906 source criticism operates at statement level

The paper does not simply assign one reliability grade to each source. It often decomposes the source into proposition-types.

Analytical formula:

> **source × statement type → evidentiary competence → proposition licensed.**

### McGee

Lovejoy calls the Dakotan evidence clear and trustworthy for use and real meaning, but immediately says McGee does not adequately distinguish semantic essence from secondary connotations. High value for distribution and usage does not confer final authority over abstraction.

### Alice C. Fletcher

Fletcher is valuable for common life and communicability/transferability, but Lovejoy rejects a narrowing of the conception to `will-power`. A strong specialist witness can be accepted for one proposition and corrected for another.

### M. V. P. / Osage material

External facts of custom are retained while translations of native terms and the classification of an act as `prayer` are downgraded. Observation and ontology are separable.

### Roger Williams and early missionary evidence

Old observers can preserve concrete lexical applications while their `god/spirit` glosses are rejected. A lexical occurrence can prove application to unusual efficacy without proving a personal deity or a single pervasive abstraction.

### Early Jesuit / Bressani `oki` material

Lovejoy explicitly says the old observations establish only concrete application to unusually powerful persons/things, not yet the abstract universal property. This is a literal statement of a proof ceiling.

### W. Jones 1905

Jones is introduced precisely when the old evidence reaches that ceiling. Grammatical gender, specialist field study and native causal explanations support a stronger claim: impersonal abstraction, omnipresence and transferability. The Jones enemy-heart case gives an indigenous explanation of absorbing `manitou` rather than leaving the transfer mechanism as an analyst's inference.

### Dorsey

The custom/informant relation is retained while Dorsey's personification of `wakonda` is rejected. Again, one source supplies both admissible and inadmissible propositions.

The source is not the atomic unit of reliability. **The claim extracted from it often is.**

## X. 1906 cross-regional proof is explicitly asymmetric

The `vera causa` architecture should not be idealized. The actual comparative execution is uneven.

### Two evidentiary tiers

Lovejoy uses:

1. **low-resolution independent-observer convergence** — many observers across North America, Oceania and Africa report similarly broad applications of `wakonda`, `manitou`, `oki`, `mana`, `atua`, `mulungu`, `ngai`, etc.;
2. **high-resolution controls** — grammar, native-language distinctions and indigenous causal explanations that discriminate property, person, efficacy and transfer.

The second tier is especially dense in the North-American packet, with an independently strong grammatical control in the Yao/Bantu packet.

### Jones: morphology as ontology control

Sac/Fox/Kickapoo grammar distinguishes animate and inanimate forms; the impersonal/abstract use of `manitou` supplies higher-resolution evidence than missionary translation.

### Hetherwick: independent Yao grammatical control

`mulungu` does not belong to the personal noun class and instead behaves as an inhering property/quality. This is a genuinely independent structural parallel to Jones.

The bibliographic control also reveals an ordinary failure: Lovejoy appears to cite Hetherwick as JAI volume 33 when the article is in volume 32 (1902), pp.89–95.

### Codrington: strong authority with adverse evidence

Codrington supplies transferability, distributed efficacy and `mana`, but also states that mana essentially belongs to personal beings as its origin. Lovejoy foregrounds efficacy/transferability and later rejects `supernormal` as semantic essence without fully dissolving the personal-origin tension.

The correct relation is selective reweighting, not identity between Codrington's theory and Lovejoy's abstraction.

### Masai / Hollis–Eliot attribution slip

The Masai evidence includes both prayer to a distinct personality and broad application of `En-ga'i`. Lovejoy attributes an interpretive suggestion to `Mr. Hollis`, but the relevant passage appears in Sir Charles Eliot's introduction to Hollis's book and speaks of Hollis in the third person. The page/book locus is useful; the authorial attribution appears compressed or mistaken.

### p.376 — observer resolution is itself graded

Lovejoy says the agreement of many independent observers is significant while explicitly acknowledging that several missed finer ramifications and meanings. Nearly all establish a minimal pattern; American evidence shows `still more`.

### p.377 — high-resolution North America becomes an analogical key

Lovejoy argues that because superficial reports use the regional power-terms similarly, it is not improbable that the richer meanings disclosed by more accurate North-American observation also belong to less richly described analogues elsewhere.

This is the critical asymmetry:

`surface cross-regional parallel`
+
`deep North-American analysis`
→ `probabilistic extension of deeper semantics to lower-resolution regions`.

Therefore the most exact assessment is:

> **Lovejoy's method is structurally critical but locally uneven. He calibrates claims more finely than whole sources, yet the final cross-regional synthesis can outrun the calibration available inside each regional packet.**

Or:

> **triangulation under asymmetric evidence**.

Both are project formulations.

## XI. Bibliographic reselection: sources migrate by proof function

The notebook-to-publication transition is visible in which sources survive and what jobs they are assigned.

### Codrington — persistence with function migration

In 005 Codrington is one authority inside a broad comparative-religion dossier. In 1906 pp.118–119 of *The Melanesians* become a principal lexical-semantic witness for `mana`.

`comparative ethnographic source`
→ `existence / semantic control for abstract category`.

### Brinton — persistence with role reversal

Brinton belongs to the comparative American authority field in/around 005 but enters the 1906 opening as a representative of an anthropomorphic/personal master theory that Lovejoy wants to displace.

`authority / evidence field`
→ `theoretical target`.

### Frazer — persistent theoretical foil

Frazer persists less as a stable empirical master source than as a recurring theory whose classifications and causal divisions Lovejoy tests. Persistence can therefore signal adversarial usefulness, not inheritance.

### Trumbull, Kingsley, Callaway — scope-driven deferral

These custom-rich sources are powerful for blood, foundation rites, fetish/amulet distinctions, sacrifice and ancestor relations in 005. They largely recede from the 1906 first-stage lexical proof because their strongest evidentiary job belongs to the later customs/application stage the article announces but postpones.

Absence is therefore not abandonment; it can be **argument-driven deferral**.

### Duff MacDonald → Hetherwick

MacDonald is useful in 005 for African custom/divinity and counterexample work. In 1906 the Yao proposition has changed: the question is grammatical ontology. Hetherwick's noun-class evidence is better fitted to that stronger lexical-semantic claim.

This is functional replacement, not a verdict that MacDonald became globally unreliable.

The bibliographic rule is:

> **track source function, not citation frequency or prestige.**

## XII. 005 → 1906 is reproof, not mere republication

Several notebook mechanisms recur in the 1906 theory only after acquiring a new warrant.

### Selected organs / transferred qualities

005 groups selected organs as possible carriers of intelligence, courage, life or sexual power and separates this from sacramental communion. In 1906 Jones's Algonkin evidence explicitly explains enemy-heart consumption as absorbing `manitou`.

`comparative mechanism inference`
→ `native lexical/causal explanation`.

### Blood and fertility

005 reconstructs blood and bodily material in agriculture as fecundative or quasi-mechanical transfer rather than necessarily sacrifice/communion. In 1906 reproductive and agricultural power becomes an application of an independently evidenced transferable energy conception.

`local fecundative mechanism`
→ `cross-cutting productive-energy category with lexical warrant`.

### Fetish versus charm/amulet

005 separates spirit-inhabitation from intrinsic/object efficacy. In 1906 `oki` and related lexical applications support the possibility that unusual power belongs to the efficacious object/property rather than requiring a personal indwelling spirit.

`causal ontology discrimination`
→ `native-language support for anti-animist classification`.

### `quasi-mechanical`

In 005 the term marks a local agrarian mechanism distinct from sacramental action. In 1906 impersonal/quasi-mechanical efficacy becomes the conceptual center of the published theory.

The change is not:

`notebook phrase` → `published doctrine`.

It is:

`local comparative discrimination`
→ `cross-case abstraction`
→ `independent lexical verification`
→ `published reaggregation`.

### Reporter criticism

005 often qualifies a report through provenance/contact history. 1906 subdivides the report itself: observed fact, lexical occurrence, translation, semantic abstraction and causal interpretation can receive different grades.

The evidentiary atom becomes smaller.

The best description is:

> **mechanism migration is accompanied by warrant migration.**

The 1906 paper changes the direction of inference at crucial points:

`custom → infer mechanism`

becomes

`native term / grammar → establish mechanism → return to custom as application`.

This is **reproof**.

## XIII. The developmental model

The cumulative sequence should now be stated without priority mythology.

### Harvard / 004

Lovejoy already works inside a philological proof culture in which witnesses, semantic relations, doctrinal functions and historical dependence can be separately tested. Direct Oldenberg/Jacobi controls prohibit an invention claim.

### Paris / 005

The evidentiary object changes from words and canonical passages to reporters, rites and comparative ethnography. Marillier's institutional environment contains explicit document criticism and relation-sensitive historical reasoning; Hubert–Mauss provide a contemporary control on genealogy-from-analogy. 005 shows a homologous proof partition operating on the new object.

### 1902

The same inferential discipline is generalized to comparative philosophy and intellectual history: resemblance, logical motive, historical transmission, recombination and survival become separable propositions.

### 1906

Lovejoy tries to solve the vulnerability of custom-based comparative mechanism inference through an independent lexical/grammatical warrant and reciprocal corroboration. The proof apparatus becomes more explicit and statement-specific.

### But 1906 execution remains uneven

The stronger North-American packet carries disproportionate interpretive weight across the cross-regional synthesis. The paper's own bibliographic and attribution slips also block methodological idealization.

The safest master formulation is therefore:

> **Lovejoy's early comparative practice develops by transporting a proof-partition discipline across evidentiary domains and repeatedly rebuilding the warrant for stronger claims. The development is real, but neither the operators nor the field practices are uniquely his, and the 1906 universalizing synthesis exceeds the local evidentiary resolution that his own method often demands.**
