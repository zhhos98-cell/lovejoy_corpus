## Why this changes the active branch

The Baltimore 1935 encounter is not an isolated anecdote followed only by later historiographical retelling. Lovejoy subsequently wrote a sustained essay on the criteria of meaning in which Carnap is his principal worked case.

More importantly, the essay itself says it was **written in 1938**. It was published in 1942 as the opening essay of F. P. Clarke and M. C. Nahm (eds.), *Philosophical Essays in Honor of Edgar Arthur Singer, Jr.* (University of Pennsylvania Press), pp. 3–23.

Lovejoy therefore returns to the `meaning / criterion / metaphysics` problem only about three years after the Baltimore exchange. There is currently **no direct evidence that the 1935 encounter caused or occasioned the 1938 essay**. But the chronology and substantive overlap make 1938 an indispensable actor-level control on what Lovejoy himself took the problem to be.

## 2. First major result: Lovejoy himself joins the problem-spaces we had been connecting analytically

At the outset Lovejoy describes the recent proliferation of theories making `meaning` a propaedeutic gatekeeper for philosophy. He then gives a striking actor-authored grouping.

He calls the broad type **`radical empiricism`** and says its species include:

- pragmatism, in one of its senses;
- operationalism;
- behaviorism;
- the special theory of relativity, in its methodological premises;
- neo-positivism.

This matters because the repository had independently connected:

`1908 pragmatist criteria of meaning`
→ `1930 relativity / operationism / criterion-versus-meaning`
→ `1935 Carnap / metaphysical meaningfulness`.

The 1938 essay supplies actor-level evidence that Lovejoy himself regarded these controversies as variants inside one historical-philosophical **meaning problem-space**.

### Warrant ceiling

This does **not** prove one unchanged Lovejoy doctrine from 1908 to 1938. It does show that the later Lovejoy consciously retrospectively grouped pragmatism, operationalism, relativity methodology and neo-positivism under a shared problem concerning the criteria and limits of meaning.

That is substantially stronger than analyst-imposed adjacency.

## 3. Scope control: Lovejoy explicitly knows Carnap's position changed

Lovejoy chooses Carnap's 1928 *Scheinprobleme der Philosophie* as his worked text but immediately inserts an important historical disclaimer.

He says it is doubtful that the 1928 volume `precisely represents the present views of its author` and describes logical positivism as unusually protean. He then says his interest is **not** the historical question of which changes Carnap or the movement later underwent, but the validity of a certain `type of argument and conclusion` represented by the 1928 text.

This is crucial for the active branch.

Do **not** write:

> `Lovejoy 1938/42 attacks Carnap's 1936 Testability and Meaning position as if unchanged from 1928.`

Safer:

> **Lovejoy deliberately uses Carnap's 1928 *Scheinprobleme* as a historically influential, lucid instance of a type of meaning-criterion argument while explicitly acknowledging that Carnap's current position may have changed.**

He nevertheless notes that `empirical verifiability`, or **`testability, as Carnap prefers to call it in more recent writings`**, remains relevant to the duality he diagnoses.

## 4. Lovejoy's preliminary semantic jurisdiction

Book pp. 4–6 define his own starting point before the Carnap audit.

Lovejoy asks what one is actually asking when asking the meaning of `meaning`. His answer is roughly referential / thought-content based:

- a term is meaningful when a user has an actual thought-content or discriminable idea that the word is intended to express;
- distinct meaning depends on clarity about which idea is being expressed;
- arbitrary stipulative definitions can only generate correspondingly verbal consequences unless some factual warrant connects them to actual usage or thought.

He therefore criticizes attempts to expel whole classes of statements as meaningless without first specifying what kind of proposition the proposed criterion of `meaning` itself is.

### Operator form

`verbal stipulation about use of the word meaning`

≠ automatically

`factual discovery that proposition S expresses no thinkable content`.

This is already a jurisdiction partition between:

- metalinguistic convention;
- empirical/psychological fact about entertainable thought-content;
- logical consequence from a definition;
- substantive philosophical conclusion about what can be thought or known.

## 5. The central diagnostic: Carnap's `experienceable` is equivocal

Lovejoy's full Carnap analysis begins by distinguishing senses of `experienceable / unexperienceable`.

### Criterion 1 — thinkability / actual thought-content

In one sense a proposed referent is `unexperienceable` when the terms used to describe it correspond to no qualities or relations of which anyone can form a conception. On this criterion, manifest meaninglessness concerns either:

- signs / sounds with no idea associated;
- combinations whose component ideas cannot coherently be put together in the asserted relation.

This criterion asks whether there is any **thinkable content** at all.

### Criterion 2 — empirical verifiability / testability

A second criterion asks whether a sentence expresses a judgment for which a thinkable experience could establish truth or falsity or answer the question.

Lovejoy emphasizes that this criterion concerns not the `what` of a possible object but the `that`: whether the proposition concerning it could be established as an empirical matter of fact.

He also notes that Carnap's concessions concerning hypotheses about theoretical entities broaden this second criterion. `Testability` becomes, in Lovejoy's words, a **double-barreled** criterion rather than a simple demand for direct perceptual verification.

## 6. The decisive relation-separation claim: the two criteria are not interchangeable

Book pp. 12–13 are the central locus.

Lovejoy says the realm of **entertainable notions, questions, and hypotheses** is not identical or coextensive with the realm of propositions conceived as empirically verifiable, whether verification is direct or inferential.

His conclusion is explicit:

> the two criteria are **not interchangeable**.

He then accuses Carnap's anti-metaphysical procedure of interchanging them: in Lovejoy's reconstruction, a **factual conclusion** that could follow only from the thinkability criterion is substituted for a **verbal conclusion** generated by applying the verifiability criterion.

### Operator map

`Criterion A: is there a thinkable / discriminable content?`

≠

`Criterion B: is there a possible empirical procedure/evidential route that settles truth or falsity?`

and therefore:

`fails B`

≠

`therefore fails A`

unless an additional bridge is demonstrated.

This is a direct mature case of **typed relation + inferential jurisdiction**. The key historical feature is not simply opposition to positivism. Lovejoy's objection is that the argument upgrades one kind of failure into another without warrant.

## 7. External-world case: different criterion, different conclusion

Lovejoy applies the distinction to Carnap's treatment of the realism dispute.

His reconstruction:

- empirical procedures can establish whether a mountain is there in the ordinary geographical sense;
- those procedures cannot directly establish a proposition about the mountain's continued existence when unperceived;
- but inability to settle the latter by perception does not show that the idea of unperceived existence cannot be entertained.

Lovejoy's explicit result is that **different criteria yield different conclusions**.

By the thinkability criterion, he says, the conclusion that the realism dispute is meaningless does not follow. By the empirical-verifiability criterion, what follows is only the much weaker proposition that perception cannot establish the continued existence of the mountain while unperceived.

### Jurisdiction form

`cannot establish P by perceptual procedure`

≠

`cannot think / mean P`.

The stronger semantic exclusion therefore requires a proof step that the empirical criterion by itself does not provide.

## 8. Other-minds case: `appearance of logicality` from criterion substitution

The final pages repeat the structure in Carnap's treatment of other minds.

Lovejoy says the argument's **`appearance of logicality`** derives from application of the second criterion, empirical verifiability, while the asserted conclusion concerns the first: unthinkability / absence of theoretical content.

His concluding partition is especially useful:

- whether propositions about other minds **have meaning**;
- whether they can be **empirically verified** in the strict sense;
- whether they may **legitimately be believed**;
- whether believing them has pragmatic/intellectual advantages;

are different questions.

Lovejoy therefore substitutes yet another question only explicitly: if one leaves the question of meaning and asks whether an unverifiable proposition may legitimately be believed, the relevant warrant changes.

### Operator form

`meaningfulness`
≠ `empirical verifiability`
≠ `truth`
≠ `legitimate belief`.

This is very close in form to the 1911 repo control:

`intelligibility / meaning`
≠ `formal implication`
≠ `evidence / verification`
≠ `historical truth`.

Do not infer unchanged doctrine across 27 years; the local objects and vocabularies differ. But the 1938 text itself is direct evidence that Lovejoy still polices transfers among semantic, evidentiary and inferential jurisdictions.

## 9. Relation to 1930 — stronger than a retrospective analyst analogy

The active 1930 note established a mediated-primary but page-located sequence:

- verifying experience need not exhaust signification;
- experimental evidence can be a sign/circumstantial evidence of something rather than its meaning;
- different verification routes need not create different proposition identities;
- criterion selection requires independent warrant.

The 1938 essay now gives a full direct-primary mature counterpart:

`thinkability / thought-content`
≠ `empirical verifiability/testability`
≠ `truth`
≠ `legitimate belief`.

The connection is also historically strengthened because Lovejoy's own opening groups **operationalism and the special theory of relativity** together with pragmatism and neo-positivism as species within the broader radical-empiricist meaning problem.

Safe conclusion:

> **By 1938 Lovejoy himself retrospectively locates the relativity/operationism and neo-positivist controversies inside one family of disputes over what empirical criteria may license us to say about meaning.**

This still does not prove that every 1930 operator survives unchanged.

## 10. Relation to Baltimore 1935 — substantive overlap, causal link open

Carnap's same-day diary on 31 Dec. 1935 records Lovejoy's objection in compact form: if the new position makes everything conventional, then logical positivists can no longer declare metaphysics meaningless.

The 1938 essay's governing question is structurally close:

> what sort of inference licenses moving from a chosen/defined criterion of meaning to the substantive expulsion of metaphysical questions as meaningless?

And pp. 6–8 explicitly distinguish:

`arbitrary verbal definition`

from

`assertoric factual proposition subject to verification/control`.

This makes the 1935–1938 overlap far more concrete than the old dossier allowed.

### Evidence ceiling

Secure:

- Lovejoy challenged Carnap publicly in 1935 on conventionalism and the right to exclude metaphysics;
- Lovejoy wrote this sustained Carnap/meaning essay in 1938;
- the 1938 essay attacks unwarranted migration from definitions/criteria to substantive semantic exclusion.

Open:

- whether the Baltimore exchange directly generated the essay;
- whether Lovejoy was responding to Carnap's Baltimore manuscript or later published `Testability and Meaning`;
- whether any correspondence linked the two episodes.

Do **not** write `Baltimore led Lovejoy to write the 1938 essay` without archival evidence.

## 11. Relation to 1908 — an even longer actor-level criteria problem, with a warning

Lovejoy's 1908 `The Thirteen Pragmatisms` had already distinguished allegedly different criteria of meaning in William James. Modern scholarship continues to debate whether Lovejoy was correct to separate those criteria.

The 1938 essay now explicitly lists pragmatism among the relevant radical-empiricist theories of meaning. This makes 1908 a legitimate antecedent in Lovejoy's own retrospective problem genealogy.

But preserve the distinction:

- 1908 object: Lovejoy differentiates Jamesian formulations / consequences under `pragmatism`;
- 1938 object: Lovejoy distinguishes thinkability from empirical verifiability in Carnap and attacks criterion substitution.

The recurrence is **criteria differentiation**, not identity of the criteria themselves.

## 12. Revised longitudinal meaning-criteria ladder

The strongest current actor-level sequence is:

### 1908 — James / pragmatism

Lovejoy separates different criteria grouped under a common theory of pragmatic meaning and refuses argumentative transfer between formulations that are not logically equivalent.

### 1930 — relativity / operationism

Lovejoy separates empirical criterion / verifying operation / evidence from semantic content and proposition identity; target attribution to Einstein is contested by McGilvary.

### 31 Dec. 1935 — Carnap in Baltimore

Carnap's same-day diary records Lovejoy objecting that conventionalism threatens logical positivism's right to exclude metaphysics as meaningless; Carnap replies at length.

### 1938 — Carnap essay composed

Lovejoy explicitly groups pragmatism, operationalism, relativity methodology and neo-positivism inside one radical-empiricist meaning problem, then argues that Carnap's anti-metaphysical reasoning interchanges two non-equivalent criteria of meaning.

### 1942 — essay published

The 1938 text appears as `On the Criteria and Limits of Meaning`, pp. 3–23, opening the Singer Festschrift.

### Current structural hypothesis

> **A durable Lovejoy problem is not merely `meaning` but the legitimacy of moving between criteria: a test can determine one relation without thereby settling a semantically stronger relation.**

Warrant: strong longitudinal structural hypothesis, now partly actor-retrospective because 1938 Lovejoy himself groups the earlier problem-spaces. It remains different from a causal genealogy of method.
