# *Political Theory* 1973–1977: internationalism, method pluralism, and regional-object roles

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **COMPLETE 20-ISSUE DIRECT SAGE TOC CENSUS / ROLE-SENSITIVE TITLE-LEVEL REGIONAL OBJECT CONTROL / REVIEW + BIBLIOGRAPHIC SURVEILLANCE DIFFERENTIATED / JHI EXISTENCE-COMPARATOR ONLY / NO AUTHOR-IDENTITY INFERENCE**

Companion ledger:

`research_notes/Political_Theory_1973_1977_regional_object_role_ledger_2026-08-31.tsv`

## Core result

The journal's early self-description must now be split into at least three different kinds of pluralism/internationalism:

1. **method / ideological pluralism**;
2. **international circulation, personnel and carrier infrastructure**;
3. **geographical / civilizational diversity of the intellectual objects treated as primary research subjects**.

The direct February 1973 `Editorial Statement` and 1977 SAGE advertisement strongly establish the first two. A bounded title-level census of all twenty issues from February 1973 through November 1977 shows that the third was much narrower in the journal's substantive article layer.

Under an explicitly defined substantive-unit denominator of **122 issue-level units** across the twenty issues, only two article-level titles directly name a non-European/non-North-American regional object in ways relevant to the present test:

- Tony Smith, `IDEALISM AND PEOPLE'S WAR: Sartre on Algeria` (1973) — a colonial/decolonization contact case whose named interpretive center remains Sartre;
- Vinit Haksar, `Coercive Proposals [Rawls and Gandhi]` (1976) — Gandhi as a comparative co-case alongside Rawls.

Within this bounded title-level article census, **no substantive unit title makes Chinese, Indian, Islamic/Arabic, African, or Japanese intellectual tradition itself the independent internally addressed historical/intellectual object**.

Yet those materials are not simply absent from the journal ecology. They appear more visibly in other carrier roles:

- Chinese statecraft in a 1974 book-review cluster;
- Averroes in a 1975 review and 1976 communication follow-up;
- multiple *Gandhi Marg* articles and a Nirvana/Yogin article in the journal's 1974 bibliographic surveillance;
- Ancient Near Eastern material in the same bibliography.

Therefore the stronger architecture is:

`METHOD / IDEOLOGICAL PLURALISM`
!= `CIVILIZATIONAL OBJECT PLURALISM`;

`INTERNATIONAL CARRIER`
!= `GLOBAL RESEARCH-OBJECT JURISDICTION`;

`BIBLIOGRAPHIC / REVIEW VISIBILITY`
!= `ORIGINAL RESEARCH-ARTICLE CENTRALITY`.

This is not a claim that early *Political Theory* was parochial in every sense. It is a claim about **role allocation**.

# I. Census scope and denominator

The census covers every issue of *Political Theory* from:

- vol. 1 no. 1, February 1973
through
- vol. 5 no. 4, November 1977.

All twenty SAGE issue TOCs were directly inspected.

The analyst-defined substantive denominator includes:

- research articles;
- named article-level comments and rejoinders;
- symposium/exchange components;
- graduate-student articles;
- translated primary text plus substantive introduction when presented as central issue content.

It excludes:

- editorials;
- book reviews / Books in Review;
- communications sections;
- bibliographies;
- announcements;
- errata/corrections.

This produces:

- 1973: **27** substantive units;
- 1974: **23**;
- 1975: **24**;
- 1976: **25**;
- 1977: **23**;
- total: **122**.

This is **not** a SAGE genre statistic, acceptance statistic or field-representativeness measure. It is a role-sensitive denominator built for one narrow question: what kinds of regional/civilizational intellectual objects become visible at the level of substantive issue titles?

Do not compare `122` directly with the 1975 submission census or APSR article counts without denominator harmonization.

---

# II. Coding rule: do not infer region from author identity

No regional classification is assigned from an author's name, nationality, birthplace or institutional affiliation.

Examples:

- Shlomo Avineri writing on Marx does not create an `Israel` regional-object case;
- Vinit Haksar counts only because **Gandhi is explicitly the named object**;
- Hwa Yol Jung's edited reader does not become an Asian-thought case merely because of the editor's biography.

The unit is the **explicit intellectual/historical object visible in the publication title**, not the demographic identity of the scholar.

Core rule:

`AUTHOR INTERNATIONALITY`
!= `OBJECT INTERNATIONALITY`.

This distinction is essential because the journal could be internationally staffed/circulated while still centering a relatively narrow historical canon.

---

# III. Regional-object role taxonomy

A simple binary `Western / non-Western` would reproduce the exact aggregation problem the project is trying to avoid.

The current census therefore distinguishes at least four roles.

## A. `INTERNAL_TRADITION_OBJECT`

A regional intellectual tradition, actor or internally developing conceptual field is itself the historical/intellectual subject.

Examples from the existing JHI comparator seed include:

- P. T. Raju, `The Development of Indian Thought` (1952);
- Buddha Prakash, `The Hindu Philosophy of History` (1955);
- Arthur F. Wright, `The Study of Chinese Civilization` (1960);
- Nicholas Rescher, `Al-Fārābī on Logical Tradition` (1963);
- Julia Ching, `Truth and Ideology: The Confucian Way ... and its Transmission` (1974).

No title-level *Political Theory* substantive unit from 1973–77 meets this specific coding threshold for China, India, Islamic/Arabic thought, Africa or Japan.

## B. `COMPARATIVE_COCASE`

A non-Western actor/tradition is paired with another case in a comparative normative/conceptual problem.

Direct early PT example:

- Vinit Haksar, `Coercive Proposals [Rawls and Gandhi]` (1976).

The SAGE author note says the paper is a sequel to Haksar's `Rawls and Gandhi on Civil Disobedience`.

This makes Gandhi genuinely part of the article's explicit analytic object.

But:

`GANDHI AS COMPARATIVE CO-CASE`
!= `INTERNAL HISTORY OF INDIAN POLITICAL THOUGHT`.

Do not upgrade the role without article-level warrant.

## C. `EXTERNAL_GAZE / CONTACT / EVENT`

A non-Western region/event enters through the thought, representation or political intervention of another canonical actor or through a colonial/contact setting.

Direct early PT example:

- Tony Smith, `IDEALISM AND PEOPLE'S WAR: Sartre on Algeria` (1973).

The direct SAGE reference architecture shows that the article is not empirically empty of Algerian/African materials: it uses Algerian demographic/economic sources and engages Fanon, Memmi and colonial/decolonization texts.

Nevertheless its named interpretive architecture is `Sartre on Algeria`.

Therefore:

`ALGERIA PRESENT AS HISTORICAL/POLITICAL FIELD`
!= `ALGERIAN INTELLECTUAL TRADITION AS PRIMARY UNIT`.

This is structurally comparable to JHI rows such as `Leibniz and China` or European representations of Madagascar: regional presence can be real without the historiographical address becoming internally regional.

## D. `REVIEW / BIBLIOGRAPHIC / COMMUNICATION VISIBILITY`

A region/tradition is admitted into the journal's awareness and discussion infrastructure without becoming an original research-article object.

This role turns out to be significant in early PT.

# IV. China appears directly in the review layer

The February 1974 `Books in Review` cluster directly includes:

Herrlee G. Creel, *The Origins of Statecraft in China: Volume One: The Western Chou Empire*.

This establishes:

`CHINESE POLITICAL-HISTORICAL OBJECT`
-> `PT REVIEW / RECEPTION INFRASTRUCTURE`.

It does not establish:

`CHINESE POLITICAL THOUGHT`
-> `PT ORIGINAL ARTICLE JURISDICTION`.

The distinction is especially valuable because Creel is already central to the repo's JHI non-Western role history. JHI had published China-focused research articles and debates long before PT's founding; PT's early visible China case, by contrast, appears here through review infrastructure.

Do not infer that the review was marginal or unimportant. Reviews are part of field construction. The point is precisely that **role matters**.

---

# V. Averroes moves from review to communication, not yet to original article

May 1975 includes a direct review record for:

`Averroes on Plato's Republic`.

November 1976 then includes Charles E. Butterworth's communication responding to Paul Sigmund's review.

This gives a traceable PT carrier sequence:

`ISLAMIC / ANDALUSIAN PHILOSOPHER`
-> `BOOK / TRANSLATION REVIEW`
-> `COMMUNICATION / FOLLOW-UP`.

That is more than a one-off bibliographic mention.

Yet it remains a different role architecture from JHI's pre-existing internally addressed Islamic-philosophy articles such as:

- Rescher on al-Fārābī (1963);
- Fakhry on al-Fārābī's reconciliation of Plato and Aristotle (1965).

Thus:

`SUSTAINED RECEPTION DISCUSSION`
!= `SAME HISTORIOGRAPHICAL ROLE AS INTERNAL CONCEPTUAL HISTORY`.

This is a useful cross-venue comparator precisely because both venues can contain the same broad civilizational material under different relations and units.

---

# VI. PT's bibliographic surveillance is substantially broader than its own article titles

The May 1974 `Political Theory Articles` bibliography is especially revealing.

Its directly searchable reference list monitors work across many journals and languages. Among the visible entries are multiple pieces from **Gandhi Marg**, including items on nonviolence, peace research, political movement, ends/means and social change.

The same bibliography also includes:

- James M. Glass, `The Yogin and the Utopian: Nirvana and the Discovery of Being` in *Polity*;
- a study of Voegelin, the Old Testament and the Ancient Near East;
- wide European-language journal coverage.

This creates an important carrier distinction:

`PT OWN ARTICLE OUTPUT`
!= `PT BIBLIOGRAPHIC FIELD MAP`.

The journal was surveilling a political-theory literature whose conceptual and geographic edges extended beyond the objects most visible in its own substantive article titles.

Compact model:

`BROAD INFORMATION INTAKE / FIELD MONITORING`
-> does not automatically become
`BROAD ORIGINAL-ARTICLE OBJECT DISTRIBUTION`.

This is analogous to the APSR distinction between accepted pipeline and printed output, but the relation is different: here the relevant contrast is **bibliographic awareness versus editorial/article centrality**.

Do not infer that every item in the bibliography had been read, endorsed, solicited, or considered publishable by PT.

---

# VII. `International` in the 1973/1977 programme should therefore be disaggregated

The inaugural February 1973 statement describes an international journal and explicitly refuses exclusive identification with a school/orientation.

The 1977 SAGE advertisement continues that public identity under Benjamin Barber.

But the 1977 ad is itself revealing: one of its advertised carrier functions is translation of previously untranslated **European** texts.

This does not prove a deliberate exclusion of other traditions.

It does show that the public category `international` cannot simply be translated into today's `global political thought`.

At least three historically distinct meanings are in play:

### `I1 — INTERNATIONAL PERSONNEL / COMMUNICATION`

Cross-national authors, editors, institutions and readership.

### `I2 — INTERNATIONAL / TRANSATLANTIC INTELLECTUAL CIRCULATION`

Translations, European/American philosophical debate, imported arguments, cross-venue exchange.

### `I3 — CIVILIZATIONAL OBJECT DIVERSITY`

Chinese, Indian, Islamic, African, Japanese, Latin-American etc. traditions treated as independent intellectual objects or co-equal historical fields.

The evidence strongly controls `I1` and `I2` for early PT.

The 1973–77 title census shows `I3` was much thinner in the substantive research layer.

Therefore:

> **INTERNATIONALISM IS A RELATION TYPE, NOT A UNIT TYPE.**

A journal can be international in circulation while remaining relatively concentrated in the civilizational units its research articles make central.

---

# VIII. What the 122-unit denominator does and does not prove

The twenty-issue census yields 122 substantive issue units under the defined coding rule.

Within those titles:

- one direct colonial/contact case: Sartre/Algeria;
- one direct comparative co-case: Rawls/Gandhi;
- zero title-level internal-tradition cases meeting the specified threshold for China, India, Islamic/Arabic thought, Africa or Japan.

This is a striking distribution, but the evidentiary ceiling must remain strict.

It does **not** prove:

- zero non-Western materials inside article bodies;
- zero non-Western authors;
- zero submissions on non-Western thought;
- editorial rejection of such submissions;
- zero Latin-American/Russian/other borderline cases under alternative geographic taxonomies;
- that generic terms such as `shaman` carry no ethnographic/non-Western material;
- that review/bibliographic material is intellectually secondary in every sense;
- that the five-year period represents the journal's later history.

The result is specifically:

> **Within early PT's visible substantive-title architecture, civilizationally internal non-Western political thought had not yet become a recurring independent research-object category, even though the journal's review and bibliographic infrastructures already registered materials beyond the Euro-American canon.**

# IX. Comparison with JHI: use existence and role, not raw shares

The existing JHI non-Western role ledgers are selective historical seeds rather than a like-for-like exhaustive five-year article census.

Therefore do **not** compare percentages such as:

`PT non-Western share`
vs
`JHI non-Western share`.

What can safely be compared is **role possibility already demonstrated by direct JHI cases**.

Before PT's 1973 launch, JHI had already published:

- internally addressed Indian intellectual history (Raju 1952; Prakash 1955);
- China/West co-comparison (Needham 1951);
- internally addressed Chinese reception/syncretism cases (Wright/Wilhelm/Schwartz 1951);
- Chinese-civilization field-method discussion (Wright 1960);
- Chinese literature/art as central comparative conference cases (Loehr/Hightower 1961);
- internally addressed Islamic philosophy (Rescher 1963; Fakhry 1965);
- Latin-American regional intellectual transformation (Ardao 1963);
- comparative Asian thought review/interface (Embree/Nakamura 1966);
- internal Confucian transmission (Ching 1974);
- Latin-American history-of-ideas method as a regional historiographical field (Gracia 1975).

Thus the safe historical contrast is not:

`JHI = GLOBAL`
vs
`PT = WESTERN`.

It is:

> **JHI had already made several internally addressed non-Western intellectual traditions available as article-level history-of-ideas objects, while early PT's explicitly pluralist specialist carrier initially displayed non-Western material more often through comparative/contact, review, communication and bibliographic roles than through independent internal-tradition research titles.**

That difference may reflect jurisdiction, inherited canon, submission ecology, disciplinary location, editorial networks or other mechanisms. The current census does not identify the cause.

---

# X. Why this matters for the Lovejoy/global-history project

This finding complicates a possible modernization story.

It would be easy to narrate:

`OLD JHI / HISTORY OF IDEAS`
-> `NEW CONTEXTUAL / POLITICAL THEORY VENUES`
-> `BROADER PLURALISM`.

The role evidence does not support that linear sequence.

The new specialist political-theory carrier was highly innovative in:

- method pluralism;
- controversy infrastructure;
- comments/replies;
- symposia;
- communications;
- school non-identification;
- international/transatlantic exchange.

But broader **civilizational research-object jurisdiction** does not follow automatically from those innovations.

Meanwhile JHI's older, eclectic history-of-ideas jurisdiction had already admitted multiple forms of internally addressed Asian, Islamic and Latin-American intellectual history.

Therefore:

`METHOD MODERNIZATION`
!= `GLOBALIZATION OF HISTORICAL UNITS`;

`SPECIALIZATION`
can increase
`ROLE CAPACITY`
without simultaneously increasing
`CIVILIZATIONAL UNIT DIVERSITY`.

This is highly congruent with the project's governing thesis that the historically admissible **unit** must be separated from the **relation** and the **carrier**.
