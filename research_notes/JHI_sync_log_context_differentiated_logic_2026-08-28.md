## Why this sync exists

The earlier 28 August reopening correctly established that Lovejoy directly practiced and named logical analysis and that his analytical `knife` itself requires historicalization. Subsequent work, prompted by a methodological correction, shows that the branch must now distinguish much more sharply between:

1. Lovejoy **discussing logic as an object**;
2. Lovejoy **explicitly using logical analysis as a method** on a non-logical object;
3. Lovejoy using **relation-sensitive / logic-shaped operations** in contexts where actor-level attribution to logic remains unproved.

The branch should no longer be governed by a simple question such as:

`How did formal logic migrate into Lovejoy's historical analysis?`

That wording presupposes precisely what remains to be demonstrated.

The revised governing question is:

> **Which analytical operations did Lovejoy mobilize in which contexts, what did `logical` mean locally, which relations did he keep distinct, and what—if anything—became portable across those contexts?**

## Mature 1941 control already present in repo

Do not duplicate the evidence file unnecessarily. `JHI_blog_Skinner1969_Lovejoy1936_1940_1941_adversarial_concordance_2026-08-20.md` already preserves the crucial 1941 direct-text result.

In `The Meaning of Romanticism for the Historian of Ideas`, Lovejoy distinguishes three kinds of relation among ideas:

1. logical;
2. psychological;
3. historical, especially genetic.

Logical and hypothetical psychological relations are initially constructions/hypotheses in the historian's analysis. The historian must return to historical evidence to determine whether they were actually operative in historical minds and tendencies.

Thus:

`analyst-level implication`
≠ `actor-recognized implication`
≠ `historically operative relation`
≠ `genetic/historical connection`.

This is a mature explicit statement of jurisdictional differentiation.

## Revised longitudinal sequence

The strongest sequence currently supported is not:

`formal logic training`
→ `one logical method`
→ `application to history`
→ `unit-idea`.

It is closer to:

### 1898
Relation-sensitive notebook work within Pāli/philology/comparative religion; local distinctions include semantics, chronology, logical inclusion/subdivision, causal role, genealogy, arrangement and textual witness. Attribution among field repertoires remains open.

### 1902–1904
Actor-explicit logical vocabulary inside historical-philosophical inquiry: `logical import`; `logically and historically`; logical/conceptual relation separated from temperament, transmission and historical location.

### 1907–1911
Direct logic-object work plus philosophical use of logical analysis. 1910 is formal-logical; 1911 explicitly limits formal logic's jurisdiction against meaning/evidence/existence/historical truth.

### 1908–1909
Logical independence and explicit `logical analysis + historical comparison` in philosophical movement analysis; components can be recombined and novelty can lie in configuration.

### 1915
Historiographical scale reduction: system → problem → separate ideas/dialectical motifs. Historical `unit` should not be silently redescribed as formal-logical atom.

### 1924
Inside literary/intellectual history, Lovejoy explicitly distinguishes logical relations from psychological/alogical association and semasiological/historical genesis. This is the key bridge showing that decomposition and composition are different problems.

### 1936
The mature methodological programme openly includes logical, pseudo-logical, affective, motivational and historical mechanisms, while making the analyst's unit-identification central.

### 1941
Logical, psychological and historical/genetic relations receive explicit separate epistemic status; analyst-generated logical hypotheses must be returned to historical evidence.

## Strongest current hypothesis

The most plausible portable feature is no longer `formal logic` as a single method.

A better candidate is:

> **typed relations + inferential jurisdiction**.

Operational shorthand:

> before allowing a claim to migrate, identify the relation actually at stake, identify what kind of evidence can warrant it, and require a new proof step before upgrading it to a stronger relation.

Examples:

- implication ≠ historical truth;
- affinity ≠ transmission;
- shared label ≠ identity;
- same conclusion ≠ same motive;
- component recurrence ≠ same historical function;
- logical compatibility ≠ historical composition;
- analyst-recognized implication ≠ actor-held doctrine;
- semantic resemblance ≠ genealogy.

This candidate is compatible with multiple local repertoires and different techniques. It is therefore historically more plausible than one transported formal procedure.

But even this remains a genealogy question. Recurrence is not transmission.

## Unit-idea problem — now partitioned

The `unit-idea` question should now be broken into at least four separate problems:

1. **analytical individuation** — how is a component isolated?;
2. **logical relation** — what does it imply, presuppose, oppose or combine with?;
3. **historical composition/function** — why is it historically bundled with other components, and what does it do in that configuration?;
4. **cross-context identity** — what licenses the historian to recognize it as the same idea across changed labels, motives, functions and combinations?

Current evidence is increasingly strong for Lovejoy's sophistication on 1–3.

The genuine Skinner-facing difficulty remains especially at 4.

Do not turn this into a rehabilitation myth. A differentiated Lovejoy can still make a contestable trans-contextual identity claim.

## Evidence ceilings

### Secure

- Lovejoy directly worked on formal logic and logical classification.
- Lovejoy explicitly used logical analysis outside formal logic.
- 1911 explicitly bounds formal logic's jurisdiction.
- 1924 explicitly distinguishes logical necessity from alogical/psychological association in historical intellectual compounds.
- 1936 explicitly admits logical, pseudo-logical, affective, motivational and historical factors in the method.
- 1941 explicitly partitions logical, psychological and historical/genetic relations and makes the first two analyst-level hypotheses subject to historical test.

### Strong hypothesis

- jurisdiction partition / typed relations may be a more durable person-level feature than any one technical logical procedure.

### Still open

- exact causal genealogy of this portable discipline;
- relative contribution of formal logic, philosophy, philology, comparative religion and historiography;
- whether Lovejoy himself ever narrates the transfer of a logical technique into history;
- exact warrant for stable cross-context identity of the unit-idea;
- intermediate 1924–1936 vocabulary beyond the controls already recovered.

### Do not claim

- formal logic caused the notebook method;
- one uniform `logical` technique operates in every genre;
- every relation-sensitive operation is actor-level logic;
- unit-ideas are simply formal-logical atoms;
- relation partition solves the Skinner problem;
- two temporally separated similar operations establish direct methodological continuity.
