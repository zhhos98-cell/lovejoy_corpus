# AOS 1897 Lovejoy membership and History of Religions Section boundary

Date: 2026-09-01

## New state-bearing distinction

Arthur O. Lovejoy's relation to the American Oriental Society in 1897-98 must be separated into three levels:

1. announced scholarly communication;
2. ordinary society membership;
3. membership in the newly organized History of Religions section.

Current evidence closes (1) and (2), but not a direct election path.

## Closed facts

The AOS 1897 Final Circular announced:

`Mr. ARTHUR O. LOVEJOY, Harvard University; Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha.`

The 1898 JAOS membership list identifies:

`ARTHUR ONCKEN LOVEJOY (Harvard University), 1689 Cambridge St., Cambridge, Mass. 1897.`

The list explains that the year following a member's address indicates year of election.

Therefore:

- Lovejoy's AOS election year: DIRECT CONFIRMED = 1897.
- Milinda-pañha communication announcement: DIRECT CONFIRMED = 1897.

## Boundary: History of Religions section

The 1898 AOS proceedings separately list a newly organized Section for the Historical Study of Religions.

Lovejoy is listed as an AOS member but not among the fourteen members of that section.

Therefore:

`AOS membership != History of Religions section membership`.

Do not describe Lovejoy as a member of the AOS History of Religions section in 1898 without further evidence.

## Research significance

This distinction parallels the broader Lovejoy project method:

- institutional proximity does not prove organizational identity;
- shared research objects do not prove shared methodological allegiance;
- chronological adjacency does not prove transmission.

The relevant question becomes narrower:

Did Lovejoy's 1897 Milinda-pañha communication participate in the same institutional reorganization of comparative religion represented by the new AOS section, or did it remain within Oriental philology/textual studies?
