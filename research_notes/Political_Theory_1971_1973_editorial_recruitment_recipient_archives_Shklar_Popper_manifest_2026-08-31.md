## Core result

The direct-full February 1973 `Editorial Statement` gives a corporate category architecture: history of political thought, modern theory, conceptual analysis, polemic argumentation, and an explicit policy against exclusive identification with one school/orientation.

The next historical question is therefore not simply `who was on the board?` but:

> **How were those categories used during actual recruitment, solicitation, and correspondence with prospective editors/contributors?**

Two recipient-side archival containers now make that question concrete.

1. **Judith N. Shklar papers, Harvard University Archives, HUGFP 118** — official collection direct; later archival scholarship points to 1971–72 correspondence with Walter Odajnyk and Robert Lamb concerning participation in the journal's foundation and Shklar's self-positioning between history of political thought and conceptual analysis.
2. **Sir Karl Raimund Popper papers, Hoover/Calisphere finding aid, Box 339, Folder 4** — direct finding-aid entry: `Political Theory (Walter Odajnyk), 1972–1981`.

These are high-value archive gates because they can test whether the journal's published pluralist architecture was already operational in editor-to-scholar recruitment and solicitation.

Preserve:

`PUBLISHED PROGRAMME`
!= `RECRUITMENT PRACTICE`

# II. Gatta's archival reconstruction supplies letter-level targets

Giunia Gatta, *Rethinking Liberalism for the 21st Century: The Skeptical Radicalism of Judith Shklar* (Routledge, 2018), explicitly reports using the Harvard Shklar papers.

Her account says that Shklar accepted an invitation in 1972 to participate in the foundation of *Political Theory* and, in correspondence with the editors, described her qualifications as lying more in **history of political thought** than in **conceptual analysis**.

This is unusually important because those are precisely two categories named in the February 1973 inaugural `Editorial Statement`.

Gatta's note identifies:

- a letter to **Robert Lamb, 28 June 1972**, `HUGFP 118`, correspondence 1959–1973, Box 1, Folder 12;
- elsewhere, letters dated **27 November 1971, 7 June 1972, and September 1972** to Walter Odajnyk and Robert Lamb, Harvard University Archives, Shklar correspondence 1959–1973, Box 1.

There is a date/description asymmetry between Gatta's chapter note (`28 June`) and the broader biographical note (`7 June` among several letters). Preserve both as reported targets rather than silently harmonizing them.

Current evidence status:

`LETTER EXISTENCE / LOCUS = SECONDARY ARCHIVAL CONTROL FROM GATTA`

`LETTER CONTENT = SECONDARY PARAPHRASE / NOT DIRECT IN REPO`.

Do not quote or reconstruct the original correspondence as if inspected.

# III. Why Shklar's self-categorization matters

If direct inspection confirms Gatta's account, the 1972 Shklar letter would connect three levels that are currently separate:

1. `EDITORIAL RECRUITMENT / INVITATION`;
2. `ACTOR SELF-CLASSIFICATION` as stronger in history of political thought than conceptual analysis;
3. `PUBLISHED 1973 VENUE TAXONOMY` containing those same categories.

This would provide actor-level evidence that editorial categories were not only public descriptive labels imposed after launch but part of the practical language through which prospective participants located themselves.

However, preserve the causal/version firewall:

`SHKLAR USED CATEGORY LANGUAGE`
!= `SHKLAR CAUSED FEBRUARY 1973 CATEGORY WORDING`;

`RECRUITMENT CATEGORY`
!= `FIXED METHOD IDENTITY OF SHKLAR'S WORK`.

Indeed, Shklar's later work itself complicates any stable history/theory split.

# V. Why the Popper file is methodologically valuable

The February 1973 statement promises a journal not exclusively identified with one school/orientation and gives space to conceptual analysis, modern theory, historical work and polemic.

Popper is therefore a useful stress test for the meaning of that pluralism.

A direct 1972–73 Odajnyk–Popper exchange could answer:

- whether a major critic of historicism was actively recruited into the journal's ecology;
- whether the editors described *Political Theory* to him through school-neutral, anti-behavioralist, philosophical, methodological, or other language;
- what kind of contribution/role was sought;
- how the editors framed relation to political science;
- whether the language resembles the public February/May editorials.

But until the folder is read:

`ARCHIVE CONTAINER EXISTS`
!= `POPPER WAS A FOUNDING EDITOR / BOARD MEMBER / INTELLECTUAL INFLUENCE`.

# VII. Relation to CSPT and founding-board overlap

These archives should help prevent a current temptation:

`CSPT NETWORK OVERLAP`
-> therefore
`POLITICAL THEORY BOARD = CSPT ORGANIZATIONAL ARM`.

That inference is not yet licensed.

Direct controls currently establish only differentiated relations:

- the journal idea arose in a Columbia CSPT chapter graduate-student meeting;
- Melvin Richter's IAS biography lists *Political Theory* editorial-board membership from 1972;
- Feldman's archival reconstruction identifies Sheldon Wolin as editor for `comparative theory`;
- Pocock published in the first issue;
- Shklar had participated in CSPT founding-era coalition formation and later archival scholarship reports direct 1971–72 correspondence with the journal founders;
- Popper has a recipient-side `Political Theory (Walter Odajnyk)` correspondence folder.

These are **different relation types**:

`ORIGIN EVENT`
!= `BOARD OFFICE`
!= `SECTION EDITORSHIP`
!= `AUTHORSHIP`
!= `RECRUITMENT CORRESPONDENCE`.

The archive work should preserve those differences rather than flatten them into `network membership`.
