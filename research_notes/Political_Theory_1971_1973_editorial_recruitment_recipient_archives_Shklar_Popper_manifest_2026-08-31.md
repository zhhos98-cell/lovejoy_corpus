## Core result

The direct-full February 1973 `Editorial Statement` gives a corporate category architecture: history of political thought, modern theory, conceptual analysis, polemic argumentation, and explicit avoidance of exclusive identification with one school/orientation.

The next historical question is therefore not simply `who was on the board?` but:

> **How were those categories and venue purposes used during actual recruitment, solicitation, and editor-to-scholar correspondence?**

Three recipient-side archive containers now make that question concrete:

1. **Judith N. Shklar papers, Harvard University Archives, HUGFP 118** — official collection direct; Gatta 2018 reports 1971–72 Lamb/Odajnyk correspondence and Shklar's self-positioning between history of political thought and conceptual analysis.
2. **Sir Karl Raimund Popper papers, Box 339 Folder 4** — direct finding-aid entry `Political Theory (Walter Odajnyk), 1972–1981`.
3. **Allan Bloom papers, University of Chicago, Box 2 Folder 10** — direct finding aid lists `Letter from Robert Lamb, November 14, 1973` inside Bloom's `Eulogy for Leo Strauss, 1973–1978` file; Bloom's `Leo Strauss` article then appeared in *Political Theory* 2.4 (Nov. 1974), pp.372–392.

These containers can test whether published pluralism was operational in editorial practice. But preserve:

`PUBLISHED PROGRAMME != RECRUITMENT PRACTICE`

until the letters are directly read.

# II. Gatta supplies Shklar letter-level targets, still secondary archival control

Giunia Gatta, *Rethinking Liberalism for the 21st Century: The Skeptical Radicalism of Judith Shklar* (2018), reports using the Harvard papers.

Her account says Shklar accepted a 1972 invitation to participate in the foundation of *Political Theory* and, in correspondence with the editors, described her qualifications as lying more in **history of political thought** than in **conceptual analysis**.

Those are precisely categories named in the February 1973 inaugural `Editorial Statement`.

Gatta reports:

- Robert Lamb letter target, **28 June 1972**, `HUGFP 118`, correspondence 1959–1973, Box 1 Folder 12;
- elsewhere, letters dated **27 November 1971, 7 June 1972, and September 1972** to Walter Odajnyk and Robert Lamb, Shklar correspondence 1959–1973, Box 1.

Preserve the reported date asymmetry (`28 June` vs `7 June` among several letters) rather than harmonizing without originals.

Evidence state:

`LETTER LOCUS / CONTENT = SECONDARY ARCHIVAL CONTROL FROM GATTA`.

Do not quote the originals as if inspected.

# V. Bloom papers: a third direct recipient-side editor letter

The University of Chicago's official preliminary inventory to the Allan Bloom Papers directly lists:

`Box 2, Folder 10 — Eulogy for Leo Strauss, 1973–1978`.

Inside that file the finding aid lists:

- two copies of the eulogy, handwritten and typed;
- **Letter from Robert Lamb, November 14, 1973**;
- Letter from Milton Himmelfarb, January 20, 1975;
- Bloom, `Un Vrai Philosophe: Leo Strauss`, 1978.

The same finding aid separately records in Box 2 Folder 17 three copies of Bloom's `Leo Strauss` eulogy as published in *Political Theory* in November 1974.

Direct journal control independently confirms:

Allan Bloom, `Leo Strauss: September 20, 1899–October 18, 1973`, *Political Theory* 2.4 (Nov. 1974), 372–392.

Thus the secure temporal relation is:

`ROBERT LAMB -> BLOOM LETTER, 14 NOV 1973`

followed later by

`BLOOM LEO STRAUSS ARTICLE -> POLITICAL THEORY, NOV 1974`.

This makes the letter a very high-value editorial-process target because it sits inside the manuscript/eulogy file itself.

But do **not** infer from sequence alone that Lamb solicited, commissioned, edited, or caused the final article.

Control:

`EDITOR LETTER BEFORE PUBLICATION != SOLICITATION / COMMISSION / REVISION CAUSATION`.

Only the original letter can identify the relation.

# VI. Why Bloom is especially useful for venue pluralism

Bloom's later 1974 article appears in the same issue as Haddock's `The History of Ideas and the Study of Politics` and immediately after the journal's August 1974 Skinner symposium.

Issue 2.4 directly juxtaposes:

- Bloom on Leo Strauss;
- Poster on Althusser;
- Hulliung on patriarchalism;
- Haddock on history of ideas and politics;
- Congleton on Plato;
- reviews and bibliography.

The Bloom recipient-side letter therefore offers a chance to test how one sharply distinctive philosophical orientation entered a venue whose February 1973 programme explicitly rejected exclusive identification with any one school.

Possible questions for the original Lamb letter:

- was Bloom invited to write specifically after Strauss's death in October 1973?
- how did Lamb describe the desired genre: eulogy, assessment, intellectual portrait, methodological intervention?
- did he invoke pluralism, dialogue, history of political thought, political philosophy, or a planned special cluster?
- was a deadline or issue placement already proposed?
- are editorial expectations or revisions stated?

Until direct inspection, all are questions rather than claims.
