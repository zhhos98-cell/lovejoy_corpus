# *Political Theory* 1973–1975: Skinner, democracy, method, and venue execution

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT ISSUE/METADATA CONTROL + DIRECT FIRST-PAGE IMAGES FOR SKINNER 1973 AND PATEMAN 1974 + DIRECT 1974 SYMPOSIUM ARCHITECTURE + DIRECT 1975 EDITORIAL CENSUS / FULL-TEXT CEILINGS EXPLICIT**

## Core result

A closer issue-by-issue reconstruction of *Political Theory*'s first three volumes makes a simple `Skinnerian contextualism institutionalized` story increasingly difficult to sustain.

The journal's early execution instead produces a rapid sequence across several problem domains:

`FOUNDING / VENUE CONSTRUCTION`
-> `EMPIRICAL DEMOCRATIC THEORY / VALUE-NEUTRAL POLITICAL SCIENCE`
-> `CRITIQUE OF SKINNER ON DEMOCRATIC THEORY`
-> `SKINNER METHOD SYMPOSIUM`
-> `HISTORY OF IDEAS METHOD AGGREGATION`
-> `EDITORIAL TAXONOMY / METATHEORY CENSUS`.

The same named actor, Quentin Skinner, moves across more than one role inside this sequence. His presence in the journal cannot safely be equated with one stable method category.

Compact rule:

`NAMED ACTOR IN VENUE`
!= `ONE METHOD ROLE`.

This is an execution-level counterpart to the 1975 editors' own warning that articles can be simultaneously historical/modern/methodological/conceptual.

## II. August 1973: Skinner appears as a critic of empirical democratic theory

Quentin Skinner, `The Empirical Theorists of Democracy and Their Critics: A Plague on Both Their Houses`, *Political Theory* 1.3 (Aug. 1973), 287–306.

SAGE metadata and the complete reference list are directly available; the first printed page is directly visible as a page image.

### Direct first-page problem framing

Skinner opens with the attack on the possibility of a purely empirical and value-neutral political science and treats the `empirical theory of democracy` as one prominent casualty of that attack.

He distinguishes the empirical theorists' scientific/neutral self-presentation from the now-common criticism that their inquiries carry normative implications.

This places Skinner's early *Political Theory* presence directly inside a jurisdictional dispute about:

- empirical political science;
- value neutrality;
- democratic theory;
- normative implications of ostensibly factual analysis.

This is **not merely a history-of-ideas-method article**.

### Direct manuscript-circulation edge

The author note on printed p.287 is especially valuable.

Skinner directly credits:

- John Burrow;
- John Dunn;
- John Thompson

with reading and commenting on successive drafts of the article.

This establishes a traceable prepublication circulation edge:

`SKINNER 1973 DEMOCRACY DRAFTS`
-> `BURROW + DUNN + THOMPSON READ/COMMENT`.

Evidence ceiling:

`DRAFT READING / COMMENT`
!= `FORMAL REFEREEING`
!= `EDITORIAL CONTROL`
!= `CAUSE OF SPECIFIC REVISION`.

The importance is cumulative. Dunn already appears in the earlier Skinner method-network evidence. The 1973 author note shows him again in a different substantive article's successive-draft circulation network.

Do not infer that Dunn supplied any specific democratic-theory claim without revision evidence.

### Reference architecture

The directly exposed reference list spans empirical political science, democratic theory, critics of behavioralism/value neutrality, linguistic philosophy, and contemporary political theory. It includes, among others, Dahl, Almond/Verba, Easton, Lipset, Sartori, Schumpeter, Duncan/Lukes, Charles Taylor, Searle, Hare, MacIntyre, and a 1972 review by John Dunn.

The article therefore visibly sits at an intersection of political-science and philosophical/methodological literatures.

## III. May 1974: Carole Pateman replies to Skinner in the same journal

Carole Pateman, `Criticising Empirical Theorists of Democracy: A Comment on Skinner`, *Political Theory* 2.2 (May 1974), 215–218.

SAGE metadata are direct and the first printed page, p.215, is directly visible as a page image.

Pateman begins by making the unresolved normative question explicit: after Skinner criticizes both empirical theorists and their critics, she asks what kind of democratic theory he regards as relevant or appropriate.

She agrees that the critics cited by Skinner have not fully substantiated their case, but argues that Skinner's own stronger presentation of the charge of conservative/ideological status does not by itself resolve the crucial dispute.

The direct first page therefore establishes:

`SKINNER 1973 ARTICLE`
-> `NAMED SAME-VENUE CRITICAL RESPONSE IN 1974`.

This is journal-mediated controversy in execution, not merely later canonical retrospective.

Again, only the first page is direct-inspected in the current environment; do not reconstruct the full four-page response from p.215 alone.

## IV. August 1974: method becomes an explicit symposium object

*Political Theory* 2.3 (Aug. 1974) is directly indexed by SAGE/JSTOR as a symposium on Quentin Skinner.

The architecture is:

1. Jonathan M. Wiener, `Quentin Skinner's Hobbes`, pp.251–260;
2. Gordon J. Schochet, `Quentin Skinner's Method`, pp.261–276;
3. Quentin Skinner, `Some Problems in the Analysis of Political Thought and Action`, pp.277–303.

The SAGE page for Skinner's response directly notes that another critique, by Charles Tarlton, also concerned the methodological writings of John Dunn and J. G. A. Pocock, even though Skinner confines his reply there to the portions about his own work.

The symposium thus makes `Skinner's method` an explicit editorially bundled controversy, rather than evidence that the journal had silently adopted the method as corporate doctrine.

Compact distinction:

`METHOD AS SYMPOSIUM OBJECT`
!= `METHOD AS VENUE ORTHODOXY`.

## VII. Revised Skinner carrier model

A retrospective method canon can make `Skinner in Political Theory` look primarily like the 1974 method symposium.

The 1973–75 issue sequence gives a less compressed carrier model:

### Skinner as democratic-theory / political-science boundary critic

1973 article on empirical theorists of democracy and value-neutral political science.

### Skinner as object of democratic-theory criticism

Pateman 1974 response.

### Skinner as explicit methodological controversy

Wiener + Schochet + Skinner symposium, Aug. 1974.

### Skinner as one participant in wider method aggregation

Haddock 1974 reference/problem architecture.

Therefore:

`SKINNER CARRIER`
!= `CONTEXTUALIST METHOD ONLY`.

A more precise model is:

`ACTOR PORTABILITY ACROSS PROBLEM DOMAINS`
+
`VENUE-BASED CONTROVERSY AGGREGATION`.

This distinction should matter when later anthologies such as Tully 1988 select primarily method texts and thereby create a portable methodological Skinner canon.

`LATER CANON SELECTION`
!= `FULL CONTEMPORARY VENUE ROLE`.
