# JHI Blog v3.4 object-category audit: `Buddhism` as topic label versus historical unit

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **FULL USAGE AUDIT / ONE MICRO-CORRECTION RECOMMENDED / TITLE AND CORE OBJECT LANGUAGE RETAINED**

## Core result

The H&T 1968 Baird control raises a useful calibration question for the JHI Blog: does the essay itself ever treat `Buddhism` as a ready-made homogeneous historical unit rather than a topic/material label?

A re-read of v3.4 shows that almost all uses are properly localized:

- `Buddhist technical terms` = description of the 1898 article's objects;
- Warren's `Buddhist formula of dependent origination` = a specific textual/doctrinal formula;
- `Buddhist classifications` = particular classificatory schemes being compared;
- Senart's `Buddhist formula` / terms `originally Buddhist` = historical provenance claims under examination;
- `Buddhist and Indian materials` = explicitly plural source/material field;
- `primitive Buddhism` = Lovejoy's own later comparative wording;
- `whether he once studied Buddhism` = biographical fact/problem framing.

These uses do not require revision.

One phrase does:

> `The question raised by Lovejoy's Buddhist notebooks...`

The article itself has already established that notebook 005 is not primarily a Buddhist/Pāli notebook: it has a strong Paris comparative-religion core and turns toward death, ritual, afterlife, sacrifice and social practice.

Calling the two notebooks collectively `Buddhist notebooks` therefore re-homogenizes a material set that the body has deliberately differentiated.

Recommended micro-edit:

> `The question raised by these notebooks is therefore not simply whether he once studied Buddhism.`

This is both stylistically cleaner and analytically safer.

---

## I. Why the title can remain

`What Was Arthur Lovejoy Doing with Buddhism?` remains a good title.

It poses the historiographic problem rather than defining a stable historical unit. The essay's answer repeatedly decomposes that broad label into:

- technical terms;
- textual witnesses;
- classifications;
- chronology;
- genealogy;
- comparative religion;
- ritual/social practice;
- later comparative uses.

Thus the title functions as an entry question whose apparent unity the article then tests.

`TOPIC LABEL`
!= `ASSUMED HOMOGENEOUS HISTORICAL UNIT`.

No title change recommended.

---

## II. `Buddhist episode` is acceptable because the sentence immediately historicizes that classification

Early v3.4 says:

`Nor was this Buddhist episode simply forgotten.`

This could look like a bounded-unit formulation in isolation. In context, however, the following sentences explicitly distinguish:

- surviving biographical fact;
- scholarly work inside that fact;
- later analytical classification.

The phrase therefore names the **biographical episode as later remembered**, not a claim that the research materials themselves form one methodologically homogeneous object.

No change necessary.

If later copyediting seeks a flatter phrase, `this Buddhist work` is possible, but it creates repetitive `work ... work inside the fact` wording and gains little analytically.

---

## III. The 005 correction is proposition-internal, not imported from Baird

The reason to change `Buddhist notebooks` is not that Baird 1968 retrospectively dictates the correct vocabulary.

The correction is licensed internally by the Lovejoy evidence already in the blog:

- notebook 004 is explicitly `Sankhya + Buddhism`;
- notebook 005 has a strong Paris core and materially different objects;
- the blog says the objects in 005 are no longer primarily Pāli terms.

Thus:

`004 TOPIC LABEL`
!= `005 OBJECT IDENTITY`.

The H&T/Baird evidence merely prompted an audit that exposed an internal overaggregation.

This is exactly the kind of calibration the project should permit:

`LATER METHOD CONTROL`
-> `CHECK OUR CATEGORY USE`
without
`LATER METHOD CONTROL -> HISTORICAL GENEALOGY CLAIM`.

---

## IV. Relation to UNIT×RELATION

The micro-correction implements the existing codebook rule in public prose:

> **UNIT TYPE BELONGS TO THE CLAIM, NOT ONCE-FOR-ALL TO THE PHYSICAL OBJECT.**

The two notebooks can be part of one essay without becoming one homogeneous historical unit.

Likewise a notebook can contain several proposition-level units.

Changing the final phrase to `these notebooks` preserves material continuity without assigning one topic ontology to both.
