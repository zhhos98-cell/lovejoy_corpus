## Trace 23 — 10 Apr 1897 Final Circular separates paper announcement from expected presence

Direct image inspection of the University of Chicago recipient copy of the AOS **Final Circular, dated 10 Apr 1897** fixes two different administrative lists on adjacent pages:

1. the `LIST OF PAPERS ANNOUNCED TO DATE`, where No. 24 is `Mr. Arthur O. Lovejoy, Harvard University; Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`;
2. a separate list introduced as members and friends whom, `From the answers to circulars it is hoped` will be present at Baltimore. Lovejoy is not in that expected-presence list.

Combined with the already controlled 18 Mar Preliminary Circular, which asks intending contributors to send Hanns Oertel **their names and the titles of their papers**, this sharpens the 1897 procedural model. The Society was tracking at least two separable states: a communication/title could be announced while the contributor was not listed as expected to attend. The minimum secure pre-10-Apr Lovejoy carrier therefore remains a **name + title notice**. The Final Circular does not supply evidence that an abstract or full manuscript had already been transmitted.

Source: University of Chicago Library, Office of the President recipient file, Box 7 Folder 13, `ofcpreshjb-0007-013.pdf`, direct screenshot inspection of the 1897 Final Circular.

Assessment: **PROCEDURAL MODEL REFINED / TITLE-SUBMISSION AND EXPECTED ATTENDANCE ARE SEPARATE STATES.**

## Trace 24 — Wilson MS-0873 is now a remote-reproduction-ready, binder-segment target

The JHU finding aid for `Daniel J. Wilson transcriptions of Arthur O. Lovejoy letters`, MS-0873, collapses the collection to one physical box but gives a useful internal structure:

- **five binders**, arranged chronologically from 1872 to 1962;
- most professional correspondence begins in **1894**;
- about **500 items / 1.25 cubic feet** total;
- originals represented are mostly JHU Lovejoy papers, with additional material transcribed from Harvard, Stanford, Washington University, and a few other sources;
- Wilson cautioned that the transcripts can contain minor errors and should be checked against originals;
- the collection is off-site and requires 48 hours' retrieval notice;
- JHU explicitly states that **single copies may be made for research purposes**.

The public finding aid exposes no item-level 1897 list. Operationally, the request should therefore be for the **smallest binder segment containing March–April 1897, with concentrated inspection around 10–24 Apr 1897**, rather than for all five binders or all ~500 items. If reproduction staff report a segment above the project's 60-page confirmation threshold, confirm scope before ordering.

Search terms inside the tranche remain: `American Oriental Society`, `AOS`, `Milinda`, `Pali/Pāli`, `Buddhist`, `upādāna`, `Lanman`, `Oertel`, `Moore`, `Gilman`, `Baltimore`.

## Trace 25 — Yale RU 120 exposes direct AOS–University Librarian correspondence, 1905–1917

The Yale `Librarian, Yale University records`, RU 120, contains a substantially earlier AOS correspondence sequence than the 1940–1959 subject file previously isolated. Under `Accession 19ND-A-109: Correspondence of John C. Schwab, 1861–1918 (bulk 1904–1917)`, the finding aid lists:

- **Box 65, Folder 922 — American Oriental Society, 1905–1909**;
- **Box 65, Folder 923 — American Oriental Society, 1909–1912**;
- **Box 65, Folder 924 — American Oriental Society, 1912–1914**;
- **Box 65, Folder 925 — American Oriental Society, 1911–1917**.

This sequence predates the 1919 Lanman resolution placing the Society's `manuscript books of record` in the Society Library. It therefore becomes the best currently identified Yale-side carrier for reconstructing how AOS library/record material was already being administered immediately before 1919. It does not extend back to 1897 and does not by itself show that a Society record book or Lovejoy manuscript was transferred through these folders.

A second RU 120 metadata cluster remains useful for later reclassification history: Box 116 Folder 1591 `American Oriental Society, 1940–1959`, adjacent to manuscript-management files including `Checklist for Mss in Yale Memorabilia`, `Checklist of Mss in Vault 107`, and a `List of manuscript accessions made from slips in order depts., 1935–1939`.

Source: Yale University Library, `Guide to the Librarian, Yale University, Records`, RU 120, `https://ead-pdfs.library.yale.edu/4568.pdf`, direct PDF inspection.

Assessment: **YALE CUSTODY ROUTE MOVED BACK TO 1905 / HIGH-VALUE PRE-1919 INSTITUTIONAL CARRIER.**

## Trace 26 — 1930 AOS library catalogue and legacy Yale location code create a catalogue-archaeology route

Two independent Yale/library controls add search vocabulary to the custody route:

1. In **1930**, Yale University Library published the 308-page `Catalogue of the Library of the American Oriental Society`, edited by Elizabeth Strout. Later manuscript scholarship cites entries in this catalogue for AOS-held Sanskrit and Persian manuscripts, so the catalogue is not merely a printed-books list. It is a fixed historical snapshot worth checking for how manuscript holdings were described and whether institutional record books were included, excluded, or handled separately. No `Letter Book II` or 1897 record-book hit has yet been recovered from the searchable web layer.
2. Yale's retrospective-conversion documentation maps the old card-catalog location **American Oriental Society** to Voyager location code **`smlaos`** and OCLC holding code **`YU4V`**. These are now concrete catalogue-archaeology search tokens for legacy AOS records. They should not be treated as manuscript shelfmarks.

Sources: `Catalogue of the Library of the American Oriental Society` (New Haven: Yale University Library, 1930), public Google Books/Google Play record; Yale University Library, `Retrospective Conversion: CJK — Catalog Location Guide`, entry `American Oriental Society = smlaos / YU4V`.

Assessment: **NEW METADATA SEARCH TOKENS / NO 1897 RECORD-BOOK IDENTIFICATION YET.**
