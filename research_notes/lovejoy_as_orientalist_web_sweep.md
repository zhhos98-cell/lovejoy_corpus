## Working question

How far can Arthur O. Lovejoy be reconstructed as an Orientalist / student and teacher of Asian religions and philosophies, especially Indian philosophy and early Buddhism, and how did this work relate to his later method of decomposing intellectual systems into historically mobile elements?

The term “Orientalist” is used here as a research category to be tested, not assumed. A strict core will include Lovejoy’s work on India, Buddhism, Pāli/Sanskrit technical vocabulary, Asian philosophies, courses, institutional affiliations, and specialist reception. An adjacent layer will include comparative religion / “primitive philosophy” where the same philological and comparative apparatus is demonstrably continuous.

## Confirmed primary corpus so far

### 1. 1898 — “The Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa”

- Arthur Oncken Lovejoy, *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 19 (1898), 126–136.
- Affiliation printed in the article: Harvard University, Cambridge, Mass.
- Full text online at NTU Buddhist Studies database: https://buddhism.lib.ntu.edu.tw/FULLTEXT/JR-ENG/love.htm
- Local/repo copy already present: `The Buddhistic technical terms upadana and upadisesa..html`.
- The paper treats `upādāna` as a pivotal link in `paṭicca-samuppāda`, argues against Senart’s identification of `upādāna` with the five skandhas, and uses the Pāli Piṭakas to distinguish the terms and reconstruct the internal structure of the causation formula.
- Direct archival bridge: notebook `MS38_004_001_061_004 — “Sankhya + Buddhism”`, now completely first-pass transcribed in four JSON batches. Its later pages are demonstrably preparatory/contemporaneous working material for the 1898 article.

### 2. 1901 — “Syllabus: The Philosophy of Buddhism”

- Daniel J. Wilson’s annotated bibliography records a Washington University Association syllabus for four lectures.
- Lecture titles:
  1. “The Place of Buddhism among the Philosophies of India”
  2–3. “Two Essential Formulas of Buddhism”
  4. “Buddhist Ethics and its Goal”
- Current discovery source: Wilson, *Arthur O. Lovejoy: An Annotated Bibliography*, entry 243, surfaced online via Scribd/search indexing.
- This is strong evidence that Lovejoy’s Buddhist work continued into his Washington University teaching, at least through 1901.
- Priority: locate an independent scan/catalog record or surviving syllabus copy.

### 3. 1906 — “The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy”

- Arthur O. Lovejoy, *The Monist* 16.3 (July 1906), 357–382. DOI: 10.5840/monist190616324.
- Oxford Academic confirms title, author, Washington University affiliation, issue and pagination: https://academic.oup.com/monist/article/16/3/357/995254
- Wilson’s annotated bibliography describes the paper as a critique of anthropomorphic accounts of “primitive” religion, emphasizing quasi-mechanical relations with supernatural power and comparing terminology among American Indians, Melanesians/Polynesians, and Bantu-speaking peoples.
- This belongs to an adjacent comparative-religion / ethnological layer rather than the strict Indian/Buddhist core, but it may preserve the same analytic technique: semantic comparison, decomposition of categories, critique of over-unified systems.

## Archival evidence

### Johns Hopkins, Arthur Oncken Lovejoy papers, MS-0038

Official finding aid: https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/54

- Papers span 1872–1963 and include lecture notes, notebooks, drafts, typescripts, galley proofs, correspondence, and annotated books.
- The JHU biographical note states that Lovejoy’s undergraduate course at Berkeley emphasized humanities and languages, that his principal interest shifted from religion to philosophy under George Holmes Howison, and that he studied at Harvard under Josiah Royce and William James before studying at the Sorbonne in 1898–99.
- The finding aid explicitly says that during the early part of the twentieth century he published on the history of religion as well as philosophy.
- Processing history records Marjorie Nicolson’s observation that Lovejoy’s research notes often formed the basis of published articles.
- This archive is therefore central for reconstructing the transition notebook → article → lecture/course.

## Biographical / disciplinary recognition

### Persons of Indian Studies (Klaus Karttunen)

Entry: https://whowaswho-indology.info/3895/lovejoy-arthur-oncken/

- Lovejoy has a dedicated entry in a specialist biographical reference for persons connected with Indian Studies.
- The entry lists the 1898 JAOS paper as his Indological publication and says that he apparently did not return to Buddhist studies. This latter statement is now demonstrably too narrow if “return” includes teaching: Wilson records the 1901 four-lecture Buddhism syllabus.
- Research implication: distinguish publication record from teaching/research notebooks. Lovejoy’s formal Indological publication footprint may be tiny while his unpublished/teaching engagement was larger.

## Later specialist reception already located

- Contemporary/modern Buddhist scholarship continues to cite the 1898 paper. Examples surfaced in searches include work on `upādāna`, `nibbāna/parinibbāna`, and comparative Nikāya studies.
- This reception must be mapped separately from generic history-of-ideas reception: who cites Lovejoy as a Buddhist philologist, for what proposition, and whether his interpretation is accepted, rejected, or merely noted.

## Immediate hypotheses to test

1. **Short formal publication career, longer research/teaching afterlife.** The single 1898 JAOS article may understate Lovejoy’s Orientalist activity because archival notebooks and the 1901 lecture syllabus extend it.
2. **A methodological bridge exists before “unit-ideas.”** Notebook 004 and the 1898 paper both decompose an apparently coherent doctrinal system into terms with different histories/functions and then reconstruct an intelligible arrangement.
3. **Comparative religion is a second bridge.** The 1906 Monist article may connect Buddhist/Indian philology to Lovejoy’s later work on primitivism, but this must be demonstrated textually rather than assumed.
4. **Institutional formation is unresolved.** We still need to identify where Lovejoy acquired enough Pāli/Sanskrit/Indological competence to work directly with specialist literature: Harvard course records, teachers, reading networks, Paris/Sorbonne/Bibliothèque Nationale, or self-directed study.
