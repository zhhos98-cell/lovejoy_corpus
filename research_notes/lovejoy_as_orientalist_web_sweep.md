## Research question

How far can Arthur O. Lovejoy be reconstructed as an Orientalist / student and teacher of Asian religions and philosophies, especially Indian philosophy and early Buddhism, and how did this work feed into comparative religion and the later history of ideas?

`Orientalist` is used as a research category specifying forms of training and practice, not as a permanent career label. The **strict core** includes Sanskrit/Buddhist-text training, Indian philosophy, Pāli/Sanskrit vocabulary, specialist publication/review/teaching. An **adjacent comparative-religion layer** includes “primitive religion/philosophy,” sacrifice, eschatology, magic and causal efficacy. A **mature global/transmission layer** includes China, India and ancient Western Asia when they enter later history-of-ideas projects through representation, transmission or specialist collaboration.

## Current chronology and evidentiary spine

### 1895–1898 — Harvard: formal Orientalist/comparative-religion formation

Daniel J. Wilson, using a Harvard Archives course list, the 1896–97 Harvard catalogue and Lovejoy’s 1896 letters, records that Lovejoy studied **Sanskrit and some sacred Buddhist texts with Charles R. Lanman**, **comparative religion with Charles C. Everett** (Vedic religion, Hinduism, Buddhism, Mazdaism, Chinese religions), and **Hebrew religion with Crawford H. Toy**. Harvard’s Lanman/Warren/Harvard Oriental Series environment independently fits this formation. Wilson also records Lovejoy as a member/later secretary of the Graduate Philosophical Society; Vivekananda addressed that Society on Vedānta on 25 March 1896, but Lovejoy’s attendance at that particular lecture remains unproved.

Remaining linguistic question: separate formal Pāli instruction and Lovejoy’s degree of independent Pāli competence.

### c.1896–1898 — notebook 004 → 1898 JAOS

`MS38_004_001_061_004 — “Sankhya + Buddhism”` is 71/71 first-pass complete. It moves through Sāṁkhya method, Buddhist agnosticism, impermanence/flux, chronological priority, dependent origination, khandhas, `upādāna/upādhi/upādisesa`, and Nirvāṇa.

It directly feeds Lovejoy, **“The Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa,” *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 19 (1898), 126–136**. The published paper extracts a narrow philological problem from a much broader notebook. The notebook closes with Lovejoy’s own decomposition: original elements = flux/Three Characteristics + dependent origination; derived elements = sense-perception theory + psychology + khandhas.

Methodological lead: historically borrowed/composite elements need not form an accidental heap; their arrangement may still be characteristic and intelligible.

### 1898–1899 — Paris: Lévi + Marillier

Wilson, citing Lovejoy’s letters of 17 October, 1 December and 5 December 1898, says Lovejoy hoped to take **Pāli Buddhism with Sylvain Lévi** and, when that course was unavailable, **occasionally went to Lévi’s home to read sacred texts with him**. He also studied Psalms with Maurice Vernes and heard Jean Réville. Exact texts/languages still require the letters themselves.

The official EPHE religious-sciences report independently names **Lovejoy among auditors who took an active part in Léon Marillier’s conferences**. Those conferences treated survival of the soul, non-moral future-life beliefs, human sacrifice, ritual anthropophagy, and funerary/expiatory/magical/fecundative mechanisms using African, North-American and non-Aryan Indian evidence.

Marillier’s **“La survivance de l’âme et l’idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés”** (1893; separate 1894 printing) has been identified as the source behind 005 abbreviations `M. Surviv.` / `Surviv.`.

### 1898–1905 — notebook 005 is longitudinal

`MS38_004_001_061_005` is 120/120 first-pass complete. It has a strong Paris 1898–99 core: Paris stationery, `Dec. 14`, `May 29`, `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`, and close overlap with Marillier’s programme.

A controlled second pass securely resolves PDF p.44 as **`Hist. Relig. — Dec. 20, 1905.`** The archival JSON itself has been corrected. The page continues primitive religion, Old Testament material, `psychē`, `nephesh/ruach`, ancestor worship and totemism. Thus 005 was reused/extended at least through 1905; page order cannot simply be equated with composition chronology.

Current model: **Marillier/EPHE comparative source collection (1898–99) → continued `Hist. Relig.` notebook work documented in 1905 → theoretical condensation in 1906.** The Washington University course/lecture behind the 1905 heading remains unidentified.

### 1901 — Buddhism becomes teaching

Wilson records **“Syllabus: The Philosophy of Buddhism,” Washington University Association, 1901**, four lectures: place of Buddhism among Indian philosophies; two essential formulas; Buddhist ethics and its goal. The original syllabus remains to be located.

### 1902 — technical Buddhist philology becomes history of a category

The full primary text of **“Religion and the Time-Process,” *American Journal of Theology* 6 (1902), 439–472**, has been recovered. Lovejoy opens with changing **categories** in the history of philosophical/theological opinion, makes **Vedānta and Buddhism the first major analytic case**, and explicitly sends readers back to his own 1898 `upādāna/upādisesa` paper.

Authorial bridge: **004 → 1898 Buddhist technical philology → 1902 comparative history of a category.**

The paper also supplies a methodological control on diffusion: conceptual resemblance between Aristotle and Vedānta does not establish borrowing where an internal Greek genealogy accounts for the conception. Across Greek, Christian and modern thought Lovejoy follows a recurring `perfectionist` presupposition/logical motive and decomposes Royce into competing inherited categories.

### 1906 — full primary “Primitive Philosophy” recovered and concorded

A complete primary PDF of **“The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy,” *The Monist* 16.3 (1906), 357–382**, has been recovered and read directly against 005.

The paper attacks the anthropomorphic/animist master theory, says heterogeneous “primitive” practices possess a recoverable **logic**, and posits a **category more fundamental than personality**: an impersonal, quasi-mechanical conception of causal efficacy. Energy/power is diffusive, unequally localized, transferable between persons/things, transmissible by contact, and subject to ritual control or insulation. Lovejoy explicitly rejects Frazer’s sharp magic/religion division and tests the category lexically through North-American `manitou/oki/wakonda`, Oceanic `mana`, and Bantu power-language.

Direct 005 concordance is strong:

- p.64: magical sacrifice operates “in a sort of machine” and can act directly on natural forces rather than divine wills;
- pp.88–90: fetish/indwelling spirit vs charm/amulet/object **intrinsic efficacy**;
- pp.84–90 and p.119: blood/body parts carry transferable qualities/power;
- pp.98–105: agricultural/fecundative rites transfer life/power rather than necessarily produce communion;
- p.105: **“quasi-mechanical and magical rather than sacramental”**;
- p.117: sacramental union comparatively exceptional;
- shared/adjacent evidence fields include Brinton, Codrington/Melanesia, Bantu/southern Africa and North-American material.

Strongest current formulation: **005 preserves a longitudinal empirical/classificatory laboratory, begun in the Marillier/EPHE setting and still active in 1905, from which the 1906 paper isolates and systematizes a cross-cutting category of impersonal, transferable, quasi-mechanical efficacy/life-energy.** This is stronger than thematic resemblance but short of proving the whole notebook is a literal article draft.

### 1907 — Indic and Hebrew strands remain active

Lovejoy reviewed Paul Deussen, **_Outline of the Vedānta System of Philosophy, According to Shankara_**, in *Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* 4.1 (1907), 23–24. Metadata is confirmed; Wilson says Lovejoy regarded the outline as useful for teachers of Hindu philosophy and urged more attention to **Rāmānuja**. The exact primary two-page review still needs extraction.

The full primary text of **“The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought,” *American Journal of Theology* 11 (1907), 228–249**, has been recovered. Lovejoy explicitly follows **Professor Toy’s authority** for the relative sequence of Wisdom literature and distinguishes **logical sequence** from absolute/relative chronology, allowing earlier intellectual stages to survive beside later ones. This makes a controlled 005(1905 Hebrew layer) → 1907 concordance a priority.

### 1907–1908 — sustained history-of-religion programme

The strand continues through **“The Entangling Alliance of Religion and History”** (1907), **“Pragmatism and Theology”** (1908), **“Religious Transition and Ethical Awakening in America”** (1908), and **“The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus”** (1908). W. Benjamin Smith replied to the last in *The Monist* 19.3 (1909). Primary close reading of the 1908/1909 debate remains pending.

## Open Court / *The Monist*: a third institutional ecology

Batches 13–14 add a new axis distinct from Harvard formation and Paris training: **publication/editorial circulation**.

The 1906 *Monist* issue places Lovejoy’s paper between Friedrich Hirth on the mariners’ compass in China and Lawrence H. Mills on the Bible, Persian inscriptions and Avesta. The 1908 issue containing Lovejoy’s Smith essay-review also carries D. T. Suzuki on early Chinese philosophy, Berthold Laufer on the Jonah legend in India, and religion-classification material. This shows the journal’s mixed philosophy/Orientalist-comparative-religion ecology; neighboring articles are context, not evidence of direct influence.

Most importantly, **Southern Illinois University Carbondale MSS 027, Open Court Publishing Company Records** is now a high-priority archive target. The 424-box collection preserves editorial/business correspondence and article/book manuscripts for *The Monist* and *The Open Court* during the Carus period; its language metadata includes Chinese, Japanese and Pāli; and its collection-level person indexing explicitly includes **Arthur O. Lovejoy (1873–1962)**.

A targeted folder-level web sweep has not surfaced a dedicated `Correspondence - Publishing: Lovejoy` record or exact manuscript title. Series 1, Sub-Series 6 is alphabetically arranged publishing correspondence; the public Box 44 listing reaches through `L`, Lanman, Laufer, Leuba and Lloyd, but the web interface does not expose where Lovejoy itself sits. Therefore **Lovejoy’s presence in MSS 027 is confirmed at collection level; exact letters/manuscripts/proofs remain unresolved.**

Best archive request: ask SIU for every MSS 027 item/folder indexed to Arthur O. Lovejoy, especially Series 1/Sub-Series 6 Publishing and Series 2 Manuscripts, c.1905–1909, and specifically the titles **“The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy”** and **“The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus.”**

This yields three distinct early ecologies:

1. **Harvard:** Lanman/Warren/Everett/Toy — formal language/text/religion formation.
2. **Paris/EPHE:** Lévi/Marillier — Buddhist reading and comparative-religion source/classification work.
3. **Open Court/*The Monist*:** editorial-publication network actively circulating Asian philosophy, Buddhism, philology and comparative religion.

The third is a circulation/editorial ecology, not a teacher-student relationship.

### 1933 — China as transmission/representation

**“The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism”** is best treated, on current source analysis, as history of cross-cultural transmission and European representations of China rather than direct Sinological philology. Its source architecture is overwhelmingly European-language: Jesuit reports/translations, Mendoza, Kircher, Leibniz, Voltaire, Temple, Le Comte, Attiret, Chambers and garden/aesthetic literature. Exact 1933 vs 1948 comparison remains pending.

### 1935 — mature expertise becomes distributed

The Preface to Lovejoy and Boas, *Primitivism and Related Ideas in Antiquity*, explicitly thanks **W. F. Albright** and **P.-E. Dumont** for supplementary essays extending the project into Ancient Western Asia and Indian literature. Mature Lovejoy thus also organizes a visible division of linguistic/area expertise rather than personally performing all Orientalist philology.

## Current evidentiary model

1. **1895–1901: direct Orientalist/comparative-religion formation and practice.**
2. **1898–1908: sustained transformation rather than abandonment:** technical philology, comparative religion, category-history, causal-efficacy theory, Vedānta review and Jewish/Christian history of religion overlap.
3. **1930s: mediated/distributed global history of ideas:** China as representation/transmission; India and ancient Western Asia through specialist collaboration.

The strongest current thesis is not “Lovejoy was continuously an Orientalist.” It is that **Orientalist and comparative-religion training formed an early laboratory for analytical operations later redistributed into history of ideas**: decomposition of apparent systems, semantic discrimination, historical layering, logical versus chronological sequencing, classification by causal mechanism, caution about borrowing/syncretism, and reconstruction of intelligible arrangements. By 1902 and 1906 Lovejoy’s own vocabulary already includes `categories`, `presuppositions`, `logical motives`, recoverable `logic`, `system`, `connectedness`, and analytical correlation. The mature `unit-idea` vocabulary should not be projected backward as already fully formed.
