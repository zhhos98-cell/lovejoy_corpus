## Working question

How far can Arthur O. Lovejoy be reconstructed as an Orientalist / student and teacher of Asian religions and philosophies, especially Indian philosophy and early Buddhism, and how did this work feed into comparative religion and the later history of ideas?

`Orientalist` is a research category specifying forms of training and practice, not a permanent career label. The **strict core** includes Sanskrit/Buddhist-text training, Indian philosophy, Pāli/Sanskrit vocabulary, specialist publication/review/teaching. An **adjacent comparative-religion layer** includes “primitive religion/philosophy,” sacrifice, eschatology, magic and causal efficacy. A **mature global/transmission layer** includes China, India and ancient Western Asia when they enter later history-of-ideas projects through representation, transmission or specialist collaboration.

## Current chronology

### 1895–1898 — Harvard: formal Orientalist/comparative-religion formation

Daniel J. Wilson, using a Harvard Archives course list, the 1896–97 Harvard catalogue and Lovejoy’s 1896 letters, records that Lovejoy studied:

- **Sanskrit and some sacred Buddhist texts with Charles R. Lanman**;
- **comparative religion with Charles C. Everett**, including Vedic religion, Hinduism, Buddhism, Mazdaism and Chinese religions;
- **Hebrew religion with Crawford H. Toy**;
- Everett’s course on the psychological basis of religious faith.

Harvard’s institutional ecology fits this formation: Lanman led Sanskrit/Indo-Iranian studies; Lanman and Henry Clarke Warren created the Harvard Oriental Series; Warren’s *Buddhism in Translations* appeared in 1896 and became a major Lovejoy authority; Lanman publicly lectured on Buddhism, Sāṁkhya/Vedānta and Sanskrit/Pāli sources in April 1896. Wilson also records Lovejoy as a member/later secretary of the Graduate Philosophical Society; Vivekananda addressed that Society on 25 March 1896. Lovejoy’s attendance at that particular lecture remains unproved.

Remaining linguistic question: separate formal Pāli instruction and the degree of Lovejoy’s independent Pāli competence.

### c.1896–1898 — notebook 004 and the 1898 Buddhist paper

`MS38_004_001_061_004 — “Sankhya + Buddhism”` is complete first pass, 71/71. It treats Sāṁkhya method, Buddhist agnosticism, impermanence/flux, chronological priority, dependent origination, khandhas, `upādāna/upādhi/upādisesa`, Nirvāṇa and related philology.

The notebook directly feeds Lovejoy, **“The Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa,” *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 19 (1898), 126–136.** The published paper extracts the late `upādāna/upādisesa` problem from a broader notebook project. The notebook closes with an explicit historical decomposition: original elements = flux/Three Characteristics + dependent origination; derived elements = sense-perception theory + psychology + khandhas.

Methodological lead: historically borrowed/composite terms need not form an accidental heap; their arrangement may still be characteristic and intelligible.

### 1898–1899 — Paris: Lévi + Marillier

Wilson, citing Lovejoy’s letters of 17 October, 1 December and 5 December 1898, says Lovejoy studied Psalms with Maurice Vernes, heard Jean Réville, hoped to take **Pāli Buddhism with Sylvain Lévi**, and when the course was not offered **occasionally went to Lévi’s home to read sacred texts with him**. Exact texts and languages still require the letters.

The official EPHE religious-sciences report for 1898–99 independently names **Lovejoy among auditors who took an active part in Léon Marillier’s conferences**. These covered survival of the soul, non-moral future-life beliefs, human sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy through funerary, expiatory, magical and fecundative mechanisms and African/North-American/non-Aryan-Indian evidence.

Marillier’s earlier **“La survivance de l’âme et l’idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés”** (1893; separate printing 1894) has been identified as the source behind 005 abbreviations `M. Surviv.` / `Surviv.`. Earlier Marillier course reports on origin-of-death myths, death as non-natural, post-mortem continuation and social rank also map closely onto 005.

### 1898–1905 — notebook 005 is longitudinal

`MS38_004_001_061_005` is complete first pass, 120/120. It contains a strong Paris 1898–99 core: Paris stationery, `Dec. 14`, `May 29`, `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`, and close overlap with Marillier’s EPHE programme.

A controlled second pass has now securely read PDF p.44 as **`Hist. Relig. — Dec. 20, 1905.`** The archival JSON itself has been corrected. The page continues primitive religion, Old Testament material, `psychē`, `nephesh/ruach`, ancestor worship and totemism. Thus the notebook was reused or extended at least through 1905; page order cannot simply be equated with composition chronology.

Current production model: **Marillier/EPHE comparative source collection (1898–99) → continued `Hist. Relig.` notebook work documented in 1905 → theoretical condensation in 1906.** The Washington University course/lecture behind the 1905 heading remains unidentified.

### 1901 — Buddhism becomes teaching

Wilson records **“Syllabus: The Philosophy of Buddhism,” Washington University Association, 1901**, four lectures: (1) place of Buddhism among Indian philosophies, (2–3) two essential formulas, (4) Buddhist ethics and its goal. The original syllabus object remains to be located.

### 1902 — Buddhist philology becomes comparative history of a category

The full primary text of **“Religion and the Time-Process,” *American Journal of Theology* 6 (1902), 439–472**, has been recovered. Lovejoy opens with changing **categories** in the history of philosophical/theological opinion and makes **Vedānta and Buddhism the first major analytic case**. Buddhist impermanence and dependent origination explain one motive for rejecting temporal process, and at the key passage Lovejoy explicitly sends readers back to his own 1898 `upādāna/upādisesa` paper.

Authorial bridge: **004 → 1898 Buddhist technical philology → 1902 comparative history of a category.**

The paper also supplies an anti-diffusionist methodological control: Lovejoy notes affinities between Aristotle and Vedānta but rejects eastern borrowing where the Greek conception can be reconstructed internally. Across Greek, Christian and modern thought he traces a recurring `perfectionist` presupposition/logical motive and decomposes Royce into competing inherited categories. He also notes that coherent philosophical formulation may be the last rather than first stage in the development of an insight.

### 1906 — full primary “Primitive Philosophy” recovered

A complete primary PDF of **“The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy,” *The Monist* 16.3 (1906), 357–382**, is now in the project File Library and has been read directly against 005. This removes the largest prior evidentiary bottleneck.

The published paper attacks the anthropomorphic/animist master theory, says heterogeneous “primitive” practices possess a recoverable **logic**, and posits a **category more fundamental than personality**: an impersonal, quasi-mechanical conception of causal efficacy. The energy/power is diffusive, unequally localized, transferable between persons/things, transmissible by contact, and subject to ritual control or insulation. Lovejoy explicitly rejects Frazer’s sharp magic/religion division and argues that the same **diffusive and transferable life-energy** organizes observances conventionally placed on both sides of that boundary. He then tests this lexically through North-American `manitou/oki/wakonda`, Oceanic `mana`, and Bantu power-language, warning that ethnographers often translate power/efficacy terms too quickly as gods or spirits.

The 005 concordance is now strong at the level of operations, vocabulary and source ecology:

- p.64: magical sacrifice operates “in a sort of machine” and may act directly on natural forces rather than divine wills;
- pp.88–90: fetish/indwelling spirit is separated from charm/amulet/object **intrinsic efficacy**;
- pp.84–90 and p.119: blood/body parts carry direct, transferable qualities or power;
- pp.98–105: agricultural/fecundative rites transfer life/power rather than necessarily produce communion;
- p.105: **“quasi-mechanical and magical rather than sacramental”**;
- p.117: sacramental union is comparatively exceptional;
- common evidence fields include Brinton, Codrington/Melanesia, Bantu/southern Africa and North-American material.

The published article itself says its aim is less to add ethnological facts than to correlate familiar data and bring out their significance. That self-description fits 005 as a longitudinal empirical/classificatory dossier. Strongest current formulation: **005 preserves an empirical laboratory, begun in the Marillier/EPHE setting and still in use in 1905, from which the 1906 paper isolates and systematizes a transversal category of impersonal, transferable, quasi-mechanical efficacy/life-energy.** This is stronger than thematic resemblance but still short of proving that the whole notebook is a literal article draft.

See `research_notes/lovejoy_as_orientalist_web_sweep_batch12_1906_primary_concordance.md` and the updated `MS38_004_001_061_005_vs_1906_Primitive_Philosophy_working_comparison.md`.

### 1907 — Indian philosophy remains active

Lovejoy reviewed Paul Deussen, **_Outline of the Vedānta System of Philosophy, According to Shankara_**, in *Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* 4.1 (1907), 23–24. PhilPapers confirms the metadata; Wilson says Lovejoy regarded the English outline as useful for teachers of Hindu philosophy and urged more work on **Rāmānuja’s Vedānta** rather than continued concentration on Śaṅkara. The public-domain 1907 journal volume has been located; exact two-page text still needs extraction.

### 1907 — Toy continuity and logical vs chronological sequence

The full primary text of **“The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought,” *American Journal of Theology* 11 (1907), 228–249**, has been recovered. Lovejoy explicitly accepts the relative sequence of Wisdom literature on **Professor Toy’s authority**, directly carrying a Harvard teacher into published 1907 work.

The paper also distinguishes **logical sequence** from absolute/relative chronology, noting that different intellectual stages can coexist and older phases can survive beside later ones. It reconstructs ethical inwardness as an indirect, gradual development through heterogeneous Wisdom texts. This makes a controlled 005(1905 Hebrew/`Hist. Relig.` layer) → 1907 concordance a priority.

### 1907–1908 — sustained history-of-religion programme

The strand continues through **“The Entangling Alliance of Religion and History”** (1907), **“Pragmatism and Theology”** (1908), **“Religious Transition and Ethical Awakening in America”** (1908), and **“The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus”** (1908). The last tests W. Benjamin Smith’s proposed grand syncretic genealogy of primitive Christianity; Smith replied in *The Monist* 19.3 (1909). Full primary close reading of this debate remains pending.

### 1933 — China as transmission/representation

**“The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism”** is best treated, on current source analysis, as history of cross-cultural transmission and European representations of China rather than direct Sinological philology. Its source architecture is overwhelmingly European-language: Jesuit reports/translations, Mendoza, Kircher, Leibniz, Voltaire, Temple, Le Comte, Attiret, Chambers and garden/aesthetic literature. Exact 1933 vs 1948 comparison remains pending.

### 1935 — mature expertise becomes distributed

The Preface to Lovejoy and Boas, *Primitivism and Related Ideas in Antiquity*, explicitly thanks **W. F. Albright** and **P.-E. Dumont** for supplementary essays extending the project into Ancient Western Asia and Indian literature. Mature Lovejoy thus also organizes a visible division of linguistic/area expertise rather than personally performing all Orientalist philology.

## Current evidentiary model

1. **1895–1901: direct Orientalist/comparative-religion formation and practice** — Sanskrit/Buddhist texts, comparative religion, Hebrew religion, 004, JAOS philology, Lévi private reading, Marillier EPHE work, Buddhism teaching.
2. **1898–1908: sustained transformation rather than abandonment** — 005 begins in Paris and remains active in 1905; the 1902 article explicitly reuses the 1898 Buddhist analysis; full 1906 primary text now shows theoretical compression of a causal-efficacy problem already present in 005; 1907 Vedānta review and Jewish/Christian history-of-religion work continue the field.
3. **1930s: mediated/distributed global history of ideas** — China as representation/transmission; India and ancient Western Asia increasingly organized through specialist collaboration.

The strongest current thesis is not “Lovejoy was continuously an Orientalist.” It is that **Orientalist and comparative-religion training formed an early laboratory for analytical operations later redistributed into history of ideas**: decomposition of apparent systems, semantic discrimination, historical layering, logical versus chronological sequencing, classification by causal mechanism, caution about borrowing/syncretism, and reconstruction of intelligible arrangements. By 1902 and 1906 Lovejoy’s own vocabulary already includes `categories`, `presuppositions`, `logical motives`, recoverable `logic`, `system`, `connectedness`, and analytical correlation. The mature `unit-idea` vocabulary should still not be projected backward as already fully formed.
