## Working question

How far can Arthur O. Lovejoy be reconstructed as an Orientalist / student and teacher of Asian religions and philosophies, especially Indian philosophy and early Buddhism, and how did this work feed into comparative religion and the later history of ideas?

`Orientalist` is used here as a research category to specify forms of training and practice, not as a permanent career label. The **strict core** includes Sanskrit/Buddhist-text training, Indian philosophy, Pāli/Sanskrit vocabulary, specialist publications, reviews and teaching. An **adjacent comparative-religion layer** includes Lovejoy’s work on “primitive religion/philosophy,” sacrifice, eschatology, magic, causal efficacy and comparative ritual. A **mature global/transmission layer** includes China, India and ancient Western Asia when they enter later history-of-ideas projects through representation, transmission or specialist collaboration.

## Current chronology

### 1895–1898 — Harvard: formal Orientalist/comparative-religion formation

Daniel J. Wilson, using a Harvard Archives course list, the 1896–97 Harvard catalogue and Lovejoy’s 1896 letters, records that Lovejoy studied:

- **Sanskrit and some sacred Buddhist texts with Charles R. Lanman**;
- **comparative religion with Charles C. Everett**, including Vedic religion, Hinduism, Buddhism, Mazdaism and Chinese religions;
- **Hebrew religion with Crawford H. Toy**;
- Everett’s course on the psychological basis of religious faith.

Harvard’s institutional ecology independently fits this formation: Lanman led Sanskrit/Indo-Iranian studies; Lanman and Henry Clarke Warren created the Harvard Oriental Series; Warren’s *Buddhism in Translations* appeared in 1896 and became a major authority for Lovejoy; Lanman publicly lectured on Buddhism, Sāṁkhya/Vedānta and Sanskrit/Pāli sources in April 1896.

A further institutional intersection is the Graduate Philosophical Society. Wilson records Lovejoy as a member and later secretary; Vivekananda addressed that Society on **25 March 1896** on Vedānta. Lovejoy’s attendance at that particular lecture remains unproved.

**Remaining linguistic question:** separate formal Pāli instruction and the degree of Lovejoy’s independent Pāli competence.

### c.1896–1898 — notebook 004 and the 1898 Buddhist paper

`MS38_004_001_061_004 — “Sankhya + Buddhism”` is complete first pass, 71/71. It treats Sāṁkhya method, Buddhist agnosticism, impermanence/flux, chronological priority, dependent origination, khandhas, `upādāna/upādhi/upādisesa`, Nirvāṇa and related philology.

The notebook directly feeds Arthur O. Lovejoy, **“The Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa,” *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 19 (1898), 126–136.** The article extracts the late `upādāna/upādisesa` problem from a broader notebook project. The notebook closes with an explicit historical decomposition: original elements = flux/Three Characteristics + dependent origination; derived elements = sense-perception theory + psychology + khandhas.

Current methodological lead: historically borrowed/composite terms need not form an accidental heap; their arrangement may still be characteristic and intelligible.

### 1898–1899 — Paris: Lévi + Marillier

Wilson, citing Lovejoy’s letters of **17 October, 1 December and 5 December 1898**, says Lovejoy’s Paris year was largely historical/comparative religion. He studied Psalms with Maurice Vernes, heard Jean Réville, hoped to take **Pāli Buddhism with Sylvain Lévi**, and, when the course was not offered, **occasionally went to Lévi’s home to read sacred texts with him**. Exact texts and reading languages still require the letters themselves.

The official EPHE Section des sciences religieuses report for 1898–99 adds a second direct strand: **Lovejoy is named among the auditors who took an active part in Léon Marillier’s conferences.** Those conferences studied survival of the soul, non-moral future-life beliefs, human sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy through funerary, expiatory, magical and fecundative mechanisms and African/North-American/non-Aryan-Indian material.

Marillier’s earlier memoir, **“La survivance de l’âme et l’idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés”** (EPHE annual, 1893; separate printing 1894), has been identified as the source behind notebook 005 abbreviations `M. Surviv.` / `Surviv.`. Earlier Marillier course reports on origin-of-death myths, death as non-natural, post-mortem continuation and social rank likewise map closely onto 005.

### 1898–1905 — notebook 005 is longitudinal, not single-date

`MS38_004_001_061_005` is complete first pass, 120/120. It contains a strong **Paris 1898–99 core**: Paris stationery, `Dec. 14`, `May 29`, `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`, and extensive overlap with Marillier’s EPHE programme.

A second-pass visual check of **PDF p.44** resolves a previously uncertain date as:

> **Hist. Relig. — Dec. 20, 1905.**

The dated outline continues primitive religious evolution, Old Testament material, `psychē`, `nephesh/ruach`, ancestor worship and totemism. Thus 005 was **reused or extended at least through 1905**. It is a composite longitudinal notebook; individual later pages must be stratified before assigning them wholesale to 1905.

Current production model: **Marillier/EPHE comparative source collection (1898–99) → continued `Hist. Relig.` notebook work documented in 1905 → theoretical condensation in 1906.**

The institutional meaning of `Hist. Relig.` remains unresolved. Washington University’s catalog/course-bulletin series is digitized, and a 1905 *Hatchet* page names Lovejoy managing editor of the Bulletin of the Washington University Association, but a specific Lovejoy course titled `History of Religion` has not yet been independently recovered.

### 1901 — Buddhism becomes teaching

Wilson’s bibliography records **“Syllabus: The Philosophy of Buddhism,” Washington University Association, 1901**, four lectures:

1. The Place of Buddhism among the Philosophies of India
2–3. Two Essential Formulas of Buddhism
4. Buddhist Ethics and its Goal

The syllabus object itself remains to be located. Its existence shows that the 1898 research was converted into teaching.

### 1902 — “Religion and the Time-Process”: primary-text bridge from Buddhist philology to history of categories

The full primary text of **“Religion and the Time-Process,” *American Journal of Theology* 6 (1902), 439–472**, has now been recovered from JSTOR.

This is a major evidentiary upgrade. Lovejoy opens by describing the changing **categories** whose history the historian of philosophical and theological opinion records. His object is the historical career of time/becoming/process across systems. He then makes **Vedānta and Buddhism the first major analytic case**: world-cycles provide a cosmological motive for negating temporal process, while Buddhist impermanence and dependent origination provide the psychological/volitional motive.

At the dependent-origination passage Lovejoy **explicitly refers readers back to his 1898 `upādāna/upādisesa` paper**. This gives an authorial primary bridge:

> **004 → 1898 Buddhist technical philology → 1902 comparative history of a category.**

The 1902 article also provides a strong methodological control on borrowing. Lovejoy notes an affinity between Aristotle’s changeless deity and Vedānta but rejects the claim that resemblance proves eastern influence, because the Greek conception can be reconstructed internally from Platonic/Eleatic/Socratic premises. He makes a similar point about Neoplatonism. Conceptual affinity therefore does not automatically establish historical diffusion.

Across Greek, Christian and modern thought he follows a recurring **perfectionist presupposition/logical motive** rather than treating each named system as an indivisible unit. Near the end, Royce’s idealism is decomposed into two incompatible logical motives and a combination of warring categories, one modern and one a survival of ancient metaphysics. Lovejoy also remarks that coherent philosophical formulation can be the **last rather than first stage** in the development of an insight, an important control for reading manuscript distinctions that precede their printed abstraction.

### 1906 — “primitive philosophy” / causal efficacy

Arthur O. Lovejoy, **“The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy,” *The Monist* 16.3 (1906), 357–382**, is bibliographically confirmed. Notebook 005 repeatedly distinguishes outward ritual form from causal mechanism, personal/spiritual agency from intrinsic efficacy, fetish from charm/amulet, sacramental union from magical action, and natural from “supernatural” efficacy. PDF p.105 calls one agrarian mechanism **“quasi-mechanical and magical rather than sacramental.”** These remain strong bridges to the published theory later called `primitive energetics`.

A direct draft relation is still unproved because a clean primary full text has not yet been ingested. The key test is now a three-layer concordance: **Marillier → 005 (1898–99 + 1905 reuse) → 1906.**

### 1907 — Indian philosophy remains active

Lovejoy reviewed Paul Deussen, **_Outline of the Vedānta System of Philosophy, According to Shankara_**, in *Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* 4.1 (1907), 23–24. PhilPapers confirms the metadata; Wilson says Lovejoy regarded the English outline as useful for teachers of Hindu philosophy and urged study of **Rāmānuja’s Vedānta** rather than continued concentration on Śaṅkara.

The public-domain 1907 journal volume has been located through Open Library/Wikisource/Internet Archive, but the exact two-page review text still needs extraction.

### 1907 — “Origins of Ethical Inwardness”: full primary text and Toy continuity

The full primary text of **“The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought,” *American Journal of Theology* 11 (1907), 228–249**, has now been recovered from JSTOR.

Lovejoy explicitly says that for the relative sequence of the Wisdom literature he follows **Professor Toy’s authority**. This is direct published evidence that Crawford H. Toy, his Harvard teacher in Hebrew religion, remained an acknowledged scholarly authority in Lovejoy’s 1907 historical work.

The paper also states a methodological distinction highly relevant to the later history of ideas: texts of one calendar period can embody different intellectual stages, so for this inquiry the **logical sequence** of the conception must be distinguished from absolute and relative chronology. Earlier phases can survive alongside later ones. The paper reconstructs Christian ethical “inwardness” as an **indirect and gradual** development through Jewish Wisdom literature, not as an isolated innovation.

Notebook 005 pp.39–44 already contains Hebrew eschatology, Job/Psalms/Ecclesiastes, `nephesh/ruach`, Greek `psychē/thymos`, `Hist. Rel.` teaching notes and the dated **Dec. 20, 1905** outline. The paper is not simply a publication of those notes, but a controlled 005(1905)→1907 concordance is now warranted.

### 1907–1908 — sustained history-of-religion publication programme

The religion strand remains dense:

- 1907, **“The Entangling Alliance of Religion and History,”** *Hibbert Journal* 5, 258–276.
- 1907, **“The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought,”** *American Journal of Theology* 11, 228–249.
- 1908, **“Pragmatism and Theology,”** *American Journal of Theology* 12, 116–143.
- 1908, **“Religious Transition and Ethical Awakening in America,”** *Hibbert Journal* 6, 500–514.
- 1908, **“The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus,”** *The Monist* 18, 597–609.

The 1908 Smith essay-review is methodologically suggestive: Wilson says Lovejoy tests a proposed vast syncretic genealogy of primitive Christianity and finds that the evidence does not sustain it. Smith replied in *The Monist* 19.3 (1909), 409–420. Full primary text of Lovejoy’s 1908 article still needs retrieval before making a precise methodological comparison.

### 1933 — China as transmission/representation

**“The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism”** is not, on present evidence, direct Sinology. The expanded 1948 version relies overwhelmingly on European-language mediation: Mendoza, Jesuit reports/translations, *Confucius Sinarum philosophus*, Kircher, Leibniz, Fénelon, Wolff, Voltaire, Temple, Le Comte, Attiret, Chambers and European garden/aesthetic literature.

The object is the historical efficacy of a **European representation of China** in the emergence of irregular landscape gardening and Romantic taste. The original 1933 version still requires exact comparison with the 1948 revision.

### 1935 — mature Orientalist expertise becomes distributed

The Preface to Lovejoy and Boas, *Primitivism and Related Ideas in Antiquity*, explicitly thanks **W. F. Albright** and **P.-E. Dumont** for supplementary essays extending the project into Ancient Western Asia and Indian literature. Albright and Dumont therefore supply named area/language expertise inside a larger history-of-ideas architecture.

Current mature-career model: direct early philology gives way partly to **distributed specialist collaboration**, not to simple disappearance of Asian material.

## Current evidentiary model

1. **1895–1901: direct Orientalist/comparative-religion formation and practice.** Sanskrit/Buddhist texts, comparative religion, Hebrew religion, notebook 004, JAOS philology, Lévi private reading, Marillier EPHE work, Buddhism teaching.
2. **1898–1908: sustained transformation rather than abandonment.** Notebook 005 begins in Paris but remains active in 1905; the 1902 time-process article explicitly reuses the 1898 Buddhist analysis; `primitive philosophy`, Jewish/Christian history of religion and a 1907 Vedānta review show overlapping continuities.
3. **1930s: mediated/distributed global history of ideas.** China appears as a representation/transmission problem; Indian and ancient Western-Asian material is organized with specialist collaborators.

The strongest current thesis is not “Lovejoy was continuously an Orientalist.” It is that **Orientalist and comparative-religion training formed an early laboratory for analytical operations later redistributed into history of ideas**: decomposition of apparent systems, semantic discrimination, historical layering, distinction between logical and chronological sequence, classification by causal mechanism, caution about borrowing/syncretism, and reconstruction of intelligible arrangements. The 1902 and 1907 primary texts substantially strengthen this because these operations are visible in Lovejoy’s own early published vocabulary: `categories`, `presuppositions`, `logical motives`, `survival`, `logical sequence`, and indirect development.

The later `unit-idea` terminology should not be projected backward as if already fully formed. The evidence currently supports continuity of **operations**, not terminological identity.
