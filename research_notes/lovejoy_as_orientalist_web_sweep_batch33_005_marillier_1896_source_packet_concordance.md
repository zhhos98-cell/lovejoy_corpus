## Research move

Batches 31–32 established a close conceptual and controversy-level relation among Marillier’s work on taboo, Mauss’s 1896 Steinmetz review, Marillier’s 1898–99 EPHE conference, and Lovejoy 005 pp.16–19. The next test is stricter and more material:

> **Can the Marillier/Mauss taboo discussion be connected to 005 not only by concepts, but by the ethnographic books and reference packets actually being used?**

The answer is now **partly yes, with an important evidentiary limit**.

We can securely identify **Brough Smyth** and **R. H. Codrington** in both the Marillier/Mauss taboo source environment and Lovejoy 005. A broader exact dossier-sheet identity is not yet demonstrated. Several other names in the Mauss/Marillier packet have not yet been securely recovered in 005, and OCR false negatives are a serious possibility.

The correct current claim is therefore:

> **shared source ecology / ethnographic infrastructure**, not yet **proof of an identical copied dossier**.

That distinction matters.

## 1. Marillier 1896 is now bibliographically fixed more securely

Léon Marillier, **“Sur le caractère religieux du tabou mélanésien”**, appeared in *Études de critique et d’histoire*, 2e série, Bibliothèque de l’École des hautes études, Sciences religieuses, **1896, 7,2, pp.35–74**.

Independent controls:

- Persée/SUDOC authority records list the separate work: https://www.persee.fr/authority/217451
- a modern Marillier bibliography gives the series/part and pp.35–74;
- Salomon Reinach later cites the study as `Tabou mélanésien (in Études de critique, 1896, p.35)`.

A direct facsimile collation of the complete pp.35–74 remains desirable. The present high-value evidence for the internal source packet comes from Mauss’s **contemporary 1896 use of the article**.

## 2. Mauss tells us what Marillier pp.53–56 contained

In the critical half of Marcel Mauss, **“La religion et les origines du droit pénal d’après un livre récent”** (*Revue de l’histoire des religions* 34 [1896], 269–295; 35 [1896], 31–60), Mauss discusses the sanction of ritual interdictions / taboo.

His footnote is exceptionally useful for reconstructing Marillier’s working packet. After citing New Britain / York Islands material, Mauss writes:

> `Voir L. Marillier, « Tabou mélanésien », pp. 53-56 ...`

and says that there **most of the texts concerning Melanesia proper were gathered**.

Electronic primary-text control:
https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/mauss_marcel/oeuvres_2/oeuvres_2_15/religion_droit_penal.html

This is not merely a citation to a theoretical claim. It identifies pp.53–56 as an **assembled source cluster**.

## 3. Reconstructed Mauss / Marillier taboo source cluster

Immediately around the Marillier citation, Mauss’s notes expose the books and articles being used to build the comparison. The cluster includes:

- **H. Hastings Romilly**, *The Western Pacific*, pp.17 and 61;
- **R. H. Codrington**, *The Melanesians*, p.69 in the taboo-sanction note; elsewhere Mauss discusses Codrington extensively, including ch. XV;
- **A. C. Haddon**, *The Ethnography of the Western Tribes of Torres Straits*, JAI XIX, pp.325, 327, 397, 407;
- **Brough Smyth**, *The Aborigines of Victoria*, I, p.62;
- **Curr**, I, p.569;
- **Taplin**, p.13;
- **Ridley**, p.186.

Further in the same critical discussion Mauss uses a wider Polynesian/Oceanic taboo corpus including, among others, **Mariner**, **Penny**, **Gaussin**, and **Ellis**.

One footnote gives unusually direct evidence of course-to-publication transmission. Mauss says that his Gaussin and Ellis references were obtained from Marillier and were **borrowed from Marillier’s course on flood myths**.

This strengthens the material model already established independently through Söderblom: Marillier’s teaching environment circulated not merely doctrines but **references, extracts and source packets**.

## 4. Secure 005 overlap no. 1: Brough Smyth

Lovejoy 005’s opening bibliography / reading list includes:

> `Brough Smyth: Aborigines of Australia`

The cleaned manuscript transcription deliberately preserves Lovejoy’s wording rather than silently normalizing the title.

A few pages later, PDF p.7 / notebook p.11 cites:

> `Brough Smyth, Aborigines of Australia, p. 428.`

Lovejoy uses the passage in his discussion of the conception of death: death is treated as a definite agent/cause rather than an abstract natural necessity.

Mauss’s taboo section, by contrast, cites Brough Smyth I,62 in the cluster surrounding the Marillier pp.53–56 reference.

### Evidentiary value

This is a **secure shared book/source**, but **not the same page citation**.

Therefore:

- good evidence for shared ethnographic infrastructure;
- no proof yet that Lovejoy copied Marillier’s Brough Smyth extract;
- potentially testable by collating Marillier pp.53–56 and the full Brough Smyth passages to see whether Lovejoy’s later uses migrate across the same volume.

## 5. Secure 005 overlap no. 2: Codrington

The same 005 opening bibliography lists:

> `Codrington: The Melanesians`

and PDF p.7 cites:

> `Codrington: The Melanesians, p. 26`

again in the origin/death problem.

Mauss’s 1896 article uses Codrington repeatedly in precisely the Melanesian religion/taboo controversy. In the taboo-sanction packet immediately adjacent to the Marillier pp.53–56 citation, Mauss sends the reader to Codrington p.69. Earlier he notes that Steinmetz had followed Codrington closely in reconstructing Melanesian mortuary religion.

### Evidentiary value

Again this is **same source, different known page**.

That difference is analytically useful. It suggests that Lovejoy was not simply copying a fixed bibliography line-for-line. He had access to or worked through the **same ethnographic authorities**, while selecting passages for his own classificatory problems.

This fits 005 as an active research notebook much better than a lecture transcript model.

## 6. Steinmetz is visibly inside 005’s later sacrifice bibliography

PDF p.62 / notebook p.125 is headed `Sacrifice / Bibliography` and contains a Steinmetz cannibalism item.

The manually corrected transcription currently reads conservatively:

> `Ritualkannibalismus — Steinmetz [?]`

Raw OCR is unstable (`Rudokannibaliumus`). The exact title **must not yet be normalized**. Mauss’s 1896 footnotes cite Steinmetz’s *Endokannibalismus*, so there is an obvious candidate, but manuscript-image verification is required before identifying Lovejoy’s p.62 item with that publication.

The secure point is narrower and still valuable:

> **Steinmetz is not merely the external opponent named in the EPHE report; a Steinmetz cannibalism item is present in Lovejoy’s own sacrifice bibliography inside 005.**

That links the early survivance/justice controversy and the later sacrifice/cannibalism section at the level of Lovejoy’s research materials.

## 7. Negative concordance matters: the packet is not yet reproduced wholesale in 005

A targeted search of the cleaned 005 transcription and canonical OCR does **not yet securely recover** the following names from the Mauss/Marillier taboo cluster:

- Romilly;
- Haddon;
- Curr;
- Taplin;
- Ridley;
- Mariner;
- Penny;
- Gaussin;
- Vieillard / Deplanche where relevant to the wider discussion.

`Ellis` appears in noisy OCR elsewhere in 005, but the current evidence does not establish that it is the same **Ellis, *Polynesian Researches*** used by Mauss. It is therefore not counted as a positive match.

This negative result must be treated cautiously because:

1. 005 handwriting is difficult and proper names are especially vulnerable to OCR failure;
2. several bibliography pages are only low/medium-confidence diplomatic transcriptions;
3. Marillier’s pp.53–56 have not yet been directly facsimile-collated by us;
4. a source can be used without being named on every page.

The present evidence therefore **does not support claiming that Lovejoy possessed or copied the whole Marillier taboo packet**.

## 8. What the source concordance changes

Before this pass, the genealogy could be framed at three levels:

1. same seminar / controversy;
2. same conceptual distinctions;
3. documented general mechanism of dossier/reference circulation in Marillier’s environment.

We can now add a fourth, more concrete level:

4. **at least part of the same ethnographic source corpus is visibly present in 005**.

The current strongest formulation is:

> **Marillier’s 1890s research workshop circulated source-based comparative problems; Mauss’s 1896 article exposes one such taboo packet; Lovejoy 005 independently shows active use of at least two of the same major ethnographic authorities, Brough Smyth and Codrington, while reorganizing evidence by causal/normative determinant.**

This is stronger than generic intellectual influence. It remains weaker than proving that Lovejoy copied specific Marillier dossier sheets.

## 9. The bibliographic page itself is evidence of research production

005 PDF p.2 is worth treating materially, not simply as front matter. It places together:

- W. Wyatt Gill;
- Brough Smyth;
- Codrington;
- Duff MacDonald;
- Rink;
- one uncertain Samoa reference.

That cluster already spans the South Pacific, Australia, Melanesia, Africa and the Eskimo/Greenland field. The notebook then extracts cases from these works and repeatedly reclassifies them by mechanism.

Thus the opening list is consistent with a workflow:

> **bibliographic acquisition → extraction of ethnographic cases → source criticism → comparative reclassification.**

The same physical notebook later creates separate sacrifice and Africa bibliographies before moving into detailed typological analysis.

This is precisely the sort of paper-based scholarly operation the paper should foreground.

## 10. `Tabou` in *La Grande Encyclopédie*: volume now secure, exact pages still open

Arnold van Gennep’s 1904 *Tabou et totémisme à Madagascar* gives a contemporary citation:

The BnF catalogue fixes *La Grande Encyclopédie*, **tome XXX**, as **Sigillateur–Thermopole**. Therefore `Tabou` belongs in volume XXX if Van Gennep’s citation is correct.

Controls:

- BnF catalogue: https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb377013071
- Wikisource scan index: https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Grande_Encyclop%C3%A9die_XXX.djvu

A useful caution: a modern bibliography of Marillier’s writings notes that some Grande Encyclopédie contributions could not be fully verified. Van Gennep’s 1904 citation is therefore the better evidence for the article’s existence; the facsimile is still needed for publication-grade coordinates.

## 11. Marillier 1901 `Notes sur la coutume, le tabou et l’obligation morale`: new pagination lead, still no primary text

Bibliographic existence is secure:

- Léon Marillier, *Notes sur la coutume, le tabou et l’obligation morale* (Paris: Félix Alcan, 1901).
- Persée/SUDOC authority: https://www.persee.fr/authority/217451

A later Paul Huvelin text cites:

> `Marillier, Notes sur la Coutume, le Tabou et l’Obligation morale ... Paris, 1901, p.391 et sqq.`

Huvelin’s surrounding discussion is highly suggestive for 005: sanctions can result mechanically from infraction because taboo is contagious and the violator can himself become taboo.

But this remains a **later relay**, not primary Marillier evidence. The citation also creates a pagination puzzle: `p.391 et sqq.` suggests that the text may have appeared within a larger journal/volume or that the electronic transcription has damaged the bibliographic carrier. We need to resolve the original publication form before using it.

## 12. Revised evidentiary ladder after batches 30–33

### Level A — institutional fact

Lovejoy actively attended Marillier’s 1898–99 EPHE conferences.

### Level B — controversy identity

The EPHE report says the survivance conference resumed/refuted Steinmetz; 005 pp.16–19 works directly through the same moral/ritual/social/source-critical problem.

### Level C — material transmission mechanism

Mauss 1896 says he borrowed references from Marillier’s course; Söderblom 1901 says Marillier lent him personal notes/dossiers.

### Level D — shared source ecology

Marillier/Mauss taboo materials and Lovejoy 005 demonstrably share **Brough Smyth** and **Codrington**; 005 also contains a Steinmetz cannibalism bibliographic item.

### Level E — conceptual transformation

005 increasingly classifies evidence by determinant/mechanism and later by direct, intrinsic, transferable and quasi-mechanical efficacy; Lovejoy 1906 isolates the latter as a general comparative category.

### Not yet established

- identical Marillier dossier sheet in Lovejoy’s hands;
- direct copying from Mauss 1896;
- exact page identity between Lovejoy’s Brough Smyth/Codrington notes and Marillier pp.53–56;
- publication-grade coordinates for Marillier’s Grande Encyclopédie `Tabou`;
- primary-text collation of Marillier 1901 `Notes...`.

## 13. Paper-level payoff

The useful story is becoming materially specific without becoming deterministic.

A defensible paper sentence would now be something like:

> **Lovejoy’s 005 notebook emerged within a Paris research environment in which comparative-religion problems were worked through circulating references, extracted ethnographic cases and thematic dossiers. Its opening reading list and later bibliographies show him using some of the same authorities that organized Marillier’s taboo work, while his notes repeatedly reorganize those materials according to causal and normative mechanism.**

The final clause is the important one. The historical interest is not simply that Lovejoy received a set of sources. It is **what he did to them**.
