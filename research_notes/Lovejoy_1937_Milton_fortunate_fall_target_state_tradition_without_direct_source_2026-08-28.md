## Why this case matters

The current genealogy branch had been modeled mainly from:

- 1904 Bruno → Spinoza: direct reading/contact accepted, causal weight limited by a wider common tradition;
- 1933 Monboddo ↔ Rousseau: strong similarity + priority + reading + acknowledgment, yet direct influence left uncertain because alternative routes remain;
- 1933/1948 China essay: carrier-indexed transmission, uptake and serial propagation permit stronger causal claims;
- 1920 Schiller → Schlegel: strong influence claim vulnerable to insufficient control of the recipient's prior state.

The Milton case adds a crucial mature relation-type:

> **a historical tradition can be securely reconstructed even when the direct source or sources of the target author cannot be positively identified.**

This means `genealogy` is not one binary property. Tradition membership, direct source-edge, target-state identity, and originality are distinct propositions.

## 1. Current primary claim control

Daniel J. Wilson's annotated bibliography summarizes Lovejoy's 1937 article as follows:

- Lovejoy examines possible literary and religious antecedents of Milton's treatment of the fortunate-fall paradox in *Paradise Lost* XII;
- **Milton's source or sources cannot be positively identified**;
- nevertheless, the idea can be documented as a long Christian tradition reaching back at least to the fourth century;
- this changes the historical judgment of the Milton passage: it is no longer surprising evidence of exceptional originality or boldness simply because the immediate source remains unknown.

Later Milton scholarship independently describes Lovejoy as demonstrating that the conception was a widespread Christian commonplace from the fourth through the seventeenth centuries.

Current evidence status:

- bibliographic identity: secure;
- Lovejoy's tradition-level conclusion: secure through Wilson + later reception;
- exact wording/page-level primary quotation: direct source lock still needed.

## 2. The genealogy architecture

The case should not be represented as:

`Ambrose / liturgy → Milton`.

Lovejoy's own evidentiary conclusion is more differentiated:

`early patristic / liturgical formulations`
→ `recurrent Christian tradition across many centuries`
→ `Milton text contains/appears to contain the same paradoxical conception`
→ **direct Milton source remains unidentified**.

Thus:

`tradition-level availability/continuity`
≠ `specific direct source`
≠ `exclusive causal dependence`.

The absence of a direct-source solution does not erase the wider historical result.

---

## 3. New genealogy dimension: target-state identification comes first

The later controversy over Lovejoy's Milton interpretation adds an important control.

Lovejoy's genealogy requires a prior proposition:

> Milton's relevant passage actually instantiates the `fortunate fall` idea in the sense Lovejoy defines.

Later scholars challenged that target interpretation. Virginia Mollenkott's 1972 `Milton's Rejection of the Fortunate Fall`, for example, disputes Lovejoy's reading of Milton while directly engaging his claim.

This means genealogy requires a stage **before** source hunting:

`identify target state T`
→ only then ask
`what historical routes can explain T?`.

If target-state identification is wrong or over-broad, a perfectly documented upstream tradition can still be genealogically irrelevant to the target passage.

This is structurally parallel to notebook 004's diagnostic-specificity gate:

`similar feature`
≠ `identified target system/idea`.

### Revised variable

Add `T = target-state identification` to the genealogy vector.

Questions:

- What exact proposition, motif or inferential structure is being attributed to the recipient?
- Is the target text better explained by a neighboring but distinct doctrine?
- Does the target author affirm the idea, dramatize it, question it, report it, or reject it?
- Is the historian tracing a proposition or merely a shared phrase/theme?

No genealogy should be scored before `T` is independently controlled.

## 4. Tradition-level continuity without edge closure

The Milton case gives a mature positive result at one scale and an explicit non-result at another.

### Positive

`C_tradition`: high enough to argue a long Christian career of the fortunate-fall conception.

### Unresolved

`C_direct-source-edge`: insufficient for positive identification.

### Historical originality

Lovejoy nevertheless uses the tradition-level result to reduce the novelty attributed to Milton's passage.

This produces another firewall:

`no identifiable direct source`
≠ `historical originality`.

An idea may be broadly available as a commonplace or tradition even if the precise carrier by which a particular author encountered it is unknown.

This is the inverse of the Monboddo/Rousseau problem:

- Monboddo/Rousseau: a concrete candidate source is known but its causal necessity is unproved;
- Milton: a broad tradition is demonstrable but no unique/concrete immediate source can be fixed.

Both cases prevent the direct edge from swallowing the wider explanatory field.

## 6. Comparison with China, 1933/1948

### China essay

The historical object is a representation of Chinese gardening with visible carriers, translation, attribution, uptake, textual reuse and downstream propagation.

Direct/serial carrier evidence is comparatively rich.

### Milton essay

The conceptual tradition is richly attested across centuries, but the immediate source(s) for Milton remain unidentifiable.

Thus the two cases support different positive genealogical claims:

- China: **carrier history**;
- Milton: **tradition/commonplace history**.

Do not force both into the same influence template.

## 7. Revised genealogy vector

The previous vector was:

`R × E × X × D × P × C`

where:

- `R` = recipient prior state;
- `E` = edge/contact existence;
- `X` = alternatives/exclusivity;
- `D` = causal delta/weight;
- `P` = downstream propagation;
- `C` = relation-specific confidence.

The Milton case shows a missing first variable and a useful distinction inside availability.

### Revised form

`T × R × A × E × X × D × P × C`

where:

- `T` = **target-state identification**: does the recipient text actually instantiate the idea being traced?;
- `R` = **recipient prior state** before the candidate source/contact;
- `A` = **ambient/tradition-level availability** of the idea or repertoire;
- `E` = **specific edge/contact/carrier** existence;
- `X` = **alternative-route/exclusivity control**;
- `D` = **delta / causal weight** attributable to the edge;
- `P` = **propagation / downstream historical operation**;
- `C` = **confidence typed to each foregoing proposition**.

This is analyst vocabulary, not Lovejoy's formal scheme.

## 8. Three distinct genealogy outcomes now visible in Lovejoy's case law

### Outcome A — direct edge + limited weight

1904 Bruno → Spinoza.

`E high`, `A high`, therefore `D/exclusivity limited`.

### Outcome B — candidate direct edge, closure withheld

1933 Rousseau → Monboddo.

`E/access plausible/high`, but `X alternatives strong`, so `D direct influence unresolved`.

### Outcome C — tradition high, direct edge unidentified

1937 fortunate fall → Milton.

`A high`, `E immediate source unknown`, yet historical commonplace/non-originality claim survives.

### Outcome D — carrier chain + propagation

1933/1948 Chinese-garden representation.

`E multiple traceable`, `P high`, actor uptake visible; causal allocation revised when earlier carriers emerge.

### Outcome E — strong edge claim vulnerable to recipient-prior-state control

1920 Schiller → Schlegel.

`E/testimony high`, but later scholarship thickens `R`, reducing the delta attributable to source contact.

This case-law typology is more useful than one ascending influence ladder.

## 9. Implication for unit-idea identity

The Milton case also sharpens the distinction between **identity** and **genealogy**.

Lovejoy can argue:

- the same or sufficiently similar `fortunate fall` idea occurs across many periods;
- the historical tradition is real;
- yet Milton's particular immediate source cannot be fixed.

But later debate reminds us that even the first proposition depends on target-state identification.

The sequence should therefore be:

`semantic/propositional individuation`
→ `target-state identification`
→ `cross-context identity/common-core judgment`
→ `ambient tradition mapping`
→ `direct edge / carrier testing`
→ `causal weight / historical function`.

A historian who skips the first two stages can construct an impeccable genealogy of the wrong thing.

---

## 10. Strongest current methodological formulation

The governing architecture now becomes:

> **problem-dependent scale → typed relation → target identification → local warrant → controlled promotion.**

Genealogy is a high-burden special case because several promotions are required:

`similarity`
→ `same target idea`
→ `historically available tradition`
→ `specific contact/carrier`
→ `causal uptake`
→ `degree of dependence`
→ `historical propagation`.

Lovejoy's mature case law repeatedly allows some stages to be established while leaving later stages open.
