> **T. W. Rhys Davids, _Buddhism: Its History and Literature_ (American Lectures on the History of Religions, First Series, 1894–1895; New York/London: G. P. Putnam’s Sons, 1896), Lecture I, especially pp.24–30 ff.**

Second, _Revue de l’histoire des religions_ vol.37 (1898) becomes a particularly revealing transatlantic co-location point. In the same volume one finds:

- Léon Marillier’s third and fourth installments of `La place du totémisme dans l’évolution religieuse`, his extended critique of F. B. Jevons;
- a review/notice of **Rhys Davids, _Buddhism: Its History and Literature_**, the exact book directly excerpted in Lovejoy 004;

- `À propos du Mahâbhârata dans la littérature bouddhique`, showing that the same journal issue-space also crossed Buddhist textual/Indian-philological questions.

The strongest formulation is therefore not that France simply `influenced` an American-trained Lovejoy. It is that **the source objects visible on the two sides of Lovejoy’s Harvard→Paris movement were themselves circulating and being recontextualized across the same transatlantic history-of-religions infrastructure.**

## 1. Resolving the uncertain source in 004

The first-pass transcription of 004 PDF p.12 / manuscript p.53 had conservatively recorded:

> `[Com. Le?], Buddhism, p. 24.`

followed by material on the chronological relation of Sāṃkhya and Buddhism.

The source is Rhys Davids’s _Buddhism: Its History and Literature_. Its title page identifies it as the **First Series, 1894–1895, of the American Lectures on the History of Religions**, with the second edition published by Putnam in 1896.

Lecture I is `Religious Theories in India before Buddhism`; its table of contents places:

- `The Sānkhya system` at p.24;
- `The Sixty-two Heresies` at p.30.

This is exactly the transition Lovejoy follows.

### Source → notebook sequence

Rhys Davids pp.24–25:

- Sāṃkhya stands logically between Vedānta and Buddhism;
- European scholars had argued that Buddhism was based on Sāṃkhya;
- Sāṃkhya could have been a `possible stepping-stone`;
- Kapila was represented as pre-Buddhist;
- therefore Sāṃkhya might have preceded Buddhism or emerged from the same intellectual movement.

Lovejoy 004 p.12 begins with precisely this cluster.

Rhys Davids pp.25–26:

- Garbe is introduced as the principal authority;
- Rhys Davids doubts that the surviving evidence carries Sāṃkhya back before Buddhism;
- the Sāṃkhya books are late;
- the Sāṃkhya Kārikā is placed long after Gotama;
- Manu and the Bhagavad Gītā establish a considerably earlier worked-out system but leave a large chronological gap.

Lovejoy 004 p.13 follows the same order and nearly the same formulations.

Rhys Davids p.26:

- the warranted conclusion is not a complete Kapilan system before Buddhism;
- rather, isolated pre-Buddhist thinkers probably elaborated views later incorporated into Sāṃkhya.

Lovejoy 004 p.14 opens by carrying forward that conclusion.

Rhys Davids pp.27–30 ff.:

- the Brahmajāla / Dīgha Nikāya gives a list of sixty-two theories;
- Rhys Davids tests one eternalist doctrine as possible evidence for Sāṃkhya;
- the detailed list follows.

Lovejoy 004 pp.14–15 immediately turns to `the sixty-two heresies`, reproducing and systematizing the list.

This is **direct source uptake**, not thematic proximity.

## 2. Lovejoy does not merely copy Rhys Davids

The direct-source identification also makes one of Lovejoy’s own interventions easier to isolate.

After Rhys Davids treats an eternalist doctrine from the Brahmajāla as a possible forecast of later Sāṃkhya, Lovejoy tests the fit more aggressively. In the notebook he notes that the Buddhist passage does not use the technical term `Prakriti`; more importantly, the passage says soul and world `give birth to nothing`, whereas Sāṃkhya makes visible beings arise from primordial matter in relation to individual souls. He therefore says he is `at a loss` to see how the passage could prove that the later Sāṃkhya system already existed as a whole.

Lovejoy then adds a different criterion: the central Sāṃkhya point is not merely eternity of matter and soul but their **separateness / dualism**, and a Majjhima-Nikāya soul/body distinction may be a more plausible analogue.

So the source-transformation relation is already visible:

> **Rhys Davids supplies the historical question and documentary corpus; Lovejoy tightens the evidentiary test and changes what would count as a discriminating Sāṃkhya marker.**

This matters for the larger project because it is the same inferential syntax visible elsewhere in 004: broad resemblance is insufficient unless the conjunction of features is discriminating enough to warrant genealogy or identity.

## 3. The American Lectures network now enters 004 directly

The book is not just another British Buddhist handbook. It is explicitly the first series of the **American Lectures on the History of Religions, 1894–1895**.

Earlier batches reconstructed the American Committee / lecture network around figures such as Crawford H. Toy and Morris Jastrow as part of the distributed American infrastructure for the historical study of religion. The exact-source identification now closes an important gap:

> `American history-of-religions lecture infrastructure → Rhys Davids lecture series → 1896 printed book → Lovejoy 004 direct extraction and criticism.`

The American field is therefore materially inside 004, not merely around it.

This does not establish that Lovejoy attended Rhys Davids’s Boston lecture in person. The evidence needed for that claim remains absent. The secure claim is textual: **he worked directly from the lecture-book produced by the network.**

## 4. RHR vol.37 (1898) as a transatlantic switchboard

The table of contents of _Revue de l’histoire des religions_ vol.37 (1898) produces an unusually dense co-location.

### A. Marillier

The volume contains the third and fourth/final installments of:

> `La place du totémisme dans l’évolution religieuse. À propos d’un livre récent: F. B. Jevons, An Introduction to the History of Religion.`

Modern bibliography confirms the full serialization:

- RHR 36 (1897): pp.208–253, 321–369;
- RHR 37 (1898): pp.204–233, 345–404.

**Correction:** any earlier project note that places the 1898 installments in vol.38 should be corrected to **vol.37**.

### B. Rhys Davids

The same volume carries a bibliographical notice/review of:

> T. W. Rhys Davids, _Buddhism, its history and literature_.

This is now demonstrably the exact book Lovejoy had used directly in 004.

### C. Trumbull

Later in the same volume appears a notice/review of:

> H. Clay Trumbull, _The Threshold-Covenant or the Beginning of Religious Rites_.

Batch 57 has independently shown that Lovejoy 005 uses Trumbull at exact-page/sequential-reading level, especially _The Blood Covenant_ pp.118–129, while _Threshold Covenant_ is present in his sacrifice bibliography and foundation/threshold problem field.

### D. Buddhist / Indian textual scholarship

The same volume includes `À propos du Mahâbhârata dans la littérature bouddhique`. The relevant `Oldenburg` here is Sergei Fedorovich Oldenburg, not Hermann Oldenberg; the names must not be conflated.

The significance is structural. RHR is simultaneously receiving:

- Buddhist/Indian textual-philological work;
- American/Anglophone general histories of Buddhism;
- American comparative-ritual works such as Trumbull;
- Marillier’s internal French critique of evolutionary/totemic theories.

That is exactly the heterogeneity visible across Lovejoy 004 and 005.

## 5. What the RHR result proves — and what it does not

It proves **circulation and co-location** at the level of a major Parisian history-of-religions periodical.

It does **not** yet prove that Lovejoy personally read RHR vol.37. A targeted full-text search of the current 005 transcriptions for `RHR`, `Revue de l'histoire des religions`, `Réville`, `Jevons`, and direct Marillier article citations has not produced a secure direct citation. What is secure in 005 is the separate inserted slip `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June` and the independently documented EPHE participation.

Therefore the warranted language is:

> **RHR vol.37 is evidence that the books and problems found on both sides of Lovejoy’s transatlantic movement were already being gathered into the same Parisian disciplinary information system.**

Not:

> `Lovejoy got Rhys Davids or Trumbull from RHR.`

And not:

> `Marillier reviewed Rhys Davids or Trumbull.`

The reviewer identities of those two notices have not yet been established and should remain unattributed.

## 6. A stronger U.S. × France model

The evidence can now be mapped as object circulation rather than national influence:

### Buddhist / philosophical line

`American Lectures on the History of Religions, 1894–95`  
→ `Rhys Davids, Buddhism: Its History and Literature, 1896`  
→ `Lovejoy 004 direct extraction + criticism`  
↘ `RHR 37, Paris, 1898 review/notice`

### Ritual / ethnographic line

`Trumbull, Blood Covenant / Threshold Covenant, U.S. print culture`  
→ `Lovejoy 005 direct source use and reclassification`  
↘ `RHR 37, Paris, 1898 review/notice of Threshold Covenant`

### French analytical/institutional line

`Marillier, RHR totemism critique, 1897–98`  
→ `Marillier EPHE seminar, 1898–99`  
→ `Lovejoy documented active participation + 005`

These lines do not have to collapse into one causal channel. Their historical importance lies in the fact that **the same young researcher moved through an American lecture/Orientalist infrastructure and a Parisian sciences-religieuses infrastructure while the source objects themselves were circulating between those worlds.**

This is a substantially stronger transatlantic intellectual-history formulation than `American formation followed by French influence`.

## 7. Relation to 004 → 005

The new source identification also suggests a more precise contrast between the two notebooks.

004 can now be seen not only as a Harvard/Sanskrit/Pāli notebook but as one built partly from the published output of an explicitly American **history-of-religions lecture project**. Its direct problem is historical-philosophical reconstruction: what can late systems, early Buddhist texts, and doctrinal resemblance warrant about Sāṃkhya/Buddhism chronology?

005, in Paris, is much more deeply immersed in comparative ethnography and ritual classification: afterlife, totemism, sacrifice, blood, anthropology, survivance, and competing causal mechanisms.

Yet RHR vol.37 shows that these were not disciplinary worlds sealed off from one another. The Paris journal itself could place Rhys Davids’s Buddhism, Buddhist Mahābhārata studies, Marillier’s totemism critique and Trumbull’s threshold rites inside one reviewing and publishing ecology.

Thus the move 004 → 005 can be described as **change of analytical center within a connected field**, rather than departure from one unrelated discipline into another.

## 8. Paper-level payoff

For a short JHI Blog piece, the Rhys Davids identification is probably more useful than the whole RHR network. It gives a clean archival microstory:

> Lovejoy opens a Harvard notebook on Sāṃkhya and Buddhism; a previously obscure citation can be resolved to a book produced by the first American Lectures on the History of Religions; he follows its argument page by page and then begins to dispute what counts as evidence for historical Sāṃkhya. Two years later, while he is moving into Parisian comparative religion, the same book is being reviewed in RHR alongside Marillier’s critique of totemic genealogies.

The long article can then use RHR and 005 to make the larger claim about transatlantic disciplinary infrastructures and the migration of evidentiary operations.

Compact formulation:

> **The Atlantic did not merely carry Lovejoy; it carried the books and problem-spaces through which he learned to make historical distinctions.**
