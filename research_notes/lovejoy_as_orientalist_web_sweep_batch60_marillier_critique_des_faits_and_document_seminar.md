# Batch 60 — Marillier as a method, not merely a doctrine: critique des faits, exhaustive bibliography, and the document seminar

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: specify what a historically defensible “Marillier influence” can mean for Lovejoy notebook `MS38_004_001_061_005` by combining Mauss’s 1902 opening lecture, Mauss’s retrospective assessment of Marillier, the 1900–1901 *Année sociologique* review circuit, and the operations already reconstructed in 005.

## Core result

The Marillier hypothesis can now be stated much more precisely.

What had previously looked from 005 like an inferred paper ecology is explicitly described by Marcel Mauss in 1902 as a **Marillier-derived research practice**. In his opening lecture after succeeding Marillier at the École pratique des hautes études, Mauss says that, as regards the **critique des faits**, he need only follow Marillier’s example. He then specifies the work to be done: exhaustive bibliographic search where possible; compensation for extremely dispersed and poorly known facts by common labor; collective exercise of critical faculties; and, in one conference, tight analysis and examination of documents relating to a defined class of facts among defined groups of societies. He calls this a **“travail de séminaire.”**

This does **not** prove that Lovejoy attended Mauss’s later seminar, nor that Lovejoy and Mauss received the same source packet. Mauss’s lecture is dated January 1902, after Lovejoy’s Paris year. Its historical value is retrospective and programmatic: Mauss explicitly attributes this mode of source work to Marillier.

The archival implication is therefore stronger and safer than a doctrine-of-influence claim:

> **Lovejoy’s 005 can now be situated within a Marillier-centered practice of bibliographic gathering, criticism of testimony, close documentary analysis, and classification, even where Lovejoy’s theoretical conclusions diverge from Marillier’s or Mauss’s.**

A compact formulation for the paper:

> **Marillier’s transmissible object was not only a theory of religion; it was a way of working on documents.**

## 1. Primary bibliographic control: Mauss’s 1902 opening lecture

JSTOR’s volume-level table of contents confirms the original publication:

Marcel Mauss, **“L’Enseignement de l’histoire des religions des peuples non-civilisés à l’École des Hautes Études. Leçon d’ouverture … (27 janvier 1902)”**, *Revue de l’histoire des religions* 45 (1902), pp. 36–55, stable article 23661805.

The methodological passage normally excerpted under the later title **“Métier d’ethnographe, méthode sociologique”** occupies roughly pp.42–54 of the original article.

The decisive sentence is Mauss’s explicit retrospective debt:

> `en ce qui concerne la critique des faits, je n’aurai qu’à suivre l’exemple que M. Marillier nous a donné.`

The same methodological run says, in substance, that he will:

- train students first in bibliographic research, as exhaustive as possible;
- confront the exceptional dispersion and uneven quality of ethnographic materials;
- compensate by common labor for the absence of a complete bibliographic manual for each social group;
- exercise critical faculties collectively;
- devote one conference to tight analysis and examination of documents about a defined order of facts in defined societies;
- conduct this as **seminar work**.

The original RHR metadata is primary-verified. The exact wording of the long methodological section is presently controlled through an electronic reproduction of the RHR excerpt and secondary quotation. Before final publication, image-level verification of pp.42–54 in the original volume remains desirable, but the combination is already sufficient for research architecture.

Primary bibliographic route:

- JSTOR volume 45 (1902), stable volume `i23657016`; article `23661805`, pp.36–55.

## 2. This is the missing institutional description of the “paper workshop”

Before this evidence, the paper-workshop model rested on several converging traces:

- Marillier’s EPHE teaching programme;
- Mauss’s use of Marillier’s bibliography and notes from different courses;
- Marillier-linked inserted leaves in 005;
- Söderblom’s student product transmitting a Marillier-generalized `mana`;
- Lovejoy’s page-specific extraction and reclassification in 005;
- Mauss’s 1896–97 insistence that textual criticism can overturn the result of merely registering a report’s gross/general content.

The 1902 lecture now gives the missing **programmatic statement**. It names the operations directly: bibliography, criticism, close analysis of documents, communal/seminar work.

This changes the status of the paper-based environment claim.

### Before Batch 60

> Marillier’s classroom/research environment plausibly involved circulation and critical sorting of documents.

### After Batch 60

> Mauss explicitly describes his source-critical programme as following Marillier’s example and immediately defines that programme in bibliographic and documentary terms.

The remaining caution is one of transmission, not of institutional method. We still cannot say that Lovejoy possessed the same bibliography or course notes that Mauss did.

## 3. Methodological debt can coexist with theoretical rejection

A major advantage of Mauss’s retrospective assessment is that it prevents “influence” from being confused with doctrinal agreement.

Pascal Le Maléfan’s reconstruction, quoting Mauss’s collected writings, reports Mauss’s judgment:

> **“Avant tout, M. Marillier nous apprit la critique des faits.”**

Mauss then characterizes Marillier’s scholarly work as one of extensive **examination and classification**:

- examination of reports by travelers and ethnographers, with the observer’s position severely discussed;
- examination of theories, arguments, and interpretations in reviews and courses;
- classification of facts rather than large-scale systematic explanation.

At the same time, Mauss criticizes Marillier’s psychological/religious theory and moves toward a sociological account of collective representations and institutions. The important historical point is therefore structural:

> **methodological inheritance and theoretical rupture are compatible.**

That gives us a disciplined model for Lovejoy. We do not need to show that Lovejoy became “Marillierian” in doctrine. It is enough to ask whether the notebook preserves operations Marillier demonstrably taught and Mauss explicitly credited to him.

---

## 4. Marillier’s teaching itself placed source criticism before explanation

Secondary reconstruction of Marillier’s EPHE programme reports that his course in the religions of “non-civilized” peoples began with a **critical study of testimony** concerning beliefs, myths, rites, and customs, before moving to psychological explanation. The sequence matters:

`testimony / source criticism`  
→ `classification / comparison`  
→ `psychological interpretation`.

This is a better institutional comparator for 005 than any simple search for repeated theoretical vocabulary. The notebook repeatedly carries out the first two operations while often refusing the explanatory generalizations offered by the sources it reads.

The safe formulation is:

> **005 is structurally compatible with a Marillier pedagogy in which source criticism and classificatory work precede explanation.**

Do not describe the notebook as a verbatim Marillier lecture transcript.

## 5. The 1900–1901 *Année sociologique* creates a direct Marillier–Mauss–Söderblom critical circuit

The table of contents of *L’Année sociologique*, t.5 (1900–1901), gives a surprisingly compact publication circuit.

Under `Sociologie religieuse`:

1. H. Hubert and M. Mauss, **Introduction**, pp.189–191.
2. M. Mauss reviews L. Marillier, **“Religion,” Grande Encyclopédie, t. XXVIII**, pp.191–197.
3. Later in the same section, M. Mauss reviews Nathan Söderblom, **La Vie future d’après le Mazdéisme à la lumière des croyances parallèles dans les autres religions**, pp.235–241.

The exact stable JSTOR items are:

- introduction: 27881248;
- Marillier review: 27881249;
- Söderblom review: 27881259.

This is not merely a network inferred from biographies. In one review volume Mauss is literally processing both Marillier’s synthetic theory of religion and Söderblom’s comparative eschatology.

The Söderblom review’s exact prose remains to be recovered. Until then its evidentiary value is publication-level interaction, not a proposition-level statement about Mauss’s reaction to Söderblom.

## 6. The 005 archive now looks less like “notes from a course” and more like a research operation

The methodological evidence changes how we should describe the notebook.

The strongest pages in 005 do not merely summarize classroom propositions. They repeatedly perform operations of the kind Mauss later names:

- accumulating dispersed references;
- recording exact pages;
- comparing reports across ethnographic works;
- checking whether a source’s interpretation follows from the observation;
- distinguishing reported form from causal mechanism;
- classifying similar rites under different causes;
- preserving a case while rejecting the source’s theoretical use of it;
- marking cases as unresolved when the evidence cannot recover historical motive.

Recent exact-source audits make this concrete.

### Trumbull

Lovejoy cites *The Blood Covenant* p.118 and p.129 and appears to move through the intervening sequence. He retains Trumbull’s blood/body facts but redistributes them away from an automatic life/covenant/union framework into curative, protective, sympathetic, and transferable efficacy.

### Kingsley

Lovejoy cites *Travels in West Africa* p.511, where Kingsley’s suhman is explicitly efficacious through an entered spirit, and turns it into the positive boundary case for a different class: an amulet requiring no spiritualistic idea. On the next manuscript page he cites Kingsley p.525 while reopening blood as a question of causal bearer.

These are exactly the kinds of source-critical moves that make “critique des faits” historically more useful than a vague claim of intellectual influence.

## 7. A revised Marillier transmission model

The evidence now supports a layered model.

### Marillier

`bibliographic accumulation`  
`+ criticism of testimony / observer`  
`+ close documentary examination`  
`+ classification`  
`+ psychological explanation`

### Mauss

Explicitly acknowledges the first four as methodological inheritance, while criticizing/replacing the psychological explanatory level with sociological explanation.

### Lovejoy

005 strongly documents the first four operations and produces its own mechanism-first causal reclassifications; 1906 later abstracts efficacy into `Primitive Energetics`.

### Söderblom

A 1901 comparative eschatology dissertation carries at least one Marillier-generalized `mana` formulation and is reviewed by Mauss in *L’Année sociologique*; exact review content remains pending.

This is not a stemma of direct copying. It is better understood as a **methodological field with divergent theoretical exits**.

Strong formula:

> **shared critical operations, divergent explanatory architectures.**

## 8. This helps solve the “influence” problem in the 1906 footnote

Lovejoy’s 1906 article says that the conclusions were reached in 1900 and were strongly influenced by studies under Marillier. If “influence” is read only as doctrine, the archival picture is awkward: 005 often diverges from Marillier, Mauss, Robertson Smith, Trumbull, and other theorists.

If “influence” is decomposed into research operations, the evidence becomes much more coherent.

The claim need not be:

> Marillier transmitted the concept of Primitive Energetics to Lovejoy.

A much stronger archival formulation is:

> **The Paris notebook suggests that one component of the influence Lovejoy later attributed to Marillier may have lain in a method of comparative source work: exhaustive collection, criticism of reports, discrimination among causal explanations, and classification of cases before synthesis.**

This remains an inference because Lovejoy does not write “Marillier taught me this method” in 005. But it is now an inference anchored in Mauss’s explicit testimony about what Marillier taught him.

## 9. Relation to Mauss’s earlier Steinmetz critique

The 1902 programme also retrospectively clarifies Mauss’s 1896–97 attack on Steinmetz.

In the Steinmetz discussion Mauss had already insisted that **criticism of texts can produce results different from the registration of their gross/general contents**, and objected to broad rubrics and excessive trust in indigenous or ethnographer interpretations.

By 1902 he names the larger operation as `critique des faits` and explicitly credits Marillier’s example.

It would be too strong to claim a fully documented direct line from the 1896 article to the 1902 programme. But the continuity is methodologically striking:

`raw report`  
→ `critical interrogation of testimony`  
→ `narrower rubrics / causal discrimination`  
→ `reclassification`.

The same sequence is visible, independently, in 005.

## 10. Historiographic consequence after Meylan

Batch 59 established that Nicolas Meylan already gives an excellent genealogy of Lovejoy’s published second-order category and explicitly compares his selection of mana analogues with Hubert and Mauss.

Batch 60 defines the archive’s complementary intervention more sharply:

> **Meylan reconstructs what the category became; Mauss’s 1902 testimony and Lovejoy’s notebook let us reconstruct how a category could be made through documentary operations.**

The article’s strongest contribution is therefore not a new box on a genealogy of theories. It is the recovery of the **research process beneath the box**.

Or, in compact form:

> **from genealogy of concepts to a microhistory of critique, bibliography, and classification.**

## 11. Draft-ready paper paragraph

> **Rather than treating Léon Marillier’s influence on Lovejoy as the transmission of a doctrine, the surviving Paris notebook allows it to be specified as a problem of research practice. When Marcel Mauss succeeded Marillier at the École pratique des hautes études, he declared that in the “critique des faits” he had only to follow his former teacher’s example; he then described a programme of exhaustive bibliographic search, collective criticism, close examination of dispersed documents, and seminar work. Mauss could at the same time reject Marillier’s psychological explanations. This separation of method from doctrine is crucial for Lovejoy. Notebook 005 does not simply repeat Marillierian theses. It collects page-specific evidence, interrogates the interpretations supplied by ethnographers, and repeatedly redistributes apparently similar rites according to different causal bearers. Trumbull’s blood-covenant cases become evidence for curative and transferable efficacy; Kingsley’s spirit-inhabited suhman becomes a boundary case against which an efficacy without an indwelling spirit can be defined. The notebook therefore makes historically visible a form of comparative classificatory labor that Mauss himself later identified as Marillier’s methodological legacy.**

## Evidence cautions

1. Mauss’s 1902 opening lecture postdates Lovejoy’s Paris period; it is retrospective evidence for Marillier’s method, not evidence Lovejoy attended Mauss’s seminar.
2. Do not infer that Lovejoy received the bibliography/course-note packet Marillier gave Mauss.
3. `critique des faits` is directly attributed by Mauss to Marillier; the longer characterization of Marillier as examination/classification is presently controlled through Mauss’s collected text as quoted by Le Maléfan and should be checked against the original/Œuvres before final quotation.
4. The *Année sociologique* Marillier and Söderblom review assignments are primary-bibliographically verified; the Söderblom review’s proposition-level content remains pending.
5. The claim that 005 reflects a Marillierian research practice is an archival inference supported by structural correspondence and Lovejoy’s later acknowledgment of Marillier, not a verbatim statement by Lovejoy.
6. Keep method and doctrine analytically separate: Mauss is the clearest contemporary demonstration that one could inherit Marillier’s critical procedures while rejecting his explanatory psychology.
