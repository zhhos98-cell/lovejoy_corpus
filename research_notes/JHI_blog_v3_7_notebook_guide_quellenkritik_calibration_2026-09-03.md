## 1. What the reader must learn before interpreting notebook 004

The notebook is not a continuous authored exposition. It mixes:

- copied / paraphrased source propositions;
- translations and lexical apparatus;
- bibliographic cues;
- questions and objections;
- local authorial supplements;
- classificatory diagrams and recompositions.

Public rule:

> **Lovejoy handwriting is a carrier, not automatic proposition authorship.**

The article should therefore teach a source-critical reading sequence:

`identify source packet`
→ `return source-owned proposition to source`
→ `locate explicit interruption / connective / reassignment`
→ `state only the Lovejoy-local residual`.

This sequence is illustrated rather than named as a formal method.

Warren describes attachment as the “tenacity of desire” and also reports a stage distinction; Lovejoy compresses that close relation into an identity problem and turns Warren’s own rule of relation-by-relation examination back on it.

The p. 123 `temporally (?) ... logically` locus remains especially valuable because source recovery lowers originality elsewhere but strengthens this recomposition: source-supplied constituents remain separable from the locally written relation between temporal antecedence and logical subdivision. The returned draft’s emphasis on the question mark is retained: one relation stays tentative while the other is specified.

Two additional source-transformation controls remain in the evidence apparatus even where the public body compresses them:

- **Hardy -> notebook -> 1898:** Hardy supplies a two-factor rebirth architecture; Lovejoy recasts it as `fact` versus `manner`, then publishes `fact of rebirth` versus `particular condition of rebirth` and gives that allocation a historical-functional rationale.

The point is not to inventory every source, but to let the reader see that source recovery changes what counts as Lovejoy evidence.

## 3. Witness criticism: notebook, AOS record, print are distinct states

Retain the directly controlled title path in compressed form:

`10 Apr 1897 — Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`
→ `22–24 Apr — No. 30 handled by title; exact meeting title unresolved`
→ `June 1897 Proceedings p. 389 — upādānam / upādāna-kkhandhā`
→ `1898 — upādāna / upādisesa`.

Frame it as witness criticism:

- public-object reallocation is direct;
- one unchanged manuscript under several titles is unproved;
- the June printed title must not be back-dated automatically to the April meeting;
- *Milindapañha*, *upādānakkhandha*, and *upādisesa* change documentary roles across the surviving carriers.

This strengthens the notebook→print argument without overclaiming manuscript descent or a recovered revision mechanism.

## 4. 005 requires material and source criticism together

Notebook 005 remains useful because scan pages can contain multiple physical/chronological witnesses.

Publicly usable controls:

- strong Paris 1898–99 core does not date the whole notebook;
- later reuse is directly demonstrated;
- host-page prose and inserted Marillier sheets are distinct witnesses;
- insertion proves accretion, not insertion date;
- ink/page order/proximity do not independently prove composition chronology;

Source-first procedure:

`identify source/seminar packet`
→ `exhaust source-owned theory/case description`
→ `locate Lovejoy ranking / reclassification / mechanism-bearer mapping / genealogy test / HOLD`.

This yields a better public claim than `Lovejoy develops a theory of primitive religion`.

## 6. Later publications are bounded exits, not organizing sections

The current public body now retains four later exits, each with a distinct job:

- **1902:** comparison != transmission. Aristotle/Vedānta resemblance can be admitted while eastern influence is rejected where an internal Greek route suffices. This is a burden-of-proof recurrence, not proof that the Buddhist notebook became a mature method.
- **1906 politics:** developmental stage and racial/national difference are explicitly separated, yet the resulting differentiation enters colonial and franchise argument. This paragraph shows that finer distinctions can carry political consequences; it is not presented as an inevitable implication of the 1890s Buddhist work.
- **1906 scalar counter-case:** the Kant essay decomposes an inherited category while “The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy” constructs a broad one. Decomposition and generalization are contemporaneous possibilities. The point is to block a tidy maturation story.
- ***Great Chain*:** Asian materials remain present but change historical function inside an explicitly Occidental narrative center. The comparison concerns narrative address and object-function, not disappearance, betrayal, or hidden Buddhist origin.

Brinton and Boas remain backstage controls only. The 1905 `primitive` notebook material remains backstage unless a later revision needs an exact bridge that the current 1906 paragraph cannot supply.

## 7. Closing historiographic gain

The strongest ending is now two-stage. First, returning from the mature book to the notebooks does not reveal a forgotten Buddhist origin; it changes the evidentiary status of a formation already known biographically. Second, the bibliography hook returns as an argument about repeated arrangement: notes arrange materials into working problems, publication narrows claims, mature books rearrange earlier materials around new narrative centers, and later bibliographies/disciplines make some arrangements easier to see than others.

The source-critical floor remains:

> a notebook page is not yet a proposition; handwriting is not yet authorship; source match is not yet assent; publication is not simply a cleaned notebook.

The returned Word draft’s more concrete final sentence is preferred over a programmatic methodological claim: Warren’s relation, Senart’s formula, and the unresolved *upādisesa* passages keep the close anchored in unfinished historical judgments.
