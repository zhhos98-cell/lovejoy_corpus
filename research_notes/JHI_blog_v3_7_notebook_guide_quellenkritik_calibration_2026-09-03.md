## Core decision

The JHI Blog should remain primarily a guided tour of notebooks 004 and 005. The repository now contains enough later Lovejoy, `primitive`, Brinton, Boas, reception, and comparative-method material to build a much larger intellectual history, but that material should function chiefly as calibration. The public essay gains more by showing readers **how the notebooks can and cannot be read** than by extending the number of later endpoints.

v3.7 therefore promotes Quellenkritik from backstage governance to visible prose.

## 1. What the reader must learn before interpreting notebook 004

The notebook is not a continuous authored exposition. It mixes:

- copied / paraphrased source propositions;
- translations and lexical apparatus;
- bibliographic cues;
- questions and objections;
- local authorial supplements;
- classificatory diagrams and recompositions.

Public rule:

> **Lovejoy handwriting is a carrier, not automatic proposition authorship.**

The article should therefore teach a source-critical reading sequence:

`identify source packet`
→ `return source-owned proposition to source`
→ `locate explicit interruption / connective / reassignment`
→ `state only the Lovejoy-local residual`.

This sequence is illustrated rather than named as a formal method.

## 2. 004 source criticism now carries argumentative weight

The Warren example remains useful precisely because Warren owns the larger dependent-origination problem and Lovejoy's contribution is a local refusal of one identity claim.

The Senart example becomes stronger after source recut: some formulations previously tempting to attribute to Lovejoy are Senart-owned, while the explicit response seam (`On Senart's account ...`, `discoverable logical system`) survives as Lovejoy-local.

The p.123 `temporally (?) ... logically` locus is especially valuable because source recovery lowers originality elsewhere but strengthens this recomposition: source-supplied constituents remain separable from the locally written relation between temporal antecedence and logical subdivision.

Add concrete corrections to prevent the notebook guide from appearing selectively source-critical:

- Rhys Davids/Warren/Childers/Oldenberg jointly own much of the dependent-origination apparatus;
- the Four Truths / nidāna parallel closes upstream to *Vinaya Texts*;
- khandha numerics and a continuity model are largely Childers-owned.

The point is not to inventory every source, but to let the reader see that source recovery changes what counts as Lovejoy evidence.

## 3. Witness criticism: notebook, AOS record, print are distinct states

Retain the Batch 221 title path:

`Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`
→ `On the meaning of the Buddhist technical terms ...`
→ 1898 two-term article.

But frame it explicitly as witness criticism:

- public-object contraction is direct;
- one unchanged manuscript under three titles is unproved;
- notebook, meeting record, and printed article should not be collapsed into one hypothetical draft genealogy.

This strengthens the notebook→print argument without overclaiming manuscript descent.

## 4. 005 requires material and source criticism together

Notebook 005 is a stronger teaching case because scan pages can contain multiple physical/chronological witnesses.

Publicly usable controls:

- strong Paris 1898–99 core does not date the whole notebook;
- later reuse is directly demonstrated;
- host-page prose and inserted Marillier sheets are distinct witnesses;
- insertion proves accretion, not insertion date;
- ink/page order/proximity do not independently prove composition chronology;
- printed source collation may identify packet boundaries or names but cannot supply illegible manuscript wording.

Source-first procedure:

`identify source/seminar packet`
→ `exhaust source-owned theory/case description`
→ `locate Lovejoy ranking / reclassification / mechanism-bearer mapping / genealogy test / HOLD`.

This yields a better public claim than `Lovejoy develops a theory of primitive religion`.

## 5. 005 Lovejoy-local residual

The public paragraph should reserve Lovejoy-specific language for operations securely surviving the source recut:

- source ranking;
- `not ritual but alimentary` reclassification;
- separation of fused rationales;
- mechanism → competent social bearer / jurisdiction;
- rival genealogy rejection;
- chronology/path testing;
- unresolved alternative mechanisms.

This lets the notebook remain intellectually active after originality claims are reduced.

## 6. Later publications should function as exits

1902, 1906, and *Great Chain* remain useful, but they should not organize the essay.

- 1902: comparison != transmission.
- 1906 Democracy: classification has political consequences.
- 1906 `Primitive Philosophy`: local decomposition can support a scalar reaggregation.
- *Great Chain*: Indic materials persist with changed function inside an Occidental narrative center.

These show what could happen to relations after the notebooks; they are not the notebooks' telos.

Brinton and Boas remain backstage controls only.

## 7. Closing historiographic gain

The strongest ending is now archival rather than programmatic:

> a notebook page is not yet a proposition; handwriting is not yet authorship; source match is not yet assent; insertion is not yet dated revision; publication is not simply a cleaned notebook.

Once those distinctions are maintained, the early Buddhist work can be historicized without either inflating Lovejoy's originality or dissolving him into his sources. The object becomes the movement among source-owned proposition, Lovejoy-local relation, working-page arrangement, public claim, and later historical reaggregation.
