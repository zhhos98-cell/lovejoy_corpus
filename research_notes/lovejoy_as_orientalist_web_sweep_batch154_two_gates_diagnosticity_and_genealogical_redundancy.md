Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: integrate the post-Batch153 discovery of Oldenberg's 1890 `Familienähnlichkeit` control with Lovejoy's notebook 004 intervention, the 1898 technical article, and the directly collated 1902 `Religion and the Time-Process`. The purpose is to distinguish two different reasons why resemblance may fail to support genealogy.

## Core result

The evidence now supports a more precise two-gate model of comparative inference than the project's earlier shorthand `resemblance ≠ borrowing`.

There are at least **two distinct failures of genealogical proof** in the 1890–1902 material.

1. **Diagnostic insufficiency.** A resemblance may be real but too generic to discriminate one proposed genealogy from neighboring possibilities. Oldenberg's 1890 `Familienähnlichkeit` formulation is the clearest baseline. Rhys Davids then tests one alleged Sāṁkhya witness by vocabulary and causal function; Lovejoy's 004 addendum asks which feature is sufficiently system-defining to count as a genuinely Sāṁkhyan parallel.
2. **Genealogical redundancy / explanatory non-necessity.** Even a strong or `obvious` affinity need not establish historical transmission if the target doctrine can be independently generated from an internal historical-conceptual sequence. Lovejoy's 1902 Aristotle/Vedānta argument is the clearest published case: the affinity is conceded rather than weakened, but the Oriental-influence hypothesis is rejected as unnecessary once the Greek genealogy is reconstructed.

These are not the same rule.

> **Gate 1 asks whether the resemblance is discriminating enough. Gate 2 asks whether transmission is historically needed or independently warranted even after a strong resemblance survives.**

The sequence therefore moves beyond a generic warning against influence claims. It partitions comparative evidence by proof level:

`similarity`  
→ `diagnostic specificity / functional fit`  
→ `competing genealogical explanations`  
→ `historical warrant for transmission`.

This is an analytical reconstruction of the operations; neither `two-gate model` nor `genealogical redundancy` is Lovejoy's own terminology.

No new GLA/GAL/COV identifier is created in this batch.

## 1. Oldenberg 1890: the first gate is specificity

The uploaded second edition of Oldenberg's _Buddha_ gives two primary controls that predate the Jacobi/Senart controversy.

At printed p.100 n.1 Oldenberg does not deny Sāṁkhya/Buddhist resemblance. He grants real `Anklänge` but says they appear to go no farther than the familiar:

`Familienähnlichkeit aller philosophischen Systeme Indiens`.

The evidentiary logic is important. A feature widely distributed across a family of Indian systems has low power to establish a **particular** line of dependence.

At p.226 n.2 Oldenberg again admits a striking fourfold parallel between Sāṁkhya/Yoga liberation theory and the Buddhist Four Truths, yet says the direction of borrowing cannot be established.

Thus two propositions are already separated by 1890:

- comparative resemblance can be real;
- historical direction does not follow automatically.

This is not a blanket anti-diffusion stance. It is a threshold problem: what is distinctive enough, and what historical evidence would determine direction?

## 2. Rhys Davids: a candidate witness must survive vocabulary and function tests

The exact source behind notebook 004 pp.12–17 is Rhys Davids, _Buddhism: Its History and Literature_ (1896), Lecture I.

Rhys Davids tests a Buddhist eternalist passage that had been used as evidence for early Sāṁkhya. His negative controls are more specific than a general family-resemblance warning:

- the technical term `Prakṛti` is absent;
- identifying the passage's eternal `world` with Sāṁkhya primordial matter generates a functional contradiction, because the passage says the world produces nothing while Sāṁkhya primordial matter is generative;
- therefore the witness does not establish a developed pre-Buddhist Sāṁkhya system.

Notebook 004 copies this argument closely. Even the first-person sentence `I am at a loss to understand...` belongs to Rhys Davids.

Rhys Davids therefore supplies an intermediate operation:

> **a resemblance becomes less probative when the alleged parallel lacks the target system's diagnostic vocabulary or performs the wrong causal role.**

## 3. Lovejoy 004: replace a low-discrimination feature with a stronger one

Lovejoy's own intervention begins at the explicit manuscript hinge:

`To R.D.'s remark it should be added...`

He does not merely repeat that resemblance is insufficient. He asks which characteristic should carry more identifying weight.

The eternity of Matter and Self is not, for this purpose, sufficiently diagnostic. Lovejoy instead emphasizes the **separateness of Self and World**, the `thorough-going dualism` on which Sāṁkhya release depends, and proposes another Buddhist passage — the soul/body distinction in the Majjhima-Nikāya — as a more plausible witness.

The operator is therefore:

`candidate resemblance`  
→ `ask what feature defines the target system for this historical question`  
→ `replace a generic feature with a higher-discrimination feature`  
→ `search for a better textual witness`.

This is the first gate in its clearest manuscript form.

It should not be turned into a Lovejoy priority claim. Oldenberg had already framed the specificity problem generally, and the still-unrecovered 1897 appendix may contain equally explicit diagnostic tests.

## 4. The 1898 article relocates the first gate from system identification to semantic/causal discrimination

Lovejoy's 1898 `The Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa` largely removes the notebook's broad Sāṁkhya/Buddhism priority discussion. The specific `thorough-going dualism` test is not made into the published article's central argument.

But the discrimination rule survives in a narrower evidentiary domain.

### `upādāna`

Apparent lexical/classificatory recurrence does not establish identity. The skandhas are not simply identical with `upādāna`; the article distinguishes the objects/conditions subject to attachment from attachment itself.

Similarly, karma and `upādāna` can both bear on rebirth while performing different causal work: the particular condition of rebirth versus the fact of rebirth as such.

### `upādi / upadhi`

Competing etymological genealogies remain live. Lovejoy explicitly says both derivations appear possible and therefore withdraws etymology as the decisive test. Meaning is reconstructed from distribution, doctrinal contrast, and usage.

Thus the 004 diagnostic question changes scale:

> **when a proposed feature is shared by competing hypotheses and cannot discriminate among them, move to a more discriminating evidentiary test.**

The notebook uses a system-defining doctrinal property; the article uses semantic distribution and causal role.

## 5. Lovejoy 1902 introduces a second failure mode: affinity may be strong but genealogy still unnecessary

`Religion and the Time-Process` gives a different kind of control.

In the Aristotle/Vedānta passage Lovejoy does **not** argue that the cross-cultural resemblance is weak, generic, or merely superficial. He explicitly treats the affinity as obvious.

Yet he rejects the proposed Oriental influence associated with the opening of the Greek world to Asia after Alexander. The reason is not Gate 1 diagnostic failure. It is that the Greek conception can be reconstructed internally from Platonic/Greek logical development; the external-transmission hypothesis adds no necessary explanatory work on the evidence presented.

This creates a second gate:

`strong affinity survives`  
→ `construct candidate historical genealogies`  
→ `ask whether internal development independently explains the target`  
→ `do not infer external transmission from affinity alone`.

The best analytical label is **genealogical redundancy / explanatory non-necessity**. It should be understood modestly: an internal genealogy does not logically disprove contact; it means resemblance no longer supplies sufficient reason to posit contact.

## 6. Why Gate 2 is not just a stronger version of Gate 1

The distinction matters because the evidentiary outcomes differ.

### Gate 1 failure

The similarity itself has low discriminatory power.

Example:
- a trait is common to many Indian systems;
- therefore its presence cannot identify a specific Sāṁkhya genealogy.

The remedy is to seek a more diagnostic feature or a better textual witness.

### Gate 2 failure

The similarity may be highly salient and comparative value is retained.

Example:
- Aristotle and Vedānta can remain obviously affine at one logical level;
- but historical dependence still requires evidence beyond affinity because an internal Greek genealogy can generate the result.

The remedy is not a more distinctive resemblance. It is **independent historical warrant capable of choosing among competing genealogies**.

This yields a useful distinction for the project:

> **specificity controls identification; independent genealogy controls transmission.**

---

## 7. 1902 also shows that failed genealogy does not cancel comparison

The broader 1902 article confirms that Lovejoy does not use these gates to destroy comparison.

- Buddhism and Platonism can share a related `logical essence` while their forms remain very different.
- Neoplatonic temperament can differ from the logical import of the ideas under analysis.
- Christianity can be decomposed into Judeo-Christian and Hellenic inheritances whose combination creates tension.
- Royce can preserve an ancient perfectionist presupposition while combining it with a newer logical motive, producing `warring categories`.

Thus comparison, logical function, and historical provenance are separable outputs.

A genealogical claim can fail while a comparative relation remains valid.

This is crucial. The method is not skeptical reduction; it is **proof-level partition**.

## 8. Revised 004 → 1898 → 1902 sequence

| Stage | Comparative problem | Control | What can survive when genealogy fails? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oldenberg 1890 | Sāṁkhya/Buddhist parallels | broad family resemblance has low specificity; borrowing direction unresolved | real parallels / common intellectual field |
| Rhys Davids 1896 → 004 copy | alleged early Sāṁkhya witness | vocabulary + causal-function mismatch | source-critical negative result |
| Lovejoy 004 addendum | which resemblance would actually identify Sāṁkhya? | select system-defining feature / diagnostic specificity | stronger candidate witness |
| Lovejoy 1898 | competing lexical/causal identifications | usage and causal role when genealogy/form cannot discriminate | semantic/systematic function |
| Lovejoy 1902 | cross-cultural philosophical affinity | internal genealogy versus external transmission | logical affinity without borrowing |

The key development is therefore not a straight line toward ever greater skepticism. It is an increasingly explicit allocation of **different evidentiary burdens to different propositions**.

## 9. Consequence for the history-of-ideas bridge

The most defensible prehistory of Lovejoy's later historical practice is now more specific than `decomposition and recombination` alone.

Before a historical component can be assigned a genealogy, at least three questions are kept apart:

1. **What exactly resembles what?**
2. **Does the resemblance concern a diagnostically relevant / functionally equivalent feature?**
3. **What independent evidence warrants one historical genealogy over another?**

Only after those controls can the historian ask how an inherited element functions in a new configuration.

By 1902 this sequence is visible at the scale of comparative philosophy rather than only Buddhist philology.

This remains pre-`unit-idea` method. The claim is about a proof architecture, not mature terminology.

---

## 10. Strongest current paper formulation

> **The early comparative problem was not simply whether two doctrines resembled one another. Oldenberg had already warned in 1890 that Buddhist–Sāṁkhya echoes might amount only to the family resemblance of Indian philosophical systems. Lovejoy's notebook makes the specificity problem operational: after copying Rhys Davids's source criticism, he asks which feature would actually discriminate a Sāṁkhyan doctrine and substitutes the dualistic separateness of self and world for generic eternity. His 1898 article relocates the same evidentiary discipline to lexical and causal function when competing genealogies cannot decide meaning. By 1902 a second control becomes explicit. Even an obvious affinity between Aristotle and Vedānta does not establish Oriental transmission when an internal Greek genealogy already accounts for the conception. The resulting method separates two questions often collapsed by comparative intellectual history: whether a resemblance is diagnostically probative, and whether historical transmission is independently warranted.**
