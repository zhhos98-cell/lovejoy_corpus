# JHI Blog — first-order thesis and historiographic entry

Date: 2026-08-20
Status: pre-pitch framing decision

## Two requirements

1. Enter an ongoing, general intellectual-history conversation rather than present an antiquarian Buddhist/Indological episode in isolation.
2. Defend a substantive historical thesis that is not merely a second-order claim about method, archives, documents, proof levels, or disciplinary construction.

## Recommended division of labor

### Historiographic entry (frame, not thesis)

Use the continuing Lovejoy–Skinner conversation as the gateway. Recent reassessment of Lovejoy (e.g. Mouton 2023) and the 2026 British Academy volume on Skinner show that the relation between long-range history of ideas and contextualism remains an active general intellectual-history problem.

The article's contribution to that conversation should be documentary and historical: it recovers a concrete early episode in Lovejoy's practice that took place in Buddhist/Indological scholarship rather than the later Western-canon material normally used to characterize him.

Do not make `Skinner was wrong` the thesis. Do not make `Lovejoy anticipated contextualism` the thesis.

### First-order substantive thesis

The strongest source-native thesis is:

> **In his 1898 intervention on Buddhist dependent origination, Lovejoy argued that historical borrowing and philosophical originality were compatible: the terms, and perhaps even the ideas, of a doctrine could be borrowed while their `arrangement and application` remained `original and characteristic`. Notebook 004 shows him working out this position inside the connected disputes over the twelve nidānas and the relation of Buddhism to Sāṃkhya.**

This is first-order because it makes a claim about Lovejoy's actual position in an actor-recognized late-nineteenth-century controversy.

A sharper polemical version:

> **Against Senart's reading of the twelve nidānas as a composite series arranged `more or less accidentally`, Lovejoy defended the possibility that inherited materials could form an intelligible and specifically Buddhist configuration.**

A broader but still substantive version:

> **Lovejoy's early Buddhist scholarship treated borrowing not as the opposite of originality but as one condition under which a new philosophical arrangement could emerge.**

## Evidence burden

Primary chain:

Senart 1896 (`ordre plus ou moins accidentel`) → notebook 004 concession/rejection → Lovejoy 1898 (`borrowed ... arrangement and application ... original and characteristic`).

Supporting chain:

Warren 1893 (`identity`) → notebook 004 (`But not so...`) → Lovejoy 1898 differentiation of desire and attachment.

Coda:

upādisesa notebook → 1898 use-over-etymology and explicit uncertainty language.

The Senart chain should carry the thesis; Warren and upādisesa should demonstrate how Lovejoy reached/defended it, not become separate theses.

## Why this enters general intellectual history

The payoff can then be stated without making it the thesis:

- Lovejoy is normally discussed through the later `unit-idea` programme and Skinner's critique.
- This early episode shows him confronting a substantive problem of how inherited intellectual materials could be transformed inside a new system.
- The episode occurred in Buddhist/Indological scholarship, which broadens the archival and geographical prehistory of the practices later associated with the history of ideas.

The article therefore contributes to the general Lovejoy/Skinner genealogy while remaining a first-order intellectual-history essay about a specific historical argument.

## Recommended pitch formula

1. Ongoing conversation: Lovejoy remains central to debates over long-range intellectual history and contextualism.
2. New evidence: notebook `Sankhya + Buddhism` + 1898 article.
3. Substantive thesis: Lovejoy defended compatibility between borrowing and originality; inherited terms/ideas did not make the arrangement itself derivative.
4. Stakes: this places an early, substantive Lovejoyian problem inside late-nineteenth-century Buddhist/Indological controversy rather than only inside the Western philosophical canon.

## Terms to avoid as the thesis

- historical warrant
- relation-type discrimination
- proof levels / proof burdens
- what documents make
- disciplinary redescription
- archival workshop as such
- provenance/configuration as the headline thesis

These can remain backstage analytic vocabulary or brief explanatory language.
