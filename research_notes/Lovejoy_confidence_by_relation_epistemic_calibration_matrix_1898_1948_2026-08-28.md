# Lovejoy — confidence by relation: an epistemic calibration matrix, 1898–1948

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **CANONICAL CALIBRATION CONTROL / CONFIDENCE MUST BE RELATION-TYPED**

## Core correction

The genealogy/identity branch should not assign a single confidence level to an entire historical case.

Lovejoy repeatedly displays a more granular pattern:

> **one relation can be treated as secure while another relation involving the same objects remains uncertain, weak, or explicitly open.**

Examples:

- Spinoza's reading of Bruno can be known while the degree of Bruno's causal importance remains limited/open;
- Monboddo's reading of Rousseau, Rousseau's chronological priority, and their doctrinal similarity can all be secure while direct influence remains uncertain;
- a word can certainly recur while doctrinal identity is rejected;
- a European actor's attribution of a style to `China` can be historically secure while the ethnographic authenticity of that attribution remains a separate question;
- a historian can identify logical implication with high analytical confidence while the historical operation/actor recognition of that implication remains to be tested.

Therefore epistemic calibration should be attached to **typed propositions**, not persons, texts, or cases as wholes.

Analyst notation:

`C_relation(claim)`

for example:

- `C_edge`;
- `C_content-identity`;
- `C_route-exclusivity`;
- `C_causal-weight`;
- `C_propagation`;
- `C_actor-recognition`;
- `C_source-authenticity`.

This is analyst notation, not Lovejoy's.

## I. 1904 Bruno–Spinoza: certainty and limitation in the same paragraph

The consolidated primary dossier controls printed p.167.

Lovejoy's formulation distinguishes at least three confidence-bearing propositions.

### Proposition A — contact/reading

He states that `we know that Spinoza read Bruno`.

Status:

`C_edge = high`.

### Proposition B — some direct influence

Because the reading relation is documented and relevant similarities exist, some direct Bruno → Spinoza influence is accepted.

Status:

`C_some-direct-influence = positive/high enough for assertion`.

### Proposition C — degree/explanatory priority

Lovejoy immediately says the degree of direct influence need not be regarded as the important question, because the relevant repertoire was available through a continuous Neo-Platonic/medieval tradition. He gives a counterfactual: without Bruno, Spinoza would still have found much of the material available through other channels.

Status:

`C_edge-exclusivity = low`;

`C_Bruno-principal-causal-weight = limited`.

### Methodological lesson

A secure edge does not inherit secure exclusivity or secure causal priority.

Confidence does not migrate automatically across relation types.

## II. 1933 Monboddo–Rousseau: high local confidence, unresolved genealogy

Several component propositions are strong:

- Rousseau is chronologically prior;
- Monboddo read Rousseau;
- the two writers share a connected doctrinal cluster;
- Monboddo acknowledges agreement and admiration.

Yet Lovejoy explicitly says whether the similarity resulted from spontaneous recurrence or direct influence cannot be answered with certainty because classical/common sources and shared contemporary evidence remain viable.

Calibration:

- `C_priority = high`;
- `C_access/reading = high`;
- `C_content-similarity = high`;
- `C_actor-acknowledgment = high`;
- `C_direct-influence = unresolved`;
- `C_route-exclusivity = low`;
- `C_causal-direction beyond chronology = unresolved`.

This is the clearest demonstration that confidence is relation-specific.

## III. 1924 Romanticisms: strong negative evidence for macro-identity

Lovejoy says attaching the same name to distinct historical episodes supplies no evidence, and scarcely a presumption, that they are identical in essentials.

This calibrates a different kind of relation.

- `C_same-label = high` as an observable lexical/historiographical fact;
- `C_macro-historical-identity from label = near zero / explicitly unwarranted`;
- `C_partial component overlap = case-dependent and demonstrable only after analysis`.

The same data point (`same label`) can therefore have high evidential status for lexical recurrence and no legitimate force for historical identity.

## IV. 1933/1948 China: attribution, authenticity, and efficacy have separate confidence profiles

The China-garden case should not be assigned one global `confidence in Chinese influence` score.

The relevant claims include:

### Actor attribution

European writers explicitly described the relevant alternative aesthetic/gardening practices as Chinese.

`C_actor-attribution = often high` where directly documented.

### Carrier/reception

Reports, translations, Temple, Addison, Pope, Walpole, Chambers, etc. provide reconstructable documentary/reception links.

`C_carrier/reuse = variable but locally high in several links`.

### Source authenticity

Whether `sharawadgi` is actually Chinese, or whether European accounts accurately represented Chinese gardening, is a separate external-source question.

`C_ethnographic/source-authenticity = separate, potentially contested`.

### Historical efficacy

Even a distorted attribution can be causally effective if actors use it as Chinese authority/example.

`C_historical-efficacy` can therefore be high even when `C_source-authenticity` is lower.

### 1948 revision

Lovejoy's replacement of `Montaigne began the fashion` with earlier voyager/missionary evidence shows local confidence being recalibrated when new chronological/carrier evidence changes the distribution of causal weight.

This is evidence-responsive confidence revision, not a fixed narrative.

## V. 1941: analytical confidence vs historical operation

`The Meaning of Romanticism for the Historian of Ideas` makes the epistemic partition actor-explicit.

Lovejoy distinguishes logical, psychological, and historical/genetic relations. Logical and hypothetical psychological analysis initially occurs in the historian's own constructive analysis; the historian must return to historical data to determine whether these relations were actually operative.

Therefore:

`C_analyst-logical-relation`
≠
`C_actor-recognition`
≠
`C_historical-operation`
≠
`C_genetic-relation`.

A logical implication can be analytically secure while its historical recognition is absent; that absence may itself become a historical fact.

This is the mature explicit form of confidence jurisdiction.

## VI. 1898 notebooks: uncertainty markers already attach locally

Notebook 004 contains local question marks, alternatives, and graded judgments rather than one global uncertainty about the Buddhist system.

Examples include:

- temporal relation queried while logical/classificatory relation is more strongly asserted;
- etymological possibilities left open while contextual usage is used to discriminate meaning;
- provenance/compositeness admitted while conclusions about configuration/function are resisted;
- textual evidence treated as relevant but not automatically conclusive for stronger doctrinal claims.

The correct historical description is therefore not `young Lovejoy was uncertain`.

It is:

> **uncertainty was distributed across specific relation-claims while other relations on the same page could be stated more firmly.**

Publication-level analysis should continue to preserve the manuscript's exact question marks and uncertainty vocabulary.

## VII. Schiller–Schlegel: calibration can fail or be contested

The 1920 Schiller genealogy prevents a triumphalist account.

Lovejoy has strong evidence for:

- contact;
- intense actor reaction;
- post-contact textual modification;
- conceptual fit.

He assigns strong causal weight to Schiller as an instrument of Schlegel's conversion.

Later scholarship thickens Schlegel's pre-contact intellectual state and questions whether the causal delta was really a conversion rather than revaluation, acceleration, or reconfiguration of already developing materials.

Thus:

`C_edge = high`

need not imply that Lovejoy's

`C_total-conversion-weight = high`

was well calibrated.

This case should remain an internal stress test showing that relation-typing tools do not guarantee perfect case performance.

## VIII. Preliminary confidence vocabulary — do not scalarize too early

The following actor phrases should be collected, but their meaning depends on the proposition they qualify.

### High-certainty / factive

- `we know ...`;
- direct documentary assertions of reading/contact;
- `no evidence` when rejecting an inferential upgrade from a datum.

### Qualified positive

- `in part`;
- `apparently`;
- `probably`;
- `seems` / `seems due`;
- `may` / `may have`;
- `presumption`.

### Explicit withholding

- `cannot be answered with certainty`;
- `cannot be determined`;
- multiple competing alternatives presented without closure;
- question marks in notebook working notes.

### Strong genealogical vocabulary

- `direct influence`;
- `derived`;
- `borrowed`;
- `origin`;
- `source`.

But these terms cannot be assigned fixed numeric weights without context. `direct influence` can coexist with explicit limitation of degree/importance, as in Bruno–Spinoza.

Therefore build a **phrase × relation-type** concordance, not a flat epistemic-strength dictionary.

## IX. Proposed calibration data structure

Each future case should be coded at proposition level:

| Field | Function |
|---|---|
| object/scale | term, proposition, motif, complex, movement, carrier |
| relation type | identity, affinity, contact, influence, genealogy, function, etc. |
| actor wording | exact confidence phrase if available |
| evidence cited | text, chronology, reading, quotation, shared source, etc. |
| alternatives considered | yes/no + type |
| Lovejoy conclusion | proposition actually asserted |
| confidence status | secure / qualified / open / rejected |
| later control | whether new evidence revises calibration |

This structure should eventually replace generic labels such as `strong evidence` at the case level.

---

## X. Main theoretical consequence

The governing architecture can now be expanded without becoming a single giant method claim:

> **problem-dependent scale → typed relation → local warrant → relation-specific confidence**.

Genealogy adds recipient-state and causal dimensions:

> **R × E × X × D × P × C**

where the final `C` is itself relation-indexed rather than a global case score.

The broader recurrence is:

> **evidence for one proposition does not inherit the certainty of another proposition merely because both concern the same objects.**

This is a stronger and more precise form of `inferential jurisdiction`.
