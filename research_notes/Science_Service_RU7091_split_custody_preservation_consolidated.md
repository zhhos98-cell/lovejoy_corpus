## 1. Canonical result

The Smithsonian Science Service material is now a finite archival problem rather than a name-search problem.

Direct Smithsonian finding-aid evidence establishes Arthur O. Lovejoy as a named correspondent in Science Service Record Unit 7091 in four annual folders:

- **1927** — Box 87 / Folder 2, `Correspondence Li - Ly, 1927`;
- **1928** — Box 97 / Folder 1, `Correspondence Li - Ly, 1928`;
- **1929** — Box 105 / Folder 1, `Correspondence Li - Ly, 1929`;
- **1940** — Box 219 / Folder 8, `Correspondence Li - L, 1940`.

These are folder-level positive presences. They do not establish document count, exact dates, direction, document form, subject, or the identity of the Science Service staff correspondent.

The 1927–29 folders belong to Series 2, `Correspondence of the Director (Edwin E. Slosson) and Senior Staff of Science Service, 1920-1929`. The 1940 folder belongs to Series 5, `Editorial Correspondence of the Director and Senior Staff of Science Service, 1921-1963`. Series custody must not be converted into a bilateral claim such as `Lovejoy ↔ Slosson` or `Lovejoy ↔ Watson Davis` without item-level evidence.

Canonical rule:

> **folder-level named presence establishes archival embedding, not sender/recipient identity, continuity of exchange, topic, or document count.**

## 2. Annual filing map: positive presence, candidate containers, and preservation gaps

The filing scheme allows Lovejoy's alphabetical position to be located even when edited folder descriptions do not name him.

### 1926 — split-series control

Series 5 reports the `I - Z` correspondence for 1926 missing, which includes Lovejoy's alphabetical position. This is not a year-wide loss because overlapping Series 2 L containers survive:

- Box 28 / Folder 14 — `Correspondence L, July 1925 - June 1926`;
- Box 31 / Folder 12 — `Correspondence L, July 1926 - June 1927`.

Therefore the correct state is:

`Series 5 expected range missing + overlapping Series 2 L folders survive`.

Do not write `Lovejoy's 1926 correspondence is missing`.

### 1940 — direct positive control

### 1941 — multi-gap Series 5 discontinuity

The Lovejoy-relevant `He - Sg` span is missing, but it is not an isolated gap. The 1941 run also reports missing `T`, `Wam - War`, and `Wa - Weav` spans, while many other alphabetical and non-correspondence 1941 files survive.

The finding aid preserves:

- **B230/F1 — `Note on missing 1941 folders`**.

This is the highest-value provenance object for the gap. Until inspected, the cause/date/history of the losses remains unresolved.

Science Service purchased and moved into 1719 N Street N.W. in 1941. Administrative files document the purchase/renovation and office activity, so the relocation is a legitimate retrieval hypothesis. It is **not** a demonstrated cause of the missing folders.

Canonical wording:

> **selected portions of the 1941 Series 5 correspondence run are missing; the Lovejoy position is therefore untestable in that run, while many other 1941 Science Service record classes survive.**

## 4. Split custody inside the Science Service documentary ecology

Science Service material is not one homogeneous filing system.

### RU7091 — correspondence

Direct named Lovejoy presences are preserved in annual director/senior-staff or editorial correspondence files.

### Accession 90-105 — biographical/portrait morgue

Box 13 contains:

`Portraits, Loo-Loz` — explicitly including **Arthur O. Lovejoy**.

This is a direct Lovejoy archival component but only at folder level. Do not infer object count, photograph count, date, photographer, publication history, or exact material form before inspection.

### Accession 90-068 — topical/informational morgue

The accession contains topical classes such as philosophy, metaphysics, psychology and religion, but no Lovejoy-specific component has been established. It is a topic-follow-up archive, not a source for assigning topics to the known Lovejoy correspondence.

Correct order:

`recover Lovejoy item -> establish actual topic -> then inspect matching morgue files`.

### Related Science Service accessions

Smithsonian finding aids document Science Service material across multiple accessions. A concrete preservation precedent exists: material absent from one RU7091 series can survive in another accession. This prevents equating `missing from one annual run` with `destroyed from the entire institutional documentary universe`.

It does **not** establish that missing Lovejoy correspondence survives elsewhere.

Canonical rule:

> **shared organizational provenance does not establish shared document identity or derivative relation.**

## 7. Preservation interpretation: 1941

The 1941 multi-gap pattern plus B230/F1 changes the problem from `Lovejoy disappears` to `the annual filing series is incomplete`.

Relevant context files include records concerning the 1941 purchase/renovation of the 1719 N Street building, trustees' meeting, staff memos and open-house invitations. Their temporal proximity warrants archival inspection but no causal narrative.

Related Science Service accessions demonstrate parallel filing/custody regimes. Therefore:

`annual correspondence gap`
≠ `year-wide institutional archive gap`
≠ `proof of destruction`
≠ `proof of cross-accession survival`.

These must remain separate evidentiary states.
