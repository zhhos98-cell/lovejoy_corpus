# I. What Jevons actually supplies to the controversy field

Jevons's 1896 book treats totemism as more than a classificatory stage in religion. It gives it a material-civilizational role.

The book argues, in substance, that totemic restrictions can contribute to the preservation/taming of animals and plants and that domestication becomes a motor of material progress. Later discussion connects the taming of domestic animals and the transition toward pastoral social forms with changes in totemic and sacrificial institutions.

Contemporary and later summaries repeatedly preserve the characteristic Jevons proposition:

> totemism `led to the domestication of plants and animals` and thus functioned as a prime mover of material progress.

The exact wording/pagination varies by edition; publication work should use the 1896 first edition rather than silently importing later-edition pagination.

This is close enough to 005 p.103 to establish Jevons as a real intellectual target, not an analyst-invented foil.

---

# II. Lovejoy p.103 asks a narrower and more destructive chronological question

005 PDF p.103 / MS p.193 does not simply assert a rival universal sequence. It asks whether a developmental theory of agricultural sacrifice is circular if it presupposes a fixed ordering of:

`WILD ANIMAL`
→ `DOMESTICATED ANIMAL / PASTORAL STAGE`
→ `AGRICULTURE / CULTIVATED PLANT`
→ associated forms of sacrifice.

Lovejoy then attacks the clock itself:

- agriculture may precede domestication in African cases;
- domestic animals need not provide agricultural labor;
- therefore one cannot use a universal material-economic sequence as an automatic chronology for sacrificial forms.

Pp.104–105 immediately show that this is not rejection of development as such. Lovejoy still allows a locally demonstrated transformation from domestic-animal sacrifice toward divine-animal/divine-representative forms, and from quasi-mechanical plant-soul rites toward later sacramental forms.

So the operator is:

`MASTER CLOCK REJECTED`
≠
`LOCAL HISTORICAL TRANSFORMATION REJECTED`.

# III. Marillier is the stronger documented mediation environment

Marillier's 1897 article is explicitly titled as a critique `à propos d'un livre récent` by F. B. Jevons, *An Introduction to the History of Religion*.

The article attacks several features directly relevant to 005:

- reduction of multiple forms of sacrifice to one original sacramental type;
- refusal to admit parallel classes of distinct but analogous practices;
- use of a preconstructed system that forces facts into artificial unity;
- inference from external resemblance to shared origin;
- rejection of an explanation merely because it does not explain the whole class.

Marillier's preferred rule is much closer to Lovejoy's notebook practice: an explanation may be partially exact without being unique, and different causal/historical mechanisms may coexist.

Lovejoy is independently documented as an active participant in Marillier's 1898–99 EPHE conferences. Therefore the field route

`JEVONS THEORY`
→ `MARILLIER PUBLIC CRITIQUE + SEMINAR PROBLEM FIELD`
→ `LOVEJOY WORKING TESTS`

has more documentary support than a bare direct-reading inference.

This does **not** mean Lovejoy did not read Jevons. It means the existing evidence does not require that edge in order to explain why the proposition was available.

# V. Why preserving the HOLD is analytically useful

The project gains precision by distinguishing three propositions that are often collapsed:

1. `JEVONS ARTICULATED A RELEVANT MASTER SEQUENCE` — **secure**;
2. `LOVEJOY WORKED IN A DOCUMENTARY/INSTITUTIONAL ENVIRONMENT WHERE THAT SEQUENCE WAS BEING CRITICIZED` — **secure/strong**;
3. `LOVEJOY PERSONALLY READ JEVONS'S 1896 BOOK WHILE WRITING p.103` — **unproved**.

Only proposition 3 remains HOLD.

This is exactly the source-critical discipline the repo is trying to maintain elsewhere:

`PROPOSITION AVAILABILITY ≠ DOCUMENTARY TRANSMISSION ≠ DIRECT READING`.
