## Core result

The proposed opening survives, with one necessary evidentiary distinction.

1. **Müller's autobiographical framing is exact**: in _My Autobiography_ he retrospectively called his 1869 Académie election a struggle `between Mommsen and myself, between classical and Oriental scholarship`, adding that `for once Oriental scholarship carried the day`.
2. **French official records independently confirm the institutional facts**: Müller became associé étranger in 1869, replacing Friedrich Gottlieb Welcker; Mommsen became associé étranger in 1895, replacing Henry Rawlinson.
3. **The 1867 Bopp succession proves the Müller–Mommsen institutional rivalry was not invented ex nihilo in the autobiography**: the Académie's official commission put Mommsen, Max Müller, and Lepsius on the candidate list; De Rossi and Diez were then added, and De Rossi won.
4. **But the exact 1869 `Mommsen versus Müller` matchup is still, in the evidence recovered here, Müller's retrospective account rather than an independently recovered candidate/vote list.** The official French record currently verifies Müller's election and the vacant Welcker seat, not yet the full 1869 slate/vote.
5. In the **same year, 1869**, the newly organized EPHE publication programme put Müller's _La stratification du langage_ together with Georg Curtius's _La chronologie dans la formation des langues indo-germaniques_ in **Bibliothèque de l'École des Hautes Études, no. 1**. The official administrative notice explicitly describes the Bibliothèque as a vehicle for conference work, members' work, translations of foreign erudition, text editions, and original memoirs.
6. Müller's _Stratification_ itself explicitly warns that generalizing from the classical Aryan and Semitic languages would violate `the simplest rules of sound induction`: the literary languages available to philologists show `fixed and petrified` states, their decay rather than growth, their Being rather than Becoming.

The historical tension is therefore real and usable:

> Müller later narrated an Académie victory as `classical` versus `Oriental scholarship`; at the same moment, a new Paris historical-philological institution was making his comparative-linguistic argument part of its first publication programme.

The correct inference is **not** that two already separate disciplines met across a fixed border. It is that disciplinary labels and institutional rank were being contested while comparative procedures, texts, and personnel circulated across the same philological field.

## 1. Müller's own 1901 retrospective: `classical and Oriental scholarship`

In _My Autobiography: A Fragment_ (1901), while recalling his Paris years and later Institut membership, Müller writes that he became a `real member` of the Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres in 1869. He then states:

> `The struggle when I was elected in 1869 was a serious one; it was between Mommsen and myself, between classical and Oriental scholarship, and for once Oriental scholarship carried the day.`

He immediately adds that Mommsen was elected in 1895 and speculates that Mommsen's political antipathies contributed to the delay.

This passage should be treated as **late autobiographical disciplinary self-positioning**. The phrase is valuable precisely because it shows how Müller chose to narrate the event around 1900; it should not be silently converted into an objective description of two fully constituted scholarly disciplines in 1869.

The autobiography's editorial preface itself underscores its retrospective/programmatic character: Müller wanted to show what he regarded as his `mission in life` and the thread connecting his labours. That makes the Classics/Orientalism binary especially suitable for historicalization rather than literal adoption.

---

## 2. Official French control: Müller did take Welcker's foreign-associate seat in 1869

The _Comptes rendus_ record that an imperial decree approved the Académie's election of Max Müller, Oxford, to the foreign-associate place left vacant by the death of **Friedrich Gottlieb Welcker**.

A later official historical list gives **17 February 1869** as the date on which Müller replaced Welcker as associé étranger.

The _Bulletin administratif de l'instruction publique_ independently records the same appointment.

Thus the institutional core of Müller's memory is secure.

### Evidentiary caution on the exact 1869 contest

The material recovered in this batch has **not yet produced the 1869 commission report or ballot showing Mommsen and Müller as the final rival candidates**. Until that is recovered, the sentence `between Mommsen and myself` remains Müller's autobiographical account, supported in plausibility by the immediately preceding 1867 competition but not independently replicated at the vote level for 1869.

This distinction is useful rather than inconvenient: the article can deliberately juxtapose Müller's later symbolic reading with the thinner administrative record.

## 3. 1867: Mommsen and Müller were already in the same formal competition

The official _Comptes rendus_ for the foreign-associate seat left vacant by **Franz Bopp** state that the Académie's commission presented three candidates:

- Theodor Mommsen;
- Max Müller;
- Karl Richard Lepsius.

De Rossi was then added on Laboulaye's proposal and Friedrich Diez on Maury's. The election went to **Giovanni Battista de Rossi**, whose appointment was subsequently approved by imperial decree.

This matters because it prevents the 1901 autobiography from being dismissed as pure symbolic fabrication. Müller and Mommsen really had been placed in direct institutional comparison by the Académie two years before the Welcker election.

But the 1867 slate also destabilizes Müller's later clean binary. The same contest contained Egyptology (Lepsius), Christian archaeology/epigraphy (De Rossi), and Romance philology (Diez). The institutional field was more mixed than `Classics versus Oriental scholarship` suggests.

---

## 4. Official control: Mommsen finally became associé étranger in 1895

The _Comptes rendus_ for 21 June 1895 record the presidential decree approving **Theodor Mommsen** as associé étranger in replacement of **Sir Henry Rawlinson**.

A December 1895 session then notes that the Académie elected a new foreign correspondent at Poona `en remplacement de M. Mommsen, élu précédemment associé étranger`.

The 1903 death notice likewise says Mommsen had belonged to the Académie as associé étranger since 1895.

Thus Müller's date is independently confirmed; his suggested political explanation remains his own interpretation unless separately documented.

---

## 5. EPHE 1869: the first Bibliothèque volume joins Müller and Curtius

The official _Bulletin administratif_ says that a ministerial order of **16 June 1869** created the `Bibliothèque de l'École des hautes études` to publish:

- collective work from the conferences;
- personal work by members of the École;
- translations of foreign scholarly memoirs;
- editions of texts with notes/commentary;
- original memoirs.

Three branches were begun simultaneously: historical-philological, natural sciences, and physical-chemical sciences.

The notice then announces the first historical-philological fascicles, including:

- Max Müller, _La stratification du langage_, translated by **Louis Havet**, an EPHE student;
- Georg Curtius, _La chronologie dans la formation des langues indo-germaniques_, translated by **Abel Bergaigne**, répétiteur at the École;
- Auguste Longnon on the pagi of Gaul.

Retrospective EPHE publication lists identify **Bibliothèque no. 1** specifically as Müller + Curtius together; no. 2 is Longnon. Modern BnF/Hachette bibliographic metadata independently gives the original 1869 Müller volume as `Bibliothèque de l'École des hautes études ; 1`, followed by Curtius.

The institutional point is stronger than merely noting a French translation. The publication process itself joined foreign comparative philology to EPHE's pedagogical personnel:

- Müller → Louis Havet, then an EPHE student and later Latin philologist;
- Curtius → Abel Bergaigne, répétiteur and Sanskritist.

This is direct evidence of method/text circulation across labels later narrated as disciplinary boundaries.

## 6. Müller's _Stratification_: the methodological proposition being circulated

The English _On the Stratification of Language_ (1868) makes the induction problem explicit.

Müller warns that the empirical field on which the `science of language` had so far rested was `extremely limited`. To generalize from that restricted evidence would, he says, violate `the simplest rules of sound induction`.

His reason is historical. The Aryan and Semitic language families known through literary documents appear only after crucial formative stages. What survives is:

- their period of decay, not growth;
- their descending, not ascending career;
- their `Being`, not their `Becoming`;
- already `fixed and petrified` languages.

The argument therefore links comparison to a problem of historical reconstruction from stratified, unevenly preserved evidence. A scholar cannot simply treat the best-preserved classical languages as a transparent sample of language history as such.

This makes the 1869 EPHE placement especially relevant to the later article's proof problem. The text being translated and institutionally circulated was itself a warning about **what an evidentiary sample licenses one to generalize historically**.

No direct line from this 1868/69 essay to Lovejoy 004 is claimed. Its role is to show the proof culture in which later disputes about `definite similarities`, borrowing, chronology, and whole systems became intelligible.

## 7. What the opening can now safely do

A compact first-order opening could proceed in three moves:

1. **Müller's memory:** in 1901 he remembered his 1869 election as `classical and Oriental scholarship` facing one another.
2. **Immediate correction:** official records show a more mixed philological world; in 1867 Müller and Mommsen had competed alongside Lepsius, De Rossi, and Diez.
3. **Institutional reversal:** in 1869 EPHE's new historical-philological Bibliothèque made Müller's comparative essay its no. 1 publication, together with Curtius, translated by Havet and Bergaigne.

Then the article can pivot:

> The boundary was being named before it was methodologically sealed. The harder question was what comparison could prove.

That leads directly into Müller/Garbe/Bankim/Mitra and then 004 without turning the opening into a generic institutional history.
