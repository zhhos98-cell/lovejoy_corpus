# APSR 1969–1972 submission/referee archive gate: Wolin and Fallers search ceiling

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **CENTRAL EDITORIAL ARCHIVE NOT LOCATED / TWO RECIPIENT-SIDE CONTAINERS DIFFERENTIATED / REFEREE ROLE NOT INFERRED**

## Core result

After the direct APSR venue-capacity control, the next causal question is manuscript-level: did political-theory underrepresentation arise from submission scarcity, referee disagreement, editorial selection, diversion to specialist journals, or some combination?

A targeted public archive search did **not** identify a centralized Ranney/Polsby APSR editorial collection with submission logs, referee files, and decision letters at item level.

Two recipient-side archival traces were located, but they license different claims and neither can yet be called an article-referee file.

1. **Sheldon Wolin Papers, Bancroft Library**: Feldman's archival apparatus identifies `Theory as Vocation`, 1968, Carton 7 Folder 13. This is the prepublication manuscript route for a paper that became `Political Theory as a Vocation`, APSR 63.4 (1969), 1062–1082. Public search did not expose Ranney/APSR submission, referee, revision, or decision correspondence attached to that manuscript.
2. **Lloyd A. Fallers Papers, University of Chicago**: official finding aid lists `Box 2 Folder 2 — American Political Science Review, 1971–1972`. Fallers also published an APSR book review in March 1972. Therefore the journal-named folder cannot safely be treated as referee correspondence without inspection; it may concern the review, other correspondence, article reviewing, or a mixture.

This produces a useful archive-governance rule:

`JOURNAL-NAMED CORRESPONDENCE FOLDER`
!= `REFEREE FILE`;

`PREPUBLICATION MANUSCRIPT LOCUS`
!= `EDITORIAL CORRESPONDENCE LOCUS`.

## I. Wolin: successful theory-manuscript route, correspondence still unlocated

Direct publication control:

Sheldon S. Wolin, `Political Theory as a Vocation`, *American Political Science Review* 63.4 (Dec. 1969), 1062–1082.

The published article states that it is a revised version of a paper delivered in September 1968 before the Conference for the Study of Political Thought.

Recent archival scholarship identifies the earlier manuscript as:

`Theory as Vocation`, 1968 — Sheldon Wolin Papers, UC Berkeley, Bancroft Library, **Carton 7, Folder 13**.

This gives a strong version route:

`1968 CSPT PAPER / MANUSCRIPT`
-> `1969 APSR PUBLISHED ARTICLE`.

But public archive/search layers currently do not expose:

- APSR submission letter;
- letter from Austin Ranney or APSR staff;
- referee reports;
- revision request;
- response-to-referees document;
- acceptance letter;
- dates of submission / revision / acceptance.

Therefore the case can establish `VERSION ROUTE` but not `EDITORIAL ROUTE`.

Do not infer that the published article's revisions were caused by APSR referees merely because the article calls itself a revised version.

`REVISED VERSION`
!= `REFEREE-CAUSED REVISION`.

---

## II. Fallers: concrete APSR folder, function unresolved

The University of Chicago Special Collections finding aid for the Lloyd A. Fallers Papers directly lists:

`Box 2, Folder 2 — American Political Science Review, 1971–1972`.

This is a genuine item-level archive gate.

However, direct APSR bibliographic control shows Fallers published a book review in *APSR* 66.1 (March 1972), pp.228–229, reviewing Michael Crowder and Obaro Ikime, *West African Chiefs*.

The folder's date range therefore overlaps a known contributor relation.

Possible contents include:

- book-review invitation / copy / editorial correspondence;
- proof/copyediting correspondence;
- unrelated APSR correspondence;
- article referee request(s);
- some mixture of these.

The finding aid itself does not specify the function.

Thus:

`APSR FOLDER EXISTS`
+
`FALLERS CONTRIBUTED BOOK REVIEW`

licenses only:

`RECIPIENT-SIDE APSR CORRESPONDENCE CONTAINER`.

It does **not** license:

`FALLERS = APSR ARTICLE REFEREE`.

If requested, the correct archive question is not `send referee file`, but `please reproduce/describe Box 2 Folder 2 so correspondence roles can be classified`.

## III. Polsby 1971–72 report confirms referee dependence but gives no public field denominator

Nelson W. Polsby's `Report of the Managing Editor, The American Political Science Review, 1971–1972`, *PS* 5.4 (1972), 439–443, has a directly exposed opening extract.

It describes office staff physically removing author names from manuscripts and says the editorial office's task is to have submitted scholarship evaluated fairly and, insofar as cooperation can be obtained from referees, promptly.

This gives contemporary process control:

`MANUSCRIPT ANONYMIZATION`
+
`REFEREE-DEPENDENT EVALUATION`
+
`EDITORIAL OFFICE PROCESSING`.

The publicly exposed extract does not provide a political-theory-specific submission count, acceptance rate, or referee-disagreement statistic.

Therefore the missing denominator remains real:

`PUBLISHED POLITICAL-PHILOSOPHY COUNT = KNOWN`

but

`POLITICAL-PHILOSOPHY SUBMISSION / REJECTION / REFEREE DISTRIBUTION = UNKNOWN`.

## IV. Why this matters for Ranney's retrospective referee account

Ranney later recalled that criticism of his preference for formal theory was fair and that classical-theory manuscripts could produce sharply divergent referee judgments.

The contemporary front matter and Polsby report establish that anonymous/referee evaluation was indeed part of APSR's manuscript architecture.

But the archive search has not yet recovered the manuscript packets needed to test Ranney's specific retrospective mechanism.

Therefore preserve three evidence layers:

1. `CONTEMPORARY PROCESS ARCHITECTURE` — direct;
2. `LATER RANNEY REFEREE-MECHANISM RECOLLECTION` — retrospective actor testimony;
3. `INDIVIDUAL REFEREE REPORTS / FIELD-LEVEL DISTRIBUTION` — still missing.

Layer 1 + layer 2 do not substitute for layer 3.

## V. Search closure and future request logic

### Central archive

No public item-level centralized collection of Ranney/Polsby APSR submission/referee files was identified in this search round.

Do not convert that search result into `the files do not survive`; only `public locator not found` is licensed.

### Recipient-side priorities

Highest-value target remains a **successful or failed political-theory article route** with dated editorial correspondence.

Priority order:

1. Wolin, if Bancroft can identify correspondence attached or adjacent to Carton 7 Folder 13;
2. any political-theory author papers with explicit `American Political Science Review` submission correspondence, 1966–72;
3. Fallers Box 2 Folder 2 only as a process-role identification sample, not yet as political-theory evidence.

### Request wording control

Ask archives for:

- correspondence with APSR / Austin Ranney / Nelson Polsby;
- submission and decision letters;
- referee reports or anonymized evaluations;
- revision requests and author replies;
- dates and folder adjacency.

Do not presuppose the archive has `referee files` unless the finding aid says so.
