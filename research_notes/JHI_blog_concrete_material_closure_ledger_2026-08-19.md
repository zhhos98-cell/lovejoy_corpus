Purpose: distinguish different kinds of documentary closure before drafting the notebook-led JHI Blog. The key correction is that not every strong sequence is the same kind of `source → notebook → publication` chain.

## 1. Three distinct closure types

### Type A — full three-node closure

`printed/polemical source → notebook intervention → 1898 published intervention`

This is the strongest possible micro-collation for the Blog because the same live proposition can be followed from an interlocutor into Lovejoy's working notebook and then into his published argument.

### Type B — source → notebook closure

`printed source / live controversy → identifiable Lovejoy uptake → identifiable authorial intervention in 004`

This can prove how Lovejoy was working inside the controversy, but it must **not** automatically be described as a textual chain into the 1898 article unless the same proposition can actually be shown there.

### Type C — notebook → print closure

`004 working argument → 1898 published formulation`

This is excellent evidence for manuscript-to-publication continuity and evidentiary calibration, even where the upstream source ecology is multi-source rather than one single source arrow.

These closure types should remain separate in the Blog. Their combination is stronger than falsely turning every parallel into one linear genealogy.

## 2. Closure A: Senart → 004 → 1898

**Status: FULL / strongest three-node closure / recommended principal Blog micro-collation.**

### A1. Senart 1896: compositeness becomes accidental order and historical filiation

Émile Senart's `À propos de la théorie bouddhique des douze nidānas` argues that the twelvefold series is composite. Ready-made scholastic categories were fused into an order `plus ou moins accidentel`; avidyā and saṃskāra are not originally Buddhist; Sāṃkhya-Yoga materials help show the enumeration's secondary and composite origin. In another formulation the sequence is a classification of categories in an `ordre supposé généalogique`.

Relevant primary corpus controls:
- `mlangescharlesd00goog...`, esp. pp. 284–287.
- master-corpus extracts now recover Senart's explicit sequence from compositeness to accidental arrangement and secondary origin.

### A2. Notebook 004, PDF p.49 / ms p.137: concede provenance, refuse functional consequence

Lovejoy's notebook concedes much of Senart's historical premise:
- terms are not all original to Buddhism;
- heterogeneous materials have been brought together;
- objective/subjective categories are oddly mixed;
- sequence is not homogeneous in strict temporal relation.

But he refuses the upgrade from those propositions to the stronger conclusion that the formula is unintelligible or accidentally arranged. The corrected page states in substance:

> `it is entirely false to say that the formula is unintelligible or that the various terms have merely been brought together in an order more or less accidental.`

The page then argues that a technical/systematic principle of arrangement is discoverable.

This is a clean proof-burden separation:

`borrowed/composite materials` ≠ `accidental/intelligible configuration`.

### A3. 1898 JAOS: the same polemic reaches print

Lovejoy explicitly names Senart, quotes the claim that `upādāna` is an abbreviation/collective designation for the five upādānaskandhas, and states that Senart's interpretation produces a repetitive, derivative, composite, practically meaningless causation formula.

Lovejoy then tests Senart's cited witnesses and says that the three he could consult `prove nothing remotely like` the interpretation they were supposed to establish. He distinguishes `upādāna` from `upādānakkhandha` in canonical usage.

Later the published article grants that the terms — `perhaps even the ideas` — may be largely borrowed, while insisting that their `arrangement and application` can nevertheless be `original and characteristic`.

Thus the full chain is:

`Senart: composite/borrowed → accidental/secondary configuration`

→ `004: grant compositeness, test semantic premise, refuse accidentality/unintelligibility`

→ `1898: explicit Senart rebuttal; cited passages fail burden; borrowed terms/ideas ≠ borrowed arrangement/application`.

### A4. Methodological yield

This is the cleanest concrete example of the Blog's larger question:

> **What does historical provenance actually entitle a historian to infer about semantic identity, causal function, configuration, and the historical identity of a system?**

It also gives the best transition out of Buddhist technical detail:

> `At close range this is an argument about upādāna. At a slightly greater distance it is a dispute over whether borrowed elements determine the historical identity of the configuration in which they appear.`

## 3. Closure B: Garbe / Rhys Davids → 004

**Status: FULL to the notebook / strongest explicit scale-of-inference control / do not falsely linearize into 1898.**

### B1. Garbe 1894 → 004 is direct source uptake

Notebook 004 opens substantively with:

`The Method of the Sankhya / Garbe p. 150`.

Lovejoy copies Garbe's account of `pramāṇa`, and especially the claim that close agreement in terminology, definitions, details and examples permits one to recognize logical material adopted from Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika as an `alien element` in Sāṃkhya.

Batch160 independently reconstructs the genealogy of this Garbean rule:
- Müller/Oldenberg had demanded more than generic similarities;
- Garbe 1892 explicitly ranks diagnostic detail/formal peculiarity above generic resemblance and separates connection from direction;
- Garbe 1894 reapplies the rule;
- Lovejoy directly copies Garbe 1894.

Only the 1894 → 004 arrow is direct Lovejoy transmission. Direct Lovejoy reading of Garbe 1892 remains unproved.

### B2. Rhys Davids → 004 supplies the chronological brake

Lovejoy extracts Rhys Davids's discussion of Sāṃkhya/Buddhism priority and its documentary gap: surviving Sāṃkhya works are later, so a strong pre-Buddhist whole-system claim remains doubtful.

### B3. 004 authorial hinge: `system existed as a whole`

On the Brahmajāla passage Lovejoy writes:

> `I am at a loss to understand how F. passage can be considered good evidence A. F. Sankhya system existed as a whole, just as we find it in F. many centuries later.`

Then the handwriting explicitly changes mode:

> `To R.D.'s remark it should be added ...`

Lovejoy asks what feature would actually be diagnostic of a genuinely Sāṃkhyan doctrine and singles out the separation of Self and World / `thorough-going dualism`, with a further Majjhima-Nikāya parallel.

This is not generic skepticism. It is **scale control**:

`recognizable doctrinal feature` ≠ `the later system already existed as a whole`.

### B4. Important closure limit

The exact `system existed as a whole` proposition is **not** shown to travel directly into the 1898 upādāna/upādisesa paper. The published paper contains an analogous distinction between borrowed terms/ideas and original arrangement/application, but that is a different polemical locus.

Therefore the safe relation is:

> Garbe / Rhys Davids → 004 gives the clearest notebook evidence for control of inferential scale; 1898 shows the same broader habit of separating historical claims at different scales, but should not be presented as the textual endpoint of this exact passage.

This correction matters for the Blog's Quellenforschung credibility.

## 4. Closure C: 004 upādisesa → 1898

**Status: FULL manuscript-to-print closure / strongest publication-continuity flash.**

### C1. Notebook 004, PDF p.66 / ms p.173

The corrected manuscript reads:

> `No one familiar with Buddhist modes of thought could suppose that anupādisesa here meant the extinction of the skandhas, i.e. physical death. To the being who has been freed from desire it is indifferent whether he dies or lives; to speak of death as the summum bonum would be the most un-Buddhistic of sentiments.`

Lovejoy concludes that the word expresses an ethical condition of freedom from attachment rather than persistence/extinction of the five skandhas.

### C2. 1898, pp.135–136: near-verbatim publication

The article reproduces the same argument almost verbatim:

> `No one familiar with Buddhist modes of thought could suppose that anupadisesa here means merely the extinction of the (present) skandhas, i. e. physical death.`

and:

> `to regard death, in itself, as the summum bonum, would be the least Buddhistic of sentiments.`

This is direct manuscript/publication continuity, not merely thematic resemblance.

### C3. Published evidentiary calibration

The article also makes explicit several proof distinctions that are already being worked through in the notebook:

- Oldenberg's interpretation is supported by citations close to conclusive for prevailing, though not universal, usage;
- competing etymologies remain possible;
- therefore meaning must be settled `rather by an examination of its use than by etymological arguments`;
- Lovejoy offers evidence `relevant but not necessarily conclusive`;
- contrary passages remain, so the matter `cannot be regarded as finally settled`.

This gives a compact sequence:

`etymology` ≠ `usage`

`usage` → `semantic / ethical function`

`relevant evidence` ≠ `conclusive evidence`

`preferred interpretation` ≠ `finally settled conclusion`.

### C4. Upstream source ecology

This is not a single-source arrow. Lovejoy is adjudicating among Oldenberg, Childers, E. Müller, Dahlmann, Rhys Davids, Sutta-Nipāta witnesses and other canonical evidence. That makes it ideal for showing the notebook as a working adjudication space, but less ideal than Senart for a single-source three-node micro-collation.

## 5. The surrounding field: why Lovejoy's distinctions are participation, not invention

The primary controversy independently exhibits different ways of controlling historical inference.

### Oldenberg

Oldenberg can grant close conceptual kinship between Sāṃkhya and Buddhist terms while insisting that their causal construction forms `eine etwas andere Figur`; proximity of elements does not settle function/configuration. He also explicitly cites canonical passages distinguishing `upādāna` from the five `upādānakkhandhas`.

### Senart

Structural repetition/heterogeneity can be made to support compositional stratification and a stronger filiation claim.

### Jacobi

Systematic analogy and causal reconstruction are permitted to carry stronger Sāṃkhya-Yoga genealogical weight.

### Garbe

Generic similarity is insufficient; detailed/formal peculiarities are more diagnostic and can underwrite connection, followed by direction.

### Rhys Davids

Chronology and late surviving documentation limit how far one can infer a pre-Buddhist complete system.

Therefore the correct historical claim is not that Lovejoy invented source criticism or contextualism. He was **working inside a field already divided over the threshold at which one evidentiary proposition could be upgraded into a stronger historical proposition**.

## 8. Drafting rule

The Blog should not pretend that all evidence forms one continuous causal genealogy. Its documentary authority will come from showing that we can distinguish:

- what Lovejoy copied;
- what he added;
- what he published;
- what was merely analogous across loci;
- what the surrounding controversy had already made available as a method problem.
