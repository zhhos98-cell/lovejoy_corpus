## Classification ambiguity is explicit actor evidence

The editors warn that an article may be read as:

- historical or modern;
- methodological or conceptual.

They classify tentatively rather than treating the categories as natural kinds.

Even more importantly, after grouping twentieth-century named-theorist essays under `historical`, they immediately concede that none of those cases is quite historical.

Thus:

`OBJECT = NAMED THEORIST`
!= `ROLE = HISTORICAL INQUIRY`.

The same editorial census identifies 23 of the 85 modern submissions as dealing with:

- methodology;
- philosophy of intellectual history;
- philosophy of social science.

These are grouped as metatheory. Five metatheory articles are reported as published, excluding additional author-centered articles that also contain metatheoretical concerns.

This is direct evidence that in 1975 `philosophy of intellectual history` remained an active editorial category inside the new political-theory venue.

> **OBJECT CLASSIFICATION != METHOD ROLE != EDITORIAL CATEGORY.**

# V. Haddock 1979 JHI: direct full-text execution case

New file:

`research_notes/Haddock_1979_JHI_historical_reconstruction_unit_transformation_relation_proofburden_2026-08-31.md`

Commit:

`e39f1d23251944413fb33638015365cc1e18147f`.

Direct source:

B. A. Haddock, `Vico's "Discovery of the True Homer": A Case-Study in Historical Reconstruction`, *Journal of the History of Ideas* 40.4 (1979), 583–602.

The full Cardiff repository PDF was recovered and relevant pages checked against page images.

## A. Method and case are reciprocal

Haddock presents `True Homer` as a concrete case through which Vico's principles of historical interpretation can be understood.

More importantly, he argues that the apparently abstract methodological principles were themselves distilled from Vico's attempts to make coherent sense of difficult historical episodes.

Thus:

`PROBLEMATIC RECONSTRUCTION`
<-> `EXPLICIT METHOD CANON`.

Control:

`EXPLICIT METHOD TEXT`
!= `PRE-EXISTING ALGORITHM APPLIED TO PASSIVE CASES`.

This matters for later method-history comparisons: programme statements and executed cases should be compared, not hierarchized automatically as theory then application.

## B. The historical unit changes

The Homer reconstruction directly alters the historical object.

In Haddock's Vico:

- early poetry is not produced by individual poets but by whole poetic nations;
- particular poets appear later as collectors/assemblers of fables and corrupted histories;
- contradictions in the Homeric corpus can be assigned to different regions/periods/traditional layers rather than forced into one timeless individual author.

Therefore:

`SAME NAME + MORE CONTEXT`
can become
`DIFFERENT HISTORICAL UNIT`.

This is one of the clearest cases in the repo for:

> **METHOD PRESSURE CAN CHANGE THE UNIT.**

It is especially useful beside later Bajpai/Tan critiques of author-centered units, while genealogical claims across those cases remain prohibited unless a traceable route is established.

## C. Direct proof-burden control for influence

Haddock discusses a proposed Vico -> Thomas Blackwell influence based on textual comparisons.

He explicitly cautions that attractive conjectural resemblance cannot be substituted for concrete historical evidence and points to alternative/documented intellectual resources in Blackwell.

Thus the same article that reconstructs contextual meaning also polices transmission claims:

`TEXTUAL / THEMATIC RESEMBLANCE`
!= `TRANSMISSION / INFLUENCE`.

This is direct actor-level support for the repo's Skinner/Mandelbaum/Wiener/Pocock evidence firewall.

## D. Philosophy and philology mutually constrain historical reconstruction

Haddock reconstructs Vico's philosophy/philology relation as reciprocal rather than adversarial.

Artifact identification, arrangement and classification already invoke theoretical criteria; abstract reasoning needs historical/philological control.

Therefore:

`DESCRIPTION / CLASSIFICATION`
!= `THEORY-FREE OPERATION`.

This unexpectedly aligns with the 1975 *Political Theory* editorial census: categories are operational acts, not natural ontologies.

## E. Historical meaning is distributed across a network of assumptions

Haddock's Vichian reconstruction treats language, customs, jurisprudence, government, authority and notions of rationality as conceptually related components of historically specific modes of thought.

A puzzling utterance is not exhausted by analytical dissection in isolation; its intelligibility requires reconstructing the surrounding structured assumptions.

Thus contextual pressure can require both:

`RELATION CHANGE`
+
`UNIT CHANGE`.

## F. Anti-anachronist genealogy of method

The article concludes by resisting inductive/deductive/hypothetico-deductive natural-science templates as the wrong historical frame for Vico.

Haddock instead locates Vico amid Renaissance philology, jurisprudence and the ancients/moderns quarrel.

Control:

`LATER ANALYTIC MODEL FIT`
!= `HISTORICAL GENEALOGY OF METHOD`.

5. **later portable canonization** — Tully/Kelley/anniversary volumes compress or reorganize the distributed ecology.

Haddock adds a sixth useful dimension:

6. **same-actor cross-venue methodological recombination and execution** — history-of-ideas label, Vichian anti-anachronism, then JHI historical reconstruction case.

The project should therefore ask not `which school won?` but:

- what historical unit was being reconstructed;
- what relation was asserted;
- what proof burden actors imposed;
- what venue/institution made the dispute recurrent;
- what later carrier made some version portable.
