# Batch 94 — The category survives by changing epistemic address: Jung 1928 and Malinowski 1915 as `etic retention / emic de-abstraction`

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: compare two downstream transformations that were previously treated separately: Jung's direct Lovejoy reception in `On Psychical Energy` (1928) and Malinowski's non-Lovejoy-named impersonal-power synthesis (1915). The question is what happens to Lovejoy's category when later psychologists/anthropologists reject the assumption that the native actor possesses the same abstract concept formulated by the comparative theorist.

## Core result

A common downstream operation becomes visible across two texts with very different citation relations:

> **the comparative category survives at the analyst's level while being withdrawn from the native actor's level.**

Lovejoy 1906 tends to align three things rather tightly:

`native lexical/grammatical pattern`
→ `native causal category`
→ `comparative philosophical abstraction`.

Jung 1928 and Malinowski 1915 both loosen that alignment.

### Jung 1928 — direct Lovejoy reception

Jung explicitly accepts Lovejoy's substantive diagnosis of efficacy/power/productivity and even the regular/manipulable energetic characterization. But he says it is wrong, strictly speaking, to call the native datum an abstract `concept of primitive philosophy`: that `concept` belongs to **our** abstraction. The indigenous datum is a representation / psychic phenomenon experienced as inseparable from the object or phenomenal relation.

### Malinowski 1915 — no visible direct Lovejoy citation

Malinowski likewise retains a comparative impersonal-power field and can describe manitu analytically as a `mechanical factor`, while explicitly rejecting the attribution to primitive people of sharply defined abstract concepts, subtle personal/impersonal distinctions, and formulated magical laws. Such forces are more often felt than conceptually apprehended.

The convergence is not evidence of a Jung–Malinowski relation and not evidence that Malinowski received this move from Lovejoy. It is a **field-level epistemic reallocation**:

`comparative analyst keeps abstraction`
while
`native actor is redescribed in representation / feeling / relation terms`.

A concise formula:

> **the category survives by changing address.**

Or more technically:

> **etic retention accompanies emic de-abstraction.**

---

## 1. Lovejoy's 1906 proof architecture makes the later problem possible

Batches 76–80 showed why the later epistemic objection is so important. Lovejoy did not simply infer an impersonal power from rituals and then treat the inference as real. He demanded a `vera causa`: native language, grammar and independently attested use were recruited to prove that the explanatory category existed independently of the customs it was then used to explain.

The strongest instances include:

- Jones's inanimate grammatical treatment of `manitou` as property/abstraction;
- Hetherwick's Yao noun-class evidence against missionary personalization of `mulungu`;
- native causal explanations of transfer and communicability;
- repeated correction of missionary glosses such as `god`, `prayer`, or personified `wakonda`.

That proof architecture licenses a strong move:

`the analyst's category`
≈
`a recoverable native causal conception`.

Lovejoy's article title itself, `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`, captures that epistemic confidence.

The downstream re-audit does not necessarily dispute the lexical/grammatical observations. It questions the final equivalence between **pattern in native use** and **abstract concept possessed by the native mind**.

---

## 2. Jung 1928: Lovejoy's evidence is admitted, Lovejoy's epistemic licensing is narrowed

Batch86 directly established the relevant Jung sequence.

Jung reopens Lovejoy's North-American source packet, routing specific claims through McGee and other ethnographic authorities. He expressly agrees with Lovejoy that the semantic center is not merely abnormality/supernormality but efficacy, power and productivity. He also accepts the stronger regular/manipulable characterization and records `primitive energetics`.

Then he changes level. Jung says, in effect:

- the comparative investigator may abstract a concept of psychic/primitive energy;
- the people under description need not possess that abstraction as a detached concept;
- the native datum is instead a representation or psychic phenomenon bound to the object/relationship;
- treating Lovejoy's `concept of primitive philosophy` as if it existed indigenously in the analyst's abstract form overlicenses the evidence.

Thus Jung's verdict is not:

`Lovejoy evidence false`.

It is:

`Lovejoy evidence valid for analyst-level abstraction`
+
`Lovejoy evidence insufficient for same-form native concept`.

### Code

`empirical power pattern` → ACCEPT.

`regular/manipulable causal characterization` → ACCEPT.

`comparative analytical abstraction` → ACCEPT / REUSE.

`same abstraction as native concept` → REJECT / NARROW.

This is an **epistemic-status correction** rather than a causal-content correction.

---

## 3. Malinowski 1915 reaches a similar epistemic structure without a visible Lovejoy edge

Malinowski's 1915 Polish synthesis works in the same broad dossier but with a different citation architecture.

He explicitly cites primary and theoretical sources including Jones, Codrington, Hewitt, Spencer/Gillen, Marett, Hubert–Mauss, Preuss, Durkheim, King and Leuba. Searchable full text does not name Lovejoy, `Primitive Energetics`, or *Monist* XVI.

His psychological claim is forceful. Theorists go wrong when they attribute to primitive people:

- sharply defined anthropomorphic spirit concepts;
- an equally sharp conceptual distinction between personal and impersonal powers;
- clearly formulated abstract magical laws.

Primitive/general forces are, for him, vague and hazy, more **felt** than abstractly conceptualized.

Yet Malinowski does not abandon comparative abstraction. He still groups mana, arungquilta, churinga, manitu and orenda into an impersonal/mysterious-power field, and describes manitu at the analyst's level as essentially a `mechanical factor` that lends mystical efficacy.

Thus his structure is:

`native cognition` → vague / affective / non-abstract;

`analytical reconstruction` → comparative impersonal/mechanical power category remains usable.

This is the same **direction of epistemic relocation** seen in Jung, despite a different citation genealogy and a different verdict on regularity.

## 4. The two texts agree on epistemic address while disagreeing on causal strength

This comparison prevents us from collapsing `etic retention / emic de-abstraction` into one theory of mana.

### Jung 1928

- direct Lovejoy citation: YES;
- efficacy/power/productivity: ACCEPT;
- regular/manipulable energetic relation: ACCEPT;
- native abstract concept in analyst's form: REJECT;
- analyst's psychological abstraction: RETAIN.

### Malinowski 1915

- direct Lovejoy citation: NO VISIBLE REFERENCE;
- vague impersonal efficacy field: RETAIN;
- analyst-level `mechanical factor`: YES;
- sharply formulated native abstract concept/law: REJECT;
- comparative category: RETAIN.

The shared operation is therefore independent of a shared causal verdict.

> **epistemic address and causal strength are separate dimensions.**

This gives the reception matrix another axis that should not be folded into `accept/reject`.

---

## 5. Lovejoy's strongest evidence becomes the object of a second-order distinction

The historiographically interesting point is that Lovejoy's best source criticism does not simply disappear downstream. It changes what later readers have to contest.

Lovejoy had already distinguished:

- native lexical application;
- grammar;
- observer translation;
- semantic core;
- causal interpretation.

Jung accepts much of this low-level differentiation. His new distinction sits one level higher:

`native-use pattern`
≠
`native possession of analyst's abstraction`.

So the evidentiary atom shrinks again.

### Batch77

`source`
→ decomposed into statement-types.

### Batch86 / Batch94

`licensed proposition`
→ decomposed into:

- empirical pattern licensed;
- analyst abstraction licensed;
- attribution of abstraction to native cognition **not** licensed.

This is a genuine escalation in source-critical granularity across reception.

## 6. The category survives because ownership changes

The phrase `category survives` can otherwise be misleading. What persists is not necessarily the original claim about **whose category it is**.

Lovejoy's 1906 construction can be schematized as:

`native language/grammar`
→ `native fundamental causal concept`
→ `comparative primitive philosophy`.

The later epistemic configuration becomes:

`native language/ritual/representation`
→ `recurring empirical relation/pattern`
→ **analyst constructs comparative category**.

The abstraction has migrated from object-language to metalanguage.

This is a stronger transformation than terminological change from `manitouism` to `dynamism` or from `primitive energetics` to `psychic energy`. It changes the **epistemic owner** of the category.

A useful formulation:

> **what begins as a reconstructed indigenous concept can survive as an explicitly second-order analytical category.**

This operation helps explain how early mana theory could remain productive even as anthropology/psychology became more suspicious of intellectualist accounts of primitive cognition.

## 7. This also changes how `citation shadow` should be interpreted

Batch91 showed that Malinowski could enter a source environment in which Lovejoy remained accessible through Leuba while the Lovejoy name disappeared.

Batch93 then showed that mechanical vocabulary itself is not a Lovejoy fingerprint because Hubert–Mauss and the wider mana field already supplied multiple mechanical analogies.

Batch94 adds a further caution:

Malinowski's similarity to Jung on **emic de-abstraction** is not evidence of a hidden Lovejoy relay either. The move may be generated by a broader anti-intellectualist / psychological reorientation of the field.

Therefore downstream similarity must be decomposed along at least three axes:

1. **citation visibility** — is Lovejoy named?
2. **causal verdict** — is regular/mechanical efficacy accepted?
3. **epistemic address** — is the abstraction attributed to the native actor or explicitly reserved for the analyst?

A text can match Lovejoy on one axis and oppose him on another.

## 8. Reception ontology after Batches81–94

The minimum useful unit is now larger than `source × proposition × evidentiary job` because each proposition also has an epistemic address.

A more complete coding object is:

`recipient`
× `source`
× `proposition`
× `evidentiary job`
× `verdict`
× **`epistemic address`**
× `citation visibility`.

For example:

### Jung 1928 × Lovejoy 1906

- proposition: regular impersonal efficacy;
- job: ethnographic/semantic evidence;
- verdict: accept;
- epistemic address: analyst abstraction, not same-form native concept;
- visibility: direct named citation.

### Malinowski 1915 × impersonal-power dossier

- proposition: mysterious/mechanical efficacy;
- job: comparative synthesis;
- verdict: retain/recombine;
- epistemic address: analyst category over vague native representations;
- Lovejoy visibility: absent.

That distinction is likely necessary for any publication-level reception database.

## 9. Paper-level payoff

A compact article formulation is now available:

> **Lovejoy's `primitive energetics` proved more durable as an analytical abstraction than as a claim about primitive conceptual consciousness. Jung's 1928 re-reading makes the separation explicit: he accepts Lovejoy's evidence for efficacy, productivity and regular manipulability while denying that the resulting `concept of primitive philosophy` exists for indigenous actors in the abstract form supplied by the scholar. Malinowski's 1915 synthesis, without naming Lovejoy, occupies a comparable epistemic arrangement: vague and affectively apprehended native forces remain available to the analyst as an impersonal, even mechanically described, comparative category. The category thus survives by changing address—from a reconstructed indigenous concept to a second-order analytical object.**

Short formula:

> **the category survives by changing epistemic owner.**

## 10. Evidence limits

1. Jung 1928 is a direct Lovejoy reception case. Proposition-level control currently comes from an original-version republication / modern critical text plus 1928 bibliographic/index controls; exact 1928 English facsimile wording remains a hold.
2. Malinowski 1915 is direct primary for anti-abstraction and analyst-level mechanical wording; no Lovejoy name appears in searchable text.
3. Similarity between Jung and Malinowski is a comparative structural result, not a transmission claim.
4. `etic retention`, `emic de-abstraction`, `epistemic address`, and `epistemic owner` are our analytical terms.
5. Lovejoy's own use of `concept` is complex and should not be reduced to a claim that every indigenous actor consciously articulated a philosophical theory in explicit scholastic form. The claim at issue is the strength with which native linguistic/ritual evidence is treated as licensing the reconstructed abstraction.
