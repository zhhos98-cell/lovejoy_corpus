# MS38_004_001_061_005 ↔ Lovejoy 1906 *Monist* concordance

## Status

This note supersedes the earlier provisional claim that the 005 → 1906 relation was only thematic. With the complete published text of Arthur O. Lovejoy, **“The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy,” *The Monist* 16.3 (1906), 357–382**, now available for direct collation, the relation can be stated more strongly.

**Current judgment:** notebook `MS38_004_001_061_005` is not a draft of the 1906 article in the narrow sense. It is, however, very likely part of the larger comparative-religion research program of which the 1906 paper explicitly describes itself as the **first chapter / first stage**. The published paper establishes the linguistic and conceptual *vera causa* — a diffused, transferable, impersonal life-energy — while repeatedly announcing a later analysis of customs, rites, sacrifice, agriculture, initiation, taboo and eschatology. Notebook 005 contains precisely that customs-and-rites analytical workshop.

The relation is therefore best described as:

> **1906 article = theoretical/linguistic prolegomenon; notebook 005 = substantial evidentiary and analytical apparatus for the projected comparative-customary continuation.**

This claim is stronger than simple thematic continuity, but still distinct from notebook 004 → 1898, where notebook passages can be followed almost directly into one narrowly delimited published article.

---

## 1. The published paper explicitly announces the kind of work preserved in notebook 005

### Published p. 359: “first chapter in a study”

Lovejoy says that the paper is **“the first chapter in a study”** intended to set forth the nature, bearings and diverse applications in custom and rite of what he takes to be the central conception of early thought. He adds that the aim is not primarily to add ethnographic data but to **correlate** familiar data and bring out their significance.

That description fits the organization of notebook 005 much better than a conventional article-draft model. The notebook is an accretive comparative dossier whose recurrent operation is to place superficially similar rites beside one another, separate their mechanisms, and test competing explanations.

### Published p. 362: “this first stage of the inquiry only”

Lovejoy then narrows the scope of the 1906 article itself. The paper presents only the **first stage** of the inquiry: evidence that North American, Oceanic and Bantu vocabularies contain terms for a universal, impersonal, communicable energy. The later analysis of customs is deferred.

### Published p. 377: language and customs must reciprocally corroborate one another

The program is made explicit again near the end. Linguistic usage establishes a *vera causa* independently of the customs to be explained; but only when customs are **examined and correlated** can the scope and applications of the concept be understood. Lovejoy says that linguistic evidence and “the analysis and comparison of customs and ceremonies” are to illuminate one another reciprocally.

Notebook 005 is overwhelmingly an analysis and comparison of customs and ceremonies. Its physical and argumentative form therefore matches the unpublished second half of the research program that the article itself announces.

## 3. The most important upgrade: p. 378 turns large portions of 005 from “omitted material” into “announced continuation”

The earlier preliminary cross-read treated future-life, sacrifice, initiation and much of the agricultural dossier as material “left outside” the 1906 article. The complete published text requires a more exact formulation.

On p. 378 Lovejoy explicitly says that his central conception lies, in different ways, beneath or within:

- several kinds of sacrifice;
- agricultural ceremonies and feasts;
- women’s taboos / supernatural powers;
- initiation rites;
- certain elements of primitive eschatology.

Those are not accidental neighboring interests. They are precisely the domains he announces for subsequent analysis. Notebook 005 contains extended working sections in every one of them.

Therefore the better model is not:

> notebook contains much material the article omits.

It is:

> notebook contains a substantial part of the **customary/ritual analysis that the published first chapter explicitly postpones**.

This materially strengthens the case that 005 belongs to the same research project.

## 4. Article and notebook divide the research labor differently

### The article’s task: establish the *vera causa*

The 1906 paper is deliberately linguistic and conceptual. It uses `wakonda`, `manitou`, `oki`, `mana`, `atua`, `andriamanitra`, `mulungu` and `ngai` to show that a non-personal, transferable efficacy is not merely an anthropologist’s inference from custom. The vocabulary independently demonstrates that such a category existed in the conceptual worlds Lovejoy was studying.

### The notebook’s task: classify what rites are actually doing

Notebook 005 works in the opposite direction from raw custom:

- Is a sacrifice feeding the dead, bargaining with an ancestor, expiating guilt, transferring evil, increasing crop fertility, renewing a god-man, protecting a building, or producing union?
- Does blood work as a medium of kinship, a life-substance, a charm, a danger, or a vehicle for spirit?
- Is eating flesh cannibalism, sympathetic transfer of qualities, ritual regulation, or communion?
- Is circumcision a sacrifice, or initiation/incorporation?
- Is a crop rite directed to a personal plant god, or conceived as quasi-mechanical fecundation?

This is exactly the “analysis and comparison of customs and ceremonies” promised at p. 377.

## 5. Specific phrase/concept bridges now secure enough to use in argument

### A. “Quasi-mechanical” is not retrospective vocabulary

The published article makes “impersonal and quasi-mechanical” central at p. 359 and returns to “well-adjusted, quasi-mechanical relations” at p. 361. Notebook p. 105 independently uses the same analytical opposition in the agricultural-sacrifice discussion: the process is quasi-mechanical/magical rather than sacramental or personal.

This is one of the strongest lexical-conceptual bridges in the pair.

### B. Transfer is the common operator

Published article:

- portions of energy are transferable between persons and things;
- blood/menstruation can transfer fertility to seed;
- eating an enemy’s heart absorbs `manitou`;
- agricultural sacrifice moves surplus life-energy to crops.

Notebook:

- blood gives vitality, health, protection or rejuvenation;
- body parts transfer intelligence/courage/sexual power;
- seed-corn receives blood/life;
- sacrificial victims furnish fertility;
- charms and body matter can protect structures.

The continuity is not “blood is mentioned in both.” The common operator is **transferable efficacy**.

### C. The spirit / non-spirit distinction is a working problem, not a binary doctrine

Published paper does not deny spirits. It argues that spirits are secondary and powerful partly because they possess unusual quantities of the same force.

Notebook likewise does not divide practices into “spiritual” versus “non-spiritual” once and for all. It distinguishes:

- fetish with indwelling spirit;
- charm with intrinsic efficacy;
- sacrifice directed to a personal spirit;
- magical action directly on natural forces;
- ancestor bargain;
- sympathetic transfer.

This is a more precise continuity than saying Lovejoy simply moved from animism to non-animism.

## 6. Source architecture: important continuities and equally important changes

### Continuities

- **Codrington** is present from the opening bibliography of notebook 005 and becomes a central authority for `mana` in the published article.
- **Brinton** appears in notebook 005 (including *The American Race*) and is a major theoretical foil in the article.
- **Frazer** is repeatedly tested in the notebook and criticized explicitly in the article.
- The notebook’s African and Oceanic comparative range matches the article’s insistence on North American, Oceanic and Bantu comparanda.

### Changes

The publication sharply changes the source-selection rule. The notebook’s sacrifice sections rely heavily on Callaway, Grout, Kingsley, Trumbull, Strack and numerous ethnographic compilations. The 1906 first chapter instead privileges sources that bear directly on **vocabulary** (`wakonda`, `manitou`, `mana`, `mulungu`, etc.).

That difference is exactly what the paper’s stated scope predicts: the article first proves the conceptual category linguistically; the customs dossier remains largely unpublished or deferred.

## 7. Chronology: the 1900 + Marillier statement is now central

Published p. 381 n. 21 is crucial. Lovejoy says:

- his conclusions were reached in **1900**;
- they were reached independently of several writers discussed later in the article;
- they were much influenced by his studies under **Léon Marillier**.

This gives a terminus for the conceptual core of “Primitive Energetics” that is much earlier than publication in 1906.

Notebook 005 physically fits a multi-stage accumulation around this period. It contains nineteenth-century source use, at least one 1899 reference, numerous inserted slips, and a back-leaf memorandum on PDF p. 120 where `Marillier` is visually legible. The surrounding words appear to be scheduling/appointment notes and need second-pass reading before they can be quoted or dated.

**Safe inference:** notebook 005 is materially compatible with Lovejoy’s own statement that the theory formed around 1900 under Marillier’s influence.

**Not yet safe:** claiming that every layer of 005 dates to 1900, or that the p. 120 Marillier note dates a specific substantive section.

### Secure

1. The 1906 article describes itself as the first chapter / first stage of a larger study.
2. It says later analysis will connect the central efficacy concept with sacrifice, agriculture, women’s taboos, initiation and eschatology.
3. Notebook 005 contains extended analyses in exactly those domains.
4. The article and notebook share the core operations of transfer, impersonal efficacy, quasi-mechanical causation, and discrimination between personal-spirit and non-personal mechanisms.
5. The article says the conclusions were reached in 1900 and influenced by studies under Léon Marillier.

### Strong inference

6. A substantial portion of notebook 005 is research apparatus for the larger study announced in the 1906 article, including material intended for later chapters or sections that were not published there.
7. The late notebook efficacy/blood/agriculture material is especially close to the conceptual core that becomes “Primitive Energetics.”

## 9. Consequence for the larger Lovejoy project

The 004 and 005 cases now show two related but distinct early Lovejoyan research operations.

### 004 → 1898

**Philological decomposition and recomposition:** inherited technical terms and doctrinal classifications are separated, historically compared, and then recomposed into an intelligible causal structure.

### 005 → c. 1900 / 1906

**Functional decomposition and conceptual abstraction:** heterogeneous rites are separated by mechanism; one recurrent mechanism — transferable, controllable, impersonal efficacy — is abstracted and named “Primitive Energetics” / `manitouism`.

The 1906 article’s explicit insistence that there is **no universal key**, because animism, ghost-beliefs and other complexes may have independent origins, is especially important. It shows that Lovejoy’s search for a fundamental concept did not require reducing all religious phenomena to one origin. A concept could instead **interpenetrate independently originated complexes**. That formulation is already very close to the later history-of-ideas problem of tracing recurring conceptual elements through heterogeneous intellectual formations.

This is a stronger and more documentable bridge to Lovejoy’s mature method than a loose retrospective analogy with “unit-ideas.”
