# Melvin Richter as a methodological switchboard: CSPT infrastructure before explicit conceptual-history synthesis, 1967–1995+

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **CSPT FOUNDATION / CONFERENCE ARCHITECTURE / 1980 EDITORIAL FRAMING DIRECTLY CONTROLLED; LATER CONCEPTUAL-HISTORY SYNTHESIS DIRECT; EARLY CAUSAL CONTINUITY SHOULD REMAIN MODULAR**

## Core result

Melvin Richter now appears as a major institutional and methodological hinge in the postwar history-of-ideas / political-thought ecology.

The important sequence is not simply:

`RICHTER DISCOVERS BEGRIFFSGESCHICHTE`
-> `COMPARES IT TO CAMBRIDGE SCHOOL`.

A longer institutional sequence is visible:

`1967 — CO-FOUNDS CSPT WITH POCOCK + NEAL WOOD`
->
`1970s — CSPT ORGANIZES RECURRING CROSS-DISCIPLINARY PROBLEM CONFERENCES`
->
`1975 — POLITICAL THEORY AND POLITICAL EDUCATION CONFERENCE`
->
`1980 — RICHTER EDITS CONFERENCE VOLUME + 50+ PAGE INTRODUCTION TRANSLATING AMONG DISTINCT DISCIPLINES/STYLES`
->
`1987 — JHI: BEGRIFFSGESCHICHTE AND THE HISTORY OF IDEAS`
->
`1995+ — EXPLICIT SYNTHESIS / COMPARISON OF CONCEPTUAL HISTORY, POCOCK, SKINNER, HISTORY OF IDEAS`.

The strongest formulation is:

> **Richter practiced methodological brokerage institutionally before he theorized it as a comparative relationship among named schools.**

This makes him a `methodological switchboard`: an actor who helps different methodological jurisdictions become mutually visible, comparable and institutionally co-present without forcing them into one unified doctrine.

This term is an analytical analogy to the repo's `Graves field-planning switchboard`, not a claim of historical transmission between Graves and Richter.

## I. CSPT foundation: Richter builds a cross-disciplinary arena before later school labels stabilize

The International Conference for the Study of Political Thought directly identifies its founders in Toronto in 1967:

- J. G. A. Pocock;
- Melvin Richter;
- Neal Wood.

Its mission is explicitly interdisciplinary/humanistic, linking intellectual history, political/social theory, philosophy and adjacent disciplines.

Recent disciplinary history reconstructs the three founders as a heterogeneous coalition rather than a methodologically uniform school.

Therefore the society itself operationalizes:

`SHARED PROBLEM JURISDICTION`
without requiring
`SHARED METHOD IDENTITY`.

That becomes a durable feature of Richter's later work.

## II. The 1970s conference sequence is already a comparative method infrastructure

CSPT's directly listed early conferences include:

- 1969 — Machiavelli;
- 1970 — Liberalism; Hegel;
- 1971 — `Political Thought and Action`;
- 1972 — `Reason, Passion and Power`;
- 1973 — `Epistemology and Political Philosophy`;
- 1974 — `Political Economy and Political Philosophy`;
- 1975 — `Political Theory and Political Education`;
- 1976 — `Political Thought in 1776`;
- 1977 — `East Asian Political Thought`;
- 1978 — `Human Nature in Political Thought`;
- 1979 — `Contemporary Citizenship`;
- 1980 — Locke / political thought of the 1680s.

This programme mixes:

- historical objects;
- epistemological questions;
- theory/action relations;
- political education;
- comparative/global objects.

It is therefore better understood as a **problem-space infrastructure** than as a society devoted to one doctrine of historical interpretation.

## III. 1975/1980: the clearest early Richter method-brokerage object

The 1975 CSPT conference `Political Theory and Political Education` became the 1980 Princeton volume edited by Richter.

Direct JSTOR control says the book contains previously unpublished papers by prominent political theorists working in **a number of disciplines and styles**.

Richter's introduction has an explicit translational purpose: to make the papers useful to readers familiar with one sort of theory but not another, and to students entering political theory and its history.

This is actor-level evidence for methodological brokerage.

The volume juxtaposes:

1. Bernard Williams — `Political Philosophy and the Analytical Tradition`;
2. Charles Taylor — `The Philosophy of the Social Sciences`;
3. Mihailo Marković — `Marxism as a Political Philosophy`;
4. Allan Bloom — `The Study of Texts`;
5. J. G. A. Pocock — `Political Ideas as Historical Events: Political Philosophers as Historical Actors`;
6. Michael Walzer — political decision-making/education;
7. Ronald Dworkin — political theory/legal education;
8. Sheldon Wolin — political theory/political commentary.

This is not a Skinner/Pocock school volume.

It deliberately puts competing methodological and disciplinary languages into one pedagogical architecture.

---

## IV. Richter refuses a single uncontested definition of political theory

In the editor's introduction Richter says there is **no uncontested definition of political theory** and notes that usages vary with:

- epistemology;
- ethics;
- philosophical method;
- political interests;
- social location of the theorist.

He adopts a broad working jurisdiction encompassing moral/political philosophy and their histories while also allowing description/explanation of political action and arrangements.

Thus Richter's organizational procedure is:

`DEFINE A BROAD SHARED JURISDICTION`
+
`PRESERVE INTERNAL METHODOLOGICAL DIFFERENCE`.

That is almost the opposite of method monopoly.

---

## V. Pocock's chapter makes the interdisciplinary problem explicit

Pocock opens his contribution with a deliberately sharp `First Law of interdisciplinary communication`: methodological debates often degenerate into one scholar telling another that they should stop doing their own job and do the critic's job instead.

Whatever the irony of the formulation, its placement in Richter's volume is analytically valuable.

The institutional problem is not only:

`WHICH METHOD IS TRUE?`

but:

`HOW CAN DIFFERENT SCHOLARLY JOBS / UNITS / PROOF RULES COMMUNICATE WITHOUT COLLAPSING INTO ONE ANOTHER?`.

That is the institutional analogue of the current repo's `UNIT × RELATION` framework.

---

## VI. Richter's later Begriffsgeschichte work can now be historicized as an extension of earlier brokerage

By 1987 Richter publishes `Begriffsgeschichte and the History of Ideas` in JHI, explicitly comparing methodological programmes and insisting that schools be reconstructed from both founders' programmes and actual performances.

By the 1990s his conceptual-history work compares:

- Koselleck / *Geschichtliche Grundbegriffe*;
- history of ideas;
- Pocock's languages/discourses;
- Skinner's conventions, linguistic use, redescription and reevaluation.

In later CSPT material Richter explicitly argues that these approaches can be compatible, mutually reinforcing and interdependent.

This should not be written as one unchanged programme from 1967 to 1995.

The secure continuity is functional/institutional:

`RICHTER REPEATEDLY BUILDS TRANSLATION CONDITIONS AMONG METHOD JURISDICTIONS`.

The precise conceptual vocabulary changes substantially.

---

## VII. Richter also reconnects the global/comparative branch

Current CSPT institutional memory notes Richter's earlier engagement with Chinese political thought and his support for comparative/global political theory.

CSPT's 1977 `East Asian Political Thought` conference shows that non-Western political thought entered this infrastructure relatively early.

Later Richter's conceptual-history work turns explicitly to translation/comparison of political and social concepts.

This creates a potentially important future bridge:

`METHOD TRANSLATION ACROSS DISCIPLINES`
->
`CONCEPT TRANSLATION ACROSS LANGUAGES/TRADITIONS`.

Do not infer that the latter was already fully articulated in the 1960s.

But the institutional repertoire for managing heterogeneous jurisdictions clearly predates the later explicit theory of conceptual translation.

## IX. Historiographic payoff

Richter complicates any story in which methodological schools first form as pure doctrines and only later encounter one another.

In this case the sequence is partly reversed:

`HETEROGENEOUS ACTORS SHARE INSTITUTION`
-> `METHODS BECOME MUTUALLY VISIBLE`
-> `COMPARATIVE METHOD LANGUAGE IS DEVELOPED`
-> `LATER SCHOOL COMPARISONS ARE CANONIZED`.

This suggests a broader history-of-knowledge proposition:

> **Institutional co-presence can precede and enable explicit methodological commensuration.**

That proposition can be tested elsewhere in the Lovejoy/JHI project without assuming a universal pattern.
