# Lovejoy as Orientalist / comparative religion — Batch 162

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: immediate reception/control for Batch161. Test whether the `decomposition–recomposition` sequence is merely a retrospective pairing of Lovejoy 1908/1909, or whether contemporaries themselves recognized a live problem of decomposition versus integration inside pragmatism.

## Core result

The immediate 1908 record confirms that **decomposition was contemporaneously recognized as a defining feature of the pragmatism controversy, and it was already being answered by explicit arguments for simultaneous integration and differentiation before Lovejoy's 1909 `Pragmatism and Realism`.**

The strongest primary control is A. C. Armstrong, `The Evolution of Pragmatism` (_Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods_ 5.24, 19 Nov. 1908, 645–650). Armstrong cites Lovejoy's `The Thirteen Pragmatisms` and describes the movement's development through both `integration and differentiation`. He accepts the need for distinctions, but insists that a large amount of `essential agreement` had existed among pragmatist leaders from the beginning. He closes by saying that analysis and distinction have recently been gaining ground, while integration and differentiation are both destined to persist.

A French contemporary survey in the November 1908 supplement to the _Revue de métaphysique et de morale_ independently names the American trend a `décomposition du pragmatisme`, explicitly places Lovejoy within it, and contrasts Max Meyer as arguing that from the pragmatist standpoint the distinctions disappear.

This materially qualifies Batch161:

> The 1908→1909 sequence remains real in Lovejoy's texts, but **integration after differentiation was already a live field-level response in 1908**. Lovejoy's stronger 1909 delta is therefore narrower: not the generic idea that a movement may contain both unity and diversity, but the specific rule that compatible inherited motives may **modify and interpret one another**, so that historical novelty may reside in the **combination itself** (`new compound in intellectual chemistry`).

## 1. Armstrong 1908 — primary text recovered through JSTOR

A. C. Armstrong, `The Evolution of Pragmatism`, _JPP&SM_ 5.24 (19 Nov. 1908), pp.645–650, DOI `10.2307/2011792`. The JSTOR PDF is directly readable and has now been inspected in full.

Armstrong's opening is decisive. He says the history of pragmatism has been confused, but that criticism from outside and reflection inside the movement have begun to clarify it. At the same time:

- there has been a `large amount of essential agreement` among pragmatic leaders from the beginning;
- opinion has crystallized around some fundamental principles;
- differences have developed around other positions entering into or correlated with the central doctrine;
- in his explicit Spencerian formula, **`both integration and differentiation have characterized the development of the movement`**.

This is already a field-level attempt to reject the false choice:

`one unified doctrine` **versus** `thirteen unrelated doctrines`.

Armstrong instead treats pragmatism as an evolving historical complex whose components can differentiate while some central relations integrate.

---

## 2. Armstrong cites Lovejoy but does not simply accept Lovejoy's fragmentation

On p.648 Armstrong turns directly to Lovejoy. He asks whether the pragmatic method itself contains internal distinctions or differences of application and says that, from the critical standpoint, Lovejoy has contended for thirteen pragmatisms.

But Armstrong immediately places Lovejoy in a wider field:

- Schiller had already emphasized the need for accurate distinctions according to specific cognitive purposes;
- Dewey had distinguished the meanings of `practical` as applied to objects, ideas, and beliefs;
- Lovejoy's thirteen are therefore one especially sharp instance of an ongoing differentiation problem, not an isolated invention of decomposition.

Armstrong accepts that distinctions are real. The issue is what follows from them.

---

## 3. Differentiation may reveal development rather than destroy movement identity

Armstrong repeatedly treats divergence as internal development.

Examples:

- methodological pragmatism is compatible with diverse philosophical conclusions;
- humanism is broader than pragmatism, and methodological differentiation is occurring even there;
- different applications of the pragmatic method need to be distinguished;
- disputes over value may indicate a genuine `cleavage within the school`;
- pragmatism's relation to metaphysics shows further differentiation.

The point is not to deny heterogeneity. It is to treat heterogeneity as something whose **historical relation** must itself be analyzed.

This creates an important contrast with a simplistic reading of Lovejoy 1908:

`logical independence of contentions`  
does not automatically entail  
`historical non-unity of the movement`.

That distinction is highly relevant to Batch160/161.

---

## 4. Armstrong's conclusion: analysis is gaining, but integration remains

Armstrong's final paragraph makes the control explicit:

- pragmatism is evolving as method, epistemology, and metaphysics;
- `integration and differentiation have both been taking place`;
- their relative predominance depends on stage of development;
- both tendencies are likely to persist;
- nevertheless the `process of analysis and distinction` has recently been gaining ground.

Thus the immediate American reception gives us a historically contemporary pair of operations:

`analysis/distinction/differentiation`  
+ `integration`.

This predates Lovejoy's 1909 `too atomistically` / `new compound` language.

Therefore do **not** attribute the generic decomposition–integration pair to Lovejoy 1909 as an invention.

## 5. French 1908 control — the movement is literally described as `décomposition du pragmatisme`

The November 1908 supplement of the _Revue de métaphysique et de morale_ surveys recent American philosophy and opens its discussion of _JPP&SM_ by saying what most strikes the reader is the American tendency to distinguish multiple meanings in pragmatist vocabulary and diverse conceptions within the school:

> `c'est une décomposition du pragmatisme`.

The survey then says:

- James, Schiller, and Bawden are attempting synthetic/general presentations;
- philosophers associated with the American discussion are simultaneously exposing what is mixed or incoherent in pragmatism;
- Max Meyer is singled out as saying that, from the pragmatist point of view, these distinctions disappear;
- Perry had begun the work and Lovejoy continues it, discovering thirteen pragmatisms;
- Dewey and Strong are also presented as performing differentiating analyses.

The survey later summarizes the situation as apparent `désagrégation et recul` of pragmatism.

This is unusually useful because `decomposition` is not a retrospective project label here. A contemporary observer in 1908 was already using almost exactly that historical description for the American debate.

## 6. Max Meyer — counter-position now securely located, primary detail still pending

Max Meyer, `The Exact Number of Pragmatisms`, _JPP&SM_ 5.12 (1908), pp.321–326, is the direct title-level counterattack to Lovejoy.

The 1908 French survey gives a contemporary synopsis: Meyer argues that **from the pragmatist point of view the distinctions disappear**. Later summaries frequently condense Meyer's position as multiplying pragmatisms by pragmatists rather than accepting Lovejoy's fixed count.

## 7. Boodin 1909 — anti-fragmentation criticism continues

J. E. Boodin's `What Pragmatism Is and Is Not`, _JPP&SM_ 6.23 (1909), pp.627–635, is another direct response in the same problem field. Its bibliographic object is secure.

Modern scholarly transcription/quotation preserves Boodin's p.630 footnote attack on Lovejoy: he calls thirteen pragmatisms a `measly allowance` given the variety of human nature and the possible applications of the pragmatic method, and accuses the `intellectualist` critic of creating prejudice against the opponent.

Boodin's opening positive programme is also clear: pragmatism is, at base, the ordinary scientific testing of hypotheses applied to philosophical hypotheses.

## 8. Attribution revision to Batch161

Batch161's strongest claim was:

`1908 decomposition/evidentiary insulation`  
→ `1909 warranted recomposition/configurational novelty`.

That textual sequence still stands.

But Batch162 shows that one part must be de-proprietized:

### Not Lovejoy-specific by 1909

- the generic need to balance differentiation with integration;
- the idea that a movement may contain real internal differences while retaining some unity;
- the claim that analysis/distinction is a stage in the historical evolution of pragmatism.

Armstrong has all three explicitly in November 1908.

### Still more Lovejoy-specific in 1909

Lovejoy's narrower formulation adds something stronger than Armstrong's coexistence of integration and differentiation:

1. make a complete inventory of historical motives;
2. combine only those that logic permits;
3. let the synthesized motives **modify and interpret each other**;
4. identify historical novelty not merely in integration, but in the **transformed configuration** produced by conjunction;
5. hence `a new compound in intellectual chemistry` whose constituents may all be old.

The safe delta is therefore:

> **from integration as movement-development to configurational transformation as a criterion of historical novelty.**

## 9. Consequence for the notebook argument

This control improves rather than weakens the archive paper.

The notebooks should not be made to foreshadow a uniquely Lovejoyian oscillation between atomism and holism. The surrounding intellectual culture was already intensely concerned with decomposition, differentiation, integration, and the identity of heterogeneous movements.

What the notebooks provide that the public pragmatism debate does not is a **documentary microhistory of the operations being performed before they are stated at meta-level**:

- source propositions are separated from one another;
- evidence types are prevented from settling unrelated questions;
- classifications are broken by diagnostic or causal relation;
- selected relations are then recomposed into a more defensible configuration.

The 1908–09 debate supplies public vocabulary and controversy context for a practice the notebooks let us watch in motion.

## 11. Historiographical guardrails

- Do not treat `decomposition` itself as a Lovejoy invention; contemporaries used the description in 1908 and Armstrong framed the field as integration plus differentiation.
- Do not claim Armstrong caused Lovejoy's 1909 formulation. Chronological availability and thematic proximity do not establish influence.
- Do not infer correspondence/reply relations among Armstrong, Meyer, Boodin, and Lovejoy without documentary evidence.
- Do not call the 1908 `contentions` unit-ideas.
- Preserve the narrower Lovejoy delta: **mutual modification under synthesis + historical novelty at configuration level**.
