## 2. A. B. Ellis source-closes the guardian-spirit localization mechanism

Alfred Burdon Ellis, *The Yoruba-Speaking Peoples of the Slave Coast of West Africa* (London: Chapman and Hall, 1894), provides a very close pre-Paris source for the first half of the p.90 summary.

Ellis describes human victims slain upon important artificial objects such as royal stools and drums. The blood gushes over the object, and the victim's ghost is understood to become a guardian-spirit attached to it. He then explicitly generalizes the principle:

- natural objects may already possess indwelling/guardian spirits;
- artificial objects can be supplied with artificial guardian spirits;
- Pacific human victims killed at the launching of war-canoes and a Dyak slave-girl crushed under the first post of a communal house are parallel cases;
- in each case the sacrifice is designed to provide a guardian-spirit.

The same book also describes a state/temple drum whose protecting spirit is that of a slave sacrificed upon it.

### Ownership

`HUMAN VICTIM`
->
`GHOST / GUARDIAN SPIRIT`
->
`LOCALIZED ON / SUPPLIED TO IMPORTANT OBJECT OR PLACE`

is **Ellis-owned at proposition/mechanism level**, S3/F/A0–A1.

p.90 cannot be used to claim that Lovejoy originated the idea that human sacrifice may localize a spirit in a material place/object.

## 3. Mary Kingsley source-closes the gateway-blood packet

Mary Kingsley, *Travels in West Africa* (1897), gives the other major p.90 packet almost directly.

In villages with spirit-protective gateways/fences, Kingsley says that when smallpox is raging or war threatens:

- the gateways are sprinkled with the blood of sacrifices;
- sheep/goats are kept for such sacrifices and blood-fines;
- villagers eat the meat;
- the meat `has nothing to do with the sacrifice to the spirits`;
- the sacrificial component is the blood;
- `the blood is the life`.

This is a particularly important source distinction because Kingsley herself already separates:

`MEAT / ALIMENTARY USE`
from
`BLOOD / SPIRIT-RELATED SACRIFICIAL USE`.

### Ownership

`EPIDEMIC / WAR DANGER`
->
`SACRIFICIAL BLOOD ON GATEWAY`
->
`PROTECTIVE / SPIRIT-RELATED FUNCTION`

is Kingsley-owned, S3/A0–A1.

The ethnographic packet is not Lovejoy-generated.

## 6. Kingsley does not by herself prove `direct non-spiritual blood efficacy`

This distinction matters.

Kingsley's gateway passage says blood is the sacrificial element offered in a spirit-protective context because `blood is the life`. Her text does **not** require a fully impersonal theory of blood acting without spirits.

Therefore it would also be an overcorrection to write:

> `Kingsley already says the blood acts directly and non-spiritually.`

She source-closes the **gateway blood / blood-life / protective sacrifice packet**, not necessarily Lovejoy's possible later reallocation of that packet into a category of intrinsic/material efficacy.

That latter move remains exactly what the manuscript image must decide.

## 7. Relation to p.87–89

The p.90 distinction is adjacent to an already inherited conceptual field:

- Tylor: strict fetishism requires a spirit-object relation;
- Lang: objects called fetishes may in fact operate through multiple reasons/mechanisms;

Thus even if p.90 ultimately proves a Lovejoy-local distinction between spirit-localization and intrinsic blood efficacy, the component categories themselves are field-owned.

The Lovejoy claim would be narrow:
