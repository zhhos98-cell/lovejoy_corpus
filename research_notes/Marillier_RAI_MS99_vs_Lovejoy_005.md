# Léon Marillier, RAI MS 99 ↔ EPHE teaching dossiers ↔ Lovejoy notebook 005

## Status

Major archival identification. The Royal Anthropological Institute of Great Britain and Ireland (RAI) holds a substantial manuscript group catalogued as **MS 99 — L. Marillier, “Notes and extracts.”** The public catalogue describes it as handwritten material in paper folders with beige cloth spines, 31 × 24.5 cm, and gives eleven thematic entries.

This corrects the earlier working assumption that Marillier's scholarly papers survive only in dispersed correspondence and isolated manuscripts. **There is at least one consolidated body of Marillier-attributed research notes.** It is not yet safe to call MS 99 a complete personal fonds or to assume every note is in Marillier's hand: provenance, handwriting, dating, and the anomalous references to `E. Torday` remain to be established from the originals/digital surrogates and RAI hand-list.

The thematic architecture of MS 99 corresponds extraordinarily closely to Marillier's EPHE teaching sequence and to the architecture of Lovejoy's `MS38_004_001_061_005`.

## 2. MS 99 is almost a material index to Marillier's EPHE conference sequence

The EPHE annual reports provide an independent chronology for the same dossiers.

### 1889–1890: death, dead, sorcery

Marillier's conference covered:

- cult of the dead and sorcery in Oceania;
- funerary rites;
- comparative myths of the origin of death;
- abode of the dead;
- trials undergone by souls after terrestrial life.

This corresponds directly to MS 99 `/1` and `/10`.

### 1890–1893: taboo and initiation

The reports cover Australian, New Zealand and Oceanic taboo, secret societies and initiation in Melanesia, with explicit conclusions that sacred/impure are initially entangled, religious interdictions arise from supernatural danger, and such interdictions need not originally be moral.

This corresponds directly to MS 99 `/9`, and probably parts of `/2` and `/10`.

### 1893–1894: survivance and justice

Marillier's printed memoir `La survivance de l'âme et l'idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés` develops the non-moral continuation thesis.

This corresponds to `/1–2`.

### 1894–1897: flood myths and marriage

EPHE reports track:

- flood myths in North America, South America, Oceania, Asia and other regions;
- marriage rites in Polynesia, Melanesia, Australia, Micronesia, northern/central Asia and North America;
- analytical separation of union rites from purification/preservation and fecundating rites.

This maps almost exactly onto MS 99 `/5–8`.

### 1897–1900: human sacrifice / anthropophagy / agrarian rites

Marillier's courses study funerary, expiatory, anthropophagic and magical human sacrifice, first in West Africa and then comparatively in southern Africa, the Guinea coast, North America and non-Aryan India, alongside animal, agrarian and fecundating sacrifice.

This maps directly onto MS 99 `/4`.

### 1900–1901: totemism + human sacrifice in America

The final year's programme was `Examen de quelques théories récentes relatives au totémisme` and `L'anthropophagie rituelle et les sacrifices humains (Amérique)`.

This corresponds to MS 99 `/3` and the wider sacrifice dossier.

**Implication:** MS 99 is not merely a random collection of Marillier notes. Its folders reproduce the main research/teaching dossiers that structured his EPHE career from 1889 to 1901.

## 4. Digital access: much better than the old catalogue implies, but MS 99 must still be verified item-by-item

The AIM25 catalogue compiled in 2007 says only one item in the whole RAI manuscript collection had then been digitised. That statement is historically obsolete for present digital-access planning.

RAI now states that visitors have full in-house access to the **Wiley Digital Archives** RAI resource. Wiley describes the RAI digital archive as nearly one million pieces and explicitly includes manuscripts, notes, fieldwork, personal papers and previously uncatalogued material. Several subscribing-library descriptions characterize the database as digitized RAI archives covering approximately 1763–2016, with digital images and searchable text/metadata.

A 2019 review of the Wiley product states that Wiley digitized everything at RAI that it could, apart from privacy/copyright/sensitivity exclusions. This makes MS 99 a **high-probability digital target**, but public web indexing has not yet produced a Wiley item identifier for `Marillier` or `MS 99`. Therefore:

- **secure:** MS 99 exists physically and is publicly catalogued;
- **secure:** RAI has a large Wiley Digital Archives digitization and grants in-house access;
- **probable, not yet confirmed:** MS 99 itself is in Wiley Digital Archives;
- **not yet known:** exact WDA collection/box/folder identifiers, image count, OCR availability, or download permissions for MS 99.

The most efficient next request to the RAI Archivist is not a generic enquiry about Marillier. It should ask specifically whether **GB 1446 MS 99 /1–11** is present in Wiley Digital Archives and, if so, for its WDA identifier / collection-box-folder hierarchy; otherwise ask for a scan estimate for `/1–4`, `/9–11` first.

## 5. The `E. Torday` problem

Three catalogue entries contain `E. Torday`:

- `/2` Folk-lore – Survivance – Völkerpsychologie `E. Torday` psychologie religieuse
- `/7` Mythes, legends `E. Torday` contes de l'Océanie
- `/10` Culte des morts `E. Torday` sorcellerie

This cannot be ignored. Emil Torday (1875–1931) worked in equatorial Africa from 1900 and his separate RAI papers are catalogued as **MS 192–198 and 424–425**, dated mainly 1908–1931. Marillier died in October 1901. The presence of Torday's name in MS 99 therefore creates a provenance problem.

Possible explanations, ranked only as hypotheses:

1. **Later archival subject labels or cross-references** inserted when the folders were arranged/catalogued.
2. **Later additions to Marillier's original folders** by another scholar/archivist, perhaps using Torday publications/material.
3. **A mixed dossier** in which Marillier-attributed papers and later material were physically combined.
4. Less likely: very early Torday material from 1900–1901 already known to Marillier. Torday was in Congo from 1900, but the public catalogue of his RAI fonds and his better-known publications point later; this requires documentary proof before use.

The solution requires image-level examination of folder covers, handwriting, inks/papers, internal dates, and bibliographic references. **Until then MS 99 must be described as “Marillier-attributed notes and extracts” rather than uniformly autograph Marillier papers.**

A useful control is that the folder headings without `Torday` reproduce EPHE course subjects demonstrably taught before 1901, often nearly verbatim. This strongly supports an authentic Marillier core even if some folders were augmented later.

## 6. Acquisition/provenance remains unresolved

The AIM25 collection-level description says that donors are noted in the RAI hand-list where known, while some collections have unknown provenance. The public MS 99 webpage does not expose a donor or acquisition date.

Do not infer an acquisition date from the running number `99`: AIM25 explicitly says manuscript numbers have no significance, and neighboring numbers contain materials of very different creation dates.

Priority archival questions:

1. Who donated or transferred MS 99 to the RAI, and when?
2. Is there an accession ledger entry or donor correspondence in RAI institutional archives?
3. Are folder-cover inscriptions in Marillier's hand, Torday's hand, or an archivist's hand?
4. Are all contents pre-1901?
5. What does `8 pts.` mean relative to `/1–11`?
6. Is the current arrangement original, inherited, or archival?
7. Is MS 99 fully/partly digitized in Wiley Digital Archives?

## 7. Research consequence

This changes the project substantially. We now have three potentially collatable documentary layers:

> **Marillier's own/attributed working dossiers (RAI MS 99)**  
> ↓  
> **Lovejoy's student/research notebook (MS38_004_001_061_005)**  
> ↓  
> **Lovejoy's conceptual abstraction by 1900 and published 1906 `Primitive Energetics`**

The central task is no longer merely to infer influence from published teaching reports. It is to **collate teacher working papers against student notes**. If MS 99 and 005 share distinctive source order, page references, case clusters, corrections, or classificatory language, the Marillier → Lovejoy transmission can be reconstructed at manuscript level.

The same MS 99 folders also offer controls outside 005 (marriage, flood) that can test whether correspondence reflects general Marillier filing practice rather than retrospective overfitting.
