# Lovejoy 1906 ↔ 1933 — `generic Man`, evolutionary relativism, and the older stage grammar behind `primitive`

Date: 2026-09-03  
Status: **1906 DIRECT PRIMARY / 1933 FULL TRANSLATION WITNESS + BIBLIOGRAPHIC PRIMARY CONTROL / NON-CAUSAL SAME-ACTOR RETROSPECTIVE COMPARISON**

## Core result

A new same-actor cross-temporal control links Lovejoy's **1906 political use of human differentiation** with his **1933 retrospective history of Rousseau and Monboddo**.

The relation is not textual dependence and not a proof that Lovejoy consciously reused one framework unchanged.

It is narrower:

> **In 1906 Lovejoy himself used a stage/population differentiation to break the Enlightenment's homogeneous `generic Man`; in 1933 he retrospectively described Rousseau/Monboddo social evolution as an early attack on a uniform conception of human nature and as a precursor of evolutionary relativism.**

This matters for the larger `primitive` project because it places the grammar of

`LIVING / CONTEMPORARY HUMAN DIFFERENCE`
→ `EARLIER DEVELOPMENTAL STAGE`
→ `DIFFERENT INSTITUTIONAL / SOCIAL FORM`

inside an eighteenth-century genealogy that Lovejoy himself recognized.

The later operational category `primitive` therefore should not be described as inventing stratified developmental time from nothing.

A stronger historical problem is:

> **how nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century human sciences stabilized, technicalized and redistributed an older developmental/stadial grammar through new disciplinary categories, populations, evidentiary regimes and institutional jurisdictions.**

## I. 1906 — Lovejoy's own political stratification is direct primary

Authority:

`research_notes/Lovejoy_1906_Democracy_direct_primary_stratification_control_2026-08-27.md`.

In `Democracy in the Twentieth Century` Lovejoy contrasts:

### eighteenth-century `Nature`

- universal rational norms;
- `man as man`;
- political institutions imagined as broadly valid across peoples/times;
- homogeneous `generic Man`.

### nineteenth-century differentiation

The conjunction of:

- **stages of social evolution**;
- **race / nationality**;

splits that generic political subject in two directions:

- **longitudinally** — historical/cultural stage;
- **cross-wise** — racial/national differentiation.

Lovejoy does not give both axes identical modal force. Stage differentiation carries the harder developmental hierarchy; racial/national difference remains consequential but more qualified.

The political operation is:

`GENERIC HUMAN SUBJECT`
→ `HISTORICALLY / POPULATION-DIFFERENTIATED SUBJECTS`
→ `INSTITUTIONAL FIT VARIES BY STAGE / POPULATION`
→ `GENERAL EVALUATIVE END RETAINED`.

This is Lovejoy's own 1906 political argument, including racialized consequences.

## II. 1933 — Lovejoy retrospectively identifies an eighteenth-century attack on uniform human nature

Object:

Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Monboddo and Rousseau`, *Modern Philology* 30.3 (1933): 275–296; reprinted in *Essays in the History of Ideas* (1948), 38–61.

Current full-access witness:

Spanish translation from the 1948 English reprint:
`https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0718-43602007000100013&script=sci_arttext`.

### A. Human distinctiveness is acquired, not primitively given

Lovejoy reconstructs Rousseau/Monboddo around perfectibility and the distinction between potentiality and actuality.

For Monboddo, distinctively human capacities are acquired through development rather than simply present in full from the beginning.

Lovejoy consequently calls Monboddo an evolutionist in a deeper sense than the biological orangutan/species question.

The historical object is therefore:

`HUMAN NATURE AS FIXED ACTUALITY`
→
`HUMAN CAPACITY + HISTORICAL DEVELOPMENT`.

### B. Lovejoy explicitly says this attacks a uniform conception of human nature

In the 1933 argument Lovejoy says this developmental way of thinking attacks both:

- primitivism;
- the `uniform conception of human nature` associated with it in earlier modern thought.

He then describes Monboddo's criticism of assuming that humans have always been essentially like contemporary civilized Europeans.

Historical variation and the stages of human development become central evidence against universalizing from one contemporary population.

### C. Lovejoy calls the result a form of evolutionary relativism

Lovejoy treats the distrust of universal political/social formulas generated by this developmental view as a precursor of an `evolutionary relativism` characteristic of nineteenth-century political and social philosophy.

Thus the 1933 retrospective genealogy is:

`UNIFORM HUMAN NATURE`
→ challenged by
`HISTORICAL VARIATION / DEVELOPMENT`
→
`SOCIAL-EVOLUTIONARY RELATIVITY OF HUMAN FORMS`.

This is strikingly compatible with the structural grammar Lovejoy himself used politically in 1906.

Compatibility is not proof of direct self-reference.

## III. 1933 — `living peoples` become evidence for earlier stages

Lovejoy's Monboddo reconstructs a proposed `History of Man` tracing the species through successive stages from near animality toward developed civilization.

The program requires studying the customs of peoples described in the period as `barbarous` rather than theorizing human nature only from civilized nations.

The inferential structure is:

`CONTEMPORARY ETHNOGRAPHIC DIFFERENCE`
→ used as evidence for
`EARLIER SOCIAL / HUMAN DEVELOPMENTAL STAGES`.

Lovejoy says Monboddo envisaged a new kind of history and an empirical science of human development grounded in such evidence, while also judging him too optimistic about how fully the reconstruction could be achieved.

This is important for the `primitive` project because the **temporalization of living human difference** is already visible in the eighteenth-century genealogy Lovejoy reconstructs.

Therefore:

`LIVING PEOPLE -> EARLIER STAGE`

is not itself an early-twentieth-century invention.

## V. Consequence for the larger `primitive` thesis

A formulation such as

> `after the Enlightenment's unified-human-history project collapsed, twentieth-century human sciences invented stratified chronology through primitive`

would now be too strong.

The 1933 Lovejoy control shows that he himself located important elements of the developmental/stadial break **within the eighteenth century**, especially Rousseau/Monboddo.

A better model is:

### Stage 1 — earlier developmental grammar

`UNIFORM HUMAN NATURE`
→ challenged by
`HISTORICAL DEVELOPMENT / STAGES`
→ contemporary human difference can stand as evidence for earlier states.

### Stage 2 — nineteenth-century expansion

Evolution, race/nation, anthropology and historical comparison multiply and harden the axes of differentiation.

### Stage 3 — late nineteenth / early twentieth disciplinary operationalization

`primitive` can then function as a technical allocation device across:

- textual strata;
- logical/conceptual strata;
- social populations;
- ritual survivals;
- developmental stages;
- geography/living peoples;
- institutional disciplinary jurisdictions.

The research novelty therefore shifts from **the invention of stratification** to **the technical organization and cross-disciplinary redistribution of stratification**.

This is compatible with the current project emphasis that the problem is not simple contradiction but layering.

## VI. A useful distinction: developmental grammar != `primitive` as operational concept

Do not collapse:

`STADIAL / DEVELOPMENTAL HUMAN HISTORY`

into

`PRIMITIVE AS TECHNICAL CATEGORY`.

The former can predate the latter and provide part of its infrastructure.

The historical question becomes:

> **What new work does `primitive` perform once a developmental grammar is already available?**

Possible functions to test rather than assume:

1. compress several temporal positions into one label;
2. map living populations onto historical stages;
3. distinguish survivals/composites within texts and rituals;
4. convert chronology into a comparative classificatory grid;
5. allow a discipline to allocate competence over heterogeneous evidence;
6. stabilize relations among logical, temporal, geographic and social `earlier-than` positions.

These are research hypotheses, not conclusions supplied by the 1933 article alone.

## VII. Political firewall

The 1906 political use has racialized and colonial consequences and must not be sanitized into neutral contextualism.

The 1933 historical reconstruction should likewise not be treated as a politically innocent genealogy merely because it is retrospective intellectual history.

At the same time:

- 1933 attribution of an eighteenth-century developmental grammar does not prove Lovejoy endorsed all Monboddo/Rousseau political implications;
- 1906 use of stage/race differentiation does not prove the 1933 article was a retrospective self-justification;
- structural similarity is not causal continuity.

Safe relation:

`1906 ACTOR POLITICAL USE`
~ structurally compatible with ~
`1933 ACTOR HISTORIOGRAPHICAL RECOGNITION OF AN EARLIER DEVELOPMENTAL GRAMMAR`.

## VIII. Strongest current formulation

> **Lovejoy's 1933 reconstruction of Rousseau and Monboddo complicates any account in which stratified human time begins with the twentieth-century category `primitive`. He traces an important break with uniform human nature into the eighteenth century itself: human capacities become developmental, social forms become stage-relative, and living peoples can be read as evidence for earlier conditions. The later historical problem is therefore not the invention of layering but the ways nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century human sciences operationalized, hardened and redistributed this older developmental grammar through categories such as `primitive`. Lovejoy's own 1906 political writing provides a non-causal same-actor control: there he explicitly split Enlightenment `generic Man` longitudinally by stages of social evolution and cross-wise by racial/national difference.**
