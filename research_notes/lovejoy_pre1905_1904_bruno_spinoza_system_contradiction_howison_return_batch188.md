## Core result

Lovejoy's 1904 paper, `The Dialectic of Bruno and Spinoza`, is a substantially stronger pre-1905 endpoint than the repo had previously registered at proposition level.

It appeared as pp. 141–174 of **Volume I of the University of California Publications in Philosophy**, a volume prepared to commemorate the seventieth birthday of Lovejoy's former Berkeley teacher **George Holmes Howison** on 29 November 1904. A University of California publications catalogue identifies the volume as a Howison commemorative collection and lists Lovejoy, then Professor of Philosophy at Washington University, as the author of item 8. Thus the paper is not merely a random 1904 history-of-philosophy publication: it is also a documented return by Lovejoy into the Berkeley/Howison philosophical publication network in which his undergraduate philosophical formation had occurred.

More important for method, H. Heath Bawden's detailed contemporary review in *The Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* (27 April 1905) describes Lovejoy's paper as an analysis of the **contradictory elements** making up Spinoza's system. Bawden says Lovejoy traces the opposition between **unity and multiplicity** and between **transcendence and immanence** backward through Neo-Platonism and Bruno. The review then quotes Lovejoy's own methodological conclusion: proper understanding requires keeping in mind **`the origin of both sides of the contradiction in a single dialectical ground`**, and consequently why Spinoza could not unequivocally surrender either side without abandoning his whole method of philosophizing.

This is directly relevant to the pre-1905 reconstruction. By 1904 Lovejoy is not simply identifying influence or resemblance. He is treating a philosophical system as a configuration of apparently contradictory propositions whose historical intelligibility depends on reconstructing the **common generative genealogy that makes both sides necessary to the system**.

The safest code is:

`CONTEMPORARY PROPOSITION-LEVEL RECEPTION + DIRECT LOVEJOY QUOTATION / SYSTEM-DECOMPOSITION AND GENEALOGICAL RECOMBINATION / PRIMARY ARTICLE FULL-TEXT AUTOPSY STILL OPEN`.

## 1. The publication is institutionally a Howison return

The University of California publication catalogue describes Volume I of its philosophy series as:

`Studies in Philosophy prepared in commemoration of the seventieth birthday of Professor George Holmes Howison, November 29, 1904.`

It identifies Lovejoy's contribution as:

`The Dialectic of Bruno and Spinoza, by Arthur Oncken Lovejoy, Professor of Philosophy in Washington University`, pp. 141–174.

The series history further states that the volume was prepared under a committee of Howison's pupils including Evander Bradley McGilvary, Charles Henry Rieber, Harry Allen Overstreet, and Charles Montague Bakewell.

The institutional inference should be kept narrow. The volume proves that Lovejoy contributed to a formal commemorative publication for his former teacher and re-entered a Berkeley philosophical network as a Washington University professor. It does **not** by itself prove direct correspondence with Howison in 1904, attendance at a birthday event, agreement with Howison's personal idealism, or participation in the editorial committee.

This matters because Lovejoy's pre-1905 trajectory now has another person-specific institutional layer:

`Berkeley / Howison undergraduate formation`

→ `Harvard / Buddhist-Indic work`

→ `Paris / comparative religion`

→ `Stanford / Washington University`

→ **`1904 contribution to Howison commemorative UC philosophy volume`**.

His intellectual movement across institutions did not erase the earlier Berkeley philosophical network.

## 2. The 1905 review gives a proposition-level account of the 1904 paper

Bawden's review is unusually useful because it does more than list the title. It summarizes the argument at the level of analytical operations.

The review calls Lovejoy's essay an `able exposition and estimate of the contradictory elements which go to make up Spinoza's system`.

It identifies two paired oppositions:

- `unity ↔ multiplicity`;
- `transcendence ↔ immanence` of the divine.

Lovejoy is said to trace those contradictions backward to their dialectic in **Neo-Platonism and Bruno**.

This is already more precise than the generic statement that the paper concerns Bruno's influence on Spinoza. The historical object is decomposed before genealogy is assigned:

`Spinoza's system`

→ isolate internally opposed components

→ identify the historical dialectic that generated both

→ explain why both remain structurally indispensable.

Evidence grade: **A-/contemporary detailed review of the original publication**. It is not equivalent to a full article autopsy, but the review is near-contemporary and supplies an explicit quotation from Lovejoy.

---

## 3. Lovejoy's quoted rule: both sides have one historical-dialectical ground

Bawden quotes Lovejoy directly. For proper interpretation, one must keep in view:

> `the origin of both sides of the contradiction in a single dialectical ground`

and therefore the impossibility for Spinoza of giving up either side unequivocally without abandoning his whole method of philosophizing.

This formulation is important because it makes the relation between **contradiction** and **genealogy** explicit.

The contradiction is not treated as evidence that the system is merely incoherent. Nor is one side simply discarded as accidental contamination. Instead:

`A and B conflict in the present system`

but

`A and B arise from one inherited dialectical problem`

therefore

`the historical genealogy explains why the present system retains both`.

That is a strong 1904 example of **historical explanation by structured incompatibility**.

It also helps refine the user's larger stratification argument: a composite system need not be flattened into one homogeneous doctrine, and contradiction need not terminate analysis. Contradiction can itself be historically produced by the persistence of distinguishable components with different logical directions.

## 4. 1904 sharpens, rather than merely repeats, the 1902 operator

Batch75 established the following direct 1902 operations in `Religion and the Time-Process`:

- Buddhism and Platonism can share a logical motive in different forms;
- Aristotle can have obvious affinity with Vedānta without historical influence;
- logical import can be separated from temperament;
- Christianity can be decomposed into Judeo-Christian and Hellenic materials;
- Royce can combine modern reflection with a surviving ancient perfectionist category, producing `warring categories`.

The 1904 Bruno–Spinoza case adds a different explanatory shape.

### 1902

`heterogeneous inherited components`

→ coexist / survive / recombine

→ generate tension in a later system.

### 1904

`apparently contradictory components inside one system`

→ trace both backward

→ recover a **single historical-dialectical ground** that generated the opposition

→ explain why the system cannot simply discard one side.

This is not yet the later formal vocabulary of `unit-ideas`. But it is closer to mature Lovejoy historical practice than a generic source-and-influence study because **the explanatory target is the internal structure of the received idea-complex, not merely the fact of transmission**.

A good pre-1905 formulation is therefore:

> **Lovejoy increasingly treats inconsistency inside philosophical systems as an historical object. The historian's task is not simply to identify which proposition came from whom, but to reconstruct why logically opposed propositions entered, remained in, and jointly organized a system.**

## 5. `Otherworldliness` persists into the 1904 Bruno paper

An independently indexed quotation attributed to printed p. 174 of the 1904 essay records Lovejoy describing a possible endpoint as a thorough-going **`metaphysical otherworldliness`**, in which what is truly real and of genuine worth is neither the fulfilment nor explanation of common temporal experience but its negation.

This lexical witness is significant because it places the explicit term `otherworldliness` inside the 1904 Bruno paper, not only in the 1898 lecture title, the 1901 Stevenson syllabus annotation, and the 1902 `Religion and the Time-Process` argument.

Current chain:

`1898 — Other Worldliness [public lecture title]`

→ `1901 — otherworldly ethics vs new temporally based ethics [Wilson-mediated syllabus annotation]`

→ `1902 — signed full philosophical use in Religion and the Time-Process`

→ **`1904 — metaphysical otherworldliness in Bruno–Spinoza historical analysis`**.

The evidence ceiling must be explicit: the p.174 wording is presently recovered through a lexical quotation index rather than a direct image/full-text autopsy of the original 1904 page. It should therefore be treated as a strong locator to verify against the primary volume, not as a substitute for page-image collation.

## 6. The Howison volume itself contains a useful negative control

The commemorative volume is intellectually plural. The contemporary review discusses McGilvary on the highest good, Rieber and Bakewell on pragmatism/idealism, Henderson on evolution and education, and other distinct contributions before reaching Lovejoy.

Therefore Lovejoy's inclusion in a Howison Festschrift does not imply doctrinal membership in a single `Howison school`. His own paper is historical and genealogical, while the volume contains contributors defending positions different from one another.

The safe network claim is:

`formal participation in Howison commemorative philosophical publication network`

not

`continued adherence to Howison's personal idealism`.

This distinction is especially important because later biographical accounts emphasize that Lovejoy moved away from the idealisms associated with Howison and Royce.

## 7. Revised person-specific pre-1905 sequence

The currently strongest chain can now be stated at increasing scale:

### 1890s — Buddhist/Indic notebook work

`witnesses / terms / doctrinal functions / competing genealogies`

### 1898 — Buddhist technical article

`borrowed terms or ideas ≠ borrowed arrangement and application`

### 1901 — public teaching

`Buddhism among Indian philosophies / two essential formulas / ethics and goal`

plus

`otherworldly ethics ↔ temporally based ethics`.

### 1902 — comparative philosophy

`affinity ≠ transmission`; `logical motive ≠ form`; historically heterogeneous categories can survive and recombine.

### 1904 — Bruno and Spinoza

`internal contradiction`

→ `decompose opposed components`

→ `trace both to a common inherited dialectical ground`

→ `explain why both remain constitutive of the system`.

This sequence does not require claiming that Buddhist philology **caused** the Bruno paper. It establishes that the proof-level distinctions visible in the Buddhist work coexist with, and by 1902–04 are deployed at, increasingly large history-of-philosophy scales.
