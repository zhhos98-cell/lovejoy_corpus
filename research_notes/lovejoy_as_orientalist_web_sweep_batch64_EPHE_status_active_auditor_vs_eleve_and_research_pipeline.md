# Batch 64 — EPHE status control: `auditeur ayant pris une part active` is not `élève titulaire`, but sits inside a research-producing conference system

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: clarify exactly what Lovejoy’s 1898–99 EPHE status licenses us to say after Batch 63’s reconstruction of Marillier’s source-critical pedagogy.

## Core result

The phrase attached to Lovejoy in Marillier’s 1898–99 report — **`auditeur ayant pris une part active aux travaux`** — is stronger than simple attendance but should not be silently upgraded to `student`, `élève titulaire`, diploma candidate, or assigned-thesis researcher.

The EPHE records explicitly distinguish:

- `élèves titulaires ayant suivi régulièrement les conférences`;
- `auditeurs ayant pris une part active aux travaux`.

For Marillier in 1898–99, Söderblom, Travers and Schaefer are listed in the first category; Lovejoy, W. S. Andrews and Lorette in the second.

N. W. Thomas’s first-person 1896 description of the EPHE helps decode the distinction. He says ordinary auditors faced essentially no admission condition beyond inscription, whereas candidates for the title of `élève` followed a longer institutional path and could eventually submit a thesis on a **critical or historical question** for the diploma. At the same time, he describes Marillier’s ordinary conference pedagogy as `work by the pupils, directed by the lecturer`, including essays read in conference, Marillier’s comments and peer discussion.

The correct Lovejoy claim is therefore:

> **Lovejoy was not merely an audience member: the official report explicitly marks him as an auditor who took an active part in the work. But the surviving status label does not show that he entered the EPHE diploma track, received a formal thesis assignment, or held `élève titulaire` status.**

This evidentiary distinction matters because it lets the paper use the institutional evidence strongly without converting a real participatory category into a stronger status the archive does not support.

## 1. Direct 1898–99 status evidence

Primary source:

Léon Marillier, `I. Religions des peuples non civilisés`, EPHE Section des sciences religieuses report for 1898–99, p.33.

Persée:
`https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1898_num_12_8_19501`

After describing the two conference blocks — survivance on Monday and human sacrifice / ritual anthropophagy on Tuesday — the report gives three different participation indicators:

- `Nombre des inscrits : 20.`
- `Elèves titulaires ayant suivi régulièrement les conférences : MM. Söderblom (diplômé), Travers, Schaefer.`
- `Auditeurs ayant pris une part active aux travaux : MM. Lovejoy, W. S. Andrews et Lorette.`

The categories are printed separately and should remain separate in our prose.

### Safe wording

- `Lovejoy was an auditor who, according to the official EPHE report, took an active part in the work of Marillier’s conferences.`
- `Lovejoy actively participated in Marillier’s 1898–99 conference work.`

### Avoid unless new evidence appears

- `Lovejoy was an EPHE élève titulaire.`
- `Lovejoy was Marillier’s diploma student.`
- `Lovejoy wrote an assigned EPHE thesis/essay for Marillier.`
- `Lovejoy was formally supervised by Marillier in the modern doctoral sense.`

## 2. N. W. Thomas 1896 explains the institutional ladder

Primary source:

N. W. Thomas, `The École Pratique des hautes Études`, *Folk-Lore* 7 (1896), 200–201.

Wikisource pages:
- p.200: `https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Folk-lore_-_A_Quarterly_Review._Volume_7,_1896.djvu/226`
- p.201: `https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Folk-lore_-_A_Quarterly_Review._Volume_7,_1896.djvu/227`

Thomas emphasizes that the School is `free`: for auditors, the only formality is inscription of one’s name at the Sorbonne.

He then describes a more demanding route for the title of `élève`:

1. follow the course for one year so that the professor can judge the candidate’s industry;
2. after nomination, follow the course for another year;
3. in either of the next two years, submit a thesis on a **critical or historical question**;
4. on a favorable report, the work might be published under the auspices of the School;
5. accepted thesis → diploma.

This is valuable not because Lovejoy necessarily followed the route — there is currently no evidence that he did — but because it demonstrates that Marillier’s conference operated inside an institution explicitly designed to turn seminar work into independent critical/historical research products.

## 3. Thomas’s own trajectory shows the research pipeline functioning

Modern biographical control by Paul Basu identifies Northcote Whitridge Thomas as having gone to the EPHE to study `primitive religion` under Marillier; in 1897 he submitted a thesis on the survival of animal cult in Wales and received the diploma.

Paul Basu, `Who was N. W. Thomas?` (2018):
`https://re-entanglements.net/n-w-thomas/`

The related study then appeared under N. W. Thomas’s name in *Revue de l’histoire des religions* 38 (1898), pp.295–347:

`La survivance du culte totémique des animaux et les rites agraires dans le Pays de Galles`.

Thus at least one Marillier-era participant gives us a visible sequence:

> **conference participation → diploma research on a critical/historical question → publication in RHR.**

Do not project this exact sequence onto Lovejoy. Use Thomas as an institutional control for what the research environment was capable of producing.

## 4. `Active auditor` is a meaningful category across EPHE reports

The phrase is not unique to Lovejoy and does not automatically mean formal student status. EPHE reports repeatedly use `auditeurs ayant pris une part active aux travaux` alongside separately named `élèves titulaires`.

That is useful in two directions.

### It prevents overclaiming

`Active auditor` cannot be translated casually as `student` if the distinction matters analytically.

### It prevents underclaiming

The report does not merely record Lovejoy among twenty registered names. It singles him out, with only two other auditors, as actively participating in the **work**.

The phrase therefore licenses a stronger participation claim than attendance alone.

## 5. The notebook evidence and the status evidence should remain independent but mutually reinforcing

The institutional status does not by itself prove notebook 005 is a course product.

The notebook does not by itself prove Lovejoy’s formal status.

The strength lies in the conjunction:

1. EPHE officially marks Lovejoy as active auditor in Marillier’s work;
2. the same report describes exactly the two research blocks that dominate 005;
3. 005 has Paris material provenance and an internal `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June` slip;
4. the notebook’s operations fit a source-critical pedagogy directly documented from Marillier’s 1888 teaching onward;
5. Thomas independently describes Marillier’s conference as student work, teacher criticism and peer discussion;
6. Mauss later identifies `critique des faits`, bibliography and seminar analysis as Marillier’s inherited practice.

This is enough to reconstruct a **course-linked research environment** without claiming a formal thesis relationship.

---

## 6. Important consequence for article vocabulary

Prefer:

- `active auditor` when translating the status literally;
- `active participant in Marillier’s conference work` when writing smoother prose;
- `course-linked` / `seminar-linked` notebook material;
- `Marillier research environment` or `Marillier conference environment`.

Use `student of Marillier` only in a broad biographical sense and only where the institutional distinction is not being discussed. For evidentiary prose, `active auditor` is better.

This is especially important if the article compares Lovejoy with Söderblom or Thomas:

- Söderblom = named `élève titulaire`, diplômé;
- Thomas = documented diploma-track participant;
- Lovejoy = named active auditor, not presently a diploma-track élève.

Their relations to the same workshop were structurally different.

## 7. New research implication

The next archival question is no longer generically `Was Lovejoy a Marillier student?` We already have a more exact answer.

The sharper question is:

> **What kinds of work could an `auditeur ayant pris une part active aux travaux` actually perform in Marillier’s conference, and does any surviving item show Lovejoy preparing or presenting one?**

Potential evidence would include:

- conference minutes;
- a named essay/presentation;
- correspondence mentioning an assignment;
- Marillier papers/dossiers bearing Lovejoy’s name;
- a second participant’s notebook mentioning Lovejoy;
- notebook 005 material whose physical/source order matches a documented shared packet.

Until such evidence appears, `active participation` is secure and `formal assigned research product` remains HOLD.

- opening methodological framing;
- conclusion;
- references to Marillier / École pratique;
- `origine`, `survivance`, `forme`, `sens`, `analogie`, `ressemblance`, `rite`, `totémisme`, `agraire`.

This is now especially valuable because Thomas is not merely a later former-student control: the article is the publication of research produced on the diploma trajectory contemporaneous with Lovejoy’s Paris approach.

## Bottom line

> **The EPHE evidence now supports a precise middle claim: Lovejoy occupied a formally recognized participatory position in Marillier’s conference work, but not the documented diploma-track position held by the strongest student controls. The archive therefore gives us participation without requiring us to manufacture supervision.**
