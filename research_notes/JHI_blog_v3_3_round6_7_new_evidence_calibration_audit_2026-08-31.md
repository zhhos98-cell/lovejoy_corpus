The new APSR / JHI / *History and Theory* / *Political Theory* evidence does **not** require reopening the substantive architecture of `JHI_blog_full_draft_v3_3_clean_submission_2026-08-29.md`.

## II. KEEP — `known but differently classified`

Current penultimate paragraph says:

> `The formation was known but differently classified.`

Keep exactly or nearly exactly.

This is now one of the strongest sentences in the piece.

The larger repo shows that disciplinary memory is not simply presence/absence:

- actors/texts can remain bibliographically present while changing carrier role;
- a method critique can move from ordinary article to symposium to anthology;
- a field can remain present in review infrastructure while weakly represented in research articles;
- a later canon can make one text foundational while leaving surrounding venue ecology less visible.

Thus the blog's problem is correctly framed as **classification and analytical centrality**, not rediscovery of a forgotten biographical fact.

## V. TIGHTEN — final paragraph should not make `disciplines` a unitary deciding actor

Current sentence:

> `later bibliographies and disciplines decide which of those arrangements become easiest to see.`

Problem exposed by new infrastructure research:

`DISCIPLINE`
is too aggregated a causal unit here.

The repo now directly differentiates:

- publication venues;
- article/review/symposium/supplement roles;
- editor programmes;
- output distribution;
- recruitment/circulation networks;
- bibliographies;
- later anthologies/canons;
- disciplinary histories.

No single `discipline` need consciously decide the canonical arrangement.

Recommended v3.4 wording:

> `later publication venues, bibliographies, and disciplinary histories make some of those arrangements easier to see than others.`

Advantages:

- distributes agency;
- incorporates carrier architecture without jargon;
- replaces intentional `decide` with observable visibility effect;
- remains fully intelligible without adding new citations or a method-history digression.

## VI. TIGHTEN — final question can specify classification rather than generic `made of`

Current final sentence:

> `It is what kind of historical work he did when he did so—and what later histories of a discipline made of that work once other versions of Lovejoy became canonical.`

This is good but now slightly less exact than the evidence permits.

Recommended v3.4 wording:

> `It is what kind of historical work he did when he did so—and how later bibliographies and disciplinary histories classified that work as other versions of Lovejoy became canonical.`

Why:

- `classified` connects directly to the article's governing problem;
- `bibliographies and disciplinary histories` are observable carriers of memory;
- avoids implying one discipline with one intention;
- preserves the blog's closing cadence and thesis.

Optional softer variant if `classified` feels too technical:

> `...and how later bibliographies and disciplinary histories positioned that work as other versions of Lovejoy became canonical.`

Default recommendation remains `classified` because it echoes the penultimate `known but differently classified` line and tightens the conceptual loop.
