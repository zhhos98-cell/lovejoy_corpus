# Lovejoy 005 — Söderblom / Marillier cross-student reattribution after Batches 203–205 (Batch 206)

Date: 2026-08-26  
Status: synced correction note  
Scope: notebook `MS38_004_001_061_005`, especially PDF pp.3–30, checked against Marillier 1893/94, Mauss 1896, the Marillier 1898–99 seminar, and Nathan Söderblom 1901. Ordinary forward chronological boundary = **1910**; later material enters only for explicit Buddhism / India / Sanskrit nodes.

## Core result

Earlier repository notes correctly identified Lovejoy 005 as an active redivision of the Marillier survivance problem, but they **overstated the originality of the determinant taxonomy itself**.

After the direct and page-addressed controls in Batches 203–205, the old formulation

> `Marillier supplies continuation vs retribution; Lovejoy inserts naturalistic/status → ritual → social → half-moral → strict moral/desert`

must be weakened.

The current evidence shows that most of the discriminanda and even much of the **moral-threshold problem** were already distributed across Marillier’s dossier, the Steinmetz controversy, and the parallel work of Marillier’s student Nathan Söderblom:

- earthly/social condition versus moral merit;
- ritual fulfillment as a non-moral determinant;
- manner of death as a non-moral determinant;
- burial/provision and other material qualifications;
- genuinely moral differentiation among the dead;
- contamination/contact as a source-critical problem;
- historical secondary moralization;
- the danger of redescribing strength, wealth, skill, manner of death, or burial provision as moral desert merely to preserve a retribution theory.

The stronger Lovejoy-specific candidate is now not **category invention** but **local redivision and operational recomposition**:

> **Lovejoy places inherited discriminanda into a compact serial testing apparatus, repeatedly asks which selector is actually operative in each borderline case, and carries the same reclassification procedure into later sections of 005.**

Even this should be described as a notebook-level delta rather than a priority claim. Söderblom’s own explicit statement that he follows Marillier with `different divisions` and from a somewhat different point of view proves that **redivision itself was a normal, actor-recognized form of student research in the workshop**.

---

## 1. What Batch 19 got right — and what now needs correction

Batch 19 established a high-value same-teacher / same-seminar comparison between Söderblom 1901 and Lovejoy 005. Its strongest findings remain secure:

- both work from Marillier’s `survivance` problem;
- both use overlapping source clusters and cases;
- Söderblom directly acknowledges Marillier’s conferences and notes;
- 005 directly contains `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`;
- the two students organize shared materials differently.

The part requiring correction is §7–8, where Lovejoy’s outline was treated as a comparatively distinctive move from Marillier’s binary opposition toward a mechanism-based taxonomy.

That formulation reflected the evidence available on 2026-08-18. Since then:

- Batch 203 recovered Marillier 1901 direct distinctions among automatic taboo consequence, group/clan danger or solidarity, and moral culpability/remorse;
- Batch 204 recovered Mauss 1896’s explicit model of later moral beliefs superposed on older eschatological survivals and old journey machinery reused for moral sorting;
- Batch 205 reconstructed, through direct Marillier evidence and Mauss’s page-addressed citations to Marillier pp.37, 42, 44, the presence of status, ritual, manner-of-death, moralized destiny, and contact criticism inside Marillier’s pre-Lovejoy survivance dossier.

Therefore the cross-student contrast must now be phrased more narrowly:

> **Söderblom and Lovejoy are not best contrasted as morphology versus a Lovejoy-invented determinant taxonomy. Both inherit a field already organized around competing determinants and a moral/non-moral discrimination problem. Their difference lies in how they redivide, order, and operationalize that field.**

## 2. Söderblom already occupies much of the alleged Lovejoy-only determinant space

Söderblom’s 1901 `La vie future d’après le mazdéisme` is not a strict upstream source for every early 005 leaf; it is a **same-workshop sibling control** produced from Marillier’s 1898–99 conference environment and directly acknowledged use of Marillier’s notes.

Its evidentiary value is therefore different from Marillier 1893/94 or Mauss 1896. It shows what another participant could produce from the same problem field and source apparatus.

### 2.1 Terrestrial condition / status

Söderblom explicitly organizes portions of his survivance typology around terrestrial condition, fitness, rank, prowess, and status. Cheyenne and other cases are not moral recompense simply because some dead obtain a superior destination.

This overlaps Lovejoy’s repeated rule that earthly rank, wealth, bodily qualification, magical power or warrior status cannot be translated automatically into merit.

### 2.2 Manner of death

Söderblom has a section organized by `les différents genres de mort`, including categories such as drowning, childbirth, violent death, warriors and suicides.

Lovejoy uses the same kinds of cases but asks more insistently what makes the manner of death consequential — ritual condition, clan injury, taboo, exclusion, status, or later moral interpretation.

Thus the **case class** is shared; the local Lovejoy delta is increasingly the repeated mechanism test applied inside the class.

### 2.3 Burial / provision

Söderblom also treats funeral provision and burial condition among the factors governing post-mortem differentiation. In his discussion of Marillier and Steinmetz, such conditions cannot simply be renamed moral deserts in order to rescue a universal retribution theory.

This further erodes the earlier idea that Lovejoy uniquely inserts material/ritual selectors between continuation and morality.

### 2.4 Moral and quasi-moral criteria

Söderblom’s explicit comparison with Marillier takes up the problem of judging allegedly moral or quasi-moral distinctions according to the categories of the societies being studied rather than the analyst’s retrospective rationalization.

The critical point is actor-level classification:

> an observer cannot save a moral-retribution theory merely by declaring strength, wealth, skill, special death or burial provision to be forms of `desert`.

This is very close to the burden of proof that organizes Lovejoy 005 pp.3–20.

Therefore Lovejoy’s repeated use of **desert** remains noteworthy as local wording and as an explicit notebook test, but it should no longer be treated as a secure conceptual invention.

## 3. Marillier’s own dossier now fills the remaining component gaps

Batch 205 supplies the decisive pre-Lovejoy controls.

### Direct Marillier 1893 summary

Marillier says post-mortem rank follows earthly social rank **rather than moral merit**; the other life continues present life rather than repairing earthly injustice; survivance is not founded on moral conceptions.

### Marillier p.44 via Mauss 1896

Mauss explicitly cites Marillier p.44 for cases in which:

- fulfillment of rites;
- kind / manner of death

can determine the future condition of the shade without converting continuation into moral retribution.

### Marillier p.42 via Mauss

Mauss points to Marillier p.42 for North-American cases in which the future condition of the just and criminal is genuinely differentiated. Thus the **moral endpoint** is also inside the earlier Marillier dossier.

### Marillier p.37 via Mauss

Mauss points to Marillier p.37 in the contamination discussion. Christian and other imported categories must be removed before an apparently moralized eschatology can be treated as evidence for an earlier local structure.

### Mauss 1896 historical superposition

Mauss then adds the historical operation: an autochthonous moral belief need not be primitive; moral beliefs can be superposed on older survivals, and old journey / two-road / bridge machinery can be reused to separate good from bad.

Once these controls are combined, the old five-stage Lovejoy originality story loses nearly all of its component-level support.

## 4. Revised attribution of the 005 early outline

The earlier repository model was:

`Marillier binary → Lovejoy multi-stage taxonomy`.

The revised model is:

`Marillier / Steinmetz / Mauss distributed determinant field + seminar dossier circulation`  
→ `parallel student redivision (Söderblom, Lovejoy)`  
→ `Lovejoy local serial ordering + repeated selector tests`.

## 5. `half-moral` must be downgraded from conceptual innovation to local compression

Before Batches 203–205, `half-moral character` could plausibly support a claim that Lovejoy inserted a genuinely new intermediate stage between non-moral continuation and moral retribution.

That reading is now too strong.

The field already contains:

- genuinely non-moral selectors;
- apparently punitive outcomes that are not moral;
- genuinely moralized outcomes;
- mixed/contact-transformed systems;
- historical superposition;
- arguments about what should count as moral or quasi-moral from the actors’ standpoint.

Therefore the publication-safe statement is:

> **`half-moral` is currently best treated as Lovejoy’s local English compression for a transitional problem already structurally present in the Marillier–Steinmetz–Mauss–Söderblom field.**

Exact lexical priority remains open.

---

## 6. `desert` remains important, but now as an operational test rather than an invented criterion

Lovejoy’s notebook repeatedly tightens the word `moral` by asking whether reward or punishment follows **desert**.

This remains analytically important because it turns an abstract controversy into a local coding rule for cases.

But the surrounding conceptual test is not unique to him. Marillier directly contrasts rank with moral merit, and Söderblom’s discussion of Marillier/Steinmetz refuses to redescribe wealth, strength, skill, manner of death or burial provision as moral deservingness merely because they correlate with differentiated future destinies.

The revised distinction is:

> **criterion shared; local operationalization and repeated use potentially Lovejoy-specific.**

Do not write that Lovejoy introduced `desert` as a new conceptual threshold into the field.

---

## 7. Söderblom’s own phrase gives the correct historical category: `different divisions`

The strongest actor-near description of what a student could do with Marillier’s materials comes from Söderblom himself. Around printed p.33 he says he takes up Marillier’s study:

> `avec d’autres divisions et à un point de vue un peu différent`.

This should now discipline the language used for Lovejoy.

Lovejoy’s notebook can be described as another **redivision** of a shared problem field:

- not passive copying;
- not invention ex nihilo;
- not merely thematic influence;
- but a reordering of inherited distinctions into a working sequence suited to his own case-testing.

This is especially appropriate because direct Marillier note circulation is documented for Söderblom. The workshop allowed source and problem continuity to coexist with student-level redivision.

For Lovejoy, actual transfer of identical Marillier sheets remains unproved.

## 8. An actor-near attribution heuristic from Lovejoy’s own 1898 Buddhist paper

Lovejoy’s 1898 Buddhist article argues that terms, perhaps even ideas, may be historically borrowed while their **`arrangement and application`** can still be original and characteristic.

This sentence concerns Buddhist dependent origination, not Lovejoy’s own notebook method. It must not be turned into evidence that he consciously theorized his Paris note-taking in the same way.

But it provides a useful **actor-near attribution heuristic** for the historian:

> when ingredients are demonstrably inherited, originality — if present — may lie in arrangement and application rather than in ownership of the ingredients.

Applied cautiously to 005, that yields a more defensible claim than the old category-invention story:

> **The component distinctions of Lovejoy’s early survivance taxonomy were largely available upstream; what the manuscript newly exposes is their particular arrangement into adjacent tests and their repeated application to ambiguous ethnographic cases.**

This is an analytical symmetry, not a causal claim.

## 9. Consequence for the 004 ↔ 005 comparison

This correction actually makes the cross-notebook relation cleaner.

Notebook 004 is already known to work with inherited philological distinctions and relations, then reassign and recombine them. The 1898 paper explicitly insists that borrowed components do not settle the identity or intelligibility of their arrangement.

Notebook 005 now looks less like a second site where Lovejoy invents new categories and more like another site where **inherited analytical components are redivided and operationally recomposed**.

This does **not** create a new `same algorithm` claim; Batches 54, 73 and 156 have already established the cross-notebook negative-case syntax, proof-level partition and diagnosticity-to-mechanism shift.

The genuinely new result of Batch 206 is attributional:

> **the same caution we already apply to 004 source uptake must now be applied more strictly to 005: document Lovejoy’s delta at the level of redivision, ordering and repeated application, not at the level of owning the categories themselves.**

## 11. Strongest current paper formulation

> **The early section of Lovejoy’s Paris notebook should no longer be read as the invention of a taxonomy absent from Marillier’s work. Marillier’s survivance dossier and the Steinmetz controversy already distributed the relevant discriminants across social rank, ritual performance, manner of death, genuinely moral destiny, source contamination and historical secondary moralization; Söderblom, another participant in the same seminar orbit, explicitly redivided this material and resisted attempts to rename strength, wealth, skill, death-type or burial provision as moral desert. Lovejoy’s documentary delta lies at a narrower level. He places these inherited discriminanda into a compact serial testing apparatus, repeatedly asks which selector is actually producing an apparent reward or punishment, and carries that reclassification procedure into other domains of the notebook. The appropriate historical category is therefore redivision and operational recomposition, not category invention.**

## 12. Evidence discipline

### Secure

- Söderblom explicitly acknowledges Marillier’s conferences and use of Marillier’s notes.
- Söderblom explicitly says he proceeds from Marillier with other divisions and a somewhat different point of view.
- Söderblom classifies survivance through terrestrial condition/status, kinds of death and other non-moral qualifications, then compares the moral criterion.
- Marillier direct 1893 summary contrasts earthly rank with moral merit and continuation with reparation.
- Mauss 1896 explicitly page-cites Marillier for moral differentiation (p.42), contamination (p.37), and ritual/manner-of-death determinants (p.44).
- Mauss 1896 explicitly models moral superposition on older eschatological journey structures.
- 005 contains an adjacent working outline and repeatedly tests apparent punishment/reward against non-moral selectors.

### Strong inference

- Lovejoy’s specific notebook arrangement is best understood as a student-level redivision of a shared Marillier problem field.
- The density and portability of repeated selector-tests across 005 are a stronger Lovejoy-specific manuscript property than any individual determinant category.

### Not proved

- that Lovejoy read Söderblom’s 1901 book before composing the relevant 005 leaves;
- that Söderblom’s printed taxonomy reproduces Marillier’s oral wording exactly;
- that Lovejoy received the same physical Marillier notes as Söderblom;
- that `half-moral` or `desert` are unique Lovejoy coinages;
- that Lovejoy consciously applied his 1898 `arrangement and application` principle to his own comparative-religion notebook practice.
