# Batch 45 — Lovejoy 005 only: Steinmetz’s universal-origin claim against Lovejoy’s mechanism pluralism

Date: 2026-08-18

## Research move

Batches 35 and 44 established the documentary and methodological setting around Steinmetz’s *Endokannibalismus* (1896): the exact title appears in Lovejoy 005; Marillier reviewed it; Mauss reclassified its field; and Marillier’s bibliographies/course notes circulated directly into Mauss’s Steinmetz research.

A further web sweep now sharpens the **substantive target** that Lovejoy’s later notebook resists. Three contemporary or near-contemporary controls converge on the same feature of Steinmetz’s argument:

> **the empirical apparatus is plural and source-critical, but the developmental conclusion is strongly universalizing: endocannibalism is treated as a probable regular custom of primitive humanity, with hunger/food conditions causally prior to later superstition.**

This gives the 005 comparison a more exact structure. Lovejoy’s later pages retain the plural motive-space but refuse to let one historical origin exhaust it.

## 1. W. I. Thomas 1897: plural motives and reliability coding, but hunger precedes superstition

Thomas’s contemporary review in *American Journal of Sociology* 2 (1897), 610–611, confirms three layers of Steinmetz’s apparatus:

- tribes/cases are tabulated;
- motives are assigned case by case;
- the reliability of information is explicitly assessed.

Thomas lists motives such as lack of food, desire for meat, relish for human flesh, and animistic belief. He also praises Steinmetz for presenting contrary evidence and handling unreliable ethnological sources unusually systematically.

But Thomas reports the developmental conclusion in emphatic form: primitive man was necessarily omnivorous, and superstition could not have led humans to cannibalism unless periodic hunger had led them to it earlier.

Thus Steinmetz’s method contains a tension:

> **many motives / many source qualities / many case-types → one causal priority story.**

## 2. A. H. Keane independently preserves Steinmetz’s terminal universal claim

In *Man: Past and Present* (1899/1900-era publication), A. H. Keane cites Steinmetz’s final pages, pp.59–60, and quotes the conclusion that there is a high probability that endocannibalism should be regarded as a **constant custom of primitive humans and of lower savages**.

This is useful because it independently fixes the end-point of Steinmetz’s argument rather than relying only on Thomas’s review summary.

The important historical contrast is now concrete:

- Steinmetz does not merely compare many forms of endocannibalism;
- he reconstructs them toward a strongly generalized primitive condition.

That is precisely the move 005 repeatedly slows down or refuses when formal resemblance outruns causal discrimination.

## 3. Deniker 1900 separates a transfer-of-quality mechanism from Steinmetz’s broad class

J. Deniker’s *The Races of Man* (1900) offers another useful near-contemporary control. In his cannibalism discussion he says that many cases are explained by beliefs that eating all or part of a person permits appropriation of that person’s virtues or qualities, often through the heart, eyes, liver, or blood.

He then treats Steinmetz separately as having grouped a very large body of cases under `endocannibalism`, understood as eating parents/relatives.

Deniker therefore preserves, in one short sequence, exactly the distinction that becomes important for 005:

> **broad behavioural class (`endocannibalism`)**
>
> versus
>
> **specific causal mechanism (appropriation / transfer of qualities through body parts or blood).**

Deniker is not evidence for Lovejoy’s reading and should not be used as a transmission node. He is a useful chronological control showing that contemporaries could already separate the transfer mechanism from Steinmetz’s general category.

## 4. Lovejoy 005 makes that separation systematic

The cleaned 005 pp.106–119 give a much finer motive/mechanism partition:

- famine;
- revenge / hatred;
- affection for relatives;
- acquisition of qualities;
- sympathetic magic;
- propitiation;
- sacramental union.

At p.108 Lovejoy explicitly allows human flesh/body parts to possess a special efficacy that belongs to **sympathetic magic rather than sacrifice or communion**.

At p.119 the transfer relation is anatomically localized:

- head/brain → intelligence;
- heart → courage/breath/life;
- sexual organs → sexual power.

At p.117 sacramental union is summarized as comparatively exceptional.

The notebook therefore performs two cuts at once:

1. **same visible act ≠ same causal mechanism**;
2. **same transfer mechanism ≠ same higher-order religious category**.

This is stronger than describing Lovejoy as simply anti-evolutionist. He continues to make developmental hypotheses, but does not allow a motive taxonomy to collapse back into one universal primitive origin.

## 5. Revised four-level comparison

The current sequence can now be rendered more precisely:

### Steinmetz, 1896

- exhaustive comparative collection;
- motive coding;
- source reliability coding;
- broad endocannibalism category;
- strong universal / naturalistic primitive-origin conclusion.

### Mauss, 1896–97

- attacks overly broad rubrics and over-trust in interpretations;
- narrows `endocannibalisme` to ritual anthropophagy of relatives;
- specifies transfer of the dead person’s virtues;
- classifies that transfer as communion.

### Marillier, 1896–99

- directly reviews Steinmetz’s exact work;
- supplies notes/bibliographies into the Steinmetz controversy;
- later EPHE work in which Lovejoy participates returns to Steinmetz-related problems;
- exact pp.113–115 classification remains unrecovered.

### Lovejoy 005

- preserves the evidence-dense comparative field;
- fragments the behavioural category into motive/mechanism classes;
- separates transfer-of-quality from communion;
- refuses one-origin inference from ritual form or frequency.

This produces a stronger paper-level object: **not simply increasingly refined theories of cannibalism, but successive redefinitions of what counts as a legitimate comparative unit and what evidentiary move licenses historical generalization.**

## 6. Strongest safe formulation

> **Steinmetz’s *Endokannibalismus* combined unusually systematic case collection, motive coding and source criticism with a strongly universalizing developmental conclusion: contemporary readers report that he made hunger causally prior to superstition and treated endocannibalism as a probable regular custom of primitive humanity. Lovejoy’s 005 notebook keeps the plural evidence field but resists precisely that collapse from many motives to one origin. Anthropophagy is redistributed among famine, revenge, kin relation, transferable efficacy, propitiation and communion, while the transfer of bodily qualities is itself detached from communion and treated as a separate causal mechanism.**
