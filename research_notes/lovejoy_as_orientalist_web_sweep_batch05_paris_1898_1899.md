# Lovejoy as Orientalist — web sweep batch 05: Paris, 1898–1899

Date: 2026-08-18

## Major evidentiary upgrade

The Paris year can no longer be described merely as a generic Sorbonne/Bibliothèque Nationale interlude. Daniel J. Wilson's *Arthur O. Lovejoy and the Quest for Intelligibility*, drawing on Lovejoy's letters to Wallace W. Lovejoy dated 17 October, 1 December, and 5 December 1898 (plus a later interview with Lewis S. Feuer), states that Lovejoy went to Paris chiefly to pursue a historical approach to comparative religion. Wilson says that Lovejoy:

- attended some Sorbonne classes;
- studied the Book of Psalms with Maurice Vernes;
- frequently heard Jean Réville lecture on the Fourth Gospel;
- **had hoped to take a course in Pāli Buddhism from Sylvain Lévi**;
- because Lévi was not offering such a course that year, **Lovejoy occasionally went to Lévi's home to read some of the sacred texts with him**;
- spent much of the year in the Bibliothèque Nationale and other libraries pursuing historical investigations into Jewish and other religious thought.

This is a major change to the current model. Lovejoy's Orientalist training did not simply end with Lanman at Harvard. At least according to Wilson's reconstruction from Lovejoy's 1898 letters, it continued in Paris through direct private reading with Sylvain Lévi.

## Independent web context for Sylvain Lévi in 1898

Independent institutional/primary-context sources fit this chronology:

- INHA/AGORHA records Lévi's first Asian scientific mission as **3 October 1897 to September 1898**, covering India, Nepal, and Japan, for linguistic, philosophical, and religious research.
- Contemporary Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres reports document his Nepal research in 1898, including Buddhist/Sanskrit manuscript acquisition and inscriptional work.
- EPHE records for 1898–99 contain Lévi/Foucher material under **"Religions de l'Inde"**; Lévi was by then director of studies for Indian religions at the EPHE and professor of Sanskrit at the Collège de France.

The mission chronology offers a plausible institutional explanation for why the specific Pāli Buddhism course Lovejoy wanted was unavailable around the beginning of his Paris year, but this causal explanation remains an inference. It should not be stated as fact unless the 1898 course programme or Lovejoy's letters say so explicitly.

## Archival route now identified

Johns Hopkins ArchivesSpace exposes a separate collection:

**MS-0873 — Daniel J. Wilson transcriptions of Arthur O. Lovejoy letters**

- one box;
- transcriptions are mostly typewritten, some handwritten;
- date span 1872–1962.

This is a high-value target because Wilson's Paris claims are footnoted directly to Lovejoy letters dated 17 October, 1 December, and 5 December 1898. If those transcriptions are in MS-0873, they may let us move from Wilson's paraphrase to Lovejoy's own wording without first locating scattered originals.

## Consequences for the chronology

The strict Orientalist core now has a stronger continuous sequence:

1. **Harvard 1896–97:** formal Sanskrit and sacred Buddhist-text study under Charles R. Lanman; comparative religion under Charles C. Everett; Hebrew religion under Crawford H. Toy.
2. **c.1896–98:** notebook `MS38_004_001_061_004`, "Sankhya + Buddhism," now fully first-pass transcribed.
3. **1898:** JAOS paper on `upādāna` and `upādisesa`.
4. **Paris 1898–99:** intended Pāli-Buddhism study; occasional private sacred-text reading with Sylvain Lévi; Psalms with Maurice Vernes; Réville on the Fourth Gospel; extensive library work in comparative/historical religion.
5. **1901:** four-lecture syllabus, "The Philosophy of Buddhism," at the Washington University Association.

This makes the early Buddhist/Indic episode look considerably less like a single-paper anomaly.

## Important caution on Pāli competence

The new Paris evidence still does **not** prove a separately completed formal Pāli course. Wilson explicitly says the desired Pāli Buddhism course was not offered. Private reading with Lévi could have involved Pāli, Sanskrit Buddhist texts, translations, or mixed materials; the exact language/texts must be recovered from the 1898 letters or other notebooks. The safest current formulation is:

> Lovejoy had confirmed formal Sanskrit/Buddhist-text training at Harvard and sought further Pāli-Buddhist study in Paris; when the intended course was unavailable, he occasionally read sacred texts privately with Sylvain Lévi.

## Washington University syllabus search — current negative result

A fresh web sweep still did not locate an independent scan of the 1901 `Syllabus: The Philosophy of Buddhism`. Wilson remains the direct bibliographic source. However, the institutional publication context is now independently confirmed:

- the Washington University Association existed and issued its own publications;
- its *Bulletin* is preserved at HathiTrust from 1903 onward;
- Washington University yearbooks document the Association and, by 1905, list **Arthur O. Lovejoy as managing editor** of the *Bulletin of the Washington University Association*.

This does not prove the 1901 syllabus survives in the same serial, but it identifies the correct institutional publication ecosystem and a likely WashU archives target.

## American Oriental Society search — current negative result

Targeted web searching still confirms only the 1898 JAOS article itself. No reliable web hit yet establishes Lovejoy's formal AOS membership, attendance at an annual meeting, or oral presentation. Keep publication in JAOS separate from institutional membership.
