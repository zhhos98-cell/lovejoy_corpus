## 0. Anti-duplication result

Repository search for the specific conjunction `human sacrifice ↔ animal sacrifice ↔ cultural advancement / evolutionary index / political status / rain-maker punishment` found no dedicated research note beyond the corrected notebook transcription.

Earlier work already establishes the general Marillier/005 habit of `same outward form, different mechanism`, and Batch219 develops suicide as a dynamic case-reallocation sequence. This batch isolates a different problem:

> **can the conspicuous material severity of a rite itself be used as an index of religious/civilizational stage?**

The notebook answer is repeatedly no.

## 1. p.67: magnitude can be political/status amplification rather than a new religious type

In the southern-African comparison, Lovejoy considers ordinary funerary killing of a wife/slave alongside extravagant human sacrifice or massacre associated with some rulers/polities.

The corrected transcription records the key analytical move:

> the difference may be **political/status** rather than a fundamentally different religious motive; the king has greater power/resources.

This blocks a common visual inference:

`more victims / more spectacular violence`
`≠ necessarily more primitive religious mechanism`.

The observed magnitude can be generated by the social capacity of the actor who performs the rite.

Evidence grade: **MEDIUM / notebook transcription**, because several ethnographic names are uncertain but the analytic contrast is legible.

## 2. pp.68–69: human → animal is not a secure advancement scale

p.68 asks whether apparent differences represent developmental stages or merely different social settings.

p.69 is stronger. The corrected transcription says Lovejoy cautions against ranking cultures by a simple scale and explicitly refuses to treat animal sacrifice as automatic evidence of advancement over human sacrifice. A human victim may belong to an older funerary logic of extreme provision/respect for a particular dead person; changing the victim species does not by itself identify the mechanism or stage.

This is the decisive negative control:

> **ritual morphology (`human` / `animal`) is not isomorphic with developmental position (`lower` / `higher`).**

The relation must be reconstructed through function, social setting, history and interpretation.

Evidence grade: **MEDIUM**, with the core analytical sentence preserved more securely than its surrounding citations.

## 3. pp.70–71: even ritualized killing is not enough to establish sacrifice

p.70 separates the killing of a rain-maker/diviner for failure to perform his function from genuine sacrifice for rain. The visible sequence can look sacrificial — specialist, ritualized death, rain context — but the operative relation is punitive/accountability rather than sacrificial efficacy.

p.71 similarly separates ritual killing, cannibalism, medicine and spirit-appropriation. Eating human flesh or handling a body in a ritualized way does not itself prove `sacrifice`.

Thus the notebook tests classifications by **operative relation**, not by the most spectacular visible act.

Comparison with Batch219:

- Batch219: same apparent post-mortem punishment → selector reallocation;
- Batch224: same/similar violent morphology → category refusal until motive/mechanism is established.

The shared rule is narrower than a master algorithm:

> **visible outcome/form does not carry its own historical or functional classification.**

## 4. pp.72–76: function can remain stable while form varies, and form can remain stable while motive varies

p.72 reads funerary human killing as social/functional continuity: attendants accompany and serve the dead. It need not imply communion with a god.

p.73 supplies a temporal reinterpretation case: burning/destruction of property can begin as prevention of the dead's return and later acquire a rationale in which destruction releases/transfers the object's spirit to the dead.

p.74 separates funerary loyalty from propitiation and serious offence.

p.76 then states the broader methodological problem directly in the corrected transcription: one outward practice may be interpreted as provision for the dead, protective/magical action, or later ritual reinterpretation; **the same visible rite need not have one motive**. Human sacrifice may even serve as a utilitarian messenger mechanism rather than worship.

Together these pages give two non-equivalences:

1. `different forms ≠ different developmental stages`;
2. `same form ≠ same motive/mechanism`.

## 5. Consequence for `primitive = stratification`

This batch adds an important negative control to the project's stratification thesis.

`Primitive` language in 005 coexists with ranked `higher stage` language, but the notebook does not permit every observable hierarchy to collapse into that developmental axis. At least four coordinates can diverge:

- **material morphology** — human/animal victim; number of victims; spectacularity;
- **social capacity/status** — ordinary household versus ruler/king;
- **operative religious mechanism** — funerary provision, propitiation, magical efficacy, communion, punishment, messaging;
- **historical/developmental interpretation** — older/later, primitive/higher, inherited/reinterpreted.

Therefore a strongly worded but safe conclusion is:

> **005 itself contains internal controls against reading ritual severity as a transparent civilization scale. Social hierarchy, ritual morphology, mechanism and developmental time are separable predicates.**

This does not erase Lovejoy's own civilizational hierarchy language elsewhere. It shows that hierarchy is multi-axial even within a notebook that still uses `higher stage` and `more developed peoples`.
