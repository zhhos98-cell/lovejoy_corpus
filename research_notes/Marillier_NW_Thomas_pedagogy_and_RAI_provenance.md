# N. W. Thomas as Marillier student witness and possible RAI provenance conduit

## Status

This note records two related findings concerning **Northcote Whitridge Thomas (1868–1936)**:

1. Thomas provides a **contemporary eyewitness description of Léon Marillier's EPHE seminar pedagogy** in *Folk-Lore* 7 (1896), pp. 200–201.
2. Thomas was appointed **Assistant Secretary and Librarian of the Anthropological Institute on 23 October 1900**, creating a plausible human/institutional route by which Marillier working papers could later have entered the RAI, where they are now catalogued as **MS 99**.

The first point is secure. The second is currently a **provenance hypothesis**, not a demonstrated acquisition history. No donation/accession record linking Thomas to MS 99 has yet been found.

---

## 1. Thomas's 1896 eyewitness account of Marillier's seminar

Primary source:

N. W. Thomas, “The École Pratique des hautes Études,” *Folk-Lore* 7 (1896), 200–201.

Wikisource provides the public-domain scan; printed p. 200 is scan/DjVu page 226.

Thomas explains that the relevant EPHE section offered about twenty-five history-of-religions courses and identifies Marillier's conference on the religions of peoples then described as “savage nations.” The subjects currently under study were **“Legends of a Deluge”** and **“The Rites of Marriage.”** This fits the 1895–1897 sequence independently documented in Marillier's EPHE annual reports and the corresponding folders in RAI MS 99 `/5–8`.

More importantly, Thomas gives a direct description of the teaching method. He characterizes the EPHE principle as:

> “work by the pupils, directed by the lecturer.”

He then describes the Marillier conference more specifically: pupils could be asked to write an essay on a defined point; the essay would be **read at the lecture, commented on by Marillier, and discussed by the other pupils**. Thomas explicitly says this judgment comes from personal experience.

This is major evidence for interpreting Lovejoy notebook 005. It establishes that Marillier's course was not simply lecture dictation. The pedagogical unit could be:

1. a defined comparative-religion problem;
2. a student's independent research/essay;
3. oral presentation in the seminar;
4. Marillier's commentary;
5. collective discussion and revision.

Therefore the mixture in 005 of bibliographic lists, analytical classifications, case-testing, dated course headings, inserted slips and apparent independent extensions is exactly compatible with the seminar practice described by another Marillier student.

It also means that manuscript correspondence between 005 and Marillier's own RAI MS 99 must not automatically be classified as copying. Shared source order or argument may reflect assignments, teacher-supplied dossiers, oral commentary, or student reports circulating through the conference.

## 2. Thomas's own Marillier trajectory

Modern reconstruction by the [Re:]Entanglements project, based on Thomas's biography and publications, states that Thomas went to the EPHE in Paris by 1894 specifically to study comparative/“primitive” religion under Marillier. His research concerned survivals of animal cult/totemism in Welsh folklore.

In 1897 he received an EPHE diploma for a thesis on the survival of animal cult in Wales. The research was published in 1898 as:

N. W. Thomas, “La survivance du culte totémique des animaux et les rites agraires dans le Pays de Galles,” *Revue de l'histoire des religions* 38 (1898), 295–347.

This identity is supported by Thomas's own publication record and modern Thomas scholarship. A later EPHE retrospective table prints the diploma holder as “Albert Thomas”; because the exact thesis title was subsequently published by **N. W. Thomas**, the retrospective expansion appears suspect and should not be used uncritically. Contemporary EPHE evidence should be checked before treating this bibliographic discrepancy as solved.

Thomas is thus an important earlier control witness for the same Marillier research environment in which Lovejoy later worked:

- comparative survivance;
- totemism and animal cult;
- agrarian rites;
- student-led research and discussion.

## 3. Thomas enters the Anthropological Institute just before Marillier's death

The RAI's institutional history database records:

> **23 October 1900** — the appointment of **N. W. Thomas, M.A., Trinity College Cambridge, as Assistant Secretary and Librarian** was confirmed.

The RAI history entry also records him as Assistant Secretary in the 1900 and 1901 Councils.

Marillier died on **15 October 1901**. Thomas was therefore working inside the Anthropological Institute's library/secretarial administration during the year immediately before Marillier's death and during the period in which any posthumous transfer of Marillier material might plausibly have been arranged.

This chronological conjunction matters because RAI MS 99 is not an arbitrary later compilation: its thematic folders reproduce Marillier's EPHE working programme with exceptional fidelity.

### Current provenance hypothesis

> Marillier seminar network → N. W. Thomas (former student; AI Assistant Secretary/Librarian from Oct. 1900) → possible facilitation/receipt/arrangement of Marillier papers → RAI MS 99.

**No direct evidence currently proves this route.** The hypothesis should be tested against RAI institutional records, especially the 1900–1903 Library/Executive/Council files and general correspondence.

## 4. Exact RAI record targets for the provenance test

The RAI catalogue now lets us narrow the institutional search considerably.

### A10 — Council Minutes

`A10:3` begins **15 May 1900** and therefore covers the Thomas appointment, Marillier's death, and the likely acquisition window.

Public catalogue summaries do not mention Marillier. This is not a negative result because the page lists only selected inserts/content, not full minute text.

### A17 / A11 — Library and Executive Committee

RAI catalogue states:

- A17 Library Committee: 24 Mar. 1898–30 Jan. 1900 recorded in Council minutes;
- from **17 Apr. 1900–13 May 1930, “Library Business” was reported in Executive Committee minutes**, A11:1–2.

Therefore the critical 1900–1903 provenance search belongs at least as much in **A11 Executive Committee** as A17.

### A18 — General Correspondence

The collection-level catalogue identifies `A18 RAI General Correspondence (office day-to-day), 1871–1968`. This is an obvious target for incoming letters about Marillier's papers, a widow/executor/family, French colleagues, or N. W. Thomas.

### A72 — Library Accessions

`A72:1` covers **13 Jan. 1891–19 Apr. 1912** and records accession number, author, title, size, volumes/pages, **name of donor**, publisher, publication date, date received, and shelf-mark.

A72 is formally a register of **books and pamphlets**, so it may not directly record MS 99. It is nevertheless worth checking for Marillier offprints/books arriving with a larger transfer and for a donor name that could identify the same provenance event.

### A19 — Daily attendance of members/visitors

`A19` covers 1 Nov. 1881–20 Oct. 1908. This may help establish Thomas's day-to-day institutional presence and identify French visitors/donors around any transfer, though it is secondary to A10/A11/A18.

## 5. A further methodological implication for Lovejoy 005

Thomas's 1896 account changes how we should model notebook production.

Earlier reconstruction used a simple chain:

`Marillier research programme → Lovejoy notebook → Lovejoy abstraction`.

The seminar was evidently more interactive. A more realistic model is:

> **Marillier working dossiers / assigned problem**  
> ↕  
> **student essays and source research**  
> ↕  
> **seminar presentation + Marillier commentary + peer discussion**  
> ↕  
> **revised student notebook / inserted slips / later independent elaboration**

This provides a concrete explanation for 005's material heterogeneity. It also creates a strong reason to seek **other student notebooks**, because the same seminar problem could have generated multiple parallel dossiers with overlapping examples but divergent analytical additions.

The highest-value comparative student witnesses remain:

- N. W. Thomas — earlier Marillier student, now additionally a possible provenance conduit to RAI;
- Nathan Söderblom — regular 1898–99 student, Uppsala diary/notebook series overlaps Lovejoy's year;
- Arnold Van Gennep — later/adjacent EPHE and Marillier network, MuCEM papers;
- Travers, Schaefer, W. S. Andrews and Lorette — identities still to be resolved from EPHE registration/attendance records.
