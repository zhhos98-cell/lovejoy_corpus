# Lovejoy / Monboddo 1904 → 1933 — same evidence, new target proposition, new evidentiary force

Date: 2026-09-03  
Status: **DIRECT 1904 PRIMARY + DIRECT ENGLISH 1933 OPEN-ACCESS REPRINT PRIMARY / SAME-SOURCE REINTERPRETATION CLOSED**

## Core result

The 1904 and 1933 Monboddo essays supply an unusually clean control for a central project rule:

> **the evidentiary force of a datum is indexed to the proposition it is asked to support.**

The historical actor and much of the source base remain the same. What changes is the target proposition.

This yields a stronger relation than generic `Lovejoy became more precise`:

`SAME SOURCE / SAME DATUM`
+
`NEW TARGET PROPOSITION`
→
`NEW RELEVANCE / NEW INFERENTIAL FORCE`.

Do not call this a stable named Lovejoy method. It is a direct same-author textual control.

## II. 1904 — the 1773 Knight letter is suggestive but relation underdetermined

Lovejoy quotes Monboddo's 1773 letter saying that the simian race is `of kin to us`, though less nearly related than the orang-outang; baboons stand to us roughly as ass to horse or goldfinch to canary.

Lovejoy's 1904 judgment is cautious:

- Monboddo does not say what relation he means;
- Lovejoy says it `may fairly be supposed` that he was thinking of collateral descent from a common ancestral species;
- the letter does not yet carry a general transformism conclusion in the 1904 argument.

At the same time Lovejoy says Monboddo nowhere directly discusses the general biological transformation of species and that calling him, with Knight, a `virtual evolutionist` is perhaps too much.

Thus in 1904:

`KINSHIP LANGUAGE`
→ possible `COMMON ANCESTRAL SPECIES`

but not yet securely

`GENERAL SPECIES TRANSFORMISM`.

---

## III. 1933 — the same 1773 letter is assigned a stronger proposition

In 1933 Lovejoy explicitly reopens the same biological question and cites his 1904 paper in the note attached to it.

He again begins from the apparent negative evidence in the published treatise: Monboddo's insistence that man and orang-outang are one species can imply that different species do not descend from one another.

He then returns to the same 1773 Knight letter.

This time Lovejoy's inference is stronger:

- the letter `apparently can only mean` that apes, monkeys and man descended from common ancestors;
- because Monboddo did not classify all of them as one species, the relation implies that descent of one species from another is possible;
- Lovejoy concludes that Monboddo accepted in principle the general possibility of transformation of species and asserted as a probable hypothesis common descent of most or all Anthropoidea.

The same source has therefore moved from:

`RELATION NOT FULLY SPECIFIED / COLLATERAL DESCENT MAY BE SUPPOSED`

in 1904

to

`COMMON DESCENT + SPECIES-TRANSFORMATION POSSIBILITY`

in 1933.

This is not merely lexical strengthening. The target proposition has changed and the source is made to carry a broader relation.

## IV. The tail material is the cleanest relation-indexed relevance control

### 1904 target

The live proposition is chiefly:

`MAN ↔ ORANG-OUTANG RELATION / SAME SPECIES / DESCENT`.

Lovejoy says Monboddo's stories of tailed men are **really irrelevant** to this main thesis, because the anthropoids with which Monboddo identifies man are themselves tailless.

He notes that Monboddo presents the material as a digression.

His joke is methodologically exact: the discussion `suggests a good deal`, but is not designed to bear any of the `weight of proof` for the principal argument.

Thus:

`TAIL STORIES`
→ suggestive / curiosity

but

`TAIL STORIES`
↛ proof of `MAN = ORANG-OUTANG / DIRECT KINSHIP`.

### 1933 target

Lovejoy still says the tail stories are **without pertinency** to the argument for our kinship with the orang-outang.

But he now assigns them to a different proposition:

`MAN + ORANG-OUTANG`
→ possible descent from `REMOTE TAILED ANCESTORS`.

He then uses Monboddo's footnote about variation in our species and the `simian tribe` to help infer a broader common-descent hypothesis.

So the same evidence remains irrelevant to proposition P1 while becoming relevant to proposition P2:

`P1 = DIRECT MAN–ORANG SPECIES/KINSHIP CLAIM`

`TAIL EVIDENCE = IRRELEVANT / NON-BEARING`

but

`P2 = REMOTE COMMON ANCESTRY / BROADER ANTHROPOID GENEALOGY`

`TAIL EVIDENCE = POTENTIALLY RELEVANT`.

This is one of the strongest direct controls in the corpus for:

> **relevance is proposition-relative, not an intrinsic property of a datum.**

## V. 1933 also changes the classification scale of `evolutionist`

After the biological audit, Lovejoy calls Monboddo an evolutionist in a `profounder sense` tied to:

- human capacities as acquired/developed rather than primitively actual;
- movement from animality toward rational/social life;
- historically changeable human nature;
- successive stages of intellectual/social development;
- an evolutionary universal history.

Thus the label is repartitioned:

`BIOLOGICAL TRANSFORMISM`
!=
`COMMON ANTHROPOID DESCENT`
!=
`GENETIC HABIT OF MIND`
!=
`SOCIAL / INTELLECTUAL EVOLUTION`
!=
`EVOLUTIONARY UNIVERSAL HISTORY`.

A label can be qualified at one proposition level and affirmed at another.

## VI. Relation to Rousseau genealogy

The same 1933 paper gives an independent relation-indexed control.

Lovejoy establishes:

- strong content similarity;
- Rousseau's temporal priority;
- Monboddo's documented reading and admiration of Rousseau;
- highly similar passages.

Yet he says the direct-influence question `cannot be answered with certainty` because alternative routes remain live:

- spontaneous recurrence;
- common classical/Epicurean sources;
- common comparative-anatomical evidence;
- common travel/ethnographic reports.

Therefore:

`SIMILARITY + PRIORITY + READING`
!=
`CLOSED DIRECT GENEALOGY`.

This is the genetic counterpart to the tail-evidence result.

---

## VII. Longitudinal significance

The 1904→1933 relation should not be summarized as `increasing caution`.

In one respect Lovejoy becomes **more assertive** in 1933: he upgrades the Knight letter and tail-related packet into support for a broader common-descent/species-transformation hypothesis.

The stable operation is instead:

`IDENTIFY TARGET CLAIM`
→ `ASK WHAT RELATION THE SOURCE ACTUALLY BEARS`
→ `REASSIGN / WITHHOLD EVIDENTIARY FORCE BY CLAIM TYPE`.

This matters for the notebook project because it supplies a mature, direct, same-source analogue of the archival rule:

> **one piece of evidence can be weak or irrelevant for one relation and important for another.**

Do not retroject the 1933 interpretation into 1898.
