# Batch 79 — 005 → 1906 bibliography is reorganized by proof function: source persistence, role migration, replacement, and deliberate deferral

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: source-level comparison between notebook 005 and Lovejoy's 1906 `Fundamental Concept`, building on the source-function distinctions of Batches 77–78. The question is no longer merely which authors occur in both objects, but **what proposition each source is made to prove at each stage**.

## Core result

The 005→1906 transition is not well described as `Lovejoy kept using the same ethnographic bibliography`. Nor is it a simple replacement of old books by newer fieldwork.

The source apparatus is **reselected and reassigned according to the proof architecture of the published argument**.

Four different source fates are visible:

1. **persistence with function migration** — Codrington remains, but moves from one source inside the broad comparative-religion dossier to a central linguistic/semantic witness for `mana`;
2. **persistence with role reversal** — Brinton remains in the intellectual field, but shifts from source/corrective authority in the notebook's American material to an opening theoretical target whose anthropomorphic master category Lovejoy rejects;
3. **persistence as theoretical foil** — Frazer remains important across both objects, but increasingly as the theory whose classifications and causal partitions Lovejoy tests rather than as a privileged empirical source;
4. **deferral or replacement by evidence better fitted to the current proposition** — Callaway, Kingsley, Trumbull, MacDonald and other custom-rich sources dominate notebook analysis but largely drop out of the 1906 first-stage lexical proof; where the African question becomes semantic/grammatical, Hetherwick replaces MacDonald as the high-resolution source.

The resulting principle is:

> **source persistence depends less on prestige than on evidentiary job.**

The bibliography is itself transformed from a custom/rite apparatus into a lexical-semantic proof apparatus.

This gives a more concrete 005→1906 genealogy than conceptual resemblance alone:

> **the research program changes not only what Lovejoy concludes, but which sources are allowed to carry which propositions.**

---

## 1. Codrington: same book, different evidentiary job

### 005

Codrington's *The Melanesians* is physically present in the opening bibliography and appears in early comparative material. In the Marillier-centered source ecology reconstructed in Batch 38, Codrington belongs to a broad packet covering death, Melanesian taboo, myth, religion and later sacrifice/mana problems.

Within 005, however, Codrington is not yet isolated as the exclusive linguistic foundation for a universal causal category. He is one comparative authority among many.

### 1906

The book's function changes sharply. Lovejoy organizes the first stage of the paper around evidence that native vocabularies contain a concept of transferable, distributed efficacy. Codrington pp.118–119 now become a principal Oceanic semantic witness for `mana`.

The source therefore migrates:

`ethnographic/comparative authority`

→

`lexical-semantic existence control for the abstract category`.

Even then Lovejoy does not inherit Codrington whole: he foregrounds transferability/efficacy and later rejects `supernormal` as semantic essence, while the personal-origin clause remains adverse evidence.

Thus this is not only **source persistence** but **function migration plus statement-level reweighting**.

---

## 2. Brinton: from weighted comparative authority to theoretical target

### 005

Daniel G. Brinton is directly present in notebook 005. A bibliographic slip gives *The American Race*, and the broader Brinton source world is relevant to the notebook's American comparative material. Batch 69 established that Brinton's own comparative method explicitly discussed careful selection of authorities and relative evidentiary weight.

In the notebook field, Brinton therefore belongs on the **evidence/corrective-authority side** of the ledger.

### 1906

Lovejoy opens the published paper by naming Brinton among theorists whose anthropomorphic/personal interpretation of early religion captures real features but mistakes them for the most fundamental category.

Brinton's function has changed:

`authority inside comparative American source field`

→

`representative of the theoretical classification to be superseded`.

This is a source-role reversal. The transition matters because it shows Lovejoy treating an established source not as permanently authoritative but as a historical proposition-bearer whose theoretical function can be separated from his empirical value.

A useful formulation is:

> **the same scholar can survive in the bibliography while moving from evidence provider to object of criticism.**

---

## 3. Frazer: persistent foil rather than stable master source

Frazer is one of the most continuous named figures across 005 and 1906, but his function is also revealing.

### 005

Frazerian problems recur in sacrifice, substitution, ritual morphology, agriculture, sympathetic transfer and developmental sequences. Lovejoy repeatedly asks whether broad formal classes actually share mechanism or history.

### 1906

Frazer is explicitly criticized for drawing too sharp a line between magic and religion and for overworking sympathetic magic while missing the cross-cutting idea of diffusive/transferable efficacy.

Thus the continuity is not:

`Frazer supplies the theory Lovejoy carries forward`.

It is closer to:

`Frazer supplies recurrent explanatory shortcuts against which Lovejoy refines mechanism and category boundaries`.

Frazer persists because he remains a **high-value theoretical foil** across changing evidentiary domains.

---

## 4. Trumbull, Kingsley, and Callaway: strong 005 sources that disappear when the proposition changes

Notebook 005 relies heavily on sources that are excellent for **ritual application** but weakly fitted to the narrow first-stage proof of 1906.

### Trumbull

*The Blood Covenant* and *The Threshold Covenant* sit inside the blood/foundation/kinship problem field. Lovejoy uses them to classify what blood or bodily material is doing: fraternity, incorporation, protection, boundary efficacy, foundation magic, or something else.

### Mary Kingsley

Kingsley supplies West-African material on blood, fetish, houses, idols and protective efficacy. She is especially useful for the question whether a physical substance/object works through an indwelling spirit or an intrinsic efficacy.

### Callaway

*The Religious System of the Amazulu* provides Zulu/ancestor material for sacrificial classification: provision, propitiation, ancestor transaction, food, social continuity and other local mechanisms.

These sources are central to the **customs-and-rites** side of the research program.

But the 1906 paper explicitly says it is presenting only the first stage: the linguistic/conceptual existence of the proposed causal category. In that proof design, custom-rich authors need not appear prominently even when their material remains relevant to the announced sequel.

Their absence/drop in the first chapter should therefore not be read as abandonment.

It is better read as **scope-driven deferral**:

`source proves applications of mechanism`

≠

`source proves existence/meaning of mechanism`.

The publication bibliography contracts because the current proposition is narrower.

---

## 5. Duff MacDonald → Hetherwick: replacement driven by evidence type

The African packet supplies the clearest case of source replacement by proof function.

### 005: MacDonald as custom/divinity evidence

At PDF p.96 Lovejoy cites Duff MacDonald while testing a theory that high/supreme gods correlate with political organization/chieftainship. MacDonald's cases matter because the relevant African deity may be neither celestial nor supreme; the source therefore functions as **counterexample-bearing ethnographic evidence** against a developmental hierarchy.

Elsewhere in the African/sacrifice dossier, similar missionary/ethnographic authorities supply descriptions of offering, ancestor relations, blood use and divine classification.

### 1906: Hetherwick as grammatical ontology evidence

The published first chapter needs a different proposition: does Bantu/Yao vocabulary itself support a non-personal property/efficacy category?

For this question MacDonald's descriptive customs are less diagnostic than Hetherwick's Yao grammar. Hetherwick can show that `mulungu` does not belong to the personal noun class and instead morphologically denotes an inhering property/quality.

Thus:

`MacDonald: ethnographic counterexample / custom classification`

→ not discarded as false, but insufficiently targeted for the new claim;

`Hetherwick: grammatical morphology / native ontology`

→ selected because it directly bears the stronger lexical-semantic proposition.

This is the clearest current example of **bibliographic reselection by inferential task**.

---

## 6. North-American material changes from belief reports to linguistic controls

The same selection shift is visible geographically.

### 005

North-American sources such as Keating, Murray, Brinton and others enter primarily as reports of beliefs and practices. Lovejoy asks about missionary contamination, moralization, manner of death, social taboo, eschatological classification, and borrowing.

### 1906

The North-American packet is rebuilt around McGee, Fletcher, Jones, Dorsey, old missionary lexical attestations and native linguistic explanations.

The evidentiary unit changes from:

`what belief/practice was reported?`

to

`what native term was used, in what grammatical form, and what mechanism did speakers themselves assign to it?`

This is more than a change of bibliography. It is a change in the **preferred carrier of causal evidence**.

The 1906 paper therefore does not merely abstract a category from the old notebook examples. It **constructs a new evidence channel** designed to establish the category independently of those examples.

That is precisely why Batch 76's `vera causa` passage matters.

## 7. Source persistence and source disappearance are both positive evidence

A conventional source study often treats only repeated citations as meaningful. Here, disappearance is equally informative.

### Persistence can mean

- the source is uniquely fitted to a new proof task (Codrington);
- the source remains a useful theory to criticize (Frazer, Brinton);
- the source supplies a shared conceptual vocabulary across stages.

### Disappearance can mean

- the source belongs to a deferred evidentiary stage (Trumbull, Kingsley, Callaway);
- the source is less diagnostic for the new proposition (MacDonald relative to Hetherwick);
- the publication deliberately narrows from customs to language.

Therefore citation survival should not be equated with intellectual importance.

The stronger rule is:

> **track source function, not citation frequency.**

---

## 8. The 005→1906 transition can now be rendered as a source-function transformation

### 005 research apparatus

Dominant source jobs:

- ethnographic witness;
- rite/custom description;
- local theological interpretation;
- contact/provenance information;
- counterexample to developmental theory;
- comparative application of sacrifice/blood/fetish/cannibalism categories.

### 1906 first-stage apparatus

Dominant source jobs:

- lexical occurrence;
- grammatical morphology;
- native semantic distinction;
- native causal explanation;
- independent evidence that the category exists;
- theoretical foil for competing master classifications.

### Published selection rule

A source survives into the 1906 first chapter when it can do one of two jobs especially well:

1. establish the lexical/semantic `vera causa`;
2. define the theory Lovejoy is trying to displace.

Custom-rich sources are mostly postponed because their principal evidentiary job belongs to the announced later analysis of applications.

This makes the bibliography itself an index of argument structure.

---

## 9. Relation to Marillier becomes more precise

This source-function transformation also improves the Paris argument.

Marillier's documented pedagogy differentiated testimony/document classes and applied different critical rules to them. Notebook 005 shows Lovejoy working on source provenance and comparative custom classification inside a Marillier-linked research environment.

By 1906, the proof architecture has become more specialized:

- ethnographic/custom sources remain relevant to application;
- linguistic sources are selected to establish conceptual existence;
- different evidentiary channels are made reciprocally corroborative rather than interchangeable.

Thus Paris need not be credited with a later linguistic method. The historically stronger claim is that Lovejoy's research practice increasingly **assigns different source classes different argumentative jobs**.

That is exactly the kind of continuity that can be demonstrated without inventing a direct methodological lesson.

---

## 10. Paper-level formulation

A defensible formulation now available is:

> **The source continuity between Lovejoy's Paris notebook and `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` is selective rather than cumulative. Codrington survives because a book that had served as one ethnographic authority in 005 could be reassigned as lexical-semantic evidence for `mana`; Brinton and Frazer survive increasingly as theoretical positions to be criticized. By contrast, Trumbull, Kingsley, Callaway and much of the African sacrifice bibliography recede from the published first chapter because they primarily document ritual applications, which Lovejoy explicitly postpones. In the Yao case, Duff MacDonald's descriptive ethnography is effectively superseded for the new proof task by Hetherwick's grammatical evidence. The change from notebook to publication is therefore visible in the bibliography itself: sources are retained, dropped, or replaced according to the proposition they are competent to establish.**

Compact formulation:

> **The bibliography migrates by function, not by prestige.**

---

## 11. Evidence cautions

1. `Drop/disappearance` is based on the directly recovered 1906 first chapter and prior full authority audit; it should not be converted into a claim that Lovejoy stopped reading or valuing the omitted source.
2. Codrington is directly present in both 005 and 1906, but a direct causal route `005 citation → 1906 citation` cannot be proved from repetition alone.
3. Brinton's exact notebook work and exact 1906 theoretical text are not the same bibliographic object; the claim concerns change in Brinton's role inside Lovejoy's source field, not reuse of one identical passage.
4. MacDonald→Hetherwick is a functional replacement in the African proof architecture, not evidence that Lovejoy consciously rejected MacDonald as unreliable.
5. Callaway/Kingsley/Trumbull remain highly relevant to the customs-stage continuation announced by 1906; omission from the first chapter is therefore positive scope evidence, not negative intellectual evidence.
6. `source persistence`, `function migration`, and `bibliographic reselection` are our analytical labels.

## 12. Highest next move

The next source-level test should go one step below authors and compare **individual propositions** that persist from 005 into 1906:

- body part → transferable quality (`005` selected organs ↔ `1906` Jones enemy-heart/manitou);
- blood → fertility/productive efficacy (`005` agricultural blood ↔ `1906` North-American reproductive/agricultural energy);
- fetish vs amulet (`005` spirit-inhabitation vs intrinsic efficacy ↔ `1906` `oki` object / amulet discussion);
- quasi-mechanical agrarian action (`005` p.105 ↔ `1906` p.377 agricultural transfer);
- reporter misclassification (`005` missionary contamination ↔ `1906` Dorsey/M.V.P./missionary gloss corrections).

For each proposition, code:

`005 source + local mechanism`

→ `1906 source + independent lexical control`

→ `what changed in warrant`.

That would demonstrate the transition as **reproof** rather than mere republication: old mechanisms are carried into print only after being given a new evidentiary basis.
