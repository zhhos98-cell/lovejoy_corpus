# CSPT -> *Political Theory*, 1967–1976: institutionalizing the Pocock/Skinner method ecology and the JHI round trip

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **CSPT FOUNDATION DIRECT INSTITUTIONAL CONTROL / POLITICAL THEORY ISSUE ARCHITECTURE DIRECT / 1973 EDITORIAL MISSION SECONDARY-DIRECT-QUOTE CONTROL**

## Core result

The Pocock/Skinner methodological programme did not merely disperse through individual articles. Between 1967 and the mid-1970s it acquired new institutional carriers that linked intellectual history, political theory, philosophy and disciplinary critique.

The sequence is now directly reconstructable:

`1967 — CONFERENCE FOR THE STUDY OF POLITICAL THOUGHT (CSPT)`
-> founded in Toronto by **J. G. A. Pocock, Melvin Richter and Neal Wood**
-> explicitly interdisciplinary / humanistic political-thought network

`1970 — POLITICAL THEORY JOURNAL PROJECT`
-> emerges from a graduate-student meeting of the Columbia University chapter of CSPT
-> founders describe the journal as an attempt to `build bridges`, stimulate controversy, clarify issues and create new perspectives

`FEB 1973 — POLITICAL THEORY 1.1`
-> editorial statement
-> Sartori, `What is Politics?`
-> **Pocock, `Verbalizing a Political Act: Toward a Politics of Speech`, pp.27–45**
-> facts/values and political-discourse comment/rejoinder cluster

`APR–JUN 1973 — JHI 34.2`
-> Parekh/Berki, `The History of Political Ideas: A Critique of Q. Skinner's Methodology`

`AUG 1973 — POLITICAL THEORY 1.3`
-> Skinner, `The Empirical Theorists of Democracy and Their Critics: A Plague on Both Their Houses`, pp.287–306

`AUG 1974 — POLITICAL THEORY 2.3`
-> dedicated opening symposium `Political Thought and Political Action: A Symposium on Quentin Skinner`
-> Jonathan M. Wiener, `Quentin Skinner's Hobbes`, pp.251–260
-> Gordon J. Schochet, `Quentin Skinner's Method`, pp.261–276
-> Skinner, `Some Problems in the Analysis of Political Thought and Action`, pp.277–303

`1976 — POLITICAL THEORY 4.2`
-> Berki/Parekh communication replying to Skinner's 1974 article.

Thus the methodological controversy migrated among journals but was simultaneously being **institutionalized inside a new political-theory venue descended from a network co-founded by Pocock and Melvin Richter**.

The result is stronger than a citation network:

`PROGRAMME / DISCUSSION`
-> `SOCIETY / CONFERENCE INFRASTRUCTURE`
-> `JOURNAL INFRASTRUCTURE`
-> `ARTICLE / SYMPOSIUM / REPLY ARCHITECTURE`.

## I. CSPT is a direct interdisciplinary bridge institution

The current International Conference for the Study of Political Thought identifies its origins directly:

- founded in Toronto in 1967;
- founders: J. G. A. Pocock, Melvin Richter, Neal Wood;
- mission: broad, humanistic thinking about politics;
- draws together intellectual history, political/social theory, philosophy and related fields;
- develops regional chapters and international networks.

The older Columbia description likewise presents CSPT as a world-wide interdisciplinary network bringing together political theorists, historians, philosophers, classicists, language scholars, sociologists and economists.

This matters for the Lovejoy/JHI project because **Melvin Richter** is simultaneously:

- a co-founder of CSPT;
- later a major mediator between JHI/history of ideas and *Begriffsgeschichte*;
- author of the 1987 JHI comparison that explicitly separates founders' programmes from actual performances.

Therefore the JHI/history-of-ideas ecology and the emerging political-thought contextualist ecology share institutional personnel before later historiography hardens them into separate method traditions.

## II. *Political Theory* grew from the CSPT network

Recent disciplinary history reconstructs the journal project as originating around 1970 at a graduate-student meeting of the Columbia University chapter of CSPT.

The founding editors, Robert Lamb and Walter Odajnyk, framed the journal as an institution intended to `build bridges` and to stimulate thought, produce controversy, clarify issues, foster understanding and generate new perspectives in political philosophy.

This is a secondary source quoting the 1973 editorial statement; the free original editorial is identified at *Political Theory* 1.1, pp.3–4 but its direct XML/PDF endpoint cache-missed in the current pass.

The safe institutional claim is nonetheless strong:

`CSPT REGIONAL NETWORK`
-> `POLITICAL THEORY JOURNAL PROJECT`.

## IV. `Verbalising` is infrastructurally downstream of Pocock's own field-building work

Pocock had co-founded CSPT in 1967.

Whatmore's correspondence reconstruction shows that in 1968–69 Pocock was:

- circulating scripts to Skinner/Dunn;
- proposing a possible joint manifesto;
- contemplating a methodological book;
- naming a `Kuhn-Skinner-Pocock` approach.

The 1973 `Verbalising` essay then appears in the **first issue of a journal born from CSPT's Columbia chapter**.

This gives an unusually visible programme-to-infrastructure sequence:

`RECIPROCAL METHOD DISCUSSION`
-> `PROGRAMME CONSCIOUSNESS`
-> `SOCIETY / CHAPTER NETWORK`
-> `NEW JOURNAL`
-> `PUBLISHED METHOD ARTICLE`.

Do not infer that CSPT or the journal commissioned `Verbalising` specifically because of Pocock's founding role without editorial correspondence.

The institutional adjacency and sequence are secure; commission causation is open.

## V. Skinner enters the new venue almost immediately

Skinner publishes `The Empirical Theorists of Democracy and Their Critics: A Plague on Both Their Houses` in *Political Theory* 1.3 (August 1973), pp.287–306.

This matters because the new journal is not only a Pocock outlet.

Within its first year it carries Skinner into a dispute over empirical democratic theory and political concepts, extending his speech-act/contextual concerns from history-of-ideas methodology into contemporary political-theory jurisdiction.

Thus:

`HISTORICAL METHOD`
-> `POLITICAL THEORY / DEMOCRACY CONCEPT CRITIQUE`.

The method programme expands through a venue designed to bridge disciplinary jurisdictions.

---

## VI. 1974 converts Skinner's method into a symposium object

Direct SAGE/JSTOR control for *Political Theory* 2.3 (August 1974) gives a three-part opening symposium:

### `Political Thought and Political Action: A Symposium on Quentin Skinner`

1. Jonathan M. Wiener, `Quentin Skinner's Hobbes`, pp.251–260;
2. Gordon J. Schochet, `Quentin Skinner's Method`, pp.261–276;
3. Quentin Skinner, `Some Problems in the Analysis of Political Thought and Action`, pp.277–303.

This is an important canon-formation intermediate state.

By 1974, before Tully 1988 or a later `Cambridge School` canon, Skinner's work is already being packaged by a journal as:

`AN IDENTIFIABLE METHOD / PROBLEM OBJECT`
worthy of
`DEDICATED SYMPOSIUM + AUTHOR RESPONSE`.

Skinner opens his response by thanking Schochet and Wiener and choosing to clarify his general approach rather than simply add more Hobbes evidence.

Thus the venue actively turns research practice into method discourse.

## X. Canon-formation consequence

The current project can now distinguish at least five stages in the making of a canonical method:

1. `RESEARCH PRACTICE`;
2. `RECIPROCAL DISCUSSION / DRAFT CIRCULATION`;
3. `PROGRAMME CONSCIOUSNESS`;
4. `INSTITUTIONAL CARRIER FORMATION` — society, chapter, journal, symposium;
5. `CANONICAL PACKAGING` — anthology, collected works, anniversary volume.

Skinner/Pocock were already inside stages 2–4 before later `Cambridge School` canonization.

The method's later memory therefore cannot be explained by text alone.
