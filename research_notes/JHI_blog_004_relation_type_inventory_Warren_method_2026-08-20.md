## Core result

The recurrence is systematic enough to matter. Notebook 004 repeatedly treats **the type of relation itself** as an object of analysis. At least ten high-value loci distinguish among different kinds of relation or different scales of historical claim.

The strongest formulation is now:

> **Notebook 004 is less a collection of Buddhist concepts than a workshop for specifying what kind of relation holds among them: identity, classificatory inclusion, causal dependence, temporal succession, logical inclusion, textual attestation, historical derivation, compositional relation, or system-level function.**

This is not uniquely Lovejoyian. Henry Clarke Warren's 1893 paper already makes relation-type discrimination explicit: `paccaya` is not one homogeneous sense of cause, Time should play only a secondary role, and each link must be tested for the relation actually involved. Warren then assigns different link-relations as antecedent condition, particular-instance/general-category, identity, cause/effect, material cause and interdependence.

The crucial Lovejoy result is therefore **selective uptake and generalization**, not invention. Lovejoy takes part in this local/Orientalist grammar of relation-discrimination, but he subjects Warren's own local assignments to further tests and carries the operation into historical scale, composition, lexical derivation and system reconstruction.

## 1. Warren 1893 explicitly supplies a grammar of heterogeneous relations

Henry Clarke Warren, `On the so-called Chain of Causation of the Buddhists` (AOS Proceedings / JAOS XVI, 1893), objects to the Western title `Chain of Causation` because it imports an overly exact category. He says:

- `cause` must be used loosely and in different senses for different links;
- `paccaya` expresses dependence but this need not be causal;
- Time is only incidentally / secondarily involved;
- each adjacent pair should be examined separately for the kind of relation involved.

Warren's own inventory includes:

- birth → old age/death: antecedent condition;
- existence → birth: general category / particular instance;
- attachment → desire: identity;
- contact → sensation: cause/effect;
- name-and-form → sense organs: material cause;
- consciousness ↔ name-and-form: interdependence;
- some resultant consciousnesses → saṅkhāras: effect/cause.

This is crucial context for 004. Relation-type discrimination is already a method problem in the Harvard/AOS Buddhist field before Lovejoy's notebook.

Publication-grade quotation should still be image-checked against the printed JAOS/Proceedings pages, but the article text and bibliographic identity are secure; Lovejoy 1898 itself cites Warren, JAOS XVI, p. xxvii.

## 2. Inventory of relation-discrimination across notebook 004

### Locus A — PDF pp.4–5 / MS pp.8–9: diagnostic resemblance → historical attribution

Source: Garbe 1894.

Dense agreement in terminology, definitions and examples can justify recognition of an `alien element` adopted from another logical school.

Relation under test:

`formal similarity` → `historical adoption`.

The notebook therefore opens with a rule about when resemblance becomes genealogically probative.

### Locus B — PDF p.17 / MS p.63: doctrine/feature ≠ whole system

Lovejoy writes that a Brahmajāla passage is not good evidence that the Sāṃkhya `system existed as a whole` just as it appears many centuries later, then adds `To R.D.'s remark it should be added...` and seeks a more diagnostic Sāṃkhyan feature.

Relation under test:

`recognizable doctrine/feature` ≠ `historical existence of complete later system`.

This is scale/unit-of-inference control.

### Locus C — PDF p.20 / MS p.71: understanding = inter-relation, not isolated terms

Lovejoy's programmatic statement says Buddhism's essential doctrine is involved in the twelve notions and their `very complicated inter-relation`, to be approached through `careful analysis`.

Relation under test:

`term meaning` + `relation among terms` → `system reconstruction`.

The explicit object is the interrelation of concepts.

### Locus D — PDF p.22 / MS p.75: Warren identity claim rejected by classification

Warren calls attachment/desire a relation of `identity`. Lovejoy writes:

`But not so if we are to follow F. present text.`

He compares four upādānas with six taṇhās / Buddhaghosa-Vibhaṅga classifications.

Relation under test:

`adjacency / semantic proximity` ≠ `identity`.

The rejection is made through classificatory testing.

### Locus E — PDF p.27 / MS p.85: diagrammatic relation among links 3–8

The page explicitly begins `The relation of F. Nidānas 3–8 inclusive may be represented thus` and maps consciousness, nāmarūpa, sense-organs, contact, sensation and desire.

Relation under test:

`serial adjacency` → decomposed sensory/psychological correspondences.

The diagram itself is a relation-mapping device.

### Locus F — PDF p.33 / MS p.97: causal fact ≠ causal manner

Under `Upādāna, Relation to Karma`, Lovejoy asks, from Hardy p.394, whether upādāna causes the `fact` of continued existence while karma causes its `manner`.

Relation under test:

`same outcome-field (rebirth)` ≠ `same causal function`.

This becomes the 1898 distinction between fact of rebirth and particular condition of rebirth.

### Locus G — PDF p.42 / MS p.123: temporal antecedence ≠ logical inclusion

Lovejoy writes:

`Viññāṇa is temporally (?) an antecedent of nāmarūpa, and logically a subdivision of it.`

Relation under test:

`temporal position` ≠ `logical/classificatory position`.

This is the clearest direct manuscript statement that one concept can stand in different relations to another on different analytical axes.

### Locus H — PDF pp.47–50 / MS pp.133–139: derivation, semantic overlap, temporal/logical order and configuration kept separate

Lovejoy notes that upādāna and upādhi can have different derivational histories yet overlapping senses; then, against the duplication argument, he grants composite provenance and non-homogeneous temporal relation while refusing the conclusion that the arrangement is merely accidental or unintelligible.

PDF p.50 makes the difficulty the relation among terms that precede one another `temporally as well as logically`.

Relations under test:

- `different derivation` vs `overlapping meaning`;
- `historical compositeness` vs `systemic intelligibility`;
- `temporal order` vs `logical order`.

### Locus I — PDF pp.51–52 / MS pp.141–143: historical source ≠ present ontological/systemic position

Lovejoy argues that avidyā and saṅkhāra may have Upaniṣadic / Sāṃkhya-Yoga backgrounds, but a Buddhist reinterpretation must still explain why they occupy their present positions in the nidāna sequence. Affinity does not prove a theory `conceived all of a piece`.

Relation under test:

`historical provenance` ≠ `present doctrinal role / position`.

This is an explicit genealogy/configuration split.

### Locus J — PDF p.56 / MS p.151: different derivation/connotation can converge in system-level function

Lovejoy says upādhi, āsava and saṁyojana have `slightly different connotations and derivations`, but `systematically/in the system` are devised for essentially the same fundamental idea: attachment to objects of physical experience.

Relation under test:

`different lexical histories/connotations` ≠ `different system-level function`.

This is especially important because the notebook's relation-discrimination is **bidirectional**. Lovejoy does not mechanically multiply differences. He can also infer functional equivalence across different derivations where the system-level evidence supports it.

This is a strong control against describing the method as skepticism or anti-genealogy.

### Locus K — PDF pp.63–66 / MS pp.167–173: etymological/material reading ≠ contextual/ethical use

The upādisesa sequence tests whether a residual term refers to material residue / persistence of skandhas or to residual ethical impurity. Contexts such as the poisoned-arrow analogy, anāgāmin and Arahat cases are used to argue for ethical use.

Relation under test:

`word-form / proposed derivation / material gloss` ≠ `meaning in contextual use`.

The 1898 article makes the partition explicit: competing etymologies remain possible; meaning must be settled by use; evidence can be `relevant but not necessarily conclusive`; contradictory passages remain.

### Locus L — PDF p.67 / MS p.175: textual witness ≠ doctrinal reconstruction

The notebook changes register explicitly to `MP. Text-criticism`, comparing Chinese versions and Pāli textual extent.

Relation under test:

`what textual witness survives / what recension carries` ≠ `what doctrine may historically be reconstructed`.

The carrier of evidence becomes an object of analysis in its own right.

### Locus M — PDF p.68 / MS p.177: original elements ≠ derived elements

The notebook closes its historical-structural analysis with:

Original Elements:
1. Flux / Three Characteristics.
2. Formula of Dependent Origination.

Derived Elements:
1. Theory of sense-perception.
2. Psychology.
3. Khandhas.

Relation under test:

`present system membership` ≠ `same historical provenance`.

The final outline explicitly stratifies a configuration into components with different historical statuses.

## 3. Why this is more than Batch73's four proof levels

Batch73 already reconstructed a four-level proof partition:

`textual witness → semantic/classificatory relation → doctrinal/causal function → historical genealogy`.

The present inventory adds a more local and actor-near point: **within those levels, Lovejoy repeatedly specifies different relation-types among the same objects.**

Especially important are:

- identity vs non-identity;
- temporal precedence vs logical inclusion;
- causal fact vs causal manner;
- derivational difference vs systemic equivalence;
- compositional provenance vs configurational intelligibility;
- doctrinal feature vs whole-system existence.

Thus the best analytical language may be `relation-type discrimination` or `relational reconstruction`, while retaining `proof-level separation` for the higher evidentiary architecture.

Do not present either as Lovejoy's named method.

## 4. Warren changes the originality claim

This inventory makes it even less defensible to say that Lovejoy invented a method of relation-discrimination.

Warren 1893 already insists that:

- dependence is not automatically causation;
- one sequence contains multiple relation types;
- temporal relation is not uniformly controlling;
- relation must be specified link by link.

Lovejoy's documentary importance lies elsewhere:

1. he demonstrably reads this field;
2. he rejects Warren's own local `identity` judgment using canonical classification;
3. he applies relation-discrimination beyond the individual nidāna links to historical scale, textual witnesses, lexical derivation, system composition and genealogy;
4. he later publishes some of these distinctions in a more selective form.

The strongest phrasing is therefore:

> **Lovejoy did not invent the distinction between kinds of relation. Notebook 004 shows him learning inside a philological field in which relation-types were already contested, then extending that discipline to the scale and genealogy of philosophical systems.**

This is stronger historically than `Lovejoy anticipated contextualism` and avoids priority mythology.

## 5. Bidirectionality is the key control

The strongest reason to regard the pattern as an inference discipline rather than a generalized skepticism is that it operates in both directions.

### Separation

- desire ≠ attachment;
- karma function ≠ upādāna function;
- temporal antecedent ≠ logical subdivision;
- borrowed/composite ≠ unintelligible;
- early doctrine ≠ whole later system.

### Convergence

- different derivations/connotations (upādhi / āsava / saṁyojana) can still express the same fundamental systemic idea;
- borrowed terms/ideas can still be arranged and applied in an original/characteristic configuration;
- historically heterogeneous elements can form an intelligible technical system.

Therefore the rule is not `always differentiate`.

It is:

> **do not allow one established relation to settle another relation without an additional warrant.**

That is the most economical current statement of the notebook's inferential practice.

## 6. Blog consequence

The Blog should not expose a thirteen-locus inventory. The inventory is internal proof that the selected examples are not cherry-picked.

For prose, the most legible sequence remains:

1. Garbe/Rhys Davids → `system existed as a whole` (scale).
2. Warren → `identity` → `But not so if we follow the text` (relation-type discrimination at term level).
3. Senart → composite provenance vs intelligible configuration (relation-type discrimination at system level).
4. upādisesa → usage vs etymology + explicit uncertainty calibration.

One sentence can then generalize:

> **Across the notebook, Lovejoy repeatedly asks not merely whether two terms or doctrines are related, but what kind of relation has actually been established — lexical identity, logical inclusion, temporal succession, causal dependence, historical derivation, or system-level function.**

This is now supported by repeated manuscript loci rather than by one interpretive flash.

## 7. Relation to later history of ideas

A cautious bridge is now possible:

> **Before Lovejoy became known for tracing ideas across time, his Buddhist notebook had already trained attention on a prior problem: what kind of relation between ideas, terms and systems had the evidence actually established?**

Do not claim that this directly caused the mature unit-idea method.

The more defensible disciplinary point is that a methodological prehistory of the history of ideas can be reconstructed from philological disputes over relation-types, scale and historical warrant in Indian intellectual traditions.

This remains a history of formation/problems, not a simple transmission genealogy.
