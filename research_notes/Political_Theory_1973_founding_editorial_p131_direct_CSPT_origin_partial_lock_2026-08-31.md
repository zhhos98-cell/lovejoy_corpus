## Core result

The inaugural `From the Editors` statement in *Political Theory* 1.2 (May 1973), pp.131–133, can now be partially upgraded from later retrospective control to direct primary evidence.

The **first printed page, p.131, is directly controlled in two independent web carriers**:

1. SAGE first-page image;
2. vLex machine-readable rendering of the same page, with DOI/date and the opening text exposed directly.

The two carriers agree on the key p.131 propositions.

It directly establishes that:

- the idea for a journal of political theory arose during a **graduate-student meeting of the Columbia University chapter of the Conference for the Study of Political Thought (CSPT)**;
- the editors then persuaded SAGE that there was a need for such a journal and that it could succeed professionally and financially;
- by 1973 they described the journal's emergence as vindicating those expectations;
- they explicitly referred to an **actual need for an international journal of political philosophy**;
- they credited the willingness of eminent Section Editors and Editorial Board members with making rapid organization of the journal and its first issue possible.

This is direct actor-level evidence for the institutional-origin edge:

`COLUMBIA CSPT CHAPTER MEETING`
-> `JOURNAL IDEA`
-> `SAGE ORGANIZATIONAL ROUTE`
-> `INTERNATIONAL POLITICAL-PHILOSOPHY VENUE`.

The direct page does **not** by itself establish every later-described purpose of the journal.

## II. The editors' own jurisdictional language

The p.131 language is also useful for field-definition history.

The editors do not describe the project simply as a specialist outlet for one interpretive method. They frame it as answering an actual need for an **international journal of political philosophy**.

This matters because later method histories can retrospectively identify the journal with particular schools, debates, or canonical figures.

The contemporary founding self-description instead places the journal at a broader jurisdictional level:

`POLITICAL PHILOSOPHY AS INTERNATIONAL VENUE / FIELD PROBLEM`

rather than

`ONE HISTORICAL METHOD AS CORPORATE DOCTRINE`.

This supports the current `METHOD AGGREGATION` / `EMBEDDED DIFFERENTIATION` model but should not be treated as proof of equal openness to every method or political-theory tradition.
