# Lovejoy 1897 title contraction → 1906 relational scale control (Batch 221)

Date: 2026-09-03  
Status: **SYNTHESIS WITH DIRECT PRIMARY CONTROLS / PUBLICATION-PATH UPGRADE**  
Scope: 1897 AOS Final Circular + Baltimore Proceedings + 1898 JAOS Buddhist technical-terms article, read against MS38_004/005 and the 1906 *Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy*.  

## Core result

The 1897 AOS evidence adds a new intermediate state between Lovejoy's notebook decomposition and his 1898 printed technical-term article.

The relevant sequence is now directly documentable:

1. **10 Apr 1897, AOS Final Circular:** Lovejoy's announced paper is `Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`.
2. **22–24 Apr 1897, final Baltimore Proceedings:** Lovejoy's No. 30 is listed instead as `On the meaning of the Buddhist technical terms ...`; Lovejoy is absent from the complete attendance list, and the paper is among those read by title near adjournment.
3. **1898, JAOS 19:** publication becomes `The Buddhistic technical terms upādāna and upādisesa`, pp. 126–136.

This does **not** prove that one fixed manuscript was simply renamed twice. It does establish something more limited and historically important:

> **Between the final-circular stage and the recorded meeting communication, Lovejoy's public-facing research object contracted from the argument of a whole Buddhist text to the meaning of technical terms.**

The contraction is independently visible in institutional documentation before the 1898 publication. It therefore cannot be treated merely as a retrospective inference from notebook versus printed article.

The strongest current synthesis is:

> **Lovejoy's early continuity lies neither in stable scale nor in stable object. It lies in recurrent relational decomposition and proof-burden control. The historical novelty lies in where he later permits reaggregation: in 1897–98 public claims contract toward term-level relations; by 1906 similarly decomposed relations can be reassembled transversely into a broad comparative category called `primitive philosophy`.**

`relational decomposition`, `proof-burden control`, `scale contraction`, `scale control`, and `reaggregation` are project analytical terms, not Lovejoy's own vocabulary.

## 2. The title shift is an evidentiary event, not merely bibliographic noise

The two 1897 documentary states differ in the scale of their object.

### 10 Apr 1897

`Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`

Object address:

- one named Buddhist text;
- its argument as a whole;
- a synthetic/critical summary.

### Final Baltimore Proceedings

`On the meaning of the Buddhist technical terms ...`

Object address:

- technical vocabulary;
- meaning / semantic determination;
- a localizable textual problem.

### 1898 publication

`The Buddhistic technical terms upādāna and upādisesa`

Object address:

- two named terms;
- Pitaka usage;
- semantic and doctrinal discrimination;
- explicit caution about what etymology and individual passages can prove.

The secure claim is therefore **scale contraction in the documentary title path**.

Do not yet claim:

- that the same manuscript moved unchanged through all three states;
- that the 10 Apr title was merely a temporary shorthand for the final article;
- that the missing words after `technical terms` in the Proceedings can be silently reconstructed as `upādāna and upādisesa` until the printed page image is directly controlled.

## 3. Why this matters for the notebook → publication relation

The current JHI draft already states that publication did not simply polish the notebook; it reduced the size of some claims.

That judgment was based principally on manuscript/publication comparison:

- MS38_004 ranges across Sāṃkhya/Buddhist chronology;
- dependent-origination structure;
- psychological classification;
- causation;
- etymology;
- textual criticism;
- borrowing and genealogy;
- the relation between imported elements and their later arrangement.

The 1898 article retains only a narrower set of claims around `upādāna` / `upādisesa`.

The 1897 title sequence now supplies an **independent public-facing intermediate control**:

`whole-text argument`

→ `technical-term meaning`

→ `two technical terms in print`.

Thus claim contraction occurs not only when comparing private notebook to final publication. A contraction is visible inside the pre-publication institutional record itself.

This upgrades the historical model from:

`private broad notebook → narrow publication`

to:

`private broad notebook`

→ `publicly announced whole-text synthesis`

→ `recorded term-level meeting communication`

→ `narrow printed technical-term article`.

The middle two states should remain distinct because the exact manuscript relation is unresolved.

## 4. Relational decomposition in MS38_004

The key continuity remains relation-type discrimination, not subject-matter identity.

Across MS38_004, Lovejoy repeatedly separates questions that an over-compressed historical account could collapse:

- term identity vs difference in use;
- temporal antecedence vs logical subdivision;
- etymology vs attested usage;
- provenance / borrowing vs function after recombination;
- resemblance vs historical connection;
- compositeness vs unintelligibility;
- classification vs proof of genealogy.

The manuscript p. 123 case remains especially diagnostic:

> `Viññāṇa is temporally (?) an antecedent of nāmarūpa, and logically a subdivision of it.`

This is not merely a Buddhist doctrinal observation. It gives a concrete example of one item occupying different positions under different relations.

The method-level inference remains bounded:

> **one relation is not allowed automatically to answer for another.**

That is the narrow sense in which `proof-burden architecture` or `relational decomposition` is justified.

---

## 5. 1898: publication narrows the proof burden

The 1898 paper makes the narrowing explicit at the level of warrant.

For `upādisesa`, Lovejoy moves among competing etymologies and textual passages but states that the meaning of `upādi` must be settled `rather by an examination of its use than by etymological arguments` (printed p. 134).

He retains contrary passages, labels some evidence `relevant but not necessarily conclusive`, and ends without claiming final settlement.

Similarly, in arguing against a strong implication drawn from Senart, Lovejoy can concede that terms, perhaps even ideas, may be borrowed while preserving the possibility that their `arrangement and application` are characteristic of the resulting Buddhist formulation.

The inferential grammar is:

`source/provenance claim`

≠ automatically

`functional/organizational claim`.

The paper therefore carries forward MS38_004's relation-type separation while reducing the number and scale of relations on which it asks publication to bear weight.

## 6. Correction to the phrase `continuity is scale-control`

`Scale-control` is useful but insufficient if it implies consistent caution against generalization.

The 1906 evidence requires a stronger distinction.

Lovejoy does **not** maintain a permanently small scale. In *The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy* he makes a very large comparative claim from heterogeneous ethnographic materials.

Existing Batch 210 establishes the operation:

`heterogeneous local data`

→ `mechanism abstraction`

→ `comparative convergence`

→ `primitive philosophy` as a collective actor-level category.

The more exact formulation is therefore:

> **continuity lies in relational decomposition; variation lies in the level at which Lovejoy permits reaggregation.**

Or, more compactly:

> **the continuity is not small scale but controlled movement across scales.**

The project should prefer the first formulation because `controlled` can otherwise overstate the adequacy of the later warrant.

---

## 7. 1906 is not a simple abandonment of the 1898 discipline

A misleading developmental narrative would be:

`careful philology`

→ `later crude anthropology`.

The evidence is more difficult.

In 1906 Lovejoy continues to distinguish:

- observer description from observer interpretation;
- local lexical evidence from imposed theological translation;
- genealogical relation from recurrent mechanism;
- personality/spirit theories from impersonal efficacy;
- institutional history from cross-cultural causal structure.

But this granular decomposition is used to support a far larger abstraction.

Lovejoy correlates terms and practices from multiple ethnographic fields and reconstru cts an impersonal, communicable, quasi-mechanical efficacy as a recurrent causal mechanism. The mechanism is then elevated into:

- a belief family (`Primitive Energetics`);
- a `fundamental concept`;
- `primitive philosophy`;
- and, at points, a predicate of `savage thought`, `most savages`, or broad populations at a cultural stage.

Thus:

> **micro-level proof discipline and macro-level universalizing ambition coexist.**

The 1906 problem is not simply failure to distinguish relations. It is the decision to treat one isolated relation as sufficient for a higher-order cross-cultural equivalence.

## 8. The crucial historical operation: decomposition can make reaggregation possible

The important sequence is now visible across 1897–1906.

### Stage A — decomposition / narrowing

A large object is broken into distinguishable relations:

- text → term;
- term → use;
- sequence → temporal relation;
- classification → logical relation;
- borrowed component → provenance;
- recombined component → present function.

### Stage B — mobility

Once a relation has been isolated, it can be detached analytically from the whole object in which it occurs.

A `primitive` relation or mechanism can therefore occur:

- inside a later religion;
- inside a Greek formation;
- inside a composite rite;
- inside an otherwise historically heterogeneous tradition.

This is the non-synchronized stratification established in Batches 208–209.

### Stage C — transverse reaggregation

Comparable relations can then be grouped across objects with different institutional histories.

The model is:

`A contains relation M`

`B contains relation M`

`C contains relation M`

while A, B and C need not share one genealogy.

Then:

`M recurs independently`

→ `M is generalized as a fundamental feature of primitive philosophy`.

The decomposition that initially protects historical heterogeneity can therefore become the technical precondition for a new totalization at a higher analytical level.

## 9. Larger `primitive` thesis: stratification rather than contradiction

This sequence strengthens the project-level argument that `primitive` should not be analyzed principally as a contradictory chronological label.

The more precise historical problem is **stratification**.

A single object can receive different positions under different ordering relations:

- temporally prior;
- logically subordinate or constitutive;
- genealogically borrowed;
- functionally central;
- culturally persistent;
- analytically `primitive` despite coexistence with later strata.

This permits historical ordering after a single homogeneous world chronology becomes difficult to sustain.

The emerging comparative grid has at least two axes:

1. **vertical / internal:** heterogeneous layers inside a tradition, text, rite, or concept;
2. **horizontal / transverse:** equivalence among similarly classified relations across traditions.

`Primitive` can operate as an index moving between these axes.

The resulting system is not equivalent to a Gatterer-style synchronized table and not reducible to a simple unilineal ladder of peoples.

It is closer to a stratigraphic comparative ordering in which one historical object may contain multiple temporal/logical statuses and one status may recur across many objects.

## 10. Where flattening occurs

The project should avoid saying that Lovejoy consistently `refuses flattening`.

A more exact diagnosis is:

1. he often refuses **premature flattening between relation types**;
2. he then selects one relation as comparatively diagnostic;
3. local differences on other axes are bracketed;
4. the selected relation is generalized across cases;
5. the resulting analyst-level abstraction can be attributed back to broad actor populations.

Thus:

> **stratification can make controlled flattening possible.**

`controlled` here refers to the operation's explicit evidentiary organization, not to a normative endorsement of its adequacy.

The most politically consequential move in 1906 is therefore not merely saying `this people is primitive`. It is subtler:

> isolate a mechanism as primitive → find it transversely in multiple populations → reconstruct it as the fundamental concept of `primitive philosophy`.

Historical diversity can remain at the level of genealogy while homogeneity is rebuilt at the level of causal cognition.

## 11. Relationship to Brinton and the next comparative target

The Lovejoy evidence now creates a sharper control for Brinton.

The question should not be only whether Brinton uses harsher or more explicit stadial vocabulary. The stronger comparison is grammatical and inferential:

- What is the immediate subject/predicate of `primitive`?
- Is primitiveness assigned to a mechanism, concept, stratum, text, rite, mind, race, people, or civilization?
- Does a relation-specific classification remain local?
- At what point is it reattributed to a whole population?
- Is cross-cultural similarity used as functional equivalence, developmental synchronization, or proof of genealogy?

This will distinguish at least three operations that can otherwise be conflated:

1. **relational primitiveness** — a mechanism/stratum is primitive;
2. **transverse reaggregation** — similar primitive mechanisms are compared across heterogeneous objects;
3. **population synchronization** — whole living groups are assigned to a common developmental stage.

The current hypothesis is that Lovejoy 1906 strongly performs (1) and (2), and partially slides toward (3) through `savage thought` / broad population predicates without reducing all of his comparison to a single stadial chronology.

Brinton is the next strong control for testing whether (3) becomes more explicit and structurally dominant.

## Current judgment

**Major synthesis upgrade.** The 1897 AOS title sequence provides a pre-publication institutional witness to scale contraction: Lovejoy's announced whole-*Milinda-pañha* argument becomes a recorded technical-term communication before the 1898 term-level article. Read with MS38_004 and the 1906 `primitive philosophy` paper, the strongest longitudinal model is not `small-scale philology → large-scale anthropology`, nor `flattening → pluralism`. It is:

> **relational decomposition → proof-burden separation → scale contraction where public warrant is narrow → portability of isolated relations → transverse reaggregation at a higher comparative level.**

This model explains how increasingly fine relation-type discrimination can coexist with, and even technically enable, a broad cross-cultural totalization. For the larger `primitive` project, the central historical problem is therefore not contradiction but the architecture by which heterogeneous temporal/logical strata are isolated, moved across objects, and reassembled into comparative world-historical categories.
