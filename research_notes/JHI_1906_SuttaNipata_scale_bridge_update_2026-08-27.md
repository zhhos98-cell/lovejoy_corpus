# JHI-facing update — 1906 Sutta Nipāta / scale bridge

Date: 2026-08-27  
Status: **ARTICLE-FACING SUPPLEMENT / USE AFTER CORE 004–005 ARCHITECTURE**

## Why this matters

The final research sweep recovered a compact bridge that is unusually efficient for a 2400-word article because it connects the 1898 Buddhist work to Lovejoy's 1906 comparative practice without claiming that Buddhism `caused` history of ideas.

## Direct evidence chain

### 1898

In `The Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa`, Lovejoy explicitly says that the *Sutta Nipāta* is the first place to turn for the original signification of Buddhist technical terms. He uses it to adjudicate `upādisesa` and Nirvāṇa against competing scholarly interpretations.

### April 1906

In the newly recovered review of Paul Elmer More's *Shelburne Essays`, Lovejoy again invokes the *Sutta Nipāta*, now specifically vv.1073–1076, to block More's attempt to give Nirvāṇa a positive concrete value/meaning.

The same review makes methodological preferences explicit. Lovejoy contrasts insufficient `induction of evidence` and inquiry into `historical conditions and antecedents` with the value of `wide-ranging comparisons` and the correlation of manifestations across French, Italian, Greek and Oriental literatures.

He also calls More's Indian/Buddhist formation an historically significant episode in the intellectual relations of `America and the Orient`, while judging the resulting negative philosophy against `our American and democratic life`.

### July 1906

`The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` correlates heterogeneous ethnographic and linguistic evidence into an impersonal/quasi-mechanical causal category and then reaggregates it upward as a fundamental concept of `primitive philosophy` / `savage thought`.

## Article-level inference

The safest bridge is:

> **1898 and 1906 show continuity of source-controlled interpretation, while the scale of the resulting claims changes.**

Do not write:

- Buddhism caused `Primitive Philosophy`;
- the *Sutta Nipāta* supplied the later category method;
- the 1906 More review proves continuous Buddhist research every year since 1898;
- Lovejoy had a mature history-of-ideas method in 1906.

## Suggested compact paragraph

> Lovejoy's Buddhist source practice did not simply vanish after 1898. In an April 1906 review of Paul Elmer More, he again appealed to the *Sutta Nipāta*—now to challenge a positive reconstruction of Nirvāṇa—while praising comparison and the correlation of thought across Greek, European, and `Oriental` literatures and faulting insufficient induction and attention to historical antecedents. Three months later, in `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`, he correlated heterogeneous ethnographic evidence into a cross-regional causal category. This does not make Buddhism the origin of his later method. It shows, more modestly, that source-level adjudication and large-scale comparison remained simultaneous parts of his intellectual practice.

## Stronger project-level formulation, probably too large for the 2400-word article

The 1906 corpus now shows four simultaneous scalar operations:

1. `Democracy`: generic Man → stage × race/national differentiation;
2. Kant: inherited macro historical rubric → local audit → macro rubric rejected;
3. More review: source-level Buddhist semantic control + cross-literature comparison/correlation;
4. `Primitive Philosophy`: local heterogeneous evidence → mechanism abstraction → cross-regional collective reaggregation.

The historiographical payoff is not a chronological story from context to abstraction. It is a problem of **warranted scale movement**.

## Placement recommendation

Use the 1906 More/*Sutta Nipāta* recurrence immediately before or inside the transition from the 1898 article/notebook discussion to the 1906 comparative paper. It can replace a longer generic paragraph claiming that `Indic materials remained important`.

For the final paragraph, retain the mature-work result: *The Great Chain of Being* explicitly centers an Occidental/Western genealogy while continuing to use Buddhist/Vedāntic materials as comparative philosophical types. This is functional repartition, not disappearance.
