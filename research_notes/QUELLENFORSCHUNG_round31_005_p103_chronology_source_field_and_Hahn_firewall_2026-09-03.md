## 2. Spencer 1898 is a very close constituent-source control

Herbert Spencer, *The Principles of Sociology*, vol. III (1898), §726, surveys African transitional forms and gives exactly the kind of lower-level ethnographic premise Lovejoy uses.

Spencer says, among other cases:

- Coast-negro agriculture proceeds without plough or beasts of burden, using a hoe-like implement;
- in Congo there are no draught animals and therefore no ploughs;
- African cultivation varies independently of the presence and agricultural use of domestic animals.

Thus Spencer supplies a particularly close contemporary proposition:

`AFRICAN AGRICULTURE != NECESSARILY DEPENDENT ON DRAUGHT-ANIMAL ECONOMY`.

This constituent is field/source-owned, not Lovejoy's discovery.

No current manuscript citation has yet tied p.103 directly to this exact Spencer section, so proposition match and transmission must remain distinct.

## 3. Eduard Hahn 1891/1896 supplies the larger anti-three-stage chronology field

Eduard Hahn's work directly attacks the old universal sequence:

`HUNTING -> PASTORALISM / ANIMAL HUSBANDRY -> AGRICULTURE`.

His 1896 *Die Haustiere und ihre Beziehungen zur Wirtschaft des Menschen* and related 1896 *Demeter und Baubo* work develop `Hackbau` as cultivation that can precede pastoralism, cattle use, plough agriculture, and draught-animal economy.

Contemporary/near-contemporary summaries make the proposition unusually explicit:

- long periods of simple cultivation can exist without cattle and plough;
- livestock domestication may arise among settled hoe-cultivators rather than preceding cultivation;
- African and American hoe-cultivators can acquire cattle husbandry historically after cultivation is already present.

Hahn therefore supplies a strong proposition-field control for the clock Lovejoy rejects.

But the repository currently has **no direct evidence that p.103 cites Eduard Hahn or his 1896 books**.

## 4. Critical disambiguation: the `Hahn` in the 005 Africa bibliography is Theophilus Hahn, not Eduard Hahn

An earlier reading-path dossier correctly noted a `Hahn` entry in the large African bibliography near pp.62–63. It would be tempting to use that occurrence to promote a Lovejoy→Eduard-Hahn reading edge for p.103.

That move is wrong.

The canonical bibliography identifies the entry as:

> `Hahn — The Supreme Being of the Khoi-Khoi [?].`

The book is **Theophilus Hahn**, *Tsuni-||Goam: The Supreme Being of the Khoi-Khoi* (London: Trübner, 1881), a South-African religion/mythology source.

Therefore:

`NOTEBOOK HAHN BIBLIOGRAPHIC NODE`
=
`THEOPHILUS HAHN / KHOI-KHOI RELIGION`

not

`EDUARD HAHN / DOMESTICATION-AGRICULTURE CHRONOLOGY`.

This firewall is substantive. The same surname cannot be used to manufacture a direct reading edge.
