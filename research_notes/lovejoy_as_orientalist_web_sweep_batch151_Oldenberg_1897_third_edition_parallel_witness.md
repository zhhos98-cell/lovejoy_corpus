## 4. Foucher 1903 is a third-edition-state parallel witness, not just a later translation

Multiple contemporary/specialist records identify:

Hermann Oldenberg, _Le Bouddha, sa vie, sa doctrine, sa communauté_, translated by Alfred Foucher, **2e édition française, revue et augmentée d'après la 3e édition allemande**, Paris: Félix Alcan, 1903.

Extent in a contemporary review: about 400/401 pages.

This wording is important. The 1903 French text was explicitly revised against the 1897 third German edition rather than merely reprinting Foucher's earlier translation of the second edition.

The French witness can therefore serve two roles:

1. locate/understand the structure of additions introduced in the third German edition;
2. provide a translation control for passages whose German page image remains difficult to extract.

It must **not** replace the German third edition for exact German quotation or pagination.

## 5. La Vallée Poussin identifies the special status of the Sāṁkhya appendix

A later specialist bibliographic note by Louis de La Vallée Poussin is unusually useful for edition history. When listing the Sāṁkhya/Buddhism controversy, he cites:

`Oldenberg, Buddha³ (1897), appendice`

and adds that this appendix was:

- **suppressed in later German editions**;
- retained in **Foucher², Paris 1903**.

This explains why searching later German editions is a poor substitute for the 1897 pp.443–455 target, while the 1903 French second edition remains potentially valuable.

The implication is source-critical:

> **the third edition is not merely a pagination variant. It preserves a controversy-specific textual state that Oldenberg later revised away or relocated.**

That makes the 1897 target more historically important, not less.

## 6. Later French / German witnesses must be kept separate

A currently searchable electronic French version is based on Foucher's **1934 fourth French edition**, revised against the seventh/final German edition. It contains a mature discussion of Buddhism/Sāṁkhya and cites later scholarship (including Oldenberg's later Upanishad work, Pischel 1917, etc.). It is therefore **not** a clean witness to the 1897 appendix.

Similarly, a 1912 Hungarian exposition cites Oldenberg's **1906 fifth edition p.277** while discussing the twelve nidānas and Senart. This is useful for later reception and confirms that the Senart/nidāna dispute persisted in the revised book, but it does not establish the exact wording of the 1897 p.273 note.

Do not collapse these textual states.

## 8. Research consequence for 004

Batch 33 had already shown that Oldenberg 1898 performs semantic/functional differentiation strongly enough to block a Lovejoy-priority claim. Batch 151 makes the **1897 third edition itself** more consequential.

Jacobi's 1898 references to p.273 and p.448ff point into a textual state that was actively being revised in response to the mid-1890s Sāṁkhya/nidāna controversy and that was not simply preserved unchanged in later German editions.

The right historical question is therefore not only:

> `What did Oldenberg believe?`

but:

> **What changed in Oldenberg's published evidentiary apparatus between the 1890 second edition, the controversy-specific 1897 third edition, the 1898 ZDMG intervention, and later revisions?**

That sequence may provide a much better control for Lovejoy 004/1898 than treating `Oldenberg` as a single stable authority.
