## 0. Anti-duplication check

Repository search for the conjunction `agriculture / domestication / vicious circle / wild → domestic animal sacrifice / anterior` found no dedicated analytical note. The material is present in the corrected transcription but has not been integrated into the Batch209–224 longitudinal model.

This is not merely another example of `same outward form, different mechanism`. It tests whether **different developmental sequences are allowed to run on different clocks**.

## 1. The reconstruction Lovejoy is testing

The surrounding pages build an evolutionary/historical hypothesis for agricultural sacrifice:

- cultivated plants and fertility rites;
- human or animal victims used to transfer life/power to crops;
- possible movement from earlier forms to sacrifice of domestic animals;
- later symbolic/personalized interpretations.

At p.103 Lovejoy sketches a branching relation among cultivated plants, domestic animals and sacrifice and then tests the chronology required by the proposed genealogy.

The problem is that one version of the theory needs something like:

`wild-animal / early agrarian sacrifice`
→ `domestic-animal sacrifice`

while also presupposing a general civilizational sequence in which:

`domestication`
→ `agriculture`.

If agriculture is needed to generate the earlier agrarian rite, while domestication is simultaneously assumed to precede agriculture, the reconstruction becomes circular or at least chronologically unstable.

## 2. Lovejoy's explicit objection: `vicious circle`

The corrected transcription at p.103 records Lovejoy formulating the objection as a **vicious circle**.

He refuses to rescue the theory by treating `domestication before agriculture` as a universal developmental law. Instead he notes that, in Africa, agriculture can be **anterior to domestication**, and that domestic animals need not supply agricultural labor.

The evidentiary move is important:

> **a proposed ritual genealogy cannot borrow a supposedly universal economic-development sequence unless that sequence is independently established for the relevant historical field.**

Thus a cross-domain stage model cannot serve as an invisible chronological scaffold.

Evidence grade: **MEDIUM-HIGH / corrected manuscript transcription**. The small diagram is partly unclear, but the objection, the `vicious circle` structure, and the Africa agriculture-before-domestication correction are all retained securely enough for analysis.

## 3. Why this is stronger than a generic anti-unilinear statement

The notebook elsewhere says or implies that multiple motives, layers and historical origins can coexist. Here Lovejoy does something more exact:

1. identifies a proposed sequence;
2. identifies a second sequence the proposal tacitly relies on;
3. notices that the two sequences generate circularity if treated as universally synchronized;
4. attacks the hidden sequence with comparative evidence;
5. allows the economic/technological order to vary by region.

This is a **chronology audit inside a genealogy**.

It therefore supplies a particularly clean 005 analogue to Batch209's 004 result that temporal, logical and classificatory order need not coincide.

## 4. Relation to `primitive = stratification`

The result prevents a flattened ladder of the form:

`primitive economy`
→ `domestication`
→ `agriculture`
→ `animal sacrifice`
→ `higher religious form`.

Lovejoy may still use `primitive`, `higher`, `later`, and developmental language. But this page shows that the coordinates cannot simply be synchronized across economy, subsistence, ritual morphology and religious interpretation.

A safe project formulation is:

> **005 does not merely stratify religious forms internally; it can also decouple the chronology of religion from the chronology of subsistence technology. Agriculture and domestication do not supply a universal master clock for sacrificial development.**

This is a stronger form of the project's `chronological non-isomorphism` claim because the non-isomorphism now crosses domains:

- subsistence/economic history;
- animal domestication;
- ritual form;
- sacrificial mechanism;
- religious interpretation.

`chronological non-isomorphism` remains our term, not Lovejoy's.

## 5. pp.104–105 as bounded continuation

p.104 reinforces the evidentiary rule: a domestic-animal sacrifice may be modified into sacrifice of a divine animal or divine representative, but the historical relation must be **demonstrated rather than assumed**.

p.105 then permits a local developmental sequence in the vegetation-soul problem: a quasi-mechanical/magical operation may precede a later sacramental sacrifice of a personal/divine representative.

This combination is crucial. Lovejoy is not rejecting historical sequence as such. He is distinguishing:

- **demonstrated / locally warranted sequence**;
- from **imported universal sequence used as an explanatory shortcut**.

That keeps the result from being redescribed as simple anti-evolutionism.
