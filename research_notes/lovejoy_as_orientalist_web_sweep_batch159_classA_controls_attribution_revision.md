## A1. Léon Marillier on S. R. Steinmetz, _Endokannibalismus_, RHR 34 (1896), pp.113–115

**Status: PRIMARY_TEXT_RECOVERED.** Page 116 supplies the boundary/signature control.

### What Marillier actually says

Printed p.113 recognizes the strong religious significance of surviving endocannibal practices and accurately states Steinmetz's thesis that religious motives are often superadded and that the original cause lies in another order of facts. Marillier nevertheless insists that the persistence of anthropophagic habits in many observed groups is inseparable from animistic beliefs embodied in practice.

Printed p.114 gives the decisive methodological statement. After summarizing Steinmetz's five categories, Marillier remarks on the **variety and disparate character of the reasons** given for cannibal practice and draws the conclusion that the cases are phenomena which **only external resemblances permit one to group together** and which are **not amenable to a single interpretation**. He then contrasts this with Steinmetz's attempt to treat the reported motives as secondary formations and to subordinate animistic conceptions to an original nutritional history.

Printed p.115 grants that physiological and economic causes may have played a decisive role in many cases, but explicitly rejects the claim that Steinmetz has established a **unique origin**. In some cases animistic beliefs or magical conceptions alone can account for the observed facts; under those conditions there is no need to posit the chronological priority of endocannibalism over exocannibalism. Marillier closes by praising the study's information while criticizing its `théories exclusives et parfois hasardées`.

### Proposition-level delta against Batches149/156

This control is stronger than the earlier reconstruction expected.

Previous project formulation:

- Steinmetz rejects crude single-motive `Simplomanie` at the level of surviving motives but retains a strong nutritional/phylogenetic origin hierarchy;
- Lovejoy 005 shifts from visible ritual morphology toward discriminating causal mechanisms.

Batch159 refinement:

- the Steinmetz description remains secure;
- but **Marillier had already published the anti-morphological operator in 1896 in nearly explicit form**: outward resemblance is insufficient for explanatory unification, and heterogeneous motives require plural interpretation;
- therefore 005 cannot be treated as independently originating this inference rule;
- the stronger historical description is **uptake/redeployment/elaboration within a Marillierian methodological environment**, with Lovejoy extending the same discipline across sacrifice, blood, foundation deposits, fetish/charm, agrarian rites, and cannibalism.

This also sharpens the contrast with Steinmetz. The issue is not `monism versus pluralism` simpliciter. It is:

> **Steinmetz: local motive plurality + later refunctionalization under a strong universal origin hierarchy**  
> **Marillier: external similarity does not warrant one explanatory class; even origin may be plural and independently animistic/magical**.

No claim of direct textual borrowing by Lovejoy from this review is made solely from similarity. The point is a secure pre-existing field articulation by Lovejoy's Paris teacher.

## A2. Hermann Jacobi on Joseph Dahlmann, _Nirvāṇa_, GGA 1897, pp.265–279; core pp.268–272

**Status: PRIMARY_TEXT_RECOVERED.** The requested 267–273 control window is now available.

### What Jacobi actually says

Printed p.268 first asks whether the epic system is older than classical Sāṁkhya, then insists that the epic already knew the **differentiating doctrines** of Sāṁkhya and Yoga. The cited Mahābhārata passage differentiates the systems through the role of Īśvara and the respective routes to liberation. Jacobi says this characterization corresponds exactly to the later classical relation and therefore blocks a loose identification of every epic use of `Sāṁkhya` with Dahlmann's reconstructed older system.

Printed p.271 warns that a philosophy serving as the speculative basis of a widespread popular religion cannot simply be identified with a philosophical school. Indian religious formations can claim mutually inconsistent authorities and absorb them into a larger synthesis.

Printed p.272 makes the discriminative rule especially clear: the difference-points are `mehrere und nicht unerhebliche`; non-recognition of Īśvara and the precise differentiation of **buddhi, ahaṅkāra, and manas** separate Sāṁkhya from Yoga, aside from Yoga's systematically developed ascetic practice. Jacobi remarks that it required a special religious eye to see the two as one — or rather, **not to see their difference**.

### Proposition-level delta against Batch153

Batch153 isolated Lovejoy's 004 addendum after Rhys Davids: eternity of Matter and Self is too generic; a more genuinely Sāṁkhya marker is the separateness of Self and World, `thorough-going dualism`.

That notebook observation remains real. But Jacobi 1897 shows that the general operator — identify a system through nontrivial differentiating doctrines rather than generic affinities — was already explicit in the contemporary Indological controversy.

Therefore:

- retain `diagnostic specificity` as an analytical description of what Lovejoy is doing;
- **withdraw any implication of Lovejoy priority or strong methodological distinctiveness for the operator itself**;
- describe 004 as a manuscript-level **application/reweighting** of a field practice;
- do not claim Lovejoy took the move directly from this 1897 review unless independent access/citation evidence is recovered.

## A3. Hermann Oldenberg, _Buddha_, dritte vermehrte Auflage, Berlin: Wilhelm Hertz, 1897

**Status: PRIMARY_TEXT_RECOVERED and edition state controlled.**

The uploaded witness explicitly reads `DRITTE VERMEHRTE AUFLAGE`, Berlin, Wilhelm Hertz, 1897. The older local Google witness previously used as a baseline remains the 1890 **second** edition and must not be substituted for the 1897 text.

### A3a. Printed pp.272–273: inherited vocabulary can still be recombined intelligibly

In the note beginning on p.272, Oldenberg accepts that Buddhism repeatedly works with inherited `Schlagworte`, but adds that it combines them in ways from which **thoughts, even if often unclear thoughts, remain recognizable**. This directly precedes the notebook/publication problem of whether composite provenance makes the nidāna series unintelligible.

On p.273 Oldenberg then addresses Senart's claim that `upādāna` is merely an abbreviation for the five `upādānakkhandha`. Oldenberg says he must **absolutely deny** the identity. He points to canonical passages explicitly distinguishing them and identifies `upādāna` as `chandarāga` — desire/attachment **with respect to** the five khandhas. The alleged duplication/confusion of the causal formula therefore disappears.

This is an exact pre-Lovejoy control for one of the strongest semantic moves in 004/1898.

### A3b. Printed pp.443–455: the Sāṁkhya/Buddhism excursus

This edition-specific excursus is methodologically richer than the earlier 1890 baseline.

**p.443.** Oldenberg says recent work by Garbe, Jacobi, Senart, and Dahlmann has made him soften his earlier skepticism and place Buddhism closer to Sāṁkhya, while still resisting the strongest dependency claims.

**p.444.** Against the idea that the unified appearance of classical Sāṁkhya proves a single creation, Oldenberg explicitly allows that old **keywords, keyword-groups, and whole category-series** may be put by new thinkers into the service of **new fundamental ideas**; an older loose worldview can later be recast into a strict systematic form and modified even at its foundations. A gradually generated formation can therefore come to look `aus einem Gusse`.

**p.445.** His task is to look for the `Hauptgedanken` in which Buddhism shares **characteristic directions** of later Sāṁkhya, while leaving open whether the preparatory thought-circles were already identical with communities bearing the Sāṁkhya name.

**pp.445–446.** The first Brahmajāla parallel, eternal Self and world, may be only a combination of common slogans whose partial similarity to Sāṁkhya is accidental or badly reported. The second body/mind parallel actually conflicts with a conspicuous Sāṁkhya doctrine because citta/buddhi and manas belong, in Sāṁkhya, to changing prakṛti.

**p.447.** Oldenberg grants `Anklänge und Berührungen` between the Buddhist causal chain and Sāṁkhya, but stresses that they answer **totally different questions**: Buddhism asks how suffering arises through psychic processes; Sāṁkhya asks how the universe evolves from the world-ground. Similar category-sequences therefore do not by themselves establish derivation.

**p.448.** Avidyā and saṁskāra may stand in neighboring positions and the concepts are genuinely related, but the precise construction differs. Such contacts are natural between neighboring systems working with related conceptual tools; the material permits little more to be asserted.

**p.449.** Agreement in the five senses plus manas `beweist natürlich wenig`; once comparison reaches the **really characteristic conceptions of Sāṁkhya**, the parallel breaks down.

**p.450.** The absence of the guṇas from Buddhism cannot bear the inference that Buddha's Sāṁkhya prototype therefore lacked the guṇas. Oldenberg's memorable formulation is that this puts too heavy a weight on a hook that cannot carry it.

**pp.451–453.** Negative coincidence — two sources both failing to mention a doctrine — is nearly meaningless. The late Aśvaghoṣa report on Arāḍa must be controlled against older Pāli, Mahāvastu, and Lalitavistara traditions and against the literary character of the _Buddhacarita_; genre and chronology undermine Jacobi's attempt to treat the passage as authentic historical testimony about Buddha's teacher.

**pp.454–455.** Against Senart's `sakkāya = satkārya` etymology, Oldenberg gives priority to **positive canonical usage**. The textual distribution fits `sakkāya/satkāya` as the complex/appearance-world organized through the five khandhas much better than the proposed Sāṁkhya etymology; the direct Sāṁkhya allusion is therefore unproved.

### Proposition-level delta against Batches153–155

The 1897 third edition independently contains, before Lovejoy's published 1898 intervention, most of the inference controls previously isolated in notebook 004:

1. composite inheritance does not imply present incoherence;
2. generic resemblance must be separated from genuinely characteristic doctrine;
3. compared sequences must be tested for **question/function**, not only formal position;
4. absence/negative coincidence carries very little genealogical weight;
5. source chronology and literary genre constrain historical inference;
6. established textual usage can defeat speculative etymology;
7. `upādāna` is not identical with the five `upādānakkhandha`.

Accordingly:

- Batch154's `two-gate model` remains useful as an **analytical reconstruction**, but its constituent controls are now demonstrably field-level rather than Lovejoy-originated;
- Batch155's manuscript-to-publication `evidentiary compression` remains an observable asymmetry in Lovejoy's writing process, but it should not be used to imply invention of the semantic/source-critical operators that survive publication;
- the 004 contribution is better located in **selection, recombination, local textual testing, and later transport**.

## Revised attribution model after all Class-A controls

The strongest defensible historical claim is no longer a developmental story in which Lovejoy independently discovers one operator in 004, transforms it into mechanism analysis in 005, and then abstracts it in 1906.

The more source-secure reconstruction is:

### German Indology proof culture

Oldenberg/Jacobi supply, before or contemporaneously with 004:

- diagnostic discrimination between generic and system-characteristic traits;
- source chronology and genre control;
- comparison by conceptual/causal role, not merely verbal/formal resemblance;
- caution about negative evidence;
- usage-over-etymology discipline;
- a model in which inherited categories can be recombined into a coherent new system.

### French comparative-religion proof culture

Marillier supplies, explicitly by 1896:

- external morphology does not define one explanatory class;
- reported motives can be heterogeneous and causally distinct;
- universal origin inferences require independent demonstration;
- surviving forms may have multiple or independently generated animistic, magical, economic, or physiological explanations.

### Lovejoy notebooks

004 and 005 are therefore best treated as a **convergence/redeployment site** where these two proof cultures can be observed operating in one research practice:

- 004: discriminating philosophical systems, meanings, and genealogical claims through local textual and functional tests;
- 005: discriminating rituals through causal mechanism rather than visible morphology, with extensive reclassification of blood, sacrifice, fetish/charm, agrarian ritual, and cannibalism;
- 1906: one recurrent causal bearer — impersonal, direct, transferable, quasi-mechanical efficacy — becomes a more general theoretical object.

The remaining potentially distinctive trajectory is thus **cross-domain transport and recombination**, not priority in the constituent inference rules.

This formulation is deliberately weaker about direct transmission than the documentary resemblance might invite. A pre-existing published control proves **prior articulation**, not that Lovejoy read that exact item. Direct uptake requires citation, notebook reference, course/access evidence, or another independent documentary link.
