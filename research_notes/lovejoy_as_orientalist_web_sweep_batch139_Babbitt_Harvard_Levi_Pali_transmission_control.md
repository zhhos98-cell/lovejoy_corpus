# Batch 139 — Irving Babbitt as a concrete Harvard-to-Lévi Pāli transmission control before Lovejoy

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: follow Batch135's unresolved question — how could Lovejoy have known to expect Pāli/Buddhist study with Sylvain Lévi in Paris? Instead of treating `Harvard advice`, `printed programmes`, or an `American student network` as abstract possibilities, this pass tests whether a resident Harvard figure had recently followed the same Pāli/Buddhist route to EPHE and returned before Lovejoy's graduate years.

## Core result

Irving Babbitt is the first concrete Harvard-based transmission control that fits the chronology closely enough to matter.

Harvard University Archives states that Babbitt, after graduating Harvard in 1889 and teaching classics in Montana for two years, left for Paris to pursue postgraduate study in **Pāli literature and Buddhist traditions at the Écoles des Hautes-Études**. He returned to the United States, received a Harvard A.M. in 1893 in classical literature and ancient Eastern languages, and returned to Harvard in **1894** as an Instructor of French. A Presses de l'Inalco scholarly reconstruction makes the Paris teacher edge explicit: Babbitt spent a year at EPHE **under Sylvain Lévi**, studying **Pāli and Sanskrit**.

This chronology overlaps Lovejoy's Harvard graduate period in a way Stickney's does not. By 1895–97, when Lovejoy was doing Sanskrit/Buddhist work under Charles R. Lanman at Harvard, Babbitt was already a resident Harvard instructor who had himself recently gone to Paris for Pāli/Buddhist study in the Lévi network.

The implication must be carefully bounded:

> **A concrete Harvard-to-Lévi Pāli transmission route existed on campus before Lovejoy left for Paris.**

What is **not** proved is the transmission event:

- no current letter says Babbitt recommended Lévi to Lovejoy;
- no current course list shows Lovejoy studying with Babbitt;
- no current advising record links the two in 1895–98.

Thus Babbitt upgrades the problem from an abstract `possible American network` to a historically specific **network topology**, while the personal edge remains open.

---

## 1. Harvard's own archive gives the Pāli/Buddhist Paris trajectory

The HOLLIS finding aid for the **Papers of Irving Babbitt, HUG 1185** states that after two years teaching Latin and Greek at the College of Montana, Babbitt:

> left Montana to pursue post-graduate studies in Pali literature and Buddhist traditions at the Écoles des Hautes-Études in Paris.

It then says he returned to the United States, continued at Harvard, and received an A.M. in **1893** in classical literature and ancient Eastern languages.

Authoritative archival source:

https://hollisarchives.lib.harvard.edu/catalog/hua10004

This is stronger than a general biography because it is the current Harvard University Archives collection description and it identifies the specific disciplinary content of the Paris study.

HOLLIS does not name Lévi in that biographical paragraph, so the exact teacher edge requires a second source.

---

## 2. The teacher edge: Babbitt studied under Sylvain Lévi

A Presses de l'Inalco scholarly chapter on Babbitt's intellectual formation states that he spent a year at the École pratique des hautes études in Paris and, **under the direction of Sylvain Lévi**, studied Pāli and Sanskrit.

Scholarly institutional source:

https://books.openedition.org/pressesinalco/43480?lang=en

This produces the specific route:

`Harvard graduate / American classicist`

→ `EPHE Paris`

→ `Sylvain Lévi`

→ `Pāli + Sanskrit`

→ `return to Harvard`.

That route is strikingly close to the pedagogical expectation Wilson later attributes to Lovejoy.

Evidence limit: this teacher edge is presently a strong scholarly reconstruction, not a directly inspected 1891–92 EPHE enrollment sheet or Babbitt letter. The next level of proof would be Babbitt's surviving student notes, a Paris registration entry, or correspondence naming Lévi.

---

## 3. Babbitt was back on the Harvard faculty before Lovejoy arrived

The same HOLLIS finding aid states that Babbitt returned to Harvard in **1894** as an Instructor of French and remained at Harvard for nearly forty years.

A contemporary Harvard Crimson announcement for the coming 1895–96 academic year independently lists **Irving Babbitt, instructor in French** and says he would conduct **French A**.

Contemporary source:

https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1895/6/8/radcliffe-elective-pamphlet/

This matters because the chronological relation is now exact enough to distinguish Babbitt from Stickney:

- Stickney left Harvard in 1895 and then lived/studied in Paris;
- Babbitt was **already teaching at Harvard from 1894**;
- Lovejoy entered Harvard graduate study in autumn 1895 and remained through the 1896–97 Sanskrit/Buddhist phase.

So Babbitt and Lovejoy had real institutional co-presence at Harvard.

Do not convert that into acquaintance. The contemporary announcement shows Babbitt taught French A; there is no evidence Lovejoy took that course.

---

## 4. Harvard itself was publicly teaching Pāli/Buddhist textual problems in Lovejoy's exact period

Babbitt was not an isolated returned specialist. On **29 April 1896**, Charles R. Lanman gave the first of three public lectures on Buddhism at the Fogg Art Museum. The Crimson says its abstract was taken from Lanman's own outline with bibliographical notes.

Lanman's first lecture explicitly included:

- relation of Buddhism to Vedism and Brahmanism;
- relation to **Sāṃkhya and Vedānta**;
- northern and southern Buddhist literature;
- **Sanskrit and Pāli**;
- the Tipiṭaka and its divisions;
- the Pali Text Society;
- the King of Siam's edition;
- Buddha legend and historical tradition.

Contemporary source:

https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1896/4/29/lecture-on-buddhism-the-first-of/

This is almost a checklist of the problem field later visible in Lovejoy notebook 004 and the 1898 JAOS article.

There is still no evidence Lovejoy attended this public lecture series. The correct use is environmental and curricular:

> **In spring 1896, during Lovejoy's Harvard graduate period, Lanman was publicly presenting Sanskrit/Pāli, Sāṃkhya/Vedānta and Buddhist textual transmission as one integrated problem field.**

---

## 5. Harvard's Pāli-Buddhist production network was material, not merely curricular

Also in **1896**, Henry Clarke Warren's *Buddhism in Translations* appeared as **Harvard Oriental Series, volume III**, edited by Charles Rockwell Lanman.

The title page identifies:

- Harvard Oriental Series, edited by Lanman;
- Cambridge, published by Harvard University, 1896;
- translations from Buddhist sacred material;
- Warren's dedication: `To My Friend and Teacher, Charles R. Lanman`.

The work's contents and bibliographic records make clear that it is a major English-language translation/selection project from the original **Pāli**.

Primary digital witness:

https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Translations

Full scan / bibliographic control:

https://books.google.com/books/about/Buddhism_in_Translations.html?id=CeIOAAAAYAAJ

This confirms that Lovejoy's immediate Harvard environment contained not only Sanskrit courses but an active publication network turning Pāli Buddhist texts into accessible scholarly objects.

Again, no direct Lovejoy reading claim is made. A source-level comparison between Warren and notebook 004 should be done separately before adding any transmission edge.

## 6. Babbitt's archive preserves exactly the early materials we would want — but it is not digital

HOLLIS's processing history for HUG 1185 maps several old call numbers into the current arrangement. Two are especially important:

- `HUG 1185.4 — Notes, graduate study` → moved to Biographical series;
- `HUG 1185.8 — Manuscript of Dhammapada` → moved to Writings and Lectures series.

The current finding aid also contains a **Student Materials, 1881–1893, 1933** subseries.

This is almost ideally targeted material for testing what Babbitt brought back from Paris:

- Pāli exercises/notes;
- Buddhist reading lists;
- Lévi/EPHE references;
- Dhammapada translation work;
- possible correspondence or course notes.

But HOLLIS explicitly reports:

`Digital content: 0`.

It also states that access to the collection requires donor permission.

Therefore this is **not** an immediate electronic-archive task. It should be retained as a later targeted Harvard archival request rather than allowed to stall the current digital Paris sweep.

## 7. Babbitt's Pāli interest persisted for decades

Babbitt's *The Dhammapada: translated from the Pāli with an essay on Buddha and the Occident* was published posthumously in 1936. Harvard's archive preserves the manuscript predecessor under the old HUG 1185.8 designation.

Bibliographic control:

https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/books/R100000136-I1970867909775190830

This does not tell us what Babbitt said to anyone in 1895–98. It does establish that his Paris Pāli study was not a disposable year-abroad episode; it remained a durable intellectual project.

That increases the plausibility that Babbitt's Paris training was part of his recognizable Harvard intellectual profile after his return, while still stopping short of proving a Lovejoy connection.

---

## 8. The transmission problem is now much narrower

Before this batch the possible routes by which Lovejoy could have learned to expect Pāli study with Lévi were:

- Lanman advice;
- printed Paris programmes;
- American students already in Paris;
- general scholarly reputation.

Babbitt adds a far more specific route:

`Babbitt studies Pāli/Buddhism at EPHE / Lévi`

→ `Babbitt returns to Harvard and is teaching there from 1894`

→ `Lovejoy arrives as Harvard graduate student in 1895`

→ `Lovejoy subsequently goes to Paris expecting Pāli/Buddhist study with Lévi`.

The middle arrow is **institutional co-presence**, not demonstrated communication.

The next question is therefore documentable and finite:

> **Is there an actual Babbitt–Lovejoy, Babbitt–Lanman–Lovejoy, or Harvard advising/curricular edge before 1898?**

Search targets:

1. Lovejoy's Harvard course/adviser records and letters for `Babbitt`;
2. Babbitt correspondence / student materials for `Lovejoy`, `Lanman`, `Lévi`, Paris, Pāli;
3. Lanman correspondence for Babbitt and Lovejoy in the same advising network;
4. Harvard course catalogues/graduate announcements for any course Lovejoy and Babbitt could have shared beyond simple campus co-presence.

Until one of these produces a document, write Babbitt as a **transmission control / plausible campus node**, never as Lovejoy's adviser.

---

## 9. Paper-level implication

A defensible formulation is now:

> **Lovejoy did not leave a Harvard world in which Pāli Buddhism and Sylvain Lévi were remote names. Irving Babbitt, who returned to Harvard's faculty in 1894, had himself pursued Pāli and Buddhist study at the École pratique des hautes études and is identified by later institutional scholarship as a student of Sylvain Lévi. At the same time Lanman's Harvard programme publicly connected Pāli and Sanskrit Buddhist literature with Sāṃkhya and Vedānta, while the Harvard Oriental Series was publishing translations from Pāli. This does not establish who recommended Lévi to Lovejoy, but it turns the route by which such an expectation could circulate from an abstract possibility into a concrete Harvard network problem.**

Compact form:

> **By 1895, Lévi's Pāli teaching already had a returned Harvard carrier.**

The compact formulation is analytical; it should not be read as proof that Babbitt personally transmitted the recommendation.

---

## Evidence cautions

1. HOLLIS directly confirms Babbitt's Pāli/Buddhist study at Écoles des Hautes-Études; the specific `under Sylvain Lévi` edge currently rests on scholarly institutional reconstruction.
2. Babbitt and Lovejoy were at Harvard simultaneously, but no contact/advising edge has been found.
3. Babbitt taught French; do not assume Lovejoy took his course.
4. Lanman's 1896 Buddhism lectures were public, but Lovejoy attendance is unproved.
5. Warren 1896 belongs to the Harvard Pāli production environment; Lovejoy reading is unproved.
6. Babbitt HUG 1185 has no digital content exposed in HOLLIS and requires donor permission; it is a later archival target, not a current electronic corpus.
