# Lovejoy on Locke — probabilistic/incomplete Chain as a borderline plenitude identity control

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **MEDIATED BORDERLINE CONTROL / DIRECT LOVEJOY FOOTNOTE IMAGE NOT YET LOCKED**

## Core result

The Locke case appears to provide a useful middle point between:

- Schelling: radical transformation but Lovejoy explicitly preserves the Chain's `essential character`;
- Whitehead: `principle of limitation` explicitly contrasted with `Infinite Fecundity`, crossing the plenitude identity boundary.

Reliable later scholarship quotes Lovejoy as noting that Locke **does not insist upon the a priori necessity of the plenitude and continuity of the chain**. Locke's own *Essay* passage presents the upward continuation of the series as `probable`, not certain.

If the Lovejoy footnote is accurately reproduced, then Lovejoy treats a substantially weakened commitment as still belonging to the Great-Chain/plenitude history.

Provisional result:

> **modal strength can weaken from necessary to probable without necessarily destroying plenitude-family identity; the stronger negative threshold appears to be reversal of the modal direction from realization/fullness to primitive selection/limitation.**

Because the exact Lovejoy footnote has not yet been directly image-locked, this conclusion remains mediated.

---

## I. Locke's own commitment is visibly weaker than strict plenitude

Locke's well-known *Essay* passage observes:

- apparent lack of gaps in the visible corporeal world;
- small steps between neighboring forms;
- reason to think species may continue upward toward divine perfection;
- explicitly conditional/probabilistic wording (`if it be probable`).

This is not the strongest rationalist thesis:

`every genuine/compossible possibility must exist by a priori necessity`.

It is closer to:

`observed gradation/continuity + divine wisdom/goodness`
→ `probable continuation/fullness beyond observation`.

The epistemic modality has changed.

---

## II. Lovejoy apparently retains Locke inside the Chain history

Later scholarship citing Lovejoy's Great Chain footnote reports his qualification that Locke does not insist upon the **a priori necessity** of plenitude/continuity and does not require their absolutely complete realization.

Lovejoy nevertheless discusses Locke as an important eighteenth-century carrier/popularizer of the Chain conception.

Thus the historical family apparently tolerates:

- weaker necessity;
- probabilistic assent;
- incomplete realization;
- empiricist rather than strictly rationalist warrant.

This is strong evidence, if the footnote transcription is accurate, that **epistemic/modal strength is not itself the identity-bearing invariant**.

---

## III. Three-point plenitude threshold

### A. Schelling — same despite major transformation

Preserved:
- generativeness;
- diversity-production;
- maximal fullness as goal.

Changed:
- static → temporal;
- simultaneous → progressive;
- top-down → bottom-up;
- permanent fullness → flying goal.

Lovejoy: `essential character` preserved.

### B. Locke — likely borderline same with weaker modality

Preserved:
- graded/continuous fullness tendency;
- positive expectation of richer series;
- no positive elevation of arbitrary selection/limitation.

Changed/weakened:
- necessity → probability;
- absolute completeness → weaker/incomplete commitment.

Lovejoy apparently retains the case inside the Chain history.

### C. Whitehead — contrast / nonidentity

Core reversal:
- fecundity/maximal realization
→ ultimate selection/particularization/limitation.

Lovejoy frames Whitehead as a `contrast` with the historical complex.

---

## IV. Revised invariant for plenitude

The plenitude identity-bearing core may therefore be thinner than:

`all possibilities necessarily exist`.

A better provisional invariant is:

> **a positive anti-selective pressure toward fullness/diversification/realization of possible forms.**

This pressure can vary in:

- epistemic status: necessary / probable;
- temporal mode: simultaneous / successive;
- degree of completion;
- theological rationale;
- metaphysical mechanism.

Identity is threatened when the orientation itself reverses:

`realization/fullness is the governing demand`
→ becomes
`selection/limitation is primitive and positive`.

---

## V. Why this matters for invariant thickness

The Locke case shows that the historian can thin away not only terminology and rationale but even **modal force**.

That makes Gate A more vulnerable but also more reconstructible.

For each unit, ask separately:

- **content direction** — what relation/order is asserted?;
- **modal force** — necessary, probable, possible, aspirational?;
- **temporal mode** — static, recurrent, developmental?;
- **scope/completeness** — total, partial, local?;
- **rationale** — theology, sufficient reason, empirical induction, aesthetics, etc.

Lovejoy may hold the first relatively invariant while allowing the others to shift.
