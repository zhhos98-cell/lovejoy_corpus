# Batch 85 — Lovejoy enters Jung through revision: 1916 `New Paths in Psychology` → 1917 enlarged `Psychology of the Unconscious Processes`

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: close the edition-history HOLD left by Batch 82 by directly comparing the first English collection of Jung's early essay, *Collected Papers on Analytical Psychology* (1916), chapter XIV `New Paths in Psychology`, with the substantially enlarged second-edition text prepared in 1917 and reprinted in 1920, chapter XIV `The Psychology of the Unconscious Processes`.

## Core result

The Lovejoy passage can now be located to Jung's **revision layer**, not to the 1916 first-edition English state of `New Paths in Psychology`.

Direct collation gives the following:

### 1916 first English collection

Jung, *Collected Papers on Analytical Psychology* (London: Baillière, Tindall and Cox, 1916), translated/edited by Constance E. Long, chapter XIV `New Paths in Psychology`, pp.352 ff.

The complete chapter is transcribed on Wikisource from the 1916 scan. Full-text search of the chapter returns:

- `Lovejoy` — **no match**;
- `primitive energetics` — **no match**;
- `mana` — **no match**;
- `primordial` — **no match**.

The chapter does contain ordinary uses of `energy`, but its argumentative centre is Freud, neurosis, erotic conflict, dream-analysis and psychoanalytic technique. It does not contain the later Robert Mayer → primordial image → dynamistic religion → Lovejoy sequence.

### revised second-edition text prepared in 1917

The second-edition text, preserved in the 1920 reprint, explicitly says in its foreword that the earlier essay had been limited to presenting an essential part of the psychological standpoint inaugurated by Freud and that recent developments required a **substantial enlargement**. The editor's and Jung's prefatory material is dated June 1917.

The enlarged chapter is retitled `The Psychology of the Unconscious Processes`.

In this revised state the new argument appears explicitly:

1. Jung treats the idea of energy and its conservation as a primordial image dormant in the absolute/collective unconscious;
2. he says this conclusion **compels** the investigator to prove that a similar primordial image really existed historically and remained effective for thousands of years;
3. he adduces geographically dispersed `dynamistic religions` as the historical evidence;
4. he distinguishes the power-conception from soul/spirit animism;
5. at precisely this proof-bearing point he names Lovejoy and adopts `primitive energetics`;
6. footnote [241] gives *The Monist*, vol. XVI, p.363.

The edition-level result is therefore strong:

> **Lovejoy is absent from the 1916 first-edition English `New Paths in Psychology` and present in the substantially enlarged second-edition text prepared in 1917.**

This allows a stronger statement than Batch 82's `secure by 1917`:

> **the Lovejoy reception belongs to Jung's revision/expansion of the essay, not to the first English state.**

The exact German first appearance still requires the 1912/1913 German textual states if we want to say `first inserted in 1917` with absolute edition-historical completeness. But between the two directly collated English-authorized states, the insertion is secure.

## 1. First-edition object is independently controlled

The Online Books Page, Open Library, Internet Archive and Wikisource independently identify the first English collection:

The 1916 Wikisource transcription derives from the Internet Archive scan and preserves chapter XIV as `New Paths in Psychology`.

The table of contents places the chapter at printed p.352.

This is not a secondary synopsis of the early essay; it is the first-edition textual object itself.

## 2. Direct negative collation: the Lovejoy packet is not in the 1916 chapter

The 1916 chapter can be searched propositionally rather than merely by citation name.

### Absent author/name

`Lovejoy` — no match.

### Absent technical label

`primitive energetics` — no match.

### Absent comparative-religion term

`mana` — no match.

### Absent later inferential architecture

`primordial` — no match.

The absence of the name alone could in principle miss an unattributed conceptual borrowing. The combined absence of the distinctive label, the mana/dynamism packet, and the primordial-image proof sequence is much stronger.

The 1916 chapter's use of `energy` concerns libido, civilization, sexual restraint and surplus emotional/vital energy. It is not the later historical proof of a universal magical-power image.

Thus the negative claim is textual, not bibliographic:

> **the local argument into which Lovejoy will later be inserted is itself absent in 1916.**

---

## 3. The second edition announces its own enlargement

The 1920 reprint preserves the prefatory matter of the second edition.

Constance Long's editor's preface is dated June 1917. Jung's own prefatory text is also dated June 1917.

The foreword to the enlarged chapter says, in substance:

- the earlier essay had been limited to an essential part of Freud's standpoint;
- recent changes in understanding the unconscious made a new edition necessary;
- the essay therefore underwent a **substantial enlargement**.

The retitling is itself significant:

`New Paths in Psychology`

→ `The Psychology of the Unconscious Processes`.

The Lovejoy material belongs to this enlarged architecture, whose new problems include collective/absolute unconscious, primordial images, psychological types, and a broader history of symbolic/psychic forms.

---

## 4. Lovejoy is inserted at the exact point where Jung needs historical proof

The revised passage gives an unusually clean sequence.

Jung first proposes that the scientific idea of energy/conservation corresponds to a primordial image in the absolute unconscious.

He then explicitly raises the proof burden:

> if that psychological genealogy is true, a similar image must be shown to have existed in the history of the human mind and to have remained effective across millennia.

Only then does he invoke comparative religion and Lovejoy.

The reception function is therefore not:

> `interesting analogy: primitive peoples also have energy ideas`.

It is:

> `psychological genealogy proposed` → **historical existence must be independently demonstrated** → `dynamistic-religion evidence` → `Lovejoy as named witness/category`.

That proof-bearing placement was established in Batch 82. Batch 85 now shows that **the placement itself is a product of revision**.

---

## 5. Revision changes the evidentiary job of comparative religion

In the 1916 `New Paths` essay comparative-historical religion is not performing the Lovejoyan proof function.

In the enlarged 1917 state, comparative religion becomes an independent historical archive for a psychological proposition about the collective unconscious.

This is a major functional change:

### early state

`psychoanalytic clinical exposition`

→ Freud / neurosis / erotic conflict / dreams / therapeutic method.

### enlarged state

`broader theory of unconscious`

→ collective/absolute unconscious and primordial images

→ modern energy idea requires a historical genealogy

→ comparative religion becomes **warrant archive**

→ Lovejoy's `primitive energetics` enters as evidence.

So the reception of Lovejoy should be described compositionally:

> **Jung did not merely add a citation to an existing discussion. He recruited Lovejoy while creating a new inferential layer in the essay.**

This is stronger than citation chronology alone.

## 7. Stronger reception chronology

The chronology can now be coded more precisely.

- **1916 first English edition:** Lovejoy packet absent from `New Paths in Psychology`.
- **June 1917 revised second-edition state:** substantial enlargement explicitly announced; Lovejoy appears in the expanded `Psychology of the Unconscious Processes` as proof-bearing historical authority.
- **1920:** reprint preserves the second-edition text and Lovejoy footnote.

The remaining German-edition question is narrower:

- whether the Lovejoy passage first appeared in a German revision in 1916/1917 before the English second edition;
- the original `Neue Bahnen der Psychologie` publication year is bibliographically given as 1912 in modern CW editorial apparatus, whereas the English second-edition foreword says the essay was `originally written in 1913`.

This discrepancy should not be silently harmonized. For present purposes, the two directly collated English textual states are sufficient to establish revision-layer insertion.

---

## 8. Historiographic payoff

A publication-ready sentence is now possible:

> **Lovejoy entered Jung's analytical psychology through revision. The 1916 first English `New Paths in Psychology` contains neither Lovejoy nor the later mana/`primitive energetics` proof sequence. In the substantially enlarged second-edition text prepared in 1917, however, Jung's new theory of primordial images generated a new historical burden of proof; at precisely that point comparative `dynamistic religions` and Lovejoy's `primitive energetics` were inserted as evidence. The citation therefore marks not simply reception of a concept but the construction of a new evidentiary layer in Jung's argument.**

A shorter formula:

> **Lovejoy is a revision-layer source in Jung.**

5. Write `inserted between the 1916 first-English state and the revised 1917 state` or `belongs to the 1917 revision layer`; avoid claiming absolute first appearance until the relevant German states are directly collated.
6. The 1912-versus-1913 dating discrepancy between modern CW editorial metadata and Jung's English-edition foreword remains unresolved and should be preserved as such.
7. `revision-layer source`, `warrant archive`, and `new evidentiary layer` are our analytical formulations.
