# Batch 58 — 005 × Mary Kingsley: exact pages, spirit-mediated suhman versus intrinsic efficacy

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: extend Batch 57’s exact-page source-transformation method from Trumbull to Mary H. Kingsley, *Travels in West Africa* (1897), and test the result against Hubert–Mauss’s independent use of the same book.

## Core result

Direct visual reinspection of notebook `MS38_004_001_061_005` resolves two Kingsley page references that were previously provisional:

- PDF p.89 / MS p.181: **`Kingsley, Travels of W. Afr. 511`** — high confidence.
- PDF p.90 / MS p.183: **`Miss Kingsley 525`** — high confidence.

The exact pages materially sharpen the argument.

Kingsley p.511 describes the **suhman** as an object into which the spirit of Sasabonsum is called to enter. Once inhabited, it can produce charms; a suhman from which the spirit has departed is described as “dead.” Lovejoy cites that exact page and then immediately makes a classificatory cut: an **amulet is entirely distinct**, because there need be **“no spiritualistic idea”** in the case. He therefore uses a spirit-mediated Kingsley case as a foil against which to construct a second class of object whose efficacy is intrinsic rather than dependent on an indwelling spirit.

Kingsley p.525 belongs to the discussion of a West-African **apparition of blood** and the wider problem of local ideas about blood. Lovejoy cites that page while asking whether blood itself can be an independent causal factor rather than merely a sign of a spirit, sacrifice, or sympathetic relation.

The resulting microhistory is:

> **same book → exact pages → explicit causal reclassification.**

More specifically:

> **Kingsley’s spirit-mediated efficacy becomes one pole of Lovejoy’s distinction; Lovejoy then creates an analytically separate class of intrinsic efficacy.**

This is direct Lovejoy → Kingsley textual uptake at exact-page level. It does not establish Kingsley as the origin of Lovejoy’s distinction, and it does not imply Lovejoy accepted Kingsley’s own conceptual categories unchanged.

## 2. Kingsley p.511: the source case is explicitly spirit-mediated

The unabridged first-edition text on p.511 is unusually diagnostic.

Kingsley explains that a person seeking a suhman prepares an object and then calls on the spirit of **Sasabonsum to enter it**. If a spirit enters, the object has become a suhman. The owner can then obtain charms “by the agency of his suhman,” including charms protecting houses and persons. Kingsley further says that a failed or abandoned suhman can be described as “dead” because **the spirit has left it**.

Primary route:

- Mary H. Kingsley, *Travels in West Africa* (1897), Chapter XXII, unabridged Wikisource transcription:
  `https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Travels_in_West_Africa,_Congo_Fran%C3%A7ais,_Corisco_and_Cameroons/Chapter_22`
- First-edition index confirms `Sasabonsum, 509, 511` and `Suhman (tutelary deity), 510, 511`.

The causal bearer in Kingsley’s p.511 case is therefore explicit:

`prepared object → indwelling/entered spirit → efficacy of suhman → derivative charms`.

That is exactly the kind of case Lovejoy needs if he is about to distinguish another type of efficacy not requiring a spirit.

## 3. Lovejoy does not simply copy Kingsley: he uses p.511 as a negative control

The most important point is the direction of the transformation.

Kingsley does **not** supply Lovejoy’s proposition that an amulet is non-spiritualistic. In the p.511 suhman passage her explanatory language is explicitly spiritualistic: spirit enters, remains, leaves, and mediates power.

Lovejoy’s move is therefore comparative and contrastive:

`Kingsley p.511: spirit inhabits suhman`  
→ `Lovejoy: this is one causal class`  
→ `amulet/charm may constitute another class`  
→ `no indwelling spirit required`  
→ `efficacy may be intrinsic to the object`.

This is stronger evidence for Lovejoy’s method than a case in which source and notebook merely agree. The source gives him an especially clear example of **spirit-mediated efficacy**, and the notebook uses it to mark the boundary of a class it refuses to collapse into all charms/amulets.

Compact formulation:

> **Kingsley provides the positive spirit case; Lovejoy turns it into the boundary condition for a non-spirit class.**

Or:

> **citation becomes contrast.**

## 5. Kingsley p.525: blood apparition and an explicit unresolved causal problem

The unabridged Chapter XXIII makes the notebook citation intelligible.

Kingsley recounts an Igalwa/Bantu case in which blood appears throughout a woman’s house and is interpreted locally as “blood come before,” an omen preceding a later death. She then generalizes cautiously that certain African ideas about blood puzzle her and gives another case concerning aversion to bloodshed in judicial execution. She explicitly warns that the underlying idea remains difficult to recover.

The first-edition index independently maps:

- `Blood, an apparition of, 522, 523`;
- `ideas among the W. African tribes with regard to, 447, 525`.

Primary route:

- `https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Travels_in_West_Africa,_Congo_Fran%C3%A7ais,_Corisco_and_Cameroons/Chapter_23`
- relevant run begins with the blood-apparition narrative and continues into the p.525 discussion of West-African ideas about blood.

The notebook therefore cites p.525 at precisely the point where Kingsley herself has moved from describing a striking phenomenon to an unresolved problem of what blood is imagined to **do**.

Lovejoy then places the case inside his own mechanism field. His surrounding note asks whether sympathetic magic explains the phenomenon completely or whether the supposed potency of blood must be treated as an independent factor. Because the manuscript wording is abbreviated, the strongest safe formulation is:

> **Lovejoy uses Kingsley’s blood material while reopening the causal bearer: spirit, sympathy, and intrinsic blood efficacy remain analytically separable possibilities.**

Do not claim that Kingsley herself teaches an impersonal-energy theory here.

## 6. Hubert–Mauss use the same book, but a different exact page

The comparison with Hubert and Mauss can now be made at exact-page level rather than merely at book-title level.

In the 1899 *Essai sur la nature et la fonction du sacrifice*, Hubert and Mauss cite:

> **Miss Kingsley, *Travels in West Africa*, p.454**

in the footnote on the red painting/anointing of idols, immediately alongside **Marillier, RHR 1898, I, p.222**.

Their citation is visible in the 1909 reprint at Wikisource:

`https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Hubert,_Mauss_-_M%C3%A9langes_d%E2%80%99histoire_des_religions,_1909.djvu/102`

Kingsley p.454 itself describes:

- idols as things in which spirits reside or which spirits haunt;
- charms/plants as residences of spirits;
- resident spirits placated with food and sacrifice;
- in emergencies, fowl/goat/sheep blood placed at the fetish hut and village gateways.

Primary Kingsley page:

`https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Travels_in_West_Africa,_Congo_Fran%C3%A7ais,_Corisco_and_Cameroons_(IA_travelsinwestafr00kingrich).pdf/496`

Thus the exact extraction pattern is now demonstrably non-identical:

- **Hubert–Mauss → Kingsley p.454:** spirit residence + sacrifice + sacrificial blood;
- **Lovejoy → Kingsley p.511:** spirit-inhabited suhman, used as foil for non-spiritualistic amulet;
- **Lovejoy → Kingsley p.525:** blood apparition / blood ideas, reopened as a problem of causal bearer.

This upgrades Batch 56’s `bibliographic convergence without extract identity` from a provisional inference to a directly demonstrated case for Kingsley.

## 7. Same book, different pages, different causal bearer

The Kingsley audit gives a second source-transformation microhistory after Trumbull, but of a different kind.

### Trumbull (Batch 57)

Lovejoy follows a sequential run of source pages and retains the examples while changing their explanatory function:

`blood-life/covenant → curative/protective/transferable efficacy`.

### Kingsley (Batch 58)

Lovejoy selects exact cases that allow an explicit causal contrast:

`spirit-inhabited object → boundary case`  
`versus`  
`amulet/charm → efficacy without spirit`.

The p.525 blood case then widens the same distinction by asking whether blood’s potency can be isolated analytically from spirit-action and sympathetic relation.

The common operation across Trumbull and Kingsley is therefore not merely skeptical pluralism. It is more precise:

> **Lovejoy extracts a source proposition, identifies its causal bearer, and then withdraws the source category’s right to govern neighboring cases.**

This is the same broader inference syntax already visible throughout 005:

`visible similarity admitted → diagnostic force withdrawn → causal bearer identified → cases redistributed by mechanism`.

## 8. Consequence for the Marillier / Hubert–Mauss comparison

The exact Kingsley pages make the shared documentary ecology model considerably stronger while simultaneously making a direct Mauss → Lovejoy dependence claim less necessary.

The three researchers can be shown to inhabit a common bibliographic/problem field without selecting the same evidence in the same way:

- Marillier’s 1898 article is cited by Hubert–Mauss beside Kingsley p.454;
- Hubert–Mauss extract the spirit-residence/sacrificial-blood node;
- Lovejoy, studying under Marillier in 1898–99, independently records Kingsley p.511 and p.525 and uses them to distinguish spirit-mediated from intrinsic/material efficacy.

The right historical model is therefore:

> **a generative research environment with shared books and problem nodes, not a copied packet producing identical notes.**

This remains a contextual/documentary inference. There is no proof that Marillier assigned Kingsley p.511 or p.525 to Lovejoy or that Lovejoy learned those exact pages from Hubert–Mauss.

## 9. Relation to 1906 `Primitive Energetics`

The Kingsley result also tightens the notebook → publication transformation.

Notebook 005 distinguishes:

- efficacy because a spirit inhabits an object;
- sympathetic efficacy through relation to a person/body/name;
- efficacy intrinsic to blood, plants, stones, charms, or amulets;
- natural/medicinal and supernatural/magical efficacy as not yet sharply differentiated within the conceptual world being reconstructed.

The 1906 article later abstracts an impersonal, communicable, quasi-mechanical category of efficacy and explicitly rejects Marett’s definition of the fundamental notion as merely the supernormal. The Kingsley pages show one concrete empirical workshop in which that distinction is being made before publication.

Again, the claim must remain developmental rather than genetic:

> **Kingsley does not “cause” Primitive Energetics; 005 shows Lovejoy using Kingsley to populate and police one of the distinctions that the 1906 causal category later generalizes.**

A strong source-microhistory paragraph now available:

> **Lovejoy’s use of Mary Kingsley reveals that citation in the Paris notebook was not passive extraction. On manuscript p.181 he points specifically to p.511 of _Travels in West Africa_, where Kingsley describes the suhman as an object made efficacious by the entry and continued presence of a spirit. Lovejoy immediately turns the case into a classificatory boundary: an amulet, he notes, is “entirely distinct,” since “there is no spiritualistic idea here.” On the next manuscript page he cites Kingsley p.525, where an apparition of blood opens onto a broader uncertainty about West-African ideas of blood, and asks whether blood’s supposed potency must be treated as an independent causal factor. Compact formulation:

> **same book, different pages, different causal bearer.**
