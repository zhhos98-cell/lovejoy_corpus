## II. Broad political-philosophy jurisdiction is direct

The opening states that the journal fills a long-standing need for a political-philosophy journal that is:

- broad in scope;
- international in coverage.

It immediately connects that need to dialogue as essential to political philosophy.

This is a corporate field/jurisdiction claim, not a manifesto for one historiographical method.

Compact distinction:

`JOURNAL JURISDICTION = POLITICAL PHILOSOPHY / DIALOGUE`

rather than

`JOURNAL JURISDICTION = ONE HISTORICAL SCHOOL`.

This directly complements the May 1973 `From the Editors` p.131 language about an actual need for an international journal of political philosophy.

## IV. The journal pre-allocates heterogeneous article roles

The inaugural statement says each issue will contain essays in different areas, explicitly naming:

- history of political thought;
- modern theory;
- conceptual analysis;
- polemic argumentation.

It also describes:

- a graduate-student article section;
- occasional single-topic special issues;
- `Review Discussions` with several contributors representing different points of view;
- `Books-in-Review`;
- invited author self-reviews/reassessments;
- a bibliographic listing of political-theory articles in other major journals.

This is a remarkably concrete **editorial architecture of heterogeneity**.

The journal was designed not merely to print diverse topics but to support different scholarly operations:

`HISTORICAL`
+
`CONTEMPORARY / MODERN THEORY`
+
`CONCEPTUAL`
+
`POLEMICAL`
+
`REVIEW / REPLY`
+
`BIBLIOGRAPHIC INFRASTRUCTURE`.

This supplies a direct programme-level antecedent to the 1975 editors' later difficulty classifying submissions as historical/modern/methodological/conceptual.

The two documents can now be paired as:

`1973 DECLARED MULTI-ROLE ARCHITECTURE`
-> `1975 EXECUTED CLASSIFICATION AMBIGUITY`.

Do not treat this as proof that the 1975 taxonomy mechanically derived from the 1973 wording; it is programme/execution congruence unless a direct editorial-development record proves causation.

## V. Dialogue is materially built into issue format

The statement does not leave `dialogue` at the level of aspiration.

Its proposed formats include:

- review discussions with multiple points of view;
- authors reviewing/reassessing their own books in response to criticism;
- polemical argumentation;
- special issues;
- direct correspondence with section editors.

The first issue itself already contains a comment/rejoinder sequence around Oppenheim/Held and another comment sequence around Kelly/Ollman.

Thus the venue architecture is consistent with:

`DIALOGUE AS PROGRAMME`
+
`DIALOGUE AS ARTICLE FORMAT`.

This gives a stronger institutional basis for interpreting the 1973 Skinner -> 1974 Pateman exchange and the August 1974 Skinner symposium as part of a venue designed to carry controversy, rather than as exceptional departures from the journal's normal form.

Again:

`FORMAT AFFORDANCE`
!= `EDITORIAL CAUSE OF A SPECIFIC DEBATE`.
