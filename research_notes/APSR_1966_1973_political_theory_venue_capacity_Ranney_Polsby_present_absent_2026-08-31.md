# APSR 1966–1973: political-theory venue capacity, Ranney–Polsby transition, and `Present and Absent`

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT 1972 EDITORIAL CENSUS FULL-PDF/PAGE-IMAGE CONTROL / RANNEY PROCESS + ARTICLE EXAMPLES DIRECT / RANNEY LATER ORAL-HISTORY SELF-REPORT CONTROL / ARTICLE-CAPACITY CLAIM DIFFERENTIATED FROM DISCRIMINATION CLAIM**

## Core result

The 1973 launch rationale for *Political Theory* can now be checked against the flagship political-science journal rather than repeated as an actor grievance.

The result is neither `APSR excluded political theory` nor `there was no publication-capacity problem`.

The strongest direct execution evidence is the APSR editors' own June 1972 comment, `Present and Absent`, which audits Volume 65 (1971). It records **46 articles**, of which the editors classify only **three** as `political philosophy`; by contrast **35** contain at least one table or figure and **13** meet their operational definition of `formal theory`. The editors themselves call political philosophy and several older areas weakly represented and worry that persistent imbalance could leave the *Review* cut off from important intellectual currents.

At the same time, APSR retained political-theory content, a durable review infrastructure explicitly titled `Political Theory, History of Political Thought, and Methodology`, and accepted/published individual theory/history-of-thought articles. Most strikingly, Walter Odajnyk — co-founder/editor of *Political Theory* — already had `The Political Ideas of C. G. Jung` accepted by APSR in 1972 and published in March 1973.

Therefore the safest historical proposition is:

> **The early-*Political Theory* publication-access complaint had an observable venue-capacity referent, but the evidence supports uneven research-article representation and role allocation rather than categorical exclusion or a demonstrated anti-theory rejection policy.**

Core firewalls:

`THEORY PRESENCE != ADEQUATE ARTICLE CAPACITY`;

`LOW REPRESENTATION != PROVEN EDITORIAL DISCRIMINATION`;

`REVIEW INFRASTRUCTURE != RESEARCH-ARTICLE SPACE`;

`INDIVIDUAL SUCCESSFUL PUBLICATION != ABSENCE OF FIELD-LEVEL CAPACITY PROBLEM`;

`ACTOR GRIEVANCE != OBSERVED VENUE MIX != EDITORIAL CAUSE`.

---

## I. Why this test was necessary

A contemporary March 1973 report on the launch of *Political Theory* attributes to Robert Lamb and Walter Odajnyk a publication-access rationale. The article says some theorists complained that established journals discriminated against their work, while others had trouble because political-science journals printed only a very small number of theory pieces; the new journal would provide a broad platform.

Companion dossier:

`research_notes/Political_Theory_1973_launch_press_publication_access_carrier_2026-08-31.md`.

That rhetoric cannot by itself establish what APSR actually published or why.

The present control therefore separates three propositions:

1. `ACTOR PERCEPTION / GRIEVANCE` — what theorists or founders said about existing journals;
2. `OBSERVED VENUE CAPACITY / CONTENT MIX` — what the flagship journal actually carried;
3. `EDITORIAL CAUSATION / DISCRIMINATION` — why the observed mix arose.

Only proposition 2 can be tested directly from published issue composition. Proposition 3 requires editorial/submission/referee evidence and must not be inferred from low counts alone.

---

## II. Ranney-era APSR did not simply eliminate theory

Austin Ranney succeeded to the APSR managing editorship in the mid-1960s. The late-1960s journal continued to publish material that cannot be reduced to behavioral empirical work.

Direct examples include:

- Arthur L. Kalleberg, `Concept Formation in Normative and Empirical Studies: Toward Reconciliation in Political Theory`, *APSR* 63.1 (1969), 26–39;
- Sheldon S. Wolin, `Political Theory as a Vocation`, *APSR* 63.4 (1969), 1062–1082;
- Giovanni Sartori, `Concept Misformation in Comparative Politics`, *APSR* 64.4 (1970), 1033–1053;
- later, Eugene F. Miller, `Positivism, Historicism, and Political Inquiry`, *APSR* 66.3 (1972), 796–817;
- Walter Odajnyk, `The Political Ideas of C. G. Jung`, *APSR* 67.1 (1973), 142–152;
- Lawrence A. Scaff, `Max Weber's Politics and Political Education`, *APSR* 67.1 (1973), 128–141.

Wolin's article is especially important institutionally: it developed from a paper delivered in September 1968 before the Conference for the Study of Political Thought. Thus a CSPT-linked critique of the discipline could itself travel into APSR publication.

The safe control is therefore:

`CSPT / THEORY CRITIQUE OF POLITICAL SCIENCE`
!= `AUTOMATIC EXCLUSION FROM APSR`.

The problem must be tested as capacity, frequency, role and selection process rather than simple presence/absence.

---

## III. Political theory had a durable APSR review infrastructure

Across the late 1960s and early 1970s APSR repeatedly used a book-review / book-note category explicitly titled:

`Political Theory, History of Political Thought, and Methodology`.

Direct issue records show the category in, among others:

- March 1967;
- June and December 1969;
- March/June/December 1970;
- 1972 Book Reviews and Essays;
- December 1973.

This is important because it demonstrates continued recognized jurisdiction for political theory and history of political thought inside the flagship journal.

But it also forces a role distinction:

`FIELD RECOGNIZED IN REVIEW INFRASTRUCTURE`
!= `FIELD HAS COMPARABLE RESEARCH-ARTICLE CAPACITY`.

A journal can maintain bibliographic/review coverage of a domain while publishing relatively few research articles from that domain.

Do not count review-section presence as equivalent to article-space allocation.

## IV. `Present and Absent` gives direct actor-level execution statistics

Primary source:

`Editorial Comment: Present and Absent`, *American Political Science Review* 66.2 (June 1972), 585–586, followed by `Articles Accepted for Future Publication`, p.587.

Direct Cambridge PDF and page images checked.

The editors audit the previous full volume, Volume 65 (1971).

They report:

- **46 articles** total;
- **35** with at least one table or figure;
- **19** dealing with political psychology, mass voting behavior, value/attitude acquisition, or low-/middle-level political participation;
- **20** closely tied to U.S. data;
- **12** explicitly about one or more foreign countries;
- **14** not classifiable geographically on that basis;
- **13** classified as `formal theory` by an intentionally operational criterion: Greek letters or algebraic expressions beyond formulas used for statistical tests.

The same audit then identifies weak representation in more traditional areas:

- public law: one American + one comparative;
- public administration / organization theory: one;
- policy analysis: three by a generous count;
- **political philosophy: three articles**;
- elite politics: two;
- international politics: two.

The important source-state point is that **the editors themselves**, not a later historian, identify these as notable absences / weak representation.

This directly licenses:

`POLITICAL PHILOSOPHY = LOW ARTICLE REPRESENTATION IN APSR VOL.65`.

It does not directly license:

`POLITICAL PHILOSOPHY MANUSCRIPTS WERE SYSTEMATICALLY REJECTED BECAUSE THEY WERE POLITICAL PHILOSOPHY`.

---

## V. The editors themselves refuse a single causal explanation

`Present and Absent` is methodologically unusually useful because it lists competing explanations rather than turning the distribution into an accusation.

The editors consider several possibilities:

- scholars may simply be producing less work in some older areas;
- good manuscripts may be going to strong specialist journals such as *Public Administration Review*, *World Politics*, *Ethics*, or law journals;
- standards might conceivably be higher in old established areas than in newer areas;
- short-run imbalance may be inevitable or even useful.

They explicitly say they have no sure way to know whether the published sample accurately reflects the best current intellectual preoccupations of political scientists.

This is direct actor-level evidence for an evidence ceiling:

`PUBLISHED DISTRIBUTION`
!= `SUBMISSION DISTRIBUTION`
!= `ACCEPTANCE RATE BY FIELD`
!= `EDITORIAL DISCRIMINATION`.

The source does, however, establish that APSR's own editors regarded the imbalance as something worth publicly correcting or guarding against.

---

## VI. The journal identifies a publication -> submission feedback mechanism

The opening of `Present and Absent` is especially important for institutional history.

The editors argue from casual observation that authors watch what appears to get through the editorial process and may infer editor preferences from the published journal. If this deters good but unlike manuscripts from being submitted, the *Review* loses access to them.

This is an actor-level **mechanism hypothesis**:

`PUBLISHED CONTENT MIX`
-> `PERCEIVED EDITORIAL PREFERENCE`
-> `FUTURE SUBMISSION MIX`.

It is not a demonstrated causal estimate.

Still, it changes how the 1973 *Political Theory* launch grievance should be interpreted. A sense that established journals were unavailable could be generated partly by the **visible output profile of the venue**, even without a formal exclusionary rule.

This supplies a plausible institutional mechanism for:

`LOW VISIBLE REPRESENTATION`
-> `SELF-SELECTION TOWARD SPECIALIST / NEW VENUES`.

Preserve:

`EDITORIAL MECHANISM HYPOTHESIS`
!= `PROVEN CAUSAL EFFECT FOR ANY INDIVIDUAL AUTHOR`.

## VII. The future-publication list demonstrates attempted correction / continuing permeability

The same June 1972 editorial package immediately follows its imbalance discussion with accepted future publications.

The list includes several items with obvious political-theory / history-of-thought / methodology relevance:

- Charles E. Butterworth, `Averroes: Politics and Opinion`;
- Eugene F. Miller, `Positivism, Historicism, and Political Inquiry`;
- Walter Odajnyk, `The Political Ideas of C. G. Jung`;
- Lawrence A. Scaff, `Max Weber's Politics and Political Education`;
- Donald D. Searing, Joel J. Schwartz, and Alden E. Lind, `Political Socialization and Political Belief Systems: An Essay on the Theoretical Relevance of Some Current Research`.

This matters because the `weak representation` diagnosis and the incoming theory/history-of-thought items coexist in the same editorial document.

Therefore:

`LOW 1971 REPRESENTATION`
!= `CLOSED DOOR`.

The editors' own metaphor is closer to keeping the venue open to more varieties of work.

## VIII. Odajnyk is a high-value counterexample to a personal-exclusion reading

Walter Odajnyk was, with Robert Lamb, a founder/editor of *Political Theory*.

Yet APSR's June 1972 accepted-future list already includes Odajnyk's `The Political Ideas of C. G. Jung`, which then appeared in *APSR* 67.1 (March 1973), 142–152.

The timing is unusually useful:

`1972 — ODAJNYK ARTICLE ACCEPTED BY APSR`

`FEB/MAR 1973 — POLITICAL THEORY LAUNCH + PUBLICATION-ACCESS RATIONALE`

`MAR 1973 — ODAJNYK APSR ARTICLE PUBLISHED`.

This directly blocks a tempting but false simplification:

`FOUNDER OF NEW THEORY JOURNAL`
!= `PERSONALLY EXCLUDED FROM APSR`.

Instead the new journal can be understood as an attempt to alter **aggregate carrier capacity, regularity, scope and jurisdiction**, even when individual founders could publish in the flagship journal.

Compact distinction:

`PERSONAL ACCESS`
!= `ADEQUATE FIELD INFRASTRUCTURE`.

## IX. Editorial succession matters: Ranney grievance and Polsby census are different states

The 1967 CSPT founding correspondence reconstructed by Feldman concerns anxiety about Austin Ranney's editorship and future publication access.

`Present and Absent`, by contrast, appears under the subsequent Nelson Polsby editorial regime and audits Volume 65 (1971).

Do not collapse these into one stable `APSR editorial policy`.

The chronological layers are:

1. **pre-/early Ranney transition, 1966–67** — political theorists express publication anxiety;
2. **Ranney execution, late 1960s–1970** — APSR still publishes political-theory/method pieces and maintains formal review procedures;
3. **Polsby execution, 1971–72** — editors publicly diagnose low political-philosophy representation in the immediately preceding volume and ask for broader submission flow;
4. **1973** — *Political Theory* launches as a dedicated broad/international platform while Odajnyk simultaneously appears in APSR.

Thus:

`1967 RANNEY-ERA FEAR`
!= `1971 POLSBY-ERA CONTENT MIX`.

The later data can show that a capacity imbalance remained visible; they cannot prove the 1967 feared mechanism caused it.

## X. Ranney's later oral-history self-report: preference existed, but the mechanism is more complicated than exclusion

A later Austin Ranney oral-history interview, published in *Political Science in America: Oral Histories of a Discipline* (University Press of Kentucky, 1991), provides retrospective actor testimony about his APSR editorship.

Ranney says in retrospect that the criticism that he **favored formal theory** was fair. He explains that he regarded formal theory as an exciting new venture worth encouraging.

In the same exchange, however, he rejects the claim that classical theorists were simply denied their fair share by editorial fiat. He recalls difficulty obtaining referee consensus on classical-theory manuscripts: readers he regarded as eminent political theorists often returned sharply divergent evaluations.

Evidence status:

`LATER ORAL-HISTORY ACTOR SELF-REPORT`.

This is valuable because it gives a concrete editorial mechanism compatible with the contemporary publication pattern:

`EDITORIAL BOOST TO NEW FORMAL THEORY`
+
`LOW / UNSTABLE REFEREE CONSENSUS FOR CLASSICAL THEORY`
-> potentially different acceptance environments.

But because the recollection was recorded long after the editorship, it should not replace contemporary submission/referee files.

It also does not establish that every classical-theory rejection followed this mechanism.

## XI. Revised interpretation of the 1967–73 publication-jurisdiction sequence

The evidence now supports a more exact sequence than `behavioralists expelled theory`:

`1967 CSPT FOUNDERS PERCEIVE PUBLICATION / DISCIPLINARY THREAT`
->
`APSR UNDER RANNEY REMAINS PERMEABLE BUT WITH EDITORIAL/REFEREE ASYMMETRIES`
->
`1971 APSR OUTPUT SHOWS ONLY 3/46 POLITICAL-PHILOSOPHY ARTICLES, WHILE FORMAL/QUANTITATIVE MODES ARE MUCH MORE VISIBLE`
->
`1972 APSR EDITORS PUBLICLY DIAGNOSE THE IMBALANCE AND WARN OF SUBMISSION FEEDBACK`
->
`1973 POLITICAL THEORY LAUNCHES A DEDICATED, SCHOOL-NONEXCLUSIVE, MULTI-ROLE CARRIER`.

This should be described as **carrier-capacity engineering under conditions of uneven disciplinary visibility**, not secession from a totally closed journal system.

A useful compact model is:

`PERMEABLE FLAGSHIP`
+
`UNEVEN ROLE / ARTICLE DISTRIBUTION`
+
`VISIBLE FEEDBACK INTO AUTHOR EXPECTATIONS`
+
`SPECIALIST-CARRIER CREATION`.

---

## XII. Relation to `embedded differentiation`

This APSR control strengthens rather than weakens the repo's `embedded differentiation` model.

Political theory did not need to become absolutely excluded from political science in order to develop its own communications infrastructure.

The direct evidence instead permits:

- continued publication in APSR;
- continued theory review infrastructure in APSR;
- continued institutional overlap;
- simultaneous perception that available article capacity and regular visibility were inadequate;
- creation of a dedicated journal with a deliberately pluralist category architecture.

Thus:

`DEDICATED INFRASTRUCTURE`
!= `TOTAL EXIT FROM PARENT DISCIPLINE`.

And:

`EMBEDDEDNESS`
!= `SATISFACTION WITH EXISTING CARRIER CAPACITY`.

## XIV. Evidence ceilings / next gate

### Directly established

- Volume 65 contained 46 articles and only three were classified by APSR editors as political philosophy;
- the same editors classified 13 as formal theory and 35 as containing at least one table/figure;
- editors themselves regarded several traditional areas as weakly represented;
- editors hypothesized a published-output -> inferred-preference -> submission feedback mechanism;
- APSR still carried political-theory research articles and an explicit political-theory/history-of-thought/methodology review category;
- theory/history/method items were already accepted for future publication in June 1972;
- Odajnyk's APSR article was accepted before *Political Theory* launched and published in March 1973.

### Not yet established

- field-specific submission counts under Ranney;
- field-specific acceptance/rejection rates;
- referee reports for classical/theory manuscripts;
- whether `political philosophy` was under-submitted, under-accepted, diverted to specialist journals, or some mixture;
- whether Richter's 1967 Ranney anxiety directly caused the 1970 journal initiative;
- whether any specific APSR rejection led to a submission to *Political Theory*.

Highest-value next archive/data gate is therefore **submission/referee-level APSR evidence**, not more article-title counting.
