## Research move

This batch continues the unfinished **French side of notebook 005**. The focus is the conceptual zone where 005 distinguishes custom, taboo, ritual sanction, social solidarity and strictly moral guilt, and later separates spirit-mediated efficacy from intrinsic / transferable magical power. Three Marillier texts now form the immediate French primary-source target set:

1. **1896 — `Sur le caractère religieux du tabou mélanésien`**;
2. **1900 — `Religion`, in *La Grande Encyclopédie*, vol. XXVIII, pp. 341–364**;
3. **1901 — `Notes sur la coutume, le tabou et l'obligation morale`**.

The 1896 item has now been bibliographically localized much more precisely and linked through Marcel Mauss's contemporary use of it. The 1900 article has a fully verified public-domain DjVu witness with exact item identity and likely scan window. The 1901 item now has a stable Sudoc record but its full text remains unresolved.

## 1. Marillier 1896 `Sur le caractère religieux du tabou mélanésien`: exact publication context resolved

Persée's Marillier authority record confirms the work as:

> Léon Marillier, *Sur le caractère religieux du tabou mélanésien*, Paris, 1896.

Pascal Le Maléfan's bibliographic reconstruction identifies the exact container and pagination:

> *Études de critique et d'histoire*, 2e série, **Bibliothèque de l'École des Hautes Études, Sciences religieuses**, vol. 7, part 2 (1896), **pp. 35–74**.

Independent Heidelberg catalogue evidence confirms the 1896 EPHE volume:

- *Études de critique et d'histoire*;
- Bibliothèque de l'École des Hautes Études, Section des Sciences Religieuses;
- volume 7;
- 1896;
- XIV + 400 pages.

The Online Books Page independently exposes this exact EPHE 1896 volume as having HathiTrust page images. Thus the obstacle is now page extraction, not bibliographic identification.

## 2. Mauss 1896 directly uses Marillier's taboo article in the same controversy that feeds 005

Marcel Mauss's two-part 1896 review `La religion et les origines du droit pénal d'après un livre récent` is available in a full electronic transcription through the Université du Québec collection, based on the original *Revue de l'histoire des religions* publication:

- vol. 34 (1896), pp. 269–295;
- vol. 35 (1896), pp. 31–60.

In the critical section on ritual sanctions, Mauss explicitly cites:

> L. Marillier, `Tabou mélanésien`, **pp. 53–56** of the offprint / *Études de critique et d'histoire religieuse* (1896), saying that most of the relevant Melanesian texts were collected there.

The substantive argument is extremely close to one of Lovejoy 005's key distinctions. Mauss argues that violation of a taboo can endanger the **existence of the clan**, triggering immediate collective/religious sanction. The causal logic is group danger / ritual solidarity, not necessarily individual moral guilt.

This gives a much tighter genealogy for 005 pp.16–20, especially Lovejoy's discussion of suicide, taboo/custom and clan solidarity:

> apparent punishment → ask whether the operative determinant is moral desert, ritual interdiction, social solidarity, or danger to the group.

The point is not that Lovejoy copied Mauss. The stronger historically grounded claim is that Lovejoy entered Marillier's 1898–99 seminar after the Marillier–Steinmetz–Mauss controversy had already produced a French analytical vocabulary for distinguishing **religious/social sanction from moral guilt**.

## 3. The EPHE publication series itself ties together the French intellectual ecology around Lovejoy

Persée's bibliographic notices for the Section des sciences religieuses identify the institutional series around the 1896 Marillier essay. The same series includes or subsequently includes:

- Maurice Vernes on Hebrew religion;
- Jean Réville on Christian origins / the Fourth Gospel;
- Sylvain Lévi on sacrifice in the Brāhmaṇas;
- Foucher on Buddhist iconography;
- later works on Assyrian magic and comparative religion.

This does not prove direct reading by Lovejoy. It does show that the texts behind his documented Paris teachers and adjacent Orientalist/comparative-religion work were being produced inside one identifiable EPHE publication infrastructure rather than in isolated disciplinary silos.

Until the text is obtained, the project should avoid claiming that Lovejoy's five-stage taxonomy is uniquely his own innovation. The 1901 title alone shows that Marillier was still explicitly formulating the custom–taboo–moral-obligation problem at the end of his career.

## 6. What can already be said about the 1900 mana passage — and what remains secondary

A modern history of the mana category reports that Marillier uses `mana` six times in the 1900 *Religion* article and gives the first occurrence at printed p.349, glossing it as the natural and supernatural gifts/powers attaching to a person and giving efficacy over persons and things.

This remains **secondary evidence pending direct facsimile capture**.

The importance is nevertheless high because a documented Marillier student, Nathan Söderblom, writes in 1901 of **`le mana de l'homme`**, explicitly attributing the expression to Marillier. The direct Söderblom primary text was already captured in batch 18.

Thus the French chain currently stands:

> Codrington 1891 → Marillier review/use 1892 → Marillier taboo/sanction analysis 1896 → Marillier `Religion` / mana 1900 → Söderblom explicit Marillier-derived `mana` 1901 → Lovejoy `Primitive Philosophy` 1906.

The missing step is direct capture of Marillier's 1892 and 1900 wording.

## 7. Strong near-contemporary relay for Marillier's theory of magic

The 1911 *Encyclopædia Britannica* article `Magic` explicitly attributes to **L. Marillier** a threefold classification:

1. magic of word/act, including mimetic rites and direct action on nature;
2. efficacy dependent on ritual qualification, ceremonial purity or initiation;
3. specially empowered persons (`mana`) whose words/presence can affect weather and fertility.

It further says that some magical rites act directly on natural processes while others work through a coerced god/spirit, and its bibliography points explicitly to Marillier's `Religion` in *La Grande Encyclopédie*.

This is a near-contemporary relay, not a substitute for the 1900 primary text. It maps remarkably closely onto 005's mechanism distinctions:

- p.64: direct action on natural forces;
- pp.82, 112–117: initiation/purity/qualification;
- pp.85–90: intrinsic efficacy versus indwelling spirit;
- pp.99–105: fecundating power transferred to crops;
- p.105: quasi-mechanical/magical rather than sacramental.

## 9. Why this matters for Lovejoy as Orientalist, not just for Marillier

The French material sharpens what Lovejoy was learning in Paris. His Orientalist/comparative-religion formation involved more than accumulating ethnographic examples. The immediate French debate was asking:

- Is a sanction moral, ritual, social or magical?
- Does efficacy inhere in a person/object or in an indwelling spirit?
- Does a rite act directly on nature or through a personal deity?
- How do taboo/custom and moral obligation become differentiated?
- When does a local term like `mana` become a comparative category of power/causation?

005 records Lovejoy repeatedly asking these exact kinds of questions. His 1906 paper then abstracts one of them — impersonal, transferable, quasi-mechanical efficacy — into a general theory of “primitive philosophy.”

The evidence now makes France a concrete intellectual and documentary setting for this transformation, rather than a generic biographical stop between Harvard and the United States.
