# Lovejoy 005 — Marillier 1894 page-addressed determinant reconstruction (Batch 205)

Date: 2026-08-26  
Status: synced  
Scope: `MS38_004_001_061_005`, especially PDF pp.3–30. Default forward chronological boundary = **1910**; later evidence is admitted only for explicit Buddhism / India / Sanskrit nodes.

## Core result

Batch 204 removed a broad originality claim from Lovejoy: the historical superposition of moral sorting upon older eschatological journey machinery was already present in Mauss 1896 inside the Steinmetz controversy.

Batch 205 pushes the correction one step further. Even though the complete Marillier 1893/94 `Survivance` pamphlet has still not been recovered page-by-page in a machine-readable direct transcription, **Mauss’s 1896 critique supplies page-addressed relays into Marillier’s memoir**. When those relays are combined with Marillier’s own direct 1893 EPHE summary, the constituent determinant classes that Lovejoy later serializes in 005 are already distributed across Marillier’s earlier programme:

- **social/status continuation** — direct Marillier 1893 official summary;
- **ritual performance** — Mauss 1896 explicitly cites Marillier p.44;
- **manner of death** — same Marillier p.44 relay;
- **moral differentiation / just vs criminal afterlife** — Mauss cites Marillier p.42 in the North-American discussion;
- **contact / imported moralization** — Mauss cites Marillier p.37 for contaminated cases;
- **continuation rather than compensation/reparation/punishment** — direct Marillier 1893 summary and direct 1898–99 seminar report.

This changes the likely intellectual allocation of Lovejoy 005 pp.3–20. The best current reconstruction is no longer:

> Marillier supplies a binary `continuation vs retribution`; Lovejoy invents the intermediate determinant taxonomy.

It is now:

> **Marillier’s earlier dossier already contains the distributed discriminanda; Lovejoy’s stronger contribution is to SERIALIZE them into an explicit working taxonomy, impose a stricter `desert` threshold, and repeatedly use that taxonomy to redistribute ambiguous cases.**

That distinction is narrower but substantially stronger.

---

## 1. Direct Marillier anchor: social rank is explicitly non-moral

Marillier’s official EPHE summary for 1893 states that the world of the dead resembles the world of the living, and that ranks there are marked **not by merit or moral value but by the rank occupied during life**. It continues: the other life is not a repair of the injustices of present life but the continuation of present life; survivance is not founded on moral conceptions.

This is already one full determinant class in Lovejoy’s later scheme:

> `post-mortem allocation by terrestrial/social status ≠ moral desert`.

Lovejoy 005 pp.3–5 and pp.20–25 sharpen and repeatedly test this proposition, but the status/desert contrast itself is directly upstream.

---

## 2. Marillier p.44 via Mauss: rites and manner of death are independent selectors

Mauss’s 1896 critical discussion says that differentiated post-mortem lives remain compatible with the continuation theory when either:

- present inequalities continue;
- **the fulfillment of rites** determines the condition of the dead;
- **the manner/kind of death** determines the nature of the shade’s future life.

The footnote attached to this sentence is explicit:

> `Marillier, loc. cit., p. 44.`

This page-addressed relay matters more than a generic statement that Mauss and Marillier shared an argument. It shows that Marillier’s own 1894 memoir contained evidence or analysis supporting the exact operation Lovejoy repeatedly performs in 005: a differentiated fate can be caused by ritual qualification or mode of death without becoming moral recompense.

### 005 correspondence

The notebook’s Greenland/drowning/childbirth material, later suicide cases, and repeated separation of ritual/manual causes from strictly moral guilt therefore cannot be assigned to Lovejoy as an invented intermediate category merely because Marillier’s compressed 1893 course summary omitted those details.

Current relation code:

> `MARILLIER p.44 RELAY → LOVEJOY SERIALIZATION / CASE TESTING`.

---

## 3. Marillier p.42 via Mauss: moral differentiation itself is inside the earlier dossier

Mauss begins the North-American part of his rebuttal by admitting that some Indigenous North-American groups did distinguish the post-mortem condition of the just and the criminal. His footnote is:

> `Voir L. Marillier, loc. cit., p. 42.`

Mauss then argues that autochthonous moral beliefs need not be primitive, and turns to Waitz / older survivals / moral superposition.

The evidentiary allocation must therefore be split carefully:

- **secure Marillier p.42 relay:** the North-American dossier contains differentiated destiny connected to moral character (`justes` / `criminels`);
- **Mauss/Waitz field-level extension:** indigenous/autochthonous ≠ primitive; older totemic and journey structures can receive later moral superposition.

This means Lovejoy’s `strictly moral` endpoint also has an upstream Marillier evidence class. What remains potentially Lovejoy-specific is not the existence of a moral endpoint but the decision to make **desert** the explicit diagnostic threshold against which every apparent punishment/reward is tested.

---

## 4. Marillier p.37 via Mauss: provenance criticism is not a Lovejoy add-on

In the same passage Mauss rejects many apparent counterexamples because of historical contact. He identifies Christian influence in a series of cases and Hindu/Buddhist influence in Nias / Java, including the Sanskrit term `naraka` as a borrowing marker. For the Christian-contamination cluster he explicitly cites:

> `Marillier, loc. cit., p. 37`.

Therefore the historical-provenance operation in 005 pp.19, 28–29 is not best described as Lovejoy independently adding source criticism to Marillier’s thesis. At minimum, page-addressed contemporary testimony locates contamination criticism inside Marillier’s own 1894 source base.

This is particularly important because 005’s later explicit Hindu-influence notes do not represent a new methodological principle; they are an application of a rule already embedded in the controversy.

## 6. What `half-moral` now means evidentially

Lovejoy’s p.20 `half-moral character` remains interesting, but its claim strength changes.

Before the page-addressed reconstruction, it was plausible to read `half-moral` as evidence that Lovejoy had independently inserted a new middle zone between Marillier’s non-moral continuation and full retribution.

Now the surrounding components are already upstream:

- ritual determinant ≠ moral;
- manner-of-death determinant ≠ moral;
- social rank ≠ moral;
- moral character can determine fate in some later/particular systems;
- imported moralization can produce mixed historical layers.

Therefore `half-moral` is more plausibly a **compression label for a transition-space already structurally available in the Marillier controversy**. It can still be Lovejoy’s own wording and can still matter as an act of systematization, but it should not carry a priority claim for inventing transitional moralization.

Publication-safe formulation:

> **Lovejoy’s `half-moral` notation is best read as a classificatory compression of a problem-space already distributed across Marillier’s survivance dossier and the Steinmetz controversy, unless a direct upstream equivalent is later recovered.**

---

## 7. The remaining Lovejoy-specific increment becomes method, not ingredient

The strongest candidate now has three linked parts.

### A. Serial explicitness

Lovejoy lays adjacent determinant types out as mutually discriminable alternatives rather than leaving them distributed across a long empirical memoir.

### B. A high threshold for the word `moral`

The notebook repeatedly asks whether an outcome is actually tied to **desert**, not merely whether an observer could describe it as reward or punishment.

### C. Repeated redistribution of ambiguous cases

Suicide, special deaths, journey obstacles, exclusion, bodily condition, ritual fidelity, clan injury and imported concepts are repeatedly moved from one candidate explanatory box to another until the operative selector is specified.

This is the methodological operation that later recurs in 005’s sacrifice / anthropophagy / efficacy sections and is consistent with the mechanism-level abstraction later visible in 1906 `Primitive Energetics`.

The paper claim should therefore increasingly be phrased as:

> **Lovejoy’s Paris notebook did not originate the individual distinctions between social, ritual, mode-of-death and moral determinants. It converted a dispersed comparative-religion controversy into a portable diagnostic procedure: identify the selector, demand evidence of desert before calling it moral, and reclassify formally similar outcomes by operative mechanism.**

---

## 8. Why this strengthens rather than weakens the larger paper

This correction removes an easy but fragile originality story and leaves a more historically interesting one.

Lovejoy is no longer positioned as inventing distinctions his Paris teacher and the surrounding controversy already possessed. Instead, the notebook becomes evidence for **epistemic compression**: a student/research participant takes distributed arguments, cases, objections and source-critical warnings and converts them into a compact reusable classificatory apparatus.

That is also materially plausible for a working notebook. The intellectual change occurs not at the level of isolated propositions but at the level of **arrangement, adjacency, test sequence and portability across domains**.

This is a stronger bridge to notebook 004 as well: the larger Lovejoy problem may increasingly concern how heterogeneous learned materials are reorganized into diagnostic sequences rather than how many individual ideas can be credited to him as original inventions.

That 004↔005 bridge still requires a separate non-duplicative control against earlier repository batches before being made a new claim.
