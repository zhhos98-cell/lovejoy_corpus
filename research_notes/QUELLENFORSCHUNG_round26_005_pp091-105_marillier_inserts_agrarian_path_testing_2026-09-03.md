## 7. p.103 — chronology objection is the first strong post-insert local analytical seam

p.103 moves from case accumulation to a branching historical problem linking:

- cultivated plants;
- domestic animals;
- agricultural/fecundative sacrifice;
- wild versus domestic victims;
- chronology of agriculture and animal domestication.

The current medium-high summary says Lovejoy detects a possible vicious circle if one assumes:

1. agricultural sacrifice begins with wild animals and later uses domestic animals;
2. domestication itself necessarily precedes agriculture.

He rejects the second premise as generally unsafe and uses African evidence to argue that cultivation can exist without draught animals or developed animal domestication.

### Field control

Late-nineteenth-century sociology/ethnology already debated the relation among hunting, pastoralism, agriculture and domestic animals. Herbert Spencer's 1898 *Principles of Sociology*, for example, surveys African societies where agriculture is carried on with hoes and without draught animals, while animal keeping and cultivation vary independently across groups.

Thus `agriculture can occur without animal labour` is not an isolated Lovejoy discovery.

### Lovejoy-local operation

What is more specific on p.103 is the **logical use of that ethnographic point to attack a sacrificial-development pathway**:

`PROPOSED SACRIFICE SEQUENCE`
+
`ASSUMED AGRICULTURE/DOMESTICATION CHRONOLOGY`
->
`CIRCULAR / UNSAFE INFERENCE`.

If the image supports the current argumentative structure, this is a strong A2 relation: Lovejoy is not merely noting African agriculture; he is using chronology external to ritual taxonomy to test whether a proposed genealogy is even historically possible.

Calibration: constituent chronology facts F/A0–A1; cross-domain path test provisional A2.

## 8. p.104 — human -> domestic-animal substitution is field-known; rejection of direct wild->domestic derivation is the local path claim

The page is W3 for:

- `the manner of the H.S. is in turn modified into sacr. of dom. an., dressed as a man & so on`;
- domestic-animal sacrifice `comes not fr. wild an. sac. directly, but by modification of H.S.`.

### Source field

Human sacrifice being replaced by an animal substitute is a standard nineteenth-century comparative-sacrifice proposition. Tylor explicitly gives cases where a domestic animal redeems or replaces a human victim. Therefore:

`HUMAN VICTIM -> ANIMAL SUBSTITUTE`

is not Lovejoy's theoretical invention.

### Local Lovejoy delta

The important p.104 claim is more constrained:

- he compares **alternative historical paths**;
- he rejects a direct `wild-animal sacrifice -> domestic-animal sacrifice` genealogy for the case/model at issue;
- he routes domestic-animal sacrifice through a modification of human sacrifice;
- the domestic animal is further assimilated to human form (`dressed as a man & so on`).

This is not simply victim substitution. It is a **path-selection / path-exclusion judgment** among source-available substitution possibilities.

Calibration:

- H.S. -> animal substitute: F/A0;
- `not directly wild-animal -> domestic-animal; instead via H.S.`: W3 / A2 local historical-path relation, absent a closer source with the same routing.

This survives Round 24's downgrade of p.69. The two pages should now be read asymmetrically:

- p.69 mobilizes a Robertson-Smith-owned animal->human counter-arrow;
- p.104 locally selects a human->domestic-animal route while excluding direct wild->domestic derivation.

The significance is **case-dependent path plurality**, not Lovejoy's invention of either substitution direction.
