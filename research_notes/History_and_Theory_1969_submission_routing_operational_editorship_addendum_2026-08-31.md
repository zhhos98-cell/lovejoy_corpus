# *History and Theory* 1969 submission routing and operational editorship: addendum

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **CONTEMPORARY MASTHEAD ROUTING DIRECTLY CONTROLLED / SKINNER-SPECIFIC HANDLING STILL OPEN**

## Core result

The *History and Theory* office-papers archive locator can now be combined with a contemporaneous routing rule from the 1969 front matter.

The printed masthead for volume 8 lists:

- George H. Nadel — `Editor`;
- Richard T. Vann — `Executive Editor`;
- Louis O. Mink — `Associate Editor`;
- Maurice Mandelbaum — member of the `Editorial Committee`.

Most importantly, it instructs authors/readers:

`Correspondence and Contributions: Executive Editor, History and Theory, c/o Wesleyan University Press, Wesleyan Station, Middletown, Connecticut 06457, USA.`

Therefore the formal incoming manuscript channel in the Skinner publication year was not an undifferentiated editorial board. It was explicitly routed through the **Executive Editor**, Richard T. Vann.

This produces a stronger S2b model:

`INCOMING MANUSCRIPT`
-> `EXECUTIVE EDITOR CHANNEL / VANN`
-> possible `INTERNAL READER MEMORANDUM / EDITORIAL CONSULTATION`
-> `DECISION / REVISION / SCHEDULING`.

The first routing edge is contemporaneously documented. The middle and later edges are only demonstrated generically for other submissions by the 1966 Mink memorandum recovered through Samuel James's archival citation. They remain OPEN for Skinner.

## I. Why the title/function distinction now matters

Wesleyan later institutional memory describes Richard Vann as the journal's editor from 1965 to 1993, while the 1969 masthead distinguishes Nadel=`Editor` and Vann=`Executive Editor`.

These are not contradictory if formal title, operational responsibility and later institutional shorthand differ.

The contemporary submission address gives strong evidence that Vann's `Executive Editor` role had real operational significance.

Hence preserve:

`FORMAL MASTHEAD TITLE`
!=
`OPERATIONAL SUBMISSION ROUTING`
!=
`LATER INSTITUTIONAL MEMORY`.

Do not use a generic `editor` label where the historical function matters.

## II. The 1966 office memorandum makes the middle stage plausible but not Skinner-specific

Samuel James 2010 directly cites Louis Mink's 13 June 1966 memorandum on Stanley Paluch's submitted paper from `History and Theory Papers, History and Theory Offices, Wesleyan University`.

That document class establishes that the journal's Wesleyan office could route a submitted manuscript into internal editorial memorandum form during precisely the years relevant to Skinner.

Thus:

`SUBMISSION -> INTERNAL MEMO`

is an empirically attested H&T workflow pattern.

But:

`H&T USED INTERNAL MEMOS IN 1966`
!=
`MINK WROTE A MEMO ON SKINNER`.

The Skinner file remains the archival target.

## III. Editorial intervention at H&T could be intellectually substantive

Hayden White later thanked Richard Vann, Louis Mink and George Nadel, explicitly identifying them as *History and Theory* editors who had pushed him, firmly but tolerantly, to pursue the kind of work collected in *Tropics of Discourse*.

This is not evidence about Skinner's submission. It is a high-value workflow control showing that this editorial group could exercise substantive intellectual pressure rather than merely administrative processing.

Therefore a surviving Skinner editorial file could plausibly contain meaningful comments. This raises the expected value of the archive search, without proving any such comments existed.

Firewall:

`SUBSTANTIVE EDITORIAL INTERVENTION DOCUMENTED FOR WHITE`
!=
`SUBSTANTIVE EDITORIAL INTERVENTION DOCUMENTED FOR SKINNER`.

## IV. Skinner's own publication testimony remains deliberately weak

In a direct interview Skinner says that he wrote `Meaning and Understanding` between 1966 and 1967 but had `a lot of difficulty getting it published`.

That proves publication delay/difficulty but does not identify:

- journals approached;
- number of rejections;
- dates of submission;
- reasons for rejection;
- date of H&T submission;
- whether the PSA 1968 performance preceded or followed H&T receipt;
- editorial readers;
- requested revisions.

Secondary sources claiming `several journals rejected it` remain below the direct-evidence ceiling until the underlying correspondence is located.

## V. Updated S2b evidence ladder

### Secure

1. Skinner manuscript existed by 1966–67.
2. Publication was difficult/delayed, by Skinner's own retrospective testimony.
3. H&T's 1969 incoming `Correspondence and Contributions` channel was the Executive Editor, Richard Vann.
4. H&T office papers preserve at least one 1966 internal memorandum on a named submission.
5. Mandelbaum separately read and critically commented on earlier Skinner drafts as a collegial reader.

### Open

1. exact date Skinner first submitted to H&T;
2. cover letter / manuscript title at submission;
3. whether H&T saw the pre-PSA or post-PSA version;
4. Vann's handling notes;
5. Mink memorandum;
6. Nadel involvement;
7. Mandelbaum formal referee role;
8. anonymous/other referee report;
9. revision request;
10. acceptance/scheduling correspondence.
