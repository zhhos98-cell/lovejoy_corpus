## Core result

The early reception of `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` was not transmission of a single intact theory. The paper broke into components that travelled at different rates and acquired different evidentiary jobs.

At minimum, reception distinguishes:

1. **substantive category** — impersonal / communicable / efficacious power;
2. **causal characterization** — regular, intelligible, quasi-mechanical efficacy / `primitive energetics`;
3. **evidentiary dossier** — linguistic, ethnographic and comparative materials used to support the category;
4. **proof architecture** — the rule that stronger propositions require stronger or independent warrants;
5. **epistemic status** — whether the comparative abstraction corresponds to an indigenous abstract `concept`, or is a modern analytical redescription.

Different readers retained and rejected different layers.

The strongest reception sequence is therefore:

> **disaggregation → reproof / warrant substitution → controversy-mediated recirculation → authorial feedback → revision-layer appropriation → epistemic re-audit.**

This is a more exact historical object than `Lovejoy influenced later mana theories`.

## I. Immediate reception is disaggregated, not all-or-nothing

### James H. Leuba, 1912

Leuba directly cites Lovejoy 1906 in *A Psychological Study of Religion* and treats the paper as a serious intervention in the preanimistic / impersonal-power problem.

He does not simply accept or reject the article.

He separates at least three propositions:

- existence of a non-personal power field such as Wakonda / Manitou / Mana — **accepted as a serious ethnographic category**;
- operation according to `quite regular and intelligible laws` — **contested**;
- description of the force as mechanical rather than magical — **downgraded/rejected**.

At the same time, Leuba explicitly recommends Lovejoy's article as a **good summary of the evidence**, alongside Irving King's chapter `The Mysterious Power`.

Thus one source can remain evidentially useful after one of its major theoretical conclusions has been rejected.

Reception formula:

`Lovejoy evidence dossier = RETAIN`

while

`Lovejoy mechanical-regularity thesis = RE-AUDIT / DOWNGRADE`.

This is the clearest early example of reception by **component-wise grading**.

---

## II. Leuba 1912 performs reproof by changing evidentiary channel

Leuba goes beyond criticizing Lovejoy's answer.

When he turns from the existence of non-personal power to the stronger problem of its psychological origin and possible priority over animism, he explicitly declines simply to reproduce the linguistic/historical evidence already summarized by Lovejoy and King.

Instead, the psychologist turns to the **child** to supplement anthropological data.

The evidentiary sequence becomes:

`ethnography / linguistic evidence`
→ establishes that non-personal-power beliefs exist

but

`psychological origin / priority over animism`
→ requires another warrant

therefore

`developmental psychology of causal cognition`
→ independent genetic control.

Leuba argues that a young child can respond to causal efficacy without first constructing a personal causal agent. Non-personal causation therefore need not be genetically derived from animistic personification.

The important historical point is not that Leuba consciously borrowed Lovejoy's method. That is unproved and unnecessary.

The publication-level structure is enough:

> **the proposition changes, so the evidentiary channel changes.**

Analytical shorthand:

> **ethnographic authority for existence; developmental psychology for genesis.**

This is reception as **reproof / warrant substitution**.

## III. James/Kallen 1911 shows packet assimilation

The posthumous *Some Problems of Philosophy* cites Lovejoy 1906 alongside Jastrow, Jevons, Frazer and Marett in a broad discussion of mana, mysterious energy, sympathetic magic and primitive philosophy.

The citation preserves Lovejoy as an authority, but the local use is compressed.

What remains visible:

- mana / mysterious energy;
- primitive magic / primitive philosophy;
- Lovejoy as one member of an authority packet.

What is not visible locally:

- `vera causa`;
- claim-relative source grading;
- distinction between concrete lexical occurrence and abstract semantic category;
- unequal regional evidentiary resolution;
- the 005→1906 reproof architecture.

This is a different reception type from Leuba:

> **the substantive conclusion survives while the warrant architecture disappears into a citation packet.**

### Authorship firewall

The 1911 book was prepared posthumously. Its prefatory history says Horace M. Kallen compared surviving typescripts and largely prepared the volume for press, with R. B. Perry also assisting.

Therefore the safe claim is:

> **the 1911 James/Kallen text cites Lovejoy.**

Do not assign the footnote personally to William James without manuscript/typescript control.

## IV. Marett–Jevons 1909 is an adjacent proof-threshold debate, not presently a Lovejoy reception node

The 1909 British debate around R. R. Marett's *Threshold of Religion* and F. B. Jevons's review is methodologically close to the Lovejoy problem.

The key distinction is:

`non-personal / preanimistic conception is analytically possible`

≠

`a historically prior preanimistic stage has been proved`.

Jevons presses the burden of historical proof; Edward Clodd's response acknowledges the difficulty of direct prehistoric evidence and relies instead on hypothesis and continuity.

In the searchable material audited in the original reception pass, Lovejoy was **not recovered as a named interlocutor**.

This is bounded negative evidence only.

It supports:

> the proof-sufficiency problem was live in the surrounding field.

It does not support:

- Marett never read Lovejoy;
- Jevons did not know the article;
- Lovejoy was deliberately ignored.

The node remains a period control rather than a direct reception edge.

## V. King 1910 enters a triangular reception environment

Irving King's *The Development of Religion* (1910), especially chapter VI `The Mysterious Power`, belongs to the same impersonal-power problem field.

A modern scholarly locator reports that King directly referred to Lovejoy and at p.163 argued that impersonal power is not `in itself a religious concept`.

However, the exact King p.163 passage and its footnote were not directly collated in the original batch chain.

Current status remains:

`Lovejoy 1906 → King 1910` = **SECONDARY-CONTROLLED / PRIMARY HOLD**.

Do not upgrade this edge until the King facsimile is closed.

What is directly secure is the later reception ecology around King.

## VI. King → Leuba → King: controversy-mediated recirculation

King's 1911 article `Some Problems in the Science of Religion` explicitly says reviews of his recent book had disclosed misunderstandings and identifies, among others:

`J. H. Leuba, Psychological Bulletin, vol. vii, p.301`.

This gives a direct bibliographic controversy sequence:

`King 1910 book`
→ `Leuba 1910 review`
→ `King 1911 restatement/reply`.

The content of Leuba's 1910 review was not directly recovered in the original batch chain.

Therefore:

- existence of Leuba review and King's reply relation = **DIRECT PRIMARY**;
- claim that Leuba 1910 discussed Lovejoy / primitive energetics = **HOLD**.

In 1912 Leuba then explicitly places **King chapter VI and Lovejoy 1906 side by side** as alternative good summaries of evidence on non-personal power, while independently re-auditing Lovejoy's stronger causal characterization.

This creates a triangular environment by 1912:

`Lovejoy 1906`
↘
`Leuba 1912`
↗
`King 1910 → Leuba 1910 review → King 1911 reply`.

The key mechanism is not simple citation but **controversy-mediated recirculation**.

A work can be decomposed into:

- evidence summary;
- theory;
- terminology;
- causal warrant;
- genetic/origin thesis.

Those components can then circulate through different textual genres — books, reviews, replies and later syntheses — with different fates.

## VII. Lovejoy 1914 turns reception into feedback

A direct bibliographic primary establishes:

Arthur O. Lovejoy, review of James H. Leuba, *A Psychological Study of Religion: Its Origin, Function and Future*, *International Journal of Ethics* 24.2 (January 1914), pp.216–220.

Thus the minimum secure sequence is:

`Lovejoy 1906`
→ `Leuba 1912 reads and criticizes Lovejoy`
→ `Lovejoy 1914 reviews Leuba`.

Daniel J. Wilson's annotated bibliography says that Lovejoy uses the review in part to correct a misconception by Leuba of Lovejoy's position in `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`.

But the exact review text had not been directly collated when the original reception batches were written.

Evidence ceiling:

- review existence / author / date / pages = **DIRECT PRIMARY**;
- that the review returns to Leuba's understanding of Lovejoy 1906 = **SECONDARY-CONTROLLED, HIGH**;
- exact proposition corrected = **HOLD**;
- exact Lovejoy wording = **HOLD**.

This distinction must remain explicit.

Even before the exact correction is closed, the historical architecture changes:

> **the original author re-enters the downstream transformation of his own concept.**

Reception is therefore a feedback system, not a one-way citation tree.

## VIII. Jung: Lovejoy becomes a revision-layer source

Jung's direct reception is now edition-controlled between two English-authorized textual states.

### 1916 first English collection

`New Paths in Psychology` contains no recovered:

- `Lovejoy`;
- `primitive energetics`;
- `mana`;
- later primordial-image / dynamistic-religion proof sequence.

### revised second-edition text prepared in 1917

The enlarged chapter `The Psychology of the Unconscious Processes` adds a new inferential layer.

Jung argues that the modern scientific idea of energy/conservation corresponds to a primordial image in the unconscious. He then says the stronger historical proposition must be proved by showing that a comparable image existed and remained effective over long periods.

At precisely that point he turns to:

- geographically dispersed dynamistic religions;
- universal magical power;
- the distinction from animistic soul/spirit interpretations;
- Lovejoy's `primitive energetics`.

The Lovejoy citation is therefore not simply appended to an unchanged paragraph.

It belongs to the **revision/expansion layer** in which comparative religion becomes a historical warrant archive for Jung's enlarged psychology of primordial images.

Safe formulation:

> **Lovejoy is absent from the 1916 first-English state and present in the substantially enlarged 1917 revision state.**

The exact German first appearance remains a narrower edition-history question.

## IX. Jung 1917: proof-bearing appropriation with source-resolution loss

In Lovejoy 1906:

`native vocabulary / grammar / custom`
→ establish a causal category
→ `primitive energetics`.

In Jung 1917:

`modern energy concept`
→ hypothesize primordial image
→ historical persistence requires proof
→ comparative dynamistic religions / Lovejoy
→ support the psychological genealogy.

Thus Lovejoy's category changes evidentiary position:

> **endpoint of ethnographic abstraction → middle term in psychological genealogy.**

Some proof syntax survives. Jung explicitly identifies a stronger proposition and requires historical evidence for it.

But Lovejoy's source hierarchy does not survive at the same granularity. The distinctions among:

- observer;
- lexical occurrence;
- gloss;
- grammatical evidence;
- semantic abstraction;
- causal application;
- regional resolution

are compressed into a broad comparative power-conception.

Compact formulation:

> **proof syntax persists; source granularity collapses.**

This is an analytical description of the textual relation, not evidence that Jung adopted Lovejoy's method consciously.

## X. Jung 1928 reopens the evidentiary packet

In `Über die Energetik der Seele` / `On Psychical Energy`, first published in 1928, Jung's use of Lovejoy becomes substantially more discriminating.

The preserved original-version textual witness shows Jung working again through specific ethnographic terms and authorities, including:

- McGee / `wakonda`;
- North-American `oki` / `manitu`;
- Hetherwick / `mulungu`;
- Melanesian `mana` / Codrington;
- other regional power terms and comparative controls.

Lovejoy now functions partly as a **bibliographic and semantic relay back into the evidence packet**.

Jung then separates two propositions.

### Proposition A — efficacy / power / productivity

Jung substantially accepts Lovejoy's semantic and causal diagnosis:

- efficacy;
- power;
- productivity;
- diffused / communicable force;
- manipulative / regular energetic relation;
- `primitive energetics` as a useful comparative designation.

Verdict: **ACCEPT / REUSE**.

### Proposition B — native abstract `concept`

Jung objects that calling the indigenous datum a `concept of primitive philosophy` turns the modern scholar's abstraction into a description of native cognition.

His distinction is:

`empirical representation / psychic phenomenon inseparable from object-relation`

versus

`analyst's abstract comparative concept`.

Verdict: **CORRECT / NARROW**.

The reception relation therefore changes from:

`1917 synthesized historical warrant`

to

`1928 decomposable evidentiary source`.

---

## XI. Jung 1928 is an external stress-test of Lovejoy's evidentiary license

The striking point is not whether Jung's own anthropology is correct by present standards.

The historically useful fact is how he grades Lovejoy.

Jung accepts Lovejoy for one proposition and rejects him for another:

`native use attested` → YES

`efficacy relation recoverable` → YES

`modern comparative abstraction useful` → YES

`same abstraction exists as a native abstract concept` → NO / overlicensed.

This structurally mirrors Lovejoy's own 1906 source practice, where a source can be admitted for observed custom or lexical occurrence while rejected as semantic or theological interpreter.

Safe formulation:

> **Jung retains the datum and revises the interpretation.**

Do not turn the structural similarity into a transmission claim about method.

---

## XII. Leuba and Jung attack different upward inferences

The paired reception control is especially useful.

### Leuba 1912

Accepts:

`non-personal power exists`.

Blocks:

`therefore it operates according to mechanical / regular law at the strength Lovejoy claims`.

### Jung 1928

Accepts:

`efficacy / power / productivity pattern exists`, and even much of Lovejoy's regular/manipulable characterization.

Blocks:

`therefore the modern comparative abstraction is itself an indigenous abstract concept`.

Thus two readers preserve lower-level propositions while rejecting different higher-level licenses.

This is the clearest reason not to code reception as assent/dissent.

A better matrix is:

| Proposition | Lovejoy 1906 | Leuba 1912 | Jung 1928 |
|---|---|---|---|
| non-personal efficacy/power | strong claim | accepts | accepts |
| regular / intelligible / quasi-mechanical action | strong claim | contests / downgrades to magical | accepts substantially |
| evidence dossier useful | yes | explicitly yes | reopens and reuses |
| independent warrant needed for stronger claim | yes | changes to child psychology for origin | 1917 uses comparative religion for primordial-image history |
| native datum is an abstract comparative concept | implicit/explicit in `primitive philosophy` framing | not central local objection | explicitly narrows/rejects |

The object that survives is therefore not one proposition but a **problem-space with redistributable warrants**.

## XIII. Reception mechanics: five distinct operations

The 1909–1928 evidence supports five reception operations.

### 1. Packet assimilation

James/Kallen 1911:

Lovejoy becomes one authority inside a broad mana / primitive-magic packet.

### 2. Proposition disaggregation

Leuba 1912:

category retained, causal characterization challenged, evidence dossier retained.

### 3. Reproof / warrant substitution

Leuba 1912:

ethnography for existence; child psychology for genesis.

### 4. Controversy and feedback

King 1910 → Leuba review → King 1911 reply → Leuba 1912 synthesis → Lovejoy 1914 review.

Arguments recirculate through genres and return to the original author.

### 5. Evidentiary repurposing and re-audit

Jung 1917:

Lovejoy becomes historical warrant for a primordial-energy-image theory.

Jung 1928:

Lovejoy is unpacked again; substantive power evidence is retained while epistemic over-abstraction is challenged.

These operations together explain why the article's later life cannot be represented by a citation count.

## XIV. Relation to the larger project

The reception history repeats, at a second order, a pattern already visible inside Lovejoy's own notebooks and publications.

Inside Lovejoy:

`source / datum`
→ separated by proposition
→ reassigned to different evidentiary jobs
→ stronger proposition may require reproof through another channel.

In reception:

`Lovejoy 1906 as source`
→ separated by proposition
→ reassigned to different evidentiary jobs
→ stronger propositions are re-proved, downgraded, or reframed.

This is not a claim of conscious methodological inheritance.

It is a historical claim about how the same published object behaves under successive acts of source criticism and disciplinary reuse.

Compact formulation:

> **The later life of `primitive energetics` was itself a history of evidentiary reassignment.**
