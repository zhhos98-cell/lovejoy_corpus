# `Primitive` as historical object-construction — historiography calibration for Lovejoy 1906

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **HISTORIOGRAPHY SWEEP / MACRO-OBJECT CALIBRATION / NO RETROJECTION**

## Core result

Recent historiography of anthropology and religion strongly supports a distinction the Lovejoy project now needs to keep explicit:

> **Careful local discrimination of evidence can coexist with asymmetrical construction of the macro object `primitive`.**

The historical question is therefore not exhausted by whether Lovejoy distinguishes causal, semantic, chronological and ritual relations correctly inside individual cases.

A second burden concerns object construction:

`WHICH POPULATIONS / PRACTICES ARE MADE TOKENS OF "PRIMITIVE PHILOSOPHY"?`
+
`BY WHAT CLASSIFICATORY LICENSE CAN LOCAL CASES REPRESENT THAT MACRO OBJECT?`.

This is a historiographic calibration, not language to retroject into Lovejoy as actor vocabulary.

## I. Pels: classification produces the anthropological object

Peter Pels, `Primitive Classification revisited` (2022), revisits the history and decolonization of anthropological classification.

The article is useful because it does not treat `primitive/native` as a neutral empirical label subsequently contaminated by politics. It asks how anthropological classification and trained scholarly judgment historically constituted objects and populations, including racialized/colonial distinctions.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1080/02757206.2022.2039889

Project use:

`CATEGORY NAME / CLASSIFICATION`
!=
`TRANSPARENT PRE-EXISTING HISTORICAL UNIT`.

This parallels the repo's later Baird/history-of-religions control but applies specifically to anthropological object formation.

## II. Clark: primitive -> civilization is simultaneously temporal and racial-imperial

Constance Areson Clark's historical work on evolution, race and civilization reconstructs the nineteenth-century anthropology of Tylor/Morgan in a political-imperial field rather than as a purely technical evolutionary sequence.

Relevant chapter/work:
Constance Clark, `Evolution for John Doe: Pictures, the Public, and the Scopes Trial Debate` / related historiography of race, civilization and anthropological evolutionary narratives; 2019 work on science, race and the primitive/civilized trajectory.

The important calibration is structural:

`PRIMITIVE -> CIVILIZED`
can operate simultaneously as:

- temporal sequence;
- cultural hierarchy;
- population classification;
- imperial/racial political ordering.

Therefore a historian must not treat the temporal axis as politically exhausted by chronology alone.

## III. Tylor historiography: comparative method and reformist science coexist with savage-stage hierarchy

Timothy Larsen's reassessment of E. B. Tylor and other historical scholarship on Victorian anthropology shows that the same comparative/evolutionary scholarship can combine:

- large-scale cross-cultural comparison;
- source criticism and attention to survivals/customs;
- reformist/scientific ambitions;
- explicit `savage / barbaric / civilized` developmental ordering.

Useful modern control:
Timothy Larsen, `E. B. Tylor, Religion and Anthropology` (2013 and related work).

The lesson for Lovejoy is not genealogical (`Tylor caused Lovejoy's politics`) but analytical:

> **fine-grained comparative operations do not by themselves dissolve the hierarchical macro object within which cases are assembled.**

## IV. Meylan: `mana` is itself a Western theoretical category with selective boundaries

Nicolas Meylan, *Mana: A History of a Western Category* (2017), is the most specific control for Lovejoy 1906.

Meylan reconstructs `mana` not simply as an Oceanic ethnographic datum but as a category transformed through Western scholarly theory.

Lovejoy 1906 participates by:

- shifting Marett's `supernormal/religious` emphasis toward causal efficacy/power;
- equating several indigenous terms as tokens of one impersonal-power family;
- promoting that family toward a `fundamental concept of primitive philosophy`.

Meylan's additional observation that not every analogous power conception entered Lovejoy's `primitive` corpus raises a crucial boundary question.

Thus:

`SEMANTIC / FUNCTIONAL ANALOGY`
!= automatically
`SAME MACRO HISTORICAL OBJECT`.

Source:
Nicolas Meylan, *Mana: A History of a Western Category* (Brill, 2017).
https://brill.com/display/title/35226

## V. Re-reading Lovejoy 1906 through two simultaneous burdens

The existing repo has established the strong side of Lovejoy's local practice.

### Local relation burden

Inside notebook 005 and the 1906 field:

- ritual killing != sacrifice automatically;
- sacrifice != sacramental communion;
- efficacy may be intrinsic / transferable rather than indwelling personal agency;
- agricultural rites can be magical/fecundative rather than propitiatory;
- type identity != chronological priority;
- apparently similar rites can require different mechanism assignments.

### Macro promotion burden

The article nevertheless moves upward:

`HETEROGENEOUS CASES`
-> `IMPERSONAL POWER / EFFICACY TYPE`
-> `PRIMITIVE PHILOSOPHY / SAVAGE THOUGHT`
-> `FUNDAMENTAL CONCEPT`.

These are different evidentiary moves.

A successful local mechanism classification does not automatically establish:

- representativeness across `primitive peoples`;
- the unity of the macro population object;
- developmental stage position;
- political/civilizational meaning.

Core rule:

> **LOCAL PROOF DISCIPLINE DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY LICENSE MACRO OBJECT PROMOTION.**

## VI. Relation to Lovejoy's 1906 political essay

`Democracy in the Twentieth Century` is indispensable because it shows Lovejoy himself using population differentiation politically in the same year.

He distinguishes:

- longitudinal stage/culture differentiation;
- cross-wise racial/national differentiation.

He qualifies the race/national inference and warns against overworking the race category, but the distinctions still enter colonial-government and Black enfranchisement arguments.

The historiography of `primitive` therefore strengthens the repo's decision to hold two claims together:

1. Lovejoy can be analytically careful about relations and evidentiary warrants.
2. The categories into which evidence is aggregated can carry hierarchical/racialized/civilizational political consequences.

Do not make one claim cancel the other.

## VII. Historiographic posture

Avoid two flattenings.

### Flattening A — pure ideology

`primitive = only racial/colonial ideology`

would erase the real local scholarly disputes over mechanism, source, chronology and category.

### Flattening B — pure epistemic refinement

`Lovejoy distinguished relation types carefully -> therefore escaped primitive/civilizational hierarchy`

would mistake local proof discipline for macro political neutrality.

Better formulation:

> **The historical interest lies precisely in their coexistence: refined evidentiary differentiation can furnish the materials for broad, asymmetrically distributed historical classifications.**
