# Charles Carroll Everett 1899 — Vedānta/Sāṃkhya psychology as a Harvard teacher control

Date: 2026-08-26
Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / DOCUMENTED LOVEJOY TEACHER PROBLEM-FIELD CONTROL / 004 ATTRIBUTION AND CHRONOLOGY FIREWALL ACTIVE**

## Core result

The full primary body of Charles Carroll Everett's `The Psychology of the Vedanta and Sankhya Philosophies`, *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 20 (1899), 309–316, is now directly collated from the public-domain Internet Archive scan/OCR.

Everett was a documented Lovejoy teacher in the 1896–97 Harvard training window: Lovejoy took Everett's comparative-religion and psychology-of-religion work before notebook 004 and the 1898 JAOS Buddhist article. The 1899 paper therefore supplies a high-value **teacher/problem-field control** for the Harvard side of the project.

Its importance is not that it proves what Everett taught Lovejoy. The publication postdates Lovejoy's 1898 article and cannot be used backwards as a textual source for it. Rather, it shows that a documented teacher was publicly working in almost the exact Sāṃkhya/Vedānta/Buddhist genealogy field while using an inferential regime partly overlapping with and partly unlike the source-critical regime visible in 004.

Best formulation:

> **Everett 1899 strengthens the claim that Lovejoy's Buddhist/Sāṃkhya work belonged to a real Harvard comparative-philosophy problem field, while simultaneously showing why `Harvard influence` cannot be treated as one method: Everett's synthetic school-psychology reconstruction, Lanman/JAOS philology, and the Oldenberg/Jacobi controversy impose different evidentiary resolutions.**

## I. Direct bibliographic and institutional control

Primary object:

Charles Carroll Everett, `The Psychology of the Vedanta and Sankhya Philosophies`, *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 20 (1899), 309–316, DOI `10.2307/592337`.

JSTOR marks the article FREE/public-domain accessible. The same JAOS volume's proceedings show that Everett presented the paper at the 1899 Cambridge meeting of the American Oriental Society in a session devoted to the **Section for the Historical Study of Religions**. Toy's `Relation between Magic and Religion` was read in the same session; Jastrow, Hopkins and other comparative-religion papers were also presented.

Lovejoy's documented pedagogy under Everett is independently controlled from Wilson/Harvard course evidence for 1896–97. Do not turn the 1899 meeting into a Lovejoy attendance claim.

## II. Everett's object is a synthetic `psychology of systems`

Everett opens by identifying what he takes to be a sharp psychological insight in both Vedānta and Sāṃkhya, then reconstructs each school through the structure of consciousness, subject/object relations and emancipation.

His analytic language is deliberately comparative and philosophical. Vedānta is read against Kant/Fichte and Sāṃkhya against modern distinctions between the `I` and the `Me`. This is not a narrowly philological article. The target is the **psychological architecture of systems**.

That difference in object matters for source criticism.

## III. Source-layer compression is actor-declared, not accidental

Everett includes an unusually useful methodological footnote. Because his object is to reach `the views of these schools of thought`, he says he makes **no distinction in his references between what an author says and what later commentators associated with that author say in the translations**.

This is a direct actor-declared source-resolution choice.

For the current project:

`Everett's target = synthetic school psychology`
→ author/commentator strata deliberately compressed.

By contrast, the Oldenberg/Jacobi/Senart controversy and notebook 004 repeatedly make chronology, genre, canonical usage and historical layer relevant to the proposition under dispute.

The contrast therefore supports a stronger multi-jurisdiction model:

> **source resolution is question-relative across the Harvard/Indological ecology; it is not one stable `comparative-religion method`.**

Do not call Everett careless on this basis. He explicitly states the tradeoff because his chosen historical object is different.

## IV. Everett marks conjecture as conjecture

When discussing how the Vedāntic insight may have been reached, Everett reviews competing positions around Śaṅkara/Upaniṣadic interpretation and says the origin is not easy to determine. He then proposes that one may **conjecture**, provided one does not forget that it is `merely conjecture`, a possible consideration suggested by the use of dreams.

This gives a direct confidence-grading control inside a synthetic philosophical article:

`plausible reconstruction`
≠
`established historical derivation`.

Everett is capable of strong system claims elsewhere; the explicit conjecture label therefore matters as an internal evidentiary boundary rather than generic caution.

## V. Imported analytic terms can distort the historical object

Everett repeatedly warns that English/modern philosophical terms may misdescribe Sāṃkhya.

He objects especially to translations of:

- `Prakṛti` as `Nature`;
- `Puruṣa/Ātman` as `Soul`;
- `matter` as a useful category for the system.

The combined `Nature`/`Soul` vocabulary, he argues, misleadingly makes Sāṃkhya look like a straightforward realist dualism, whereas his reconstruction treats its foundation as idealistic. He goes so far as to say the system puts ordinary terminology `at defiance`.

This is a direct analyst-category/object warning:

`available modern label`
≠
`historically adequate relation inside the system`.

Everett's solution is not contextualist in a modern Skinnerian sense; he substitutes a modern psychological reconstruction. But the semantic warning is real and should be kept distinct from the reconstruction he builds with it.

## VI. Relation-specific comparison between Vedānta and Sāṃkhya

Everett argues that Sāṃkhya starts substantially from the position reached by Vedānta but redivides the psychological field. In his reconstruction:

- Vedānta's world rests on ignorance/illusion;
- Sāṃkhya places `Intellect` in the corresponding structural role;
- Sāṃkhya recognizes subject and object within the sphere that Vedānta had already made psychological;
- its `lines of division are drawn within the sphere recognized by the Vedanta`;
- it accepts what Everett calls Vedānta's fundamental psychological doctrine while developing a dual structure inside it.

Thus similarity is not described merely as a list of common doctrines. Everett assigns the systems a structural relation and asks what transformation occurs inside the inherited/problem field.

## VII. Strong genealogy: Sāṃkhya from Vedānta

Everett moves beyond structural comparison to a strong historical claim. He says it is `obvious` that a system like Sāṃkhya could have developed only out of an idealistic system like Vedānta, while noting that it need not have been solipsistic. He aligns this with Garbe's conclusion that Sāṃkhya, though very ancient, is later than the earliest Upaniṣads.

This is materially stronger than the conjecture language used earlier for the origin of a particular Vedāntic insight.

The article therefore contains multiple confidence levels:

- one origin hypothesis = explicitly `merely conjecture`;
- Sāṃkhya-from-Vedānta developmental relation = asserted strongly;
- particular technical/translation identifications = argued through system structure.

This is useful precisely because Everett does **not** apply one uniform evidentiary confidence to every proposition.

## VIII. Dahlmann enters the teacher control directly

Everett then says his reconstruction throws light on the early blending of Sāṃkhya and Vedānta exhibited by Father Joseph Dahlmann in *Nirvāṇa*. He argues that what would look like an improbable union of idealism and realism becomes intelligible once Sāṃkhya itself is reconstructed as fundamentally idealistic.

This places Everett directly inside the **same late-1890s Dahlmann/Garbe/Sāṃkhya genealogy controversy** that forms part of notebook 004's source ecology.

A footnote also invokes the position that it was only a step from the Sāṃkhyan view of soul to the Buddhist denial of soul, citing the Sāṃkhya literature.

Safe result:

> **A documented Lovejoy Harvard teacher was publicly engaging Garbe, Dahlmann and the Sāṃkhya-to-Buddhist problem immediately after Lovejoy's own 1898 JAOS intervention.**

Unsafe result:

- Everett's 1899 argument caused Lovejoy's 1898 paper;
- Everett taught these exact published claims in 1896–97;
- Lovejoy read the 1899 paper before any particular notebook leaf unless a later-dated/reused leaf is independently established.

## IX. Relation to 004 and the Oldenberg/Jacobi field controls

Everett is especially valuable because he is **not simply another instance of the same proof regime**.

### Everett 1899

- reconstructs whole-school psychological architecture;
- deliberately compresses author/commentator source strata for that purpose;
- uses modern psychological comparison as an analytic aid;
- warns against misleading imported terms;
- marks one origin reconstruction as conjecture;
- makes a strong Vedānta→Sāṃkhya developmental claim;
- uses that claim to make Dahlmann's reported blending more intelligible.

### Oldenberg/Jacobi/Senart controversy and 004

The direct primary controls instead foreground:

- source chronology and literary genre;
- canonical usage over speculative etymology;
- diagnostic specificity rather than generic resemblance;
- function/question-sensitive comparison;
- direction-of-borrowing uncertainty;
- decomposition of semantic, causal and genealogical propositions.

### Historiographical consequence

Lovejoy's Harvard formation should therefore not be narrated as `Everett supplied comparative method`, `Lanman supplied languages`, and Paris later supplied context in a linear sequence. The evidentiary ecology is more interesting:

> **Everett's synthetic psychology, Lanman's Sanskrit/Buddhist-text training, AOS/JAOS philological controversy and later Paris comparative-religion work offered different ways of deciding what counted as the relevant historical object. Notebook 004 can be read as an object produced where those jurisdictions overlap, not as the application of one teacher's method.**

This strengthens the current project rule that source competence and claim type must remain distinct.

## X. Relation to Lovejoy's later comparative-category work

Everett's use of modern philosophical language to reconstruct Indian systems is a useful contextual analogue for Lovejoy's later comparative category-history, especially the 1902 move between Buddhism, Vedānta, Plato/Aristotle and other traditions.

Do not claim continuity or influence from Everett without a direct edge. The safe point is narrower:

- Lovejoy's confirmed Harvard comparative-religion teacher publicly practiced cross-tradition psychological reconstruction;
- Lovejoy later practiced cross-tradition category comparison;
- the two differ substantially in source resolution and inferential architecture.

The difference is as informative as the resemblance.

## XI. JHI-facing consequence

Everett permits the Harvard section of the Blog argument to become more concrete without becoming an influence story.

Compact formulation:

> **At Harvard, Lovejoy learned comparative religion from a scholar who could read Vedānta and Sāṃkhya through modern psychology, warn that inherited translations distorted their internal relations, and nevertheless make bold developmental claims linking the systems. At the same moment, Indological controversy demanded a different resolution of textual, semantic and genealogical proof. Lovejoy's 004 notebook sits at this junction of comparative synthesis and philological warrant.**

This is stronger than `Harvard exposed him to Asian philosophy`; it also preserves the attribution firewall.

## Evidence ceilings

### Secure direct primary

- Everett, `The Psychology of the Vedanta and Sankhya Philosophies`, *JAOS* 20 (1899), 309–316, full body directly collated.
- The paper was presented in the 1899 AOS Cambridge session of the Section for the Historical Study of Religions.
- The article directly engages Garbe and Dahlmann and explicitly discusses Vedānta/Sāṃkhya developmental relations.
- Everett directly states his author/commentator compression rule and marks one reconstruction as merely conjectural.

### Secure independent pedagogy

- Lovejoy took comparative religion / psychology-of-religion work with Everett in 1896–97.
- Everett's comparative-religion teaching included Vedic religion, Hinduism and Buddhism.

### Hold / chronology firewall

- the 1899 paper postdates Lovejoy's 1898 JAOS article;
- the exact degree to which its published theses reproduce Everett's 1896–97 classroom teaching is unproved;
- Lovejoy attendance at Everett's 1899 AOS presentation is unproved;
- no claim of Everett→004 textual dependence is licensed by this control.

### Do not claim

- Everett caused Lovejoy's relation-specific proof architecture;
- Lovejoy's 1898 position was a response to Everett 1899;
- Harvard comparative religion had one unified evidentiary method;
- Everett's modern psychological categories can be treated as transparent translations of Indian concepts.
