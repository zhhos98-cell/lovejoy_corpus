# JHI Blog Round 11 — global reception / translation stress test, 1902 to present

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **LONG-RUN METHOD CALIBRATION / 1902 + GREAT CHAIN SAFE / NO RETROJECTION / NO BLOG REWRITE REQUIRED**

## Core result

The recent global-intellectual-history / circulation / translation literature does **not** invalidate the blog's 1902 Lovejoy proposition:

`RESEMBLANCE != HISTORICAL TRANSMISSION / GENEALOGY`.

It changes the modern historian's **positive burden once transmission is claimed**.

A present cross-cultural movement claim may require attention to:

- intermediaries;
- translators;
- multilingual transformation;
- local reception/appropriation;
- media/material carriers;
- asymmetrical power;
- strategic representation;
- network/circulation processes.

Therefore the long-run calibration is:

`LOVEJOY 1902 NEGATIVE INFERENCE CONTROL`
!=
`PRESENT GLOBAL-INTELLECTUAL-HISTORY POSITIVE TRANSMISSION MODEL`.

The public blog should keep the source-specific Lovejoy language and should **not** retrospectively turn him into a circulation/translation theorist.

## I. 2013 Global Intellectual History: circulation becomes an explicit field problem

Samuel Moyn and Andrew Sartori, eds., *Global Intellectual History* (Columbia UP, 2013).

Direct publisher / JSTOR description:

https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7312/moyn16048

https://cup.columbia.edu/book/global-intellectual-history/9780231534598/

The collection explicitly asks how to write histories of the:

- production;
- dissemination;
- circulation

of ideas within global/transnational frames.

It treats global intellectual history as an emerging field containing multiple approaches rather than one settled method.

This shifts emphasis from a binary source-route question toward processes by which ideas move and are transformed.

Do not read this back into Lovejoy 1902.

## IV. 2021 JHI `Translation in Action`: translation becomes constitutive practice

Lisa Hellman and Birgit Tremml-Werner, `Translation in Action: Global Intellectual History and Early Modern Diplomacy`, JHI 82.3 (2021), 453–467.

Direct PubMed abstract:

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34275942/

JHI issue listing:

https://www.jhiblog.org/2021/07/23/jhi-issue-82-3-now-available/

The article's abstract makes several positive transmission burdens explicit:

- translation enables intercultural negotiation;
- translation can generate inaccuracies, misunderstandings or deliberately skewed representations;
- many actors can function as translators;
- power relations among actor types, languages and communication forms are dramatically asymmetrical;
- local translation practice constitutes global political encounter.

This is considerably richer than:

`SOURCE A -> RECEIVER B`.

Modern relation:

`SOURCE / TEXT`
-> `INTERMEDIARY / TRANSLATOR`
-> `LANGUAGE / MEDIA / POWER CONDITIONS`
-> `LOCAL READING / STRATEGIC REPRESENTATION`
-> `TRANSFORMED OBJECT`.

The article notes that global intellectual history has emphasized:

- networks;

Its synthetic claim is that knowledge is often studied as a **transformative phenomenon** rather than a stable item moving intact.

This provides another modern ceiling:

## VI. Stress test of Lovejoy 1902

The blog currently says:

Lovejoy called Aristotle/Vedānta affinity obvious but eastern-influence theory gratuitous because Greek internal intellectual development supplied an alternative account.

Then:

> `Resemblance did not have to become genealogy.`

### Round-11 verdict: KEEP

This is a negative evidentiary proposition:

`SIMILARITY / AFFINITY`
!=
`SUFFICIENT PROOF OF HISTORICAL TRANSMISSION`.

Nothing in modern global intellectual history weakens it.

Modern work generally **raises**, rather than lowers, the burden for a positive transmission story by asking what actual intermediaries, translations, practices and power relations produced the connection.

### Critical restriction

Do not upgrade the 1902 sentence to:

- Lovejoy anticipated circulation theory;
- Lovejoy had a reception-history method;
- Lovejoy understood translation as constitutive transformation;
- Lovejoy rejected all external influence explanations.

His actual move is local:

`AFFINITY OBSERVED`
+
`INTERNAL GREEK DEVELOPMENT AVAILABLE`
-> `EASTERN INFLUENCE NOT ESTABLISHED`.

That remains sufficient for the blog.

## XI. Final Round-11 rule

Long-run field history supports a hierarchy of claims:

### Minimum negative inference

`SIMILARITY != TRANSMISSION`.

### Positive historical connection

needs some demonstrable route / intermediary / carrier evidence.

### Stronger reception/transformation claim

needs evidence for what receivers/translators/intermediaries did to the object.

### Global/asymmetry claim

needs evidence for networks, languages, media, institutions and power differentials at the appropriate scale.

Therefore:

> **A stronger transmission/reception/global proposition does not inherit the evidence sufficient for a weaker similarity or source-contact proposition.**

This is fully compatible with the repo's governing proof-burden model.
