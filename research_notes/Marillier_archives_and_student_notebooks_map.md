# Léon Marillier archives, working papers, and student-notebook targets

## Status

This is an archival reconnaissance map prompted by the identification of Lovejoy notebook `MS38_004_001_061_005` with Léon Marillier's 1898–1899 EPHE conferences. The aim is to locate (1) Marillier's own surviving manuscripts/working materials, (2) EPHE institutional records that can reconstruct his seminar population and administration, and (3) notebooks/papers of other students who may preserve independent records of his teaching.

**Current result:** no single publicly catalogued, consolidated `Fonds Léon Marillier` has yet been identified. The evidence instead points to a **dispersed archive**: institutional EPHE files, small manuscript/correspondence groups in several collections, family/intellectual-network correspondence, and the personal fonds of students. A 1901 obituary explicitly states that Marillier had accumulated extensive working materials over ten years and expressed the hope that a good portion would be published. Their subsequent disposition remains unresolved.

The highest-priority student archive is **Nathan Söderblom at Uppsala University Library**. The second is **Arnold Van Gennep at the MuCEM**. At present, no catalogue record explicitly labelled `notes de cours de Marillier` has been found in either fonds; this is an onsite/finding-aid problem, not evidence of absence.

## 1. Marillier's own archive: what is securely known

### 1.1 Extensive working materials existed at his death

A 1901 notice in the *Revue internationale de l'enseignement* states that it would be desirable to publish a good part of **“les matériaux qu'il avait amassés pendant dix années d'incessant et d'intelligent labeur.”**

This is crucial evidence. Marillier died with a substantial scholarly working archive. The present problem is provenance after 1901, not whether such materials existed.

Anatole Le Braz's later introduction to *La légende de la mort* adds another direct witness: during Marillier's final illness he spoke about works he was leaving **incomplete or unfinished** and instructed Le Braz not to republish his 1892 introduction/notes because he considered them too hastily done. This confirms continuing revision and unfinished research at death.

### 1.2 No centralized public Marillier fonds located yet

The current EPHE public inventory of researcher archives does not list a dedicated Marillier personal fonds. This must not be converted into a claim that no fonds survives: the EPHE inventory is an evolving discovery tool, and Marillier material is already demonstrably dispersed elsewhere.

### 1.3 Calames: three concrete Marillier manuscript records

Persée's Marillier authority record exposes three Calames manuscript descriptions:

1. `[F. 282-287] Article de Léon Marillier sur le livre de Charles Richet, Essai de psychologie générale, publié en 1888 dans la Revue philosophique` — 6 feuillets.
2. `[F. 276-279] Marillier, Léon (20 décembre 1896-9 novembre 1897)`.
3. `[F. 148-151] Trois lettres de Léon Marillier à Xavier Léon (1893-1900)` — 4 feuillets.

Direct Calames item identifiers previously recovered:
- Xavier Léon letters: `UNIC13479`
- Richet article manuscript: `Calames-20119221695229711`
- 1896–1897 record: `Calames-2014122121239571`

The holding institutions for all three have not yet been securely recovered from public snippets, so they should not be assigned by inference.

### 1.4 Bibliothèque historique de la Ville de Paris: three autograph letters

**Secure holding:** Bibliothèque historique de la Ville de Paris, Bibliothèque Marie-Louise Bouglé, fonds Céline Renooz.

- cote: `4-MS-FS-16-0641`
- date: 1890
- extent: 4 folios
- content: 3 autograph letters by Marillier on the letterhead of the **Société pour les recherches psychologiques**.

This is a small but concrete personal-manuscript node and may help reconstruct Marillier's psychology/psychical-research network.

### 1.5 Anatole Le Braz correspondence

Pascal Le Maléfan published **fourteen letters and cards from Marillier to Anatole Le Braz, 1887–1900** in *Inédits* 18 (2023), pp. 182–228. The article describes them as part of a rediscovered correspondence and gives important biographical/intellectual context.

The physical holding place of the originals has not yet been established from the public catalogue evidence gathered in this sweep. Treat as `repository TBD` until the edition or archive catalogue identifies provenance explicitly.

### 1.6 Private/dispersed papers remain plausible

Le Maléfan's 2005 biographical reconstruction explicitly noted that some documents cited in the literature remained inaccessible because they belonged to **private fonds**. This supports a dispersed-survival model and makes family/intellectual-network provenance especially important.

High-value network fonds to pursue include:
- Anatole Le Braz / Le Braz family material;
- Charles Seignobos and Cécile Chaley/Marillier family connections;
- Xavier Léon / *Revue de métaphysique et de morale*;
- Jean Réville / *Revue de l'histoire des religions*;
- Charles Richet / Société de psychologie physiologique / psychical-research networks;
- Félix Alcan / publishing correspondence, if surviving.

## 2. EPHE institutional archive: probably the best reconstruction tool

### 2.1 Ministry oversight files

Archives nationales, Pierrefitte-sur-Seine:

`F/17/13618` — Ministry of Public Instruction files relating to EPHE Vth Section, 1885–1933.

EPHE's official archive inventory says this group includes:
- reports and organisation;
- creation of courses and candidatures;
- free/temporary conferences;
- personnel nominations, promotions, salaries and correspondence;
- **students: nominations (1889–1932)**;
- posters/programmes.

Potential Marillier yield: appointment/course files, correspondence, administrative reports, evidence surrounding his 1888 free course and 1890 chair, and possibly student dossiers.

### 2.2 Vth Section institutional archives

Archives nationales:

`20190568/001-399` — EPHE Vth Section, Sciences religieuses, 1886–2005.

Most relevant blocks:
- presidency: `/1-46`
- assemblies/instances: `/47-61`
- secretariat: `/69-120`
- personnel: `/121-165`
- **scolarité / student administration: `/185-368`**
- publications: `/369-399`.

The student block is the best current route to resolve the unidentified 1898–99 auditors `W. S. Andrews`, `Lorette`, `Travers`, and `Schaefer` from registration records rather than name-guessing.

### 2.3 2025 digitization project

EPHE announced in May 2025 that a major corpus of its first century's scientific archives had been digitized. The official announcement specifically stresses **registration registers and attendance lists** as exceptional instruments for measuring the school's scientific influence, alongside section assembly registers and scholar-specific course/correspondence files.

Finding-aid/digitization target recorded in earlier sweep: `FRAN_IR_061975` in the Archives nationales SIV.

**Immediate task:** locate 1898–99 Vth Section registration/presence registers and extract full identities, addresses, nationalities and course combinations for Marillier's students/auditors. This could turn `Travers`, `Schaefer`, `Andrews`, and `Lorette` into searchable archival persons.

## 3. Student archive target no. 1: Nathan Söderblom — Uppsala

### 3.1 Why Söderblom is the strongest candidate

Marillier's official 1898–99 EPHE report lists:
- `Söderblom (diplômé), Travers, Schaefer` as titular students regularly attending;
- `Lovejoy, W. S. Andrews, Lorette` as auditors actively participating.

The EPHE diploma tables identify Söderblom's 1898 diploma work:

**`Traces dans le Mazdéisme d'une ancienne conception sur la survivance des morts`**.

Persée's Marillier authority data explicitly catalogues the work as by Nathan Söderblom **“sous la direction de Léon Marillier.”** It was published in expanded form as *Les Fravashis* (Paris, 1899).

This is extremely close to Marillier's `survivance` seminar and to Lovejoy 005 pp. 1–30. Söderblom is therefore the ideal independent witness to compare with Lovejoy.

### 3.2 Surviving personal archive

Uppsala University Library, Carolina Rediviva:

- archive: `Nathan Söderbloms efterlämnade papper`
- shelfmark: **`N. Söderblom`**
- format: non-digital
- contents explicitly include correspondence, **manuscripts, notes**, speeches and extensive working material.

The Alvin record provides an appended PDF finding aid for `C. Manuskript` and `E. Handlingar i särskilda ämnen`, plus paper catalogues at the manuscripts unit.

The public record's diary/notebook summary lists notebooks for 1894–95 and 1901–08 among other periods; it does **not** expose an item labelled 1898 Marillier lecture notes in the web summary. Absence at this level is not probative because the archive is very large and detailed access still depends on paper/finding-aid catalogues.

### 3.3 What to request/search at Uppsala

Priority keywords/dossiers:
- Paris / 1896–1901;
- EPHE / École pratique des hautes études;
- Marillier;
- `Les Fravashis`;
- `Traces dans le Mazdéisme...`;
- survivance / vie future / eschatologie;
- handwritten research notebooks or loose notes from Paris;
- correspondence with Marillier;
- correspondence with Jean Réville, Albert Réville, Sabatier, Mauss;
- manuscripts preceding *Les Fravashis* (1899) and *La vie future d'après le Mazdéisme* (1901).

If a Marillier-linked notebook survives here, it would provide the strongest external control on Lovejoy 005: same teacher, same seminar, same year, related research question.

### 3.4 Important restriction distinction

The public personal papers `N. Söderblom` are not the same as the separate **Nathan Söderblom family archive** (`Acc9999`), which Alvin says is closed until 2031. Do not treat the closure of the family archive as applying to the main scholarly papers.

## 4. Student archive target no. 2: Arnold Van Gennep — MuCEM

### 4.1 Direct Marillier connection

Van Gennep studied at EPHE in both historical/philological and religious-sciences sections. Biographical catalogues state that he followed Marillier's teaching; his 1904 EPHE memoir **`Tabou et totémisme à Madagascar`** was begun under Marillier and completed under Marcel Mauss after Marillier's death.

Marillier's final 1900–01 course report lists **van Gennep** among titular students, alongside Söderblom.

### 4.2 Surviving fonds

MuCEM, Centre de conservation et de ressources:

**Fonds Arnold Van Gennep, series `4P`.**

FranceArchives item `4P/132`, `Documents rites de passage`, proves that this is a true scholarly working archive, not merely correspondence or photographs. It includes:
- extracted texts;
- Van Gennep's handwritten notes;
- bibliographic references;
- correspondence;
- chapter-organized research dossiers;
- manuscript material.

No item explicitly indexed `Marillier` or `notes de cours` has surfaced in web search. The full finding aid should be searched manually for early EPHE/Paris strata rather than relying on item-level indexing.

### 4.3 What to search in 4P

- 1896–1904 study notebooks;
- `Madagascar`;
- `tabou` / `totémisme`;
- `Marillier`;
- `Robertson Smith` / `Jevons` / `Frazer`;
- `religions des peuples non civilisés`;
- `sacrifice` / `anthropophagie`;
- drafts/research cards for *Tabou et totémisme à Madagascar*.

### 4.4 External comparative evidence: Frazer correspondence

Trinity College Cambridge's Frazer papers preserve several Van Gennep letters. A 26 January 1906 letter (`Add. MS c/57/21`) tells Frazer that *Tabou et totémisme à Madagascar* contains Van Gennep's theory of sanctity/taboo, explicitly differentiated from Robertson Smith, and his positive/negative rite distinction. These letters are useful for reconstructing how Marillier-trained analytical problems developed after the seminar even if no classroom notebook survives.

## 5. Marcel Mauss — possible witness, but currently weaker for lecture notes

Collège de France, Salamandre:

**Fonds Marcel Mauss – Henri Hubert, `57 CDF 1-170`.**

The catalogue includes `57 CDF 40-3`, **“Notes de cours d'élèves”**, but these are in the Institut d'ethnologie teaching series and should **not** be mistaken for Mauss's own notes as a pupil of Marillier.

Targeted public-catalogue searches have not yet surfaced an item explicitly described as notes taken from Marillier. The fonds remains worth a full finding-aid/onsite search because Mauss wrote the major 1902 intellectual obituary of Marillier and worked immediately in the succession of the chair/research programme.

Evidence level: **possible, not confirmed**.

## 7. Additional Marillier-network archival leads

### Xavier Léon
Three Marillier letters, 1893–1900, are catalogued in Calames. These may document philosophical-network relations and perhaps publication/research plans.

### Charles Richet / psychical research
A six-folio manuscript article by Marillier on Richet's *Essai de psychologie générale* survives in Calames; BHVP also has three 1890 letters on Société pour les recherches psychologiques letterhead. These are promising for reconstructing Marillier's own note/revision habits outside comparative religion.

### Anatole Le Braz
Fourteen Marillier letters/cards have been published. Because Le Braz was both brother-in-law and collaborator, his archive/family provenance is a leading candidate for post-1901 dispersal of papers.

### Revue de l'histoire des religions / Jean Réville
Marillier became co-director of RHR with Jean Réville in 1896. Editorial correspondence could contain drafts, reading notes, referee comments, and plans for unfinished work. No dedicated archival cluster has yet been located in this sweep.

### Frazer correspondence / Trinity College Cambridge
Even where Marillier himself is absent, correspondence of his students and collaborators with Frazer can reconstruct the intellectual afterlife of his seminar. Van Gennep's letters are already a concrete example.

## 9. Current archival hypothesis

The disappearance of a unified Marillier Nachlass may itself be historically significant. He died suddenly at 38 after the August 1901 Le Braz family catastrophe; his wife had died in that catastrophe, and contemporary testimony says he left substantial unfinished work. His intellectual archive may therefore have been divided among family, collaborators, journals, students, and institutional files rather than accessioned as one scholar's fonds.

For the Lovejoy project this makes **student notebooks unusually important**. Notebook 005 may presently be one of the clearest first-person surviving records of Marillier's seminar that has been identified, but it would be premature to call it unique until Söderblom, Van Gennep and the unresolved 1898–99 students are checked.

The decisive comparison to seek is:

> **Lovejoy 005 ↔ Söderblom Paris/Fravashis working notes ↔ Van Gennep early EPHE/Madagascar notes ↔ Marillier printed course reports.**

If two independent student notebooks reproduce the same classifications, bibliography, lecture dates, or sequence of examples, we can reconstruct Marillier's oral seminar with much greater precision and distinguish lecture material from Lovejoy's own additions.
