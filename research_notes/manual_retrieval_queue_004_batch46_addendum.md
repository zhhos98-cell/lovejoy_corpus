## P0 — Paul Oltramare, _La formule bouddhique des douze causes_ (1909)

This has become the highest-value **reception** target, separate from the still-P0 **source-context** packet Jacobi 1897 + Dahlmann 1896.

Full citation:

**Paul Oltramare, _La formule bouddhique des douze causes: son sens originel et son interprétation théologique_ (Genève: Georg & Cie, 1909), 53 pp.**

### Why this is now P0

La Vallée Poussin’s 1913 _Théorie des douze causes_ explicitly calls Oltramare 1909 an **excellent summary of the history of Western interpretation** of Pratītyasamutpāda. Poussin’s visible survey list names Burnouf, Spence Hardy, Childers, Kern, E. Hardy, Warren and Rhys Davids; Lovejoy is not visible in that list.

A direct read of Oltramare therefore answers a much stronger reception question than generic web searches:

> Did a 1909 specialist survey of the Western interpretations of the twelve causes remember/cite Lovejoy’s 1898 JAOS intervention?

If yes, we get a secure early reception hit. If no, the result is still limited but historically meaningful: a near-contemporary specialist synthesis could omit Lovejoy even though it reviews the exact interpretive lineage in which he intervened.

Do **not** infer omission until the whole 53 pages or at least the survey/bibliography has been searched directly.

## Later field-control result already secure

La Vallée Poussin 1913 explicitly distinguishes:

- general/philosophical intelligibility of Pratītyasamutpāda;
- origins of the formula;
- exact original value of the terms;
- later systematic interpretations.

He further says that logical/intelligible analysis is indispensable but does **not necessarily have historical value** and may only yield a plausible reconstruction of real development.

This makes Oltramare 1909 especially valuable: Poussin uses it precisely to delegate the **history of Western interpretation** while he keeps logical analysis and historical reconstruction methodologically separate.
