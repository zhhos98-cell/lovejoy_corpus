# Lovejoy 005 — suicide as case reallocation: from static taxonomy to a revisable selector procedure (Batch 219)

Date: 2026-08-26  
Status: **DIRECT NOTEBOOK-TRANSCRIPTION SYNTHESIS / MATERIAL PAGE-IMAGE AUDIT PENDING**  
Scope: `MS38_004_001_061_005`, especially PDF pp.3–6, 11, 14–20, controlled against Batches203–207 and the 1909→1910 Kant self-revision in Batches217–218. Ordinary forward boundary remains 1910.

## Core result

Batches203–207 forced a major attribution correction: most of the component distinctions in Lovejoy 005’s early survivance block — status, ritual qualification, manner of death, clan/social consequence, moral differentiation, contact contamination and historical secondary moralization — are already distributed across Marillier, Mauss and the same Marillier workshop. The older repository formula

> `Marillier binary → Lovejoy invents a five-stage determinant taxonomy`

is therefore too strong.

Batch219 asks a different question:

> **What does Lovejoy’s notebook actually do with a shared determinant field?**

The most diagnostic answer is visible in the trajectory of **suicide / special manner of death**. Lovejoy does not simply place suicide into one pre-existing box. Across adjacent pages the same apparent `punishment` is repeatedly reassigned as the operative selector changes:

> `manner of death`  
> → `ritual/manual qualification`  
> → `custom / taboo`  
> → `social obligation / clan solidarity`  
> → `exclusion / blood-contact consequence`  
> → `later imported or higher-stage moral interpretation`  
> → only when outcome tracks **desert** does the case qualify as strictly moral.

This makes the early taxonomy better understood as a **revisable case-allocation procedure** than as a static ladder of stages.

The notebook’s distinctive documentary property is therefore increasingly:

> **keep the ethnographic outcome fixed, vary the candidate selector, and reclassify the case until the mechanism actually licensed by the evidence is isolated.**

This is a narrower and stronger Lovejoy-level claim than category invention.

`case reallocation`, `selector procedure`, and `dynamic taxonomy` are project analytical terms, not Lovejoy’s vocabulary.

---

## 1. The trajectory begins before the suicide pages: differentiated destiny is not yet moral

The front of 005 establishes a burden of proof rather than a positive classification.

### PDF p.3

Lovejoy asks what a genuinely **moral** future-life conception would mean. Continuation of wealth/status/power into the next life cannot simply be redescribed as recompense for virtue.

### PDF p.4

He repeats the rule for ritual devotion. A person may obtain a favorable future condition because sacrifice or ritual operates in a quasi-mechanical way. Formally this can look like `reward`, but the operative relation is not moral judgment.

### PDF p.6

The notebook explicitly lists **manner of death** as another selector: suicide, drowning and childbirth can determine different post-mortem destinations.

The initial problem is therefore already relational:

> `different destiny` does not tell us **what relation selected it**.

The observer-visible outcome is underdetermining.

---

## 2. Greenland p.11: an apparently privileged destination is reassigned from reward to magical/ritual qualification

PDF p.11 gives the cleanest early case control.

The Greenland material distinguishes post-mortem abodes and associates the more favorable condition with drowning, death in childbirth and related qualifications. Lovejoy explicitly writes that **no moral notion is implied**.

He then makes the stronger move: what may look like compensation, negligence or reward/punishment is re-described as a problem of ritual or magical qualification.

Thus the case changes category without the observed outcome changing:

`better afterlife after special death`

is first available to an outside observer as

`reward / compensation`

but Lovejoy reallocates it to

`manner-of-death + ritual/magical condition`.

This is already the operative grammar later concentrated in the suicide sequence.

---

## 3. The pp.14–15 outline is not best read as a finished staircase

The early comparison note `MS38_004_001_061_005_pp001-030_vs_Marillier_Survivance.md` described pp.14–15 as a Lovejoy-created sequence:

> naturalistic continuity → ritual determinant → social/community determinant → strictly moral guilt.

After Batches203–207, the **components** of that sequence are substantially field-inherited. Söderblom’s same-workshop publication and Marillier/Mauss controls show that terrestrial condition, rites, manner of death, burial, moral/quasi-moral distinctions and contact transformations were already available discriminanda.

The value of pp.14–15 now lies elsewhere.

The adjacent outline turns dispersed discriminanda into **candidate selectors that can be applied serially to one case**. What follows on pp.16–20 demonstrates that the list is operational rather than merely developmental.

So the best reading is:

> **the outline is a working decision-space, not simply a universal civilization ladder.**

It can still have developmental ordering language; the crucial point is that Lovejoy immediately uses its categories to redistribute ambiguous examples.

---

## 4. PDF p.16: an `intermediate` appearance is withheld from morality

At p.16 Lovejoy describes an intermediate form in which supernatural determination can appear to lead toward morality, but destiny still depends on:

- manner of death;
- manual/ritual acts;
- social rather than moral elements.

This is significant because `intermediate` does not automatically mean `partly moral` in the sense of half-desert. It identifies a **problematic zone of classification** where similar outward distinctions may be generated by different selectors.

The notebook is therefore already asking:

> Which relation in this mixed case is doing the work?

---

## 5. PDF p.17: suicide first moves from `punishment` to custom/taboo

The suicide problem becomes explicit at p.17.

Lovejoy sees a report in which most dead go to a better world while those violating custom or committing suicide are treated differently. An observer can easily code this as punishment for wrongdoing.

Lovejoy blocks that inference.

Violation of:

- custom;
- taboo;
- ritual interdict

is **not itself equivalent** to violation of a moral order.

So suicide’s first reallocation is:

`punished suicide`

≠ automatically

`moral guilt`

and may instead be

`custom/taboo/ritual violation`.

The case remains open.

---

## 6. PDF p.18: the same suicide case moves again, into clan solidarity / social injury

At p.18 the notebook does not stop with `taboo`.

Lovejoy asks why suicide would be criminal at all and proposes another selector:

> **solidarity of clan**.

Whether one kills another person or oneself, one may be understood as striking at the clan through one of its members. The resulting exclusion from social/religious rights and connections therefore need not express individual moral desert.

Blood/contact and group membership become additional mechanisms.

This produces a second reallocation:

`suicide → taboo violation`

becomes, under another explanatory hypothesis,

`suicide → injury/danger to clan → exclusion`.

The notebook does not erase the former possibility. It multiplies candidate relations and asks which one better explains the actual sanction.

This is precisely why the same case is diagnostically useful.

---

## 7. Marillier control: the selector distinction is shared, the serial case use is the Lovejoy-level delta

Batch203 recovered Marillier 1901’s own primary distinctions:

- automatic consequences of taboo/contact;
- group danger through `solidarité de clan`;
- precautionary social reaction;
- moral culpability/remorse.

Mauss 1896 shows that magical/social/penal decomposition was already public before Lovejoy’s Paris seminar participation.

Therefore p.18 cannot support a claim that Lovejoy invented clan-solidarity analysis.

But the notebook remains distinctive as a documentary object because it uses **one borderline case** to traverse several available explanations.

The proper attribution split is:

> `selector vocabulary / distinction = substantially field-shared`  
> `serial reallocation of suicide across selectors = directly visible Lovejoy notebook operation`.

This is an application claim, not a priority claim.

---

## 8. PDF p.19: the case can later acquire a genuinely different historical layer

At p.19 Lovejoy introduces time into the selector trajectory.

In later periods, moral ideas can infiltrate from:

- a `higher stage of culture`;
- historical contact;
- missionary influence.

Suicide can then genuinely come to occupy a specially punitive post-mortem position.

This is not merely another competing explanation of the same synchronic evidence. It allows the **same practice/category to change historical relation**:

`earlier suicide sanction`

→ social/ritual/non-moral mechanism

while

`later suicide sanction`

→ moralized punishment under imported or newly developed conceptions.

Thus one named case may carry different selector-types at different historical layers.

This is a particularly clear 005 instance of the chronological non-isomorphism established in Batches208–209.

---

## 9. PDF p.20: `desert` functions as a stopping rule, not the source of the taxonomy

At p.20 Lovejoy writes of a `half-moral character` and then gives the sharpest diagnostic rule:

> an afterlife does not become moral until fate / punishment / reward depends on **desert**.

Batches206–207 show that neither transitional morality nor the moral/non-moral criterion can securely be credited to Lovejoy as an invention. Söderblom already uses moral/quasi-moral language and rejects the retrospective recoding of wealth, strength, death-type, burial or ritual qualification as desert.

What is directly distinctive in 005 is the role of `desert` inside the running procedure.

It acts as a **stopping criterion**:

- if the selector is status → keep reclassifying;
- if ritual qualification → keep reclassifying;
- if custom/taboo → keep reclassifying;
- if group/clan consequence → keep reclassifying;
- if contact/imported layer → date and separate the layers;
- only if allocation is actually keyed to deservingness does the label `moral retribution` become admissible.

So `desert` is best treated as an operational coding threshold, not as an original Lovejoyan moral concept.

---

## 10. The dynamic taxonomy differs from a simple evolutionary stage sequence

005 unquestionably contains developmental vocabulary such as:

- `higher stage of culture`;
- `more developed peoples`;
- primitive / intermediate / later moralized forms.

Batch219 does not erase that language.

But the suicide sequence shows another simultaneous architecture:

### Static-stage reading

`ritual → social → half-moral → moral`.

### Dynamic-case reading

`one apparent punishment`

→ test ritual cause

→ test custom/taboo

→ test clan/social injury

→ test source provenance

→ test later historical overlay

→ admit moral classification only at desert threshold.

The second architecture is not reducible to the first. It is a **diagnostic algorithm over candidate relations**.

This matters for the larger project because `primitive` can then function not only as a point on a whole-population ladder but as an address assigned to whichever relation survives the selector test as older / non-moral / more basic.

## 11. Direct connection to the 1909→1910 Kant self-revision

Batches217–218 document a published before/after event:

`1909 Kant = evolutionist`

→ closer source investigation

→ `1910 evolution` split into cosmic / anthropological / biological-transformist / organic-mechanistic predicates.

The 005 suicide sequence is **not the same kind of evidence**.

- In 1909→1910 we have explicit authorial self-correction across publications.
- In 005 we currently have successive notebook pages testing one case under different relations.
- Until the original page-images are audited, we do **not** claim visible crossings-out or a pen-level correction event.

The structural continuity is nevertheless precise:

> **a broad observer-visible category is treated as provisional; closer discrimination splits the predicate and reallocates the object by relation.**

For suicide:

`punishment` → ritual/social/contact/moral predicates.

For Kant:

`evolutionist` → multiple non-isomorphic evolution predicates.

The common output is not skepticism for its own sake. It is **smaller classificatory units**.

## 14. Strong current formulation

> **Lovejoy’s early survivance notes are better read as a dynamic classification procedure than as the invention of a new taxonomy. The relevant discriminants — status, ritual qualification, manner of death, clan consequence, moral judgment and historical contamination — were substantially available in the Marillier workshop. What the notebook makes unusually visible is their serial application to a single ambiguous case. Suicide moves from a mere manner-of-death category through custom, taboo, clan solidarity and social exclusion; later moralization is then separated as a historical overlay, while `desert` functions as the threshold for admitting genuinely moral retribution. The categories are inherited to a significant degree; the documentary delta lies in reallocation.**

Project shorthand:

> **shared categories, revisable case allocation.**
