## Final-batch result

The end of notebook 005 does not suddenly introduce `mana`, `wakonda`, `orenda`, `manitou`, or an explicit heading corresponding to the 1906 article. It nevertheless strengthens the underlying methodological bridge.

### p.106–109 — cannibalism decomposed by mechanism

Lovejoy refuses to infer religious or sacramental meaning from ceremony, repetition, publicity, or collective feasting alone. He separates famine, revenge, affection, acquisition of qualities, sympathetic magic, propitiation, and sacramental union as distinct possible mechanisms for anthropophagy.

This is the same analytical habit visible in the 1906 problem: an outward practice/category must be decomposed until the kind of efficacy or causal relation involved becomes clear.

### p.110–111 — propitiation versus communion

Human sacrifice to water or hostile spirits is classified as propitiatory: a dangerous being receives a victim to avert harm. Lovejoy contrasts this with communion or sacramental feasting directed toward a friendly/protective deity. Similar visible actions can therefore belong to different causal/religious structures.

### p.110 — anti-stadial argument

Lovejoy rejects a universal human-sacrifice → animal-substitution sequence as poorly demonstrated. Limited substitution cases may exist, but he also considers changing social/economic valuation of human life. Again, he refuses to infer a single evolutionary law from formal resemblance.

### p.112–116 — initiation is not sacrifice by default

Circumcision is explicitly rejected as a partial human sacrifice. Whipping, scarification, seclusion, menstruation restrictions, secret-society ordeals, and other initiation practices are analyzed through incorporation, purification, fortification, blood-fraternity, social regulation, and changed status.

### p.117 — synthetic conclusion on sacrifice

This is one of the most important pages in the entire notebook. Lovejoy concludes that **alimentary** and **expiatory/propitiatory** sacrifices occur frequently, whereas **sacramental union** is comparatively exceptional. This directly attacks a single communion theory of sacrifice.

The page confirms that notebook 005 is ultimately a project in **classification by mechanism**, not a collection of exotic ritual examples.

### p.118 — funerary killing

Companion-killing and burial of servants/retainers are classified as provision, communication, status-continuity, fear/propitiation, or later rationalization, rather than automatically as mystical union.

### p.119 — transferable efficacy closes the main argument

Lovejoy returns to eating selected body parts and the belief that qualities can be transferred:

- head/brain → intelligence;
- heart → courage/life;
- sexual organs → sexual power.

He classifies this primarily as sympathetic / transferable efficacy, not sufficient evidence of sacramental cannibalism. This is a particularly strong conceptual bridge to the 1906 concern with communicable power/efficacy.

The sustained comparative-religion argument ends here. The loose slip on p.119 and back-leaf memorandum on p.120 are physically separate fragments.

## Updated judgment

Completion of the notebook strengthens but does not close the 005→1906 case.

What is now strong:

- the entire latter half of 005 is organized by causal/ritual mechanisms;
- `quasi-mechanical` appears explicitly at p.105;

- sacramental/communion explanations are treated as one special mechanism among several, not the master key;
- Lovejoy repeatedly attacks false unities, premature evolutionary sequences, and inference from outward form.
