# Lovejoy 1906 `logic` of practices vs 1910 formal logic — two-axis semantic control

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **CANONICAL CONTEXT-DIFFERENTIATION REFINEMENT / LEXICAL FALLACY CONTROL**

Primary controls:

- Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`, *The Monist* 16.3 (1906), 357–382, especially pp.359–361, 378–382;
- Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Treatment of 'Opposition' in Formal Logic`, *Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* 7 (1910), 101–105.

Field controls:

- R. R. Marett, *The Threshold of Religion* (1914 ed.), Preface, discussing Lovejoy's 1906 objection;
- contemporary/near-contemporary comparative-religion and anthropology repertoires, including L. Marillier's mechanism-sensitive classification of rites;
- modern historiography of `mana`, which places Lovejoy's 1906 intervention in the Tylorian/intellectualist debate over explanation, causation, efficacy and rationality.

## Core correction

The presence of the actor word `logic` in Lovejoy is not sufficient to code a passage as either:

- formal logic;
- explicit logical analysis as a method.

The 1906 primitive-philosophy paper is the decisive lexical control.

Lovejoy says the heterogeneous and apparently contradictory body of practices/beliefs is not haphazard superstition: it possesses a **`logic` that can be unraveled**. He aims to make the scattered customs **`connectedly intelligible`** by reconstructing an underlying conception of causation: impersonal, diffusive, transferable and quasi-mechanical efficacy.

In this local context, `logic` is best read as:

> **a latent causal-conceptual order imputed to/reconstructed within the historical-ethnographic object.**

It is not the same local sense as the 1910 formal-logical paper, where Lovejoy constructs an exhaustive denotative relation-space and derives proposition-level inference rules.

Therefore the previous one-dimensional `A1/A2/A3` coding needs a second axis.

---

# I. Why 1906 is not simply `formal logic applied to primitive religion`

## 1. The object is comparative religion / ethnographic causal belief

The paper's stated problem is to reinterpret a large field of rites, terms and beliefs conventionally explained through anthropomorphism, animism, personality or the supernatural.

The proposed category is a theory/conception of **causation**:

- impersonal;
- quasi-mechanical;
- diffused/localized in persons and objects;
- communicable/transferable;
- regulable/insulatable;
- practically manipulable through rites.

The relevant historical task is:

`scattered language + custom + ritual expectation`
→ reconstruct the causal conception that makes them connectedly intelligible.

There is no Vennian case-space, categorical opposition calculus, or formal proposition theory doing the primary work.

---

## 2. `Logic` is predicated of the material, not named as the historian's technical discipline

The crucial grammar is not:

> `I shall apply formal logic to these ethnographic materials.`

It is approximately:

> these apparently heterogeneous practices **have a logic** that can be reconstructed/unraveled.

The locus of `logic` is therefore the **historical object as analytically reconstructed**.

Lovejoy explicitly recognizes an analyst/actor distance: the resulting propositions may be more abstract, systematic and coherent than historical actors themselves could formulate, but he argues that the consistency can be inferred from language and custom.

Thus the operation is:

`actor practices / vocabularies`
→ `historian reconstructs latent consistency`
→ `causal-conceptual system attributed back to primitive thought`.

This is a major Gate-A problem, because the historian's abstraction must be warranted before it can be re-addressed as actor-level `philosophy`.

---

# II. Field context: the dispute is about explanation/efficacy, not formal-logical technique

## Marett

Marett later summarizes Lovejoy's objection as a disagreement over which side of mana should receive explanatory priority:

- Marett: supernormal / mysterious / affective shock;
- Lovejoy: efficacious / powerful / productive.

Lovejoy's point is that the force is mysterious because it is powerful, rather than powerful because it first strikes the actor as mysterious/supernormal.

This is a dispute about:

- causal interpretation;
- affect versus efficacy;
- explanatory priority;
- what kind of historical psychology/religious conception is being reconstructed.

It is not a dispute over formal-logical validity.

## Modern mana historiography

Recent historical work accurately characterizes Lovejoy's 1906 position as remaining within a Tylorian/intellectualist paradigm: so-called primitive religion is treated in terms of explanation, causation and rational/intelligible order. Lovejoy's mana/energetics is a `theory and praxis of causation`, working according to regular/intelligible laws.

This provides a field-level semantic control for the word `logic`.

## Marillier / EPHE

Lovejoy's Paris comparative-religion environment already classified outwardly similar rites by different operative reasons/mechanisms: expiatory, funerary, anthropophagic, magical, fecundative, etc. The 1898–99 EPHE report explicitly records Lovejoy as taking an active part in Marillier's work.

This makes mechanism-sensitive connectedness locally available without requiring a formal-logic source.

Do not infer that Marillier supplied the word `logic` itself; the point is field repertoire, not lexical transmission.

---

# III. 1910 is a different local sense

In `The Treatment of 'Opposition' in Formal Logic`, logic itself is the explicit disciplinary object.

Lovejoy:

- enumerates five mutually exclusive and jointly exhaustive denotative relations between two terms/classes;
- maps categorical propositions onto subsets of compatible cases;
- derives inference from inclusion, overlap, disjointness and exhaustion;
- explicitly suspends judgment when multiple compatible cases survive;
- cites Venn and Schröder and situates the exercise inside symbolic/algebraic logic.

This is:

`formal relation-space`
→ `proposition compatibility`
→ `licensed inference`.

The semantic and disciplinary difference from 1906 is therefore substantial.

---

# IV. Replace one-axis logic coding with two axes

The previous branch used:

- `A1 logic-object`;
- `A2 explicit-logical-method`;
- `A3 analyst-resemblance / logic-shaped`.

This remains useful but conflates **locus** with **semantic sense**.

Use two independent axes instead.

## Axis 1 — LOCUS / STATUS OF `LOGIC`

### `O` — logic as disciplinary object

The text is about logic itself.

Examples:
- 1907 Kant judgment-table paper, mixed logic-object/history-of-logic;
- 1910 opposition paper;
- 1911 existence/formal-logic jurisdiction paper.

### `M` — explicit logical analysis as historian/philosopher's method

The object is not logic, but Lovejoy explicitly invokes logical analysis/relation-testing as a procedure.

Examples:
- 1902 `logical import`;
- 1904 `logically and historically`;
- 1908 `logically independent`;
- 1909 `logical analysis and historical comparison`;
- 1941 historian's logical-relation analysis.

### `I` — immanent/latent `logic` attributed to the historical object

Lovejoy reconstructs a logic/order/coherence said to be embodied in doctrines/practices, even if actors do not explicitly formulate it.

Control example:
- **1906 primitive philosophy: heterogeneous practices `have a logic` that can be unraveled; causal order reconstructed from language/custom.**

### `R` — analyst-only structural resemblance

The present project judges an operation `logic-shaped`, but Lovejoy does not himself invoke logic in the relevant passage/context.

Examples:
- portions of 1898 notebook relation partition;
- source/warrant matrices where logic attribution is not actor-explicit.

These loci must never be collapsed.

# V. Axis 2 — SEMANTIC MODE OF `LOGICAL/LOGIC`

Recommended current taxonomy:

### `F` — formal/propositional/denotative

- class relations;
- implication;
- opposition;
- compatibility/incompatibility;
- exhaustive possibility-space.

Control: 1910.

### `C` — conceptual/dialectical

- logical import of a doctrine;
- conceptual position in a problem-space;
- implication/presupposition among philosophical claims.

Controls: 1902, 1904, 1941 in part.

### `K` — causal-systemic / connected intelligibility

- a set of practices/beliefs has an underlying causal-conceptual order;
- reconstructing the rules/presuppositions that make heterogeneous phenomena intelligible together.

Control: **1906 primitive philosophy**.

(`K` is project notation for causal/systemic coherence, chosen to avoid collision with `C`.)

### `P` — psychological/associative relation contrasted with logic

Usually not itself `logic`, but necessary as a contrast category in 1924/1941.

### `MC` — metacognitive / analytical self-consciousness

`logical self-consciousness` = explicit scrutiny of assumptions/implications.

Control: 1936.

The taxonomy can grow if primary texts require it. It should not be treated as a fixed ontology.

# VII. Why 1906 matters for the unit-idea genealogy

The 1906 operation is historically important precisely because it is **not formal logic**.

Lovejoy does three things:

1. reconstructs an immanent causal order from dispersed practices and terms;
2. treats that order as sufficiently coherent to form a comparative category;
3. reattributes the abstraction to collective actors as a `fundamental concept` / `primitive philosophy`.

This is a Gate-A aggregation operation:

`heterogeneous tokens`
→ `latent causal-systemic invariant`
→ `comparative type`
→ `actor-level philosophy`.

Its chief vulnerability lies in aggregation/reattribution, not formal invalidity.

That is why later Jung can accept much of Lovejoy's efficacy evidence while objecting that the abstract `concept of primitive philosophy` is produced by the modern analyst's mentality.

---

# VIII. Connection to relation-indexed evidentiary valence

The 1906 paper also confirms the newer governing metarule.

The same historical independence of North-American, Oceanic and African fields:

- weakens a common-transmission genealogy;
- strengthens Lovejoy's claim that a recurrent causal type is independently instantiated.

This is not formal logic, but it has a logician-like structural feature:

> the inferential force of evidence depends on the relation-predicate under test.

The historical domain supplies the evidence and the category; formal logic is only one later arena in which a comparable restriction of inferential transfer becomes technically explicit.

---

# IX. Revised branch firewall

Never infer from the word `logic` alone:

`logic vocabulary`
→ `formal logic`
→ `formal-logical method`
→ `formal logic caused the historical analysis`.

Instead ask in order:

1. Where is `logic` located: object, explicit method, immanent historical order, or analyst resemblance?
2. What local semantic work does it perform?
3. What disciplinary problem is being solved?
4. What evidence would be required to show transfer between contexts?

## Current shorthand

> **In 1906 Lovejoy says ethnographic practices have a `logic` that can be unraveled, but the local meaning is a reconstructed causal-systemic intelligibility of belief and rite, situated in the intellectualist anthropology/religion debate. In 1910 logic is the disciplinary object and the operation is formal denotative case-space inference. The shared word cannot carry methodological identity across the two contexts.**
