# Batch 99 — From marked ellipsis to inherited quotation packet: Codrington 1891 → Durkheim 1912 → Swain 1915 → Hardy 1966

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: test the highest-value implication of Batch97: whether Durkheim's shortened Codrington quotation remained an isolated source operation or became a transmissible quotation object. Primary/near-primary controls are Codrington 1891, contemporary pre-Durkheim reprints that preserve the personal-origin clause, Durkheim 1912, Swain's 1915 English translation, and Alister Hardy's 1966 *The Divine Flame* / Gifford Lecture III.

## Core result

Batch97 showed that Durkheim's quotation of Codrington visibly ellipses the sentence stating that spirits possess/impart mana and that mana `essentially belongs to personal beings to originate it`. Batch99 now establishes that this shortened quotation did not remain local to Durkheim's French book.

J. W. Swain's 1915 English translation preserves the same shortened structure:

`force distinct from physical power`
→ `mana`
→ `nonphysical / supernatural / physical manifestation`
→ `not fixed / conveyable`
→ **ellipsis**
→ `all Melanesian religion consists in getting/using mana`.

More than fifty years later, Alister Hardy, in Lecture III of *The Divine Flame*, explicitly announces that his Durkheim quotations come from Swain's 1915 translation, then reproduces the same Codrington packet in the same theoretical sequence. The personal-origin clause remains absent; Hardy follows the quotation immediately with Durkheim's `same anonymous and diffused force` / `same impersonality` commentary.

This creates a documentable transmission chain:

`Codrington full hybrid statement`
→ `Durkheim marked truncation`
→ `Swain preserves truncation in English`
→ `Hardy inherits the truncated packet while presenting Durkheim`.

The key result is therefore:

> **a selective quotation can become a portable documentary object whose omissions travel with it.**

Or:

> **citation shadow can be inherited materially, not merely conceptually.**

This is stronger than generic influence. The quotation's textual shape itself is transmitted.

---

## 1. Pre-Durkheim controls show that the full personal-origin clause was independently circulating

The omitted sentence was not an obscure clause available only by consulting Codrington's full monograph.

### 1892 — *Popular Science Monthly*

A short notice on `Melanesian Ghosts` reproduces the mana definition with the personal-origin clause intact: spirits have and impart mana; mana essentially belongs to personal beings to originate; it may act through water, stone or bone; Melanesian religion consists in getting/using the power.

### 1911 — *Encyclopædia Britannica*, `Taboo`

The Britannica article likewise reproduces Codrington's definition with the adverse clause intact. It then develops an electrical-charge analogy around taboo and mana.

### 1911 — *Encyclopædia Britannica*, `Religion`

Another Britannica treatment goes further: under `the sacred as the potent`, it specifically quotes Codrington's statement that mana `essentially belongs to personal beings to originate it`, before moving to Melanesian religious practice.

These controls matter because they show that the hybrid Codrington packet—impersonal efficacy plus personal origination—was a normal quotable object immediately before Durkheim.

Thus Durkheim did not merely inherit a field convention in which the personal-origin sentence had already vanished from all circulation.

### Code

`1891 source`
→ `1892 full public reprint`
→ `1911 full reference-work reprints`
→ **personal-origin clause still visibly available**.

This strengthens the significance of the 1912 ellipsis without licensing a claim about Durkheim's private intent.

---

## 2. Swain 1915 carries the Durkheim quotation architecture into English

Swain's translation does not restore Codrington's omitted sentence. The translated quotation retains Durkheim's selection and ellipsis.

This is important for reception history because the English-speaking reader of *The Elementary Forms of the Religious Life* now encounters a Codrington passage already transformed by Durkheim's proof requirements.

The textual inheritance is:

### Codrington 1891

`mobile impersonal power`
+
`spirits possess/impart`
+
`personal beings originate`
+
`material mediation`
+
`religious manipulation`.

### Durkheim/Swain packet

`mobile impersonal power`
+
`religious manipulation`

with the personal-origin bridge absent from the quoted packet.

Immediately after the quotation, Swain preserves Durkheim's interpretive closure: `same anonymous and diffused force`, `same impersonality`, and the argument that the more generalized mana validates the Australian totemic principle.

So Swain is not merely translating individual sentences. He transmits a **quotation architecture plus its inferential adjacency**.

---

## 3. Hardy 1966 gives a direct downstream inheritance case

Alister Hardy's *The Divine Flame* supplies unusually clean provenance.

At the beginning of his extended Durkheim discussion, Hardy states that **all the quotations he will make from Durkheim are from J. W. Swain's 1915 English translation**.

He then reconstructs Durkheim's comparative argument:

- Australian totemism;
- Sioux `wakan`;
- Fletcher's common-life formulation;
- mana as exact equivalent of wakan/orenda;
- `He quotes from Codrington's study The Melanesians (p. 118) as follows:`;
- the same shortened Codrington passage;
- `Durkheim now comments on Codrington's view of Mana:`;
- the same `anonymous and diffused force / same impersonality` interpretation.

The nested provenance is therefore explicit:

`Hardy`
→ presenting `Durkheim`
→ through `Swain 1915`
→ who contains Durkheim's shortened quotation of `Codrington`.

This is not a speculative source pathway inferred from verbal similarity. Hardy identifies the intermediate translation himself.

### Result

The omitted personal-origin clause has now survived at least two transformations:

1. French authorial excerpt → English translation;
2. English translation → mid-century restatement/quotation in natural theology.

The omission becomes part of what a later reader receives as **the Codrington passage relevant to Durkheim's mana**.

---

## 4. This is `citation-shadow inheritance` in a literal documentary sense

Batch91 used `citation shadow` for a broader reception situation in which a source/problem field can travel without a visible direct citation edge.

Batch99 gives the term a stricter documentary meaning.

Here the shadow has a reproducible textual object:

`full source passage`
→ `selective canonical excerpt`
→ `translated excerpt`
→ `later re-quotation of excerpt`.

The downstream reader may know the authority `Codrington`, the title *The Melanesians*, and even the page number while still receiving only a propositionally filtered slice of what that page says.

Thus bibliographic specificity does not guarantee proposition-level fidelity.

A useful formula:

> **exact citation can coexist with inherited source reduction.**

And:

> **citation accuracy and evidentiary completeness are independent variables.**

This belongs directly with Batches77 and 89, where author/source authority was shown to be proposition-relative.

---

## 5. The omission propagates together with its interpretive frame

The most important feature is that the shortened quotation does not travel alone.

In Durkheim/Swain it is immediately followed by:

- `anonymous and diffused force`;
- `same impersonality`;
- `same ubiquity`;
- equivalence with wakan/orenda;
- cross-cultural validation of the Australian force.

Hardy reproduces not only the shortened quotation but the same adjacent Durkheim commentary.

Therefore what travels is better represented as a **quotation–interpretation packet**:

`selected primary-source words`
+
`authoritative interpretive gloss`
+
`theoretical function`.

The packet can then be cited as though it were simply `Codrington on mana`, when historically it is:

`Codrington filtered through Durkheim's warrant structure`.

This is a more exact model of how classic ethnographic sentences become disciplinary commonplaces.

---

## 6. Hardy is especially revealing because his project reverses the ideological use while retaining the packet

Durkheim used the shortened Codrington passage in a sociological proof of impersonal religious force and ultimately society as the real referent.

Hardy uses Durkheim in a very different intellectual project: a mid-twentieth-century `natural history of religion` aimed at finding evidence for a real religious/spiritual dimension in human life.

He is sympathetic to Durkheim and Marett, and the lecture eventually recruits anthropology into Hardy's natural-theological project.

Yet despite this major change in endpoint, the **documentary packet remains stable**:

`wakan/common life`
→ `mana as exact equivalent`
→ shortened Codrington
→ `same impersonality`.

This demonstrates that quotation packets can survive changes in theoretical destination.

A useful reception rule:

> **documentary form may be more stable than theoretical use.**

This parallels the earlier finding that Lovejoy's evidence dossier could survive rejection of Lovejoy's causal thesis.

---

## 7. Pre-Durkheim full quotations act as a negative control on the inheritance chain

The 1892 and 1911 reprints are essential because otherwise one might claim that Hardy's shortened quotation simply reflects the standard abbreviated form of Codrington already circulating everywhere.

But the historical record now shows at least two competing quotation forms before/around Durkheim:

### Full hybrid packet

`impersonal transferable power`
+
`spirits impart`
+
`personal beings originate`
+
`material media`.

### Durkheim packet

`impersonal transferable power`
→ ellipsis
→ `religious acquisition/use`.

The latter then receives a very clear transmission route through Swain to Hardy.

Thus the project can distinguish:

- **field-wide quotation availability**;
- **lineage-specific quotation inheritance**.

That distinction will matter in future citation-network work.

---

## 8. This provides a method for detecting documentary lineages without overclaiming influence

A robust `quotation packet fingerprint` can use:

1. identical start/end boundaries;
2. identical ellipsis position;
3. same omitted proposition;
4. same surrounding interpretive sentences;
5. explicit intermediary attribution where available.

Hardy meets all five at high confidence because he explicitly frames the passage as Durkheim's use of Codrington and names Swain as his Durkheim translation.

This is a much stronger transmission indicator than shared words such as `mana`, `force`, `mechanical`, or `impersonal`.

The broader methodological shift is therefore:

> **from lexical fingerprinting to excerpt-boundary fingerprinting.**

Or:

> **omissions can function as positive evidence of textual descent.**

That is a useful principle well beyond this one case.

---

## 9. Relation to Lovejoy

This chain does not establish any direct Lovejoy → Durkheim → Hardy transmission.

Its importance for the Lovejoy project is comparative and methodological.

Lovejoy 1906 uses the same Codrington source but, as Batch97 showed, preserves the adverse personal-origin proposition as part of the source problem and reweights the semantic axis toward efficacy/power/productivity.

Durkheim's branch instead yields a stable excerpt in which that directional contradiction is absent. Once translated and inherited, later readers can encounter `Codrington on mana` through a Durkheimian documentary object.

The two branches can be represented as:

### Lovejoy branch

`Codrington full mixed witness`
→ proposition-relative reweighting.

### Durkheim branch

`Codrington full mixed witness`
→ marked truncation
→ Swain translation
→ Hardy re-quotation.

This is a genuine **branching textual history of one ethnographic authority**.

---

## 10. Paper-level formulation

A strong paragraph now available is:

> **The evidentiary transformation of Codrington's mana did not end with Durkheim. Contemporary reprints show that the sentence making personal beings the originators of mana remained fully quotable in 1892 and in the 1911 Encyclopædia Britannica. Durkheim's 1912 excerpt, by contrast, visibly ellipsed the passage between mana's conveyability and the claim that Melanesian religion consists in acquiring or directing it, thereby excluding the statements that spirits impart mana and that personal beings originate it. Swain's 1915 English translation preserved that excerpt boundary. Half a century later Alister Hardy, explicitly working from Swain while presenting Durkheim, reproduced the same shortened Codrington passage and immediately followed it with Durkheim's gloss of mana as the `same` anonymous, diffused, and impersonal force. The omission had therefore become transmissible: not an absence in the archive, but a feature of a lineage-specific quotation packet.**

Compact formulation:

> **The omission became part of the quotation.**

And methodologically:

> **excerpt boundaries can carry provenance.**

## Evidence limits

1. Hardy's reproduction securely follows his Durkheim/Swain exposition, but this does not prove he never separately consulted Codrington.
2. The claim is textual descent of a quotation packet, not intellectual dependence for Hardy's whole theory of religion.
3. The 1892 and 1911 controls establish availability of the full clause, not that Durkheim read those exact publications.
4. Ellipsis remains openly marked at each stage; the issue is inherited proposition selection, not concealed textual alteration.
5. Search-based detection of additional intermediate re-quotations is incomplete. The established chain is sufficient for one secure inheritance case but should not be treated as exhaustive.

## High-value next tests

1. Search 1915–1966 books/articles for the **same excerpt boundaries and ellipsis position** to determine whether Hardy is one node in a broader Durkheim/Swain quotation family.
2. Test whether `Fletcher common life` travels in the same way through Durkheim/Swain/Hardy. Hardy already reproduces Fletcher via Durkheim; exact source-vs-excerpt collation could show whether a larger North-American evidence packet is inherited alongside the Codrington truncation.
3. Build a `canonical quotation packet` register for Lovejoy-adjacent authorities (Codrington, Fletcher, Hewitt, Jones, Hetherwick) so later reception can be classified by **which textual version of an authority** is actually circulating.
