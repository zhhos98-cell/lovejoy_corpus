## 2. What is no longer available as a Lovejoy originality claim

After source control, the following propositions must **not** carry the burden of Lovejoy's distinctiveness:

- non-moral afterlife determinants such as rank, occupation, mode of death, skill, ritual qualification, burial or sacrifice;
- honorific / expiatory / mystical-sacramental sacrifice as basic class families;
- sacrifice as magical force or magical compulsion;
- magic as impersonal, direct or quasi-mechanical action on nature;
- vitality transfer from god-man to successor;
- blood/common meal as kinship or communion mechanism;
- animal→human substitution as a possible historical route;
- human→animal substitution as a possible historical route;
- object-soul transmission through funerary destruction;
- blood as life, strength, curative or transferable efficacy;
- circumcision as blood covenant / fraternity / incorporation;
- strict fetishism as requiring a spirit-object relation;
- charms or magical objects with mechanisms not reducible to indwelling spirit;
- agrarian/fecundative sacrifice, vegetation spirit, corn spirit in animal/plant/human forms;
- multiple mechanisms of ritual anthropophagy;
- initiation as status transition involving seclusion, mutilation, ordeal, purification and group incorporation;
- survivance of rites after original belief changes.

These belong, in varying combinations, to Marillier, Robertson Smith, Frazer, Tylor, Trumbull, Mannhardt, Cameron, Grout, Kingsley, Lang and the wider ethnographic field.

This correction is substantive. It removes the temptation to treat notebook density as proof of theoretical invention.

## 3. What remains distinctive after subtraction

The surviving 005 operator is narrower and stronger:

### A. Source evaluation

p.49 turns comparative reading itself into an evidentiary hierarchy:

`SOURCE AUTHORITY -> HOW FAR DOES THEORY EXCEED EVIDENCE?`

The explicit Tylor note makes this an actor-level norm rather than a retrospective characterization.

### B. Case reclassification

p.55 and pp.79–80 show that inherited ethnographic cases can be reassigned:

`SURFACE FORM / SOURCE DESCRIPTION`
->
`WHAT MECHANISM ACTUALLY FITS?`

Examples:

- cannibalism can be `not ritual but alimentary`;
- an ancestral cow rite can be transactional without being deferred payment and can simultaneously instantiate substitution/scapegoat logic.

### C. Mechanism indexed by social bearer

p.66 adds a relation not supplied merely by naming ritual types:

`MECHANISM -> WHO IS COMPETENT / RESPONSIBLE TO PERFORM IT?`

Collective expiation and specialist magical technique have different social jurisdictions.

### D. Historical-form reinterpretation

p.65 and p.73 distinguish a continuing outward practice from the elements or rationales presently fused within it:

`SAME / CONTINUING FORM != SAME ORIGINAL MEANING`.

The novelty claim is not survivance theory; it is the local analytical reassignment performed on particular cases.

### E. Rival-path testing

pp.103–104 are among the most important post-insert seams:

`PROPOSED RITUAL GENEALOGY`
+
`INDEPENDENT CHRONOLOGY / SOCIAL CONDITIONS`
->
`EXCLUDE UNSUPPORTED PATH`
->
`RETAIN CASE-SPECIFIC ROUTE`.

This is stronger than saying Lovejoy `reverses an evolutionary arrow`. The page tests whether a path is historically licit.

### F. Source adjudication between competing explanations

p.112 gives a particularly clean instance:

`PARTIAL-HUMAN-SACRIFICE THEORY OF CIRCUMCISION`
-> **REJECT**

`INITIATION / BLOOD-COVENANT / INCORPORATION`
-> **PREFER**.

The categories are inherited; the judgment among them is local.

### G. Alternative-mechanism fork

p.119 asks whether selected body parts work because:

- each organ is the seat of a specific desired property; or
- a part stands for the whole and transfers general force.

Again the source field supplies both ingredients; the notebook's interest lies in keeping rival explanatory relations distinct.

`1906 REAGGREGATES ONE FAMILY OF NON-PERSONAL / QUASI-MECHANICAL RELATIONS AT A BROADER COMPARATIVE SCALE`.
