# Batch 133 — Lévi 1898 return-route chronology audit: Vimy is hard; the 'Saigon on the way back' gloss is not

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: audit an apparent contradiction that emerged after Batch132. A modern web page paraphrasing Victor Goloubew says Sylvain Lévi stopped in Saigon while returning from Japan 'at the end of 1898' and there persuaded Paul Doumer to create a permanent archaeological mission. That chronology conflicts with the primary Paris/France anchors now assembled around Lovejoy's October–December 1898 Paris letters.

## Core result

The contradiction is real, but it can be localized.

The **hard chronology** remains intact:

1. Lévi's own 1899 travel account describes the return corridor from **Nagasaki to Moscow via Vladivostok and Siberia**.
2. Current AGORHA institutional reconstruction dates the first mission to **3 October 1897–September 1898** and likewise says he returned via Russia.
3. The EPHE annual report records **Sylvain Lévi physically at Vimy, Pas-de-Calais, on 9 October 1898**, representing the École pratique at the inauguration of Abel Bergaigne's monument.
4. Wilson's earliest cited Lovejoy Paris letter is 17 October 1898.

Therefore the statement that Lévi was still 'returning from Japan at the end of 1898' and then stopped in Saigon **cannot be used as chronology**.

What survives is a different, weaker proposition. Victor Goloubew's 1935 retrospective does contain a narrative in which a short Saigon stop and a conversation between Lévi and Paul Doumer helped bring about a permanent archaeological mission in Indochina. But the currently recovered quotation does **not itself provide an exact date for that stop**. The phrase 'while traveling back from Japan at the end of 1898' is the framing supplied by the modern Angkor Database page, not wording we have yet verified in Goloubew's original pages.

The correct treatment is therefore:

> **retain the Lévi–Doumer/Saigon episode as a retrospective institutional-history lead; demote the modern 'end of 1898 return from Japan' dating; do not let it displace the primary 9 October Vimy anchor.**

This matters to Lovejoy because Batch132's compressed chronology — Lévi back in France by 9 October, Lovejoy's cited correspondence beginning 17 October — remains supported by a contemporary institutional record.

---

## 1. Hard anchor: Lévi is in France on 9 October 1898

The EPHE Annuaire's report on the inauguration of the Abel Bergaigne monument states that the ceremony took place at Vimy on **9 October 1898**. It then explicitly lists the École pratique's representatives, including:

`G. Monod, Emile Chatelain, S. Lévi, L. Finot ...`

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1900_num_1_1_2270

This is not a reconstructed travel date. It is a named participant in a dated French public ceremony. It is therefore the strongest present return terminus.

Consequently:

`Lévi in France no later than 9 Oct 1898`

is secure.

---

## 2. Lévi's own published return route runs north through Russia

Lévi's 1899 article is titled:

`De Nagasaki à Moscou par la Sibérie`.

The opening page describes the Japan-to-Siberia route, including Nagasaki, Vladivostok and the maritime connections used to enter the Russian Far East.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/geo_0003-4010_1899_num_8_40_6122

The article does not, in the currently exposed OCR opening, supply the calendar dates needed for a day-level itinerary. But it is direct first-person evidence that the final homeward leg from Japan was conceptualized and traveled as the northern Siberian route.

AGORHA independently synthesizes the mission as 1897–1898, India–Nepal–Japan, followed by return through Russia. That institutional record gives the event interval as `03/10/1897–09/1898`.

Institutional authority source:

https://agorha.inha.fr/detail/759

Thus the final-return model used in Batch132 remains:

`Japan → Vladivostok / Siberia → Moscow → France`

rather than:

`Japan → Saigon → France`.

---

## 3. What Goloubew actually contributes at present

Victor Goloubew's 1935 memorial essay `Sylvain Lévi et l'Indochine` is securely identified on Persée, pp.550–574.

Source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/befeo_0336-1519_1935_num_35_1_3981

A modern page reproduces a substantial Goloubew passage in which he says that a brief stop in the capital of Cochinchina mattered because, during an interview between Lévi and Paul Doumer at the Saigon Government Palace, the creation of a permanent archaeological mission was decided. The passage then moves to the 15 December 1898 order creating the Mission archéologique de l'Indo-Chine and the appointment of Louis Finot.

The quote is historically important, but two evidentiary layers must now be separated:

### Goloubew-level claim

`Saigon stop + Lévi–Doumer interview → retrospective narrative of institutional genesis`.

### Modern Angkor framing

`while traveling back from Japan at the end of 1898`.

The second is not currently verified as Goloubew's wording. It directly conflicts with Vimy on 9 October and the Siberian return route. It is therefore **demoted**.

This does not prove Goloubew's Saigon episode is false. A Saigon visit could in principle belong to another point in the Asian itinerary, or the later memorial could compress several stages of institutional negotiation. Exact dating requires direct collation of Goloubew's surrounding pages or another contemporary travel source.

---

## 4. Primary December 1898 administrative record does not require a late-1898 Lévi presence in Saigon

The Académie's contemporary record for 9 December 1898 reproduces a ministerial letter dated **8 December 1898**. The Minister states that the Governor-General of Indochina proposes founding a permanent archaeological mission and asks the Académie to accept scientific patronage.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_1898_num_42_6_71307

The recovered text names the Governor-General's proposal, the projected remit, the Académie's role and the mechanism for appointing a director. It does **not** name Lévi.

The formal 15 December 1898 order then cites the Académie's opinion of 9 December.

Primary regulatory witnesses:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/befeo_0336-1519_1901_num_1_1_2167

https://education.persee.fr/doc/revin_1775-6014_1899_num_38_2_4032

This means there is no administrative necessity to place Lévi in Saigon in December 1898. The formal creation sequence can be reconstructed independently of Goloubew's later causal anecdote.

---

## 5. Competing retrospective foundation narratives must remain separate

Goloubew's 1935 memorial foregrounds Lévi's personal intervention with Doumer.

A later Académie account at the EFEO fiftieth anniversary instead says the initiative came from **Auguste Barth, Émile Sénart and Michel Bréal**, who submitted their project to Doumer; the mission was then created on 15 December 1898.

Source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_1952_num_96_1_9890

These are not identical causal stories. Neither retrospective account should be silently harmonized into a single chain without the correspondence linking the stages.

For the Lovejoy project the correct response is conservative: the foundation-history dispute is contextual and should not alter the securely dated Lévi-return sequence.

## 6. New digital archive target: French Tokyo legation microfilms can potentially settle the Japan departure

A much more useful discovery emerges from the chronology audit. The French diplomatic archives expose an exact 1898 file:

**697PO/B/52 — Correspondance (arrivée-départ) avec Kobe (janvier-septembre 1898), Nagasaki (1898).**

The finding aid explicitly says that **digital reproductions are attached to the description**, derived from microfilm `2MI/1253`.

Digital finding-aid record:

https://archivesdiplomatiques.diplomatie.gouv.fr/ark:/14366/fjdshx85vp0g

FranceArchives mirror:

https://francearchives.gouv.fr/facomponent/9e8dc972d2dfa58658b954637a69920a3f9b9a35

This may be the best new electronic movement-control source because Lévi's final Japanese departure used Nagasaki and the file covers Nagasaki in 1898. It is not guaranteed to name him: it is consular correspondence, not a passenger register. But a hit on `Lévi`, `Levi`, `professeur au Collège de France`, `mission`, or a French scholarly traveler could fix the departure window independently.

The digital search order should be:

1. inspect the attached microfilm images around **August–September 1898**;
2. search/scan outgoing and incoming Nagasaki correspondence for `Lévi/Levi`;
3. look for references to passage to Vladivostok, French academic mission, letters of introduction or customs/baggage/book shipments;
4. if a date appears, place it against Vimy 9 October and the Siberian travel account.

Because the images are already attached online, this is a digital target, not an archive-trip target.

## 7. Consequence for Batch132

Batch132's central transition claim does **not** need withdrawal. Its strongest chronological sentence remains supportable:

> Lévi had returned to France by 9 October 1898, eight days before the earliest Wilson-cited Lovejoy Paris letter of 17 October.

What should be blocked is any future interpolation of a late-1898 Saigon stop into the sequence.

The safe sequence remains:

`1897–98 Buddhist/Asian mission`

→ `Japan`

→ `return via Siberia/Russia by early Oct at latest`

→ `Vimy, 9 Oct 1898`

→ `Lovejoy letter, 17 Oct`

→ `EPHE teaching resumption in November`

→ `Collège first-semester opening 5 Dec`.

The Saigon/Doumer episode is currently placed in a separate box:

`retrospective institutional-foundation narrative — date unresolved`.
