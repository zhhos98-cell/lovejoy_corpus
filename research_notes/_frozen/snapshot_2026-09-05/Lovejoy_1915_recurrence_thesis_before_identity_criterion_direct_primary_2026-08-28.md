# Lovejoy 1915 — recurrence thesis before identity criterion

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / PROGRAMMATIC HINGE / UNIT-IDEA GENEALOGY**

Primary: Arthur O. Lovejoy, review of Frank Thilly, *A History of Philosophy*, *Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* 12.10 (13 May 1915), especially p.277.

Direct public text recovered through PDCnet search indexing.

## Core result

1915 is the clearest currently recovered point at which Lovejoy explicitly joins:

- historiographical `unit` language;
- Windelband's problem-centred history;
- a finer third unit, `separate ideas` / `dialectical motifs`;
- recurrence across diverse combinations and problems;
- a proposed `history of ideas as such`.

But the same passage explicitly says the components still require discrimination, enumeration and examination, and that the whole conception needs further explanation and justification.

Therefore the strongest formulation is:

> **By 1915 Lovejoy had stated the recurrence thesis before he had supplied an adequate identity criterion.**

This is a programmatic hinge, not a fully stabilized mature method.

---

## I. The methodological problem is explicitly the choice of units

Lovejoy says that the chief methodological question for the writer of a history of philosophy concerns the **units** with which the historian primarily deals.

He prefers, within a well-marked period, grouping philosophers by a shared **problem** rather than treating the whole **system** as the primary unit.

His reason is relational: the reasonings of several philosophers about one problem can reveal more significant relations than grouping all of one philosopher's reasonings together.

He identifies Windelband's history as the exemplar of this problem-centred plan.

Thus the first explicit scale shift is:

`system`
→ `problem`.

---

## II. Lovejoy then proposes a third, finer unit

The key sentence introduces:

> `A third type of unit`

consisting of:

- `separate ideas`;
- `dialectical motifs`.

He calls them the `primary and active elements of philosophical thought`.

These elements are said to:

- recur repeatedly;
- appear `more or less disguised`;
- enter very diverse combinations;
- appear in connection with many different problems.

The scale therefore becomes:

`system`
→ `problem`
→ `separate idea / dialectical motif`.

This is direct actor-level evidence, not retrospective project vocabulary.

---

## III. Recurrence is asserted before recognition is operationalized

The phrase `recur again and again, more or less disguised` contains the mature identity problem in compressed form.

To assert recurrence, the historian must be able to recognize:

`token A`
+
`token B under different appearance`
→ `same motif/unit`.

But the 1915 passage does not state a rule for such recognition.

Instead Lovejoy immediately says much remains to be done in the:

- discrimination;
- enumeration;
- examination

of these elemental components.

Thus:

`recurrence postulated`
→ `identity technology still under construction`.

This is the crucial chronological result.

---

## IV. The programme is explicitly unfinished

Lovejoy says no general history has yet taken these motifs as its primary units and adds that this may not be entirely unfortunate because the preliminary analytical work remains incomplete.

He then says scholarly effort most needs to move toward a `history of ideas as such`.

But he explicitly states that the whole conception needs much more **explanation and justification** than a review permits and hopes to set it out more adequately later.

Therefore 1915 should not be treated as a mature-method endpoint.

It is a proposal with deferred warrant.

---

## V. Relation to 1924

The later Romanticisms paper shows why the 1915 recurrence claim is not trivial.

Lovejoy can allow broad historical complexes to form a non-essentialist overlap structure: different Romanticisms share different components pairwise without one common macro-essence.

At the same time, the analytical programme depends on recognizing particular components when they recur.

Thus 1924 clarifies a distinction only implicit in 1915:

`macro family resemblance / overlap`
≠ `identity of a recurrent component`.

The second relation requires stronger discrimination.

---

## VI. Relation to 1936

*The Great Chain of Being* narrows the operative units in that book to especially definite and explicit propositions/principles and associated corollaries.

This supplies a practical identity technology absent from the 1915 review:

`propositional content + inferential/corollary profile`
→ candidate unit recognition.

But it still does not fully specify how much contextual/semantic change is compatible with identity.

Thus 1936 partially fills, rather than eliminates, the 1915 deferred-criterion problem.

---

## VII. Relation to 1944

In the Spitzer reply Lovejoy explicitly characterizes the recurrence/discriminability propositions as **working hypotheses** whose truth and range are matters of historical fact to be tested case by case.

This creates a non-linear methodological trajectory:

### 1915

`recurrent disguised elemental units` announced;
`discrimination/examination/justification` explicitly incomplete.

### 1936

practical proposition/principle recognition profile becomes more definite.

### 1941

semasiology precedes cross-context recognition; relation types separately tested.

### 1944

recurrence itself explicitly framed as an empirical working hypothesis.

Therefore mature Lovejoy should not be represented as progressively reifying an initially modest empirical unit. There is also a movement toward **explicit provisionalization** of the recurrence claim.

---

## VIII. Why formal logic does not close the 1915 gap

The 1907/1910 formal-logic work can sharpen:

- classification criteria;
- exhaustive relation-space;
- compatibility;
- inferential restriction.

But the 1915 problem is empirical-historical:

> how does a motif remain identifiable when `more or less disguised` in new historical configurations?

Formal logic cannot by itself specify historical semantic equivalence.

The 1915 hinge therefore strengthens the distinction between:

- aggregation/equivalence warrant;
- relation/inference warrant.

---

## IX. Strongest current developmental statement

> **Lovejoy's explicit history-of-ideas programme begins not with a finished ontology of unit-ideas but with a proposed reduction of historiographical scale. By 1915 he posits recurrent, partially disguised dialectical motifs as primary units while simultaneously acknowledging that their discrimination and justification remain unfinished. The later identity problem is already visible at the moment the programme is announced.**
