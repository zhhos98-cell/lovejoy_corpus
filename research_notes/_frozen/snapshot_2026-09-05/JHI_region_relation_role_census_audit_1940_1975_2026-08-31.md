# JHI region × relation-role census audit, 1940–1975

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **EXPANDED SEED / ROLE-DIVERSIFICATION MODEL STRONG / NOT YET EXHAUSTIVE ISSUE-BY-ISSUE CENSUS**

## Core result

The expanded JHI execution census now contains **40 seeded non-Western / cross-regional cases**:

- 1940–1955 file: 17 cases;
- 1956–1975 file: 23 cases.

The sample is now large enough to reject a simple stage narrative, but not yet large enough to estimate article shares or make claims about complete regional representation.

The strongest result is:

> **JHI's midcentury internationalization is better described as accumulation and diversification of relation-roles than as a linear transition from Western representation to autonomous non-Western intellectual history.**

Compact formula:

`ROLE DIVERSIFICATION`
!=
`LINEAR DECENTERING`.

Internally addressed regional thought, West-facing source genealogies, comparative co-cases, representation studies, reception/transformation histories, field-method interventions and transmission histories coexist for decades.

This materially improves the larger Lovejoy/JHI argument because it prevents `globalization` from becoming another retrospective teleology.

## II. The decisive anti-stage controls

### India: internal object -> West-facing influence can coexist

1952 P. T. Raju, `The Development of Indian Thought`:

`INDIA = INTERNALLY DEVELOPING INTELLECTUAL TRADITION`.

1955 Buddha Prakash, `The Hindu Philosophy of History`:

`HINDU THOUGHT = DIRECT INTELLECTUAL-HISTORICAL OBJECT`.

But 1967 Dale Riepe, `Emerson and Indian Philosophy`:

`INDIA = SOURCE / INFLUENCE IN AMERICAN INTELLECTUAL GENEALOGY`.

Therefore:

`INTERNAL ADDRESS`
does not supersede
`WEST-FACING SOURCE ROLE`.

The relation-role depends on the historical question, not simply the date.

### Islamic philosophy: internal object and Europe-facing transmission coexist

1963 Nicholas Rescher, `Al-Fārābī on Logical Tradition`:

`AL-FĀRĀBĪ = ACTOR WITHIN ARABIC / ISLAMIC LOGICAL TRADITION`.

1965 Majid Fakhry, `Al-Farabi and the Reconciliation of Plato and Aristotle`:

`AL-FĀRĀBĪ = INTERNAL PHILOSOPHICAL RECOMBINATION`.

1967 Warren Gates, `The Spread of Ibn Khaldun's Ideas on Climate and Culture`:

`IBN KHALDUN = SOURCE / TRANSMISSION PROBLEM TOWARD EUROPE`.

Again, internal history does not replace influence history.

### China: almost every role exists at once

China has the broadest relation-role repertoire in the current sample:

- source/antecedent for Europe: Leibniz, Wordsworth, Chinese garden, Justi, Jesuit Figurists;
- comparative co-case: Needham, Hamburger, Loehr, Hightower;
- recipient/rejecter/transformer: 1951 Chinese Reactions symposium;
- field-method object: Wright 1960;
- regional review field: Schirokauer;
- internal transmission: Julia Ching 1974;
- transpacific comparative thought/network: Nakamura / Embree;
- East-West encounter: Paul Carus.

This makes China the strongest case against a single-direction diffusion model.

### Latin America: reception -> internal field -> historiographical self-object

Current sequence:

- 1949 Elizabeth Flower, `The Mexican Revolt Against Positivism`;
- 1954 Richard Morse, `Toward a Theory of Spanish American Government`;
- 1963 Arturo Ardao, `Assimilation and Transformation of Positivism in Latin America`;
- 1971 Jeffrey Klaiber, `The Non-Communist Left in Latin America`;
- 1972 Ronald Hilton, `Is Intellectual History Irrelevant? The Case of the Aztecs`;
- 1975 Jorge Gracia on Zea's *Positivism in Mexico*.

This is the clearest current regional sequence toward **history of ideas becoming itself an explicit regional historiographical field**, but even here it should not be written as simple maturation from derivative reception to autonomy. Ardao's `assimilation and transformation` formulation already makes reception an active regional intellectual process.

## III. Ursula Lamb 1956 is a major methodological node

`Religious Conflicts in the Conquest of Mexico` deserves elevation above census status.

Lamb opens from a contemporary problem: modern history has produced repeated compulsory contacts between previously distinct civilizations. Anthropology had developed `acculturation` as a field, while historians had less often made the fate of rival idea-systems under such contact their central object.

Her conquest case therefore treats:

`ARMED INTERCULTURAL CONTACT`
+
`RIVAL RELIGIOUS WORLD-VIEWS`
+
`ATTEMPTED SUBSTITUTION / CONVERSION`
+
`ACCULTURATION`

as a **history-of-ideas problem**.

This is important for the larger project because it provides a midcentury JHI route from ideas to contact, coercion and social transformation that is neither standard Lovejoyian unit-idea tracking nor later Skinnerian contextualism.

It should be read in full in a later direct-text pass.

## IV. Relation-role taxonomy now looks more useful than region counting alone

The present coding suggests at least nine recurrent roles:

- `R1 INTERNAL_CONCEPTUAL_HISTORY` — a regional intellectual tradition is itself the historical object;
- `R2 SOURCE_ANTECEDENT` — non-Western material supplies an antecedent/source inside another genealogy;
- `R3 COMPARATIVE_COCASE` — traditions are made co-cases for a shared conceptual problem;
- `R4 TRANSMISSION / CONTACT` — movement, mediation, borrowing, circulation, influence;
- `R5 REACTION_TRANSFORMATION` — receiving actors reject, select, assimilate, syncretize or transform;
- `R6 REGIONAL_HISTORIOGRAPHY` — the region becomes a scholarly field with sources, methods and review discourse;
- `R7 REPRESENTATION` — the object is a Western/colonial concept/image of a non-Western people/place;
- `R8 IDEA_TO_ACTION / PRESENT_IDENTITY` — ideas are related to practice, conflict, colonization or contemporary identity politics;
- `R9 FIELD_METHOD / INTERFACE` — explicit reflection on how intellectual history or area scholarship should be organized.

The major historiographic gain is that a single article can occupy several roles. This avoids forcing each region into one developmental stage.

Relation-role coding should remain multi-label.

## V. New model of JHI internationalization

The current evidence supports a layered model:

### Layer 1 — object expansion

More regions, languages and traditions appear as research objects.

### Layer 2 — role diversification

Those objects can function as source, comparator, recipient, transformer, internal tradition, representation or field-method problem.

### Layer 3 — actor/network expansion

Regional specialists and scholars such as Wright, Nakamura, Fakhry, Ardao, Ching and others enter publication/review/editorial networks.

### Layer 4 — infrastructure convergence

AAS / Committee on Chinese Thought, International Society, Latin-American historia de las ideas, area-studies institutions and JHI partially intersect.

### Layer 5 — canon lag

The Dictionary can include substantial non-Western entries while still retaining a predominantly Western reference architecture.

Therefore:

`MORE NON-WESTERN OBJECTS`
!= `SYMMETRICAL ROLES`
!= `DIVERSE GOVERNANCE`
!= `GLOBAL CANON`.

This dovetails with the project's existing formula:

`PROGRAMME != PRACTICE != INFRASTRUCTURE != CANON`.

## VI. The strongest asymmetry now visible: China vs Africa

### China

Current sample is dense across almost every role and links outward to a well-documented institutional chain:

`ACLS CHINESE STUDIES`
-> `COMMITTEE ON CHINESE THOUGHT`
-> `AAS / ROCKEFELLER`
-> `JHI / INTERNATIONAL SOCIETY / NAKAMURA NETWORK`
-> `EDITORIAL / DICTIONARY PRESENCE`.

### Sub-Saharan Africa

The current seed remains strikingly thin:

- 1943 Madagascar appears through European `noble savage` representation;
- 1967 Ibn Khaldun gives a North-African / Islamic transmission case;
- the separate ACLS Negro Studies dossier proves that serious field-building and relation-specific proof debates existed outside JHI;
- later African intellectual-history scholarship explicitly appropriated history-of-ideas methods in African Studies venues.

The current JHI seed has **no securely identified internally addressed Sub-Saharan African intellectual-history article for 1940–75**.

This is a research finding only at the level of the present seed, not yet a publication-ready absence claim. It must be tested by issue-by-issue TOC/title/abstract census before writing `JHI excluded Africa` or equivalent.

This is now the highest-value census gap.

## VIII. A stronger article claim than `JHI became global`

The best present sentence is:

> **Between the 1940s and 1970s JHI did not simply expand from a Western canon toward a global one. It accumulated new ways of relating intellectual traditions: non-Western materials could remain sources and representations within European histories even while the same journal published internally addressed Asian and Latin-American intellectual histories, comparative co-cases, histories of reception and transformation, and explicit debates over regional scholarly method. Internationalization changed the repertoire of admissible relations faster than it equalized the canon.**

This formulation explains why old and new modes can coexist without treating the coexistence as inconsistency.

## IX. Next census protocol

To turn the seed into a defensible census:

1. enumerate every JHI issue 1940–1975 from JSTOR;
2. extract all article/note/review titles and authors;
3. flag regional terms plus named non-Western actors/traditions that titles may not reveal;
4. classify `article / note / review / symposium / discussion` separately;
5. assign multi-label relation-role codes;
6. distinguish `internal address` from `West-facing relational address`;
7. record whether the author is a regional specialist and institutional location;
8. sample full text for ambiguous titles;
9. calculate by five-year blocks, not just raw totals;
10. only then compare regional shares and role distributions.

High-priority search controls:

- Sub-Saharan Africa / Negro / African / Ethiopia / Ghana / Nigeria / Congo / Bantu / Yoruba / etc.;
- South Asia beyond `India/Hindu/Buddhism`: Sanskrit, Vedanta, Jain, Sikh, Mughal, Pakistan, Ceylon/Sri Lanka;
- Islamic world beyond canonical philosopher names;
- Japan and Southeast Asia;
- Latin America beyond Mexico;
- reviews, which currently appear to be a major route by which regional fields enter JHI before or alongside full articles.
