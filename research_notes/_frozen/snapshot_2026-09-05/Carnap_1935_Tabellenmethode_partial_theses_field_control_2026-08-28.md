# Carnap 1935 `Tabellenmethode` — partial-thesis separation as field control

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / NEGATIVE ATTRIBUTION CONTROL / SAME-YEAR BALTIMORE CONTEXT / RESTART-SAFE**

## Why this matters

The active Lovejoy branch has already moved away from treating `decomposition` or `analysis` as a proprietary Lovejoy technique. A newly recovered Carnap letter now gives an unusually sharp same-year control.

On **2 June 1935**, seven months before the Baltimore APA encounter, Rudolf Carnap wrote to Otto Neurath about the definition of `physicalism`. Carnap insists that several theses Neurath wants to gather under that name should be kept separate because they are logically independent. He then explicitly invokes what he calls Neurath's usual **`Tabellenmethode`**, glossing it as the **clear separation of the different partial assertions in an overall view**.

This is not evidence of influence on Lovejoy. Its value is adversarial and field-level: **the operation `composite doctrine → distinguish independent partial theses → refuse consequence-transfer across them` was explicitly practiced within logical empiricism in the very year of the Lovejoy–Carnap encounter.**

## 1. Immediate argumentative context

Carnap's letter ranges across several disputes internal to scientific empiricism:

- empirical control of logical propositions;
- descriptive syntax;
- Schlick, Hempel and Neurath on propositions/facts;
- terminology and the definition of `physicalism`;
- the thesis that all propositions are revocable.

The methodological point emerges while Carnap is trying to prevent Neurath's definition of `physicalism` from absorbing additional theses that are not entailed by it.

Carnap proposes a comparatively narrow definition of physicalism:

> every scientific proposition can be translated into a physicalist, or better a physicalistic, language.

He then says the further claims Neurath mentions should receive separate names even if the group happens to endorse them as well.

## 2. The key independence claim

The strongest example concerns the thesis of the **revocability of all propositions**.

Carnap says it should not be included under `physicalism` because it is **entirely independent** of physicalism in the defined sense: one can be a physicalist while either accepting or rejecting the revocability thesis.

Operator form:

`membership in doctrine D`

≠

`logical commitment to auxiliary thesis T`

when:

`D + T` is possible

and

`D + not-T` is also possible.

Therefore a shared movement/doctrine label must not transfer commitment from D to T.

## 3. Carnap's explicit `Tabellenmethode`

Carnap then reminds Neurath that Neurath is normally in favor of the **`Tabellenmethode`** and immediately explains what he means by it:

> `deutliche Trennung der verschiedenen Teilbehauptungen in einer Gesamtauffassung`

That is: **clear separation of the various partial assertions within an overall position/view**.

He says this method should be applied to physicalism as well.

The methodological structure is explicit and actor-authored:

`Gesamtauffassung`
→ identify `Teilbehauptungen`
→ test their independence
→ give distinct theses distinct names
→ prevent the umbrella term from importing commitments not entailed by its core definition.

This is an exceptionally clean direct-primary control for the generic operation of proposition insulation.

## 4. Comparison with Lovejoy 1908 — structural homology, no genealogy

Lovejoy's `The Thirteen Pragmatisms` (1908) had argued that the historical name `pragmatism` covered multiple contentions that were logically independent in the relevant sense:

- one may accept one without accepting the others;
- refuting one does not thereby refute the rest;
- shared historical naming encourages illegitimate argumentative transfer.

Carnap's 1935 letter has a strikingly similar operator shape:

### Lovejoy 1908

`historical movement label`
→ disaggregate contentions
→ test logical independence
→ block proof/refutation transfer.

### Carnap 1935

`Gesamtauffassung / doctrine label`
→ separate `Teilbehauptungen`
→ test logical independence
→ block commitment transfer.

### Warrant

This is **structural homology / field comparison only**.

There is currently no evidence that:

- Carnap learned this operation from Lovejoy;
- Lovejoy learned it from Carnap/Neurath;
- either actor knew the other's earlier use;
- the two operations have an identical philosophical genealogy.

The proper historiographical use is negative attribution control:

> **decomposition plus proposition insulation cannot carry the burden of identifying a uniquely Lovejoyian analytical method.**

## 5. Consequence for interpreting Baltimore 1935

This same-year control sharpens the Baltimore interpretation.

The encounter should not be narrated as:

`Lovejoy = analytical splitter`

versus

`Carnap = monolithic verificationist`.

Carnap himself was explicitly policing logical independence among component theses in June 1935, and his 1936 `Testability and Meaning` distinguishes meaning from verification and testability from confirmability.

The more plausible contrast is therefore:

> **both actors practiced analytical separation, but they disagreed over which distinctions and bridges licensed which philosophical consequences.**

This supports the active formulation of the Baltimore clash as a dispute between **rival jurisdiction architectures**, not a transition from non-analysis to analysis.

Lovejoy's December objection, as Carnap records it, asks what Carnap's conventionalism permits logical positivists to conclude about metaphysics. Lovejoy's 1938 essay then asks what one criterion of meaning permits Carnap to conclude under another criterion. Carnap's June letter shows that Carnap too was highly sensitive to illicit bundling and commitment transfer.

Thus the historically interesting contrast moves one level upward:

`shared discipline of separation`
+
`different typing of relations / criteria / permissible consequence`
→
`substantive dispute`.

## 7. Additional methodological warning from the same letter

Earlier in the same letter Carnap criticizes formulations that may encourage an empiricist or psychologist reading of logic and mathematics and presses Neurath to formulate his position as clearly as Carnap believes he has formulated his own in `Formalwissenschaft und Realwissenschaft` and *Logische Syntax der Sprache*.

He also criticizes Neurath for retaining a vulnerable formulation (`propositions can only be compared with propositions`) even after Neurath explains that he means something more qualified.

This adds another same-document control:

`intended qualified position`

≠

`publicly retained overstrong formulation`.

A critic may legitimately attack the latter if the actor continues to carry it along.

This is relevant to the Lovejoy–Einstein/McGilvary control: historical reconstruction must distinguish an actor's strongest attributable formulation from a critic's reconstruction of what the doctrine supposedly entails.

## 8. Current conclusion

> **Carnap's 2 June 1935 letter to Neurath provides direct evidence that proposition-level disaggregation and anti-transfer discipline were explicit tools inside logical empiricism immediately before the Baltimore encounter. Carnap invokes the `Tabellenmethode` as the clear separation of partial assertions in an overall view and uses logical independence to keep the revocability thesis outside the definition of physicalism. This blocks any attempt to make decomposition or proposition insulation uniquely Lovejoyian and strengthens a more precise Baltimore model: two analytical cultures, both capable of disaggregation, disputing the jurisdiction and consequences of their distinctions.**
