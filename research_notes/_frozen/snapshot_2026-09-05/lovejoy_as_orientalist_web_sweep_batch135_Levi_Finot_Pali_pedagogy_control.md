# Batch 135 — Lévi–Finot Pāli pedagogy before Lovejoy: the missing-course story has an immediate institutional precedent

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: test whether Wilson's claim that Lovejoy arrived in Paris hoping to study Pāli Buddhism with Sylvain Lévi was institutionally plausible, or whether it projects an anachronistic / vague expectation onto a Sanskrit chair that was not actually teaching Pāli in the late 1890s.

## Core result

The expectation is historically plausible in a much stronger sense than previously established.

A near-contemporary student control, **Marcel Mauss**, later recalled a sequential training regime under Sylvain Lévi and Louis Finot: after rigorous Sanskrit grammar, chrestomathy, translation exercises and metrics, **the following year Lévi and Finot initiated their students into Pāli and Buddhist translation**. This is participant testimony about the same Paris Indianist teaching network in the immediate pre-mission years.

The annual institutional record independently fixes Mauss inside that network in 1895–1897:

- in **1895–96**, EPHE V Section `Religions de l'Inde` names Mauss among those taking an active part while Louis Finot, replacing Foucher, taught Bhagavad-Gītā and Raghuvaṃśa;
- in **1896–97**, the same section lists Mauss as an `élève titulaire` while Finot again replaced Foucher and taught Vedānta exegesis;
- Mauss's prosopographical record places him in Paris on a study bursary in **1895–97**, attending Lévi/Finot in the IV Section and Indian/religion teaching in the V Section.

The exact academic year of the remembered Pāli/Buddhist-translation phase is not yet fixed by a primary course register, so it should not be forced from Mauss's phrase `l'année suivante`. But the testimony is enough to establish the crucial point:

> **Pāli plus Buddhist translation was part of the Lévi–Finot pedagogical repertoire in the immediate pre-1897 mission training cycle.**

This materially changes the Lovejoy problem. His reported hope to study Pāli Buddhism with Lévi in 1898–99 is no longer simply biographically plausible because Lévi knew Pāli. It matches a recent form of instruction actually remembered by another student of the same teachers.

---

## 1. Mauss gives the closest pedagogical control yet found

A modern scholarly reconstruction quotes Mauss's own retrospective description of his training under Lévi and Finot. The sequence is unusually specific. Finot imposed elementary but rigorous Sanskrit grammar, metrics, versions and themes; Lévi used Böhtlingk's chrestomathy, Bergaigne's grammar and literary texts; then:

> `L'année suivante, il nous initiait avec Finot au pâli et à la traduction bouddhique...`

The same recollection continues with Manu and Indian theatre under Lévi.

Current online scholarly witness:

- *L'invention de l'Inde*, chapter 7, CNRS Éditions / OpenEdition.

The quotation is from Mauss's later writings and is therefore retrospective rather than a contemporary timetable. It should nevertheless be treated as first-person participant testimony about pedagogical practice, not as a modern inference.

The important distinction is:

`Mauss remembers Pāli + Buddhist translation with Lévi/Finot`

≠

`a formal annual Pāli course is already located in the surviving programme`.

The first is now strong; the second remains to be document-level closed.

---

## 2. Primary 1895–96 record: Mauss is already active in the same teaching network

The EPHE V Section report for 1895–96 says that Louis Finot replaced Alfred Foucher, who was on leave. Finot explained the **Bhagavad-Gītā** in the first semester and **Raghuvaṃśa**, considered especially from the standpoint of religious beliefs and practices, in the second.

The report then names:

`MM. Lederer, Mauss, A. de Pompignan ...`

among those who took an active part.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1895_num_9_5_19435

This gives a hard institutional anchor for Mauss in the Lévi–Foucher–Finot Indian-studies network before the mission cycle.

It does not mention Pāli. That matters because it establishes that the V-Section course report is only one layer of his actual Indianist training.

---

## 3. Primary 1896–97 record: Mauss is an `élève titulaire`

The next V Section report says Foucher remained away on mission and, on Sylvain Lévi's proposal, Finot again replaced him. Finot studied the metaphysical principles of Vedānta exegesis through Śaṅkara's commentary on the Vedānta-Sūtras.

The report explicitly gives:

`Élèves titulaires : M. Mauss et Mme Mabel-Bode.`

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1896_num_10_6_19458

Thus Mauss's later recollection of a two-stage Lévi/Finot training process belongs to a demonstrably continuous institutional relationship across the two years immediately before Lévi's 1897–98 Asian mission.

A prosopographical record independently gives Mauss as a Paris study bursary holder from **1895 to 1897**, and as an EPHE student attending the IV Section conferences of Lévi and Finot as well as Indian/religion work in the V Section.

This chronology makes the most natural working hypothesis:

`first Sanskrit training phase → subsequent Pāli/Buddhist-translation phase`

inside the 1895–97 window.

But because the exact session has not yet been identified in a contemporary IV-Section programme/report, this remains a **window assignment**, not a publication-grade year claim.

---

## 4. The control is stronger because Lévi's teaching could be individualized

The same body of Mauss testimony contains another important point. Mauss later said that Lévi's course on the **Brāhmaṇas** in 1896–97 was personally directed to him and became the basis of Lévi's 1898 study of sacrifice.

This is a very useful control for the Lovejoy problem because it shows that the public timetable is not an exhaustive model of pedagogical interaction.

The historical configuration can be:

`advertised institutional course`

+

`student-specific text work / supplementary instruction`

without contradiction.

That is exactly the evidentiary form Wilson describes for Lovejoy in 1898–99: the desired Pāli-Buddhism course was not formally offered, but Lovejoy occasionally went to Lévi privately to read sacred texts.

This does **not** prove Lovejoy's private readings. It makes the reported mode of instruction institutionally recognizable rather than exceptional.

---

## 5. Lévi's Buddhist teaching before the mission was already extensive

Pāli is only one part of the control. Primary EPHE V-Section reports show that Lévi was directly teaching Buddhist texts before the mission.

For 1894–95, the report states that in November–December Lévi's conferences included:

- explanation of the **Abhidharma-kośa**;
- study of **northern Buddhism**;
- epigraphic documents.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1894_num_8_4_19412

Thus a student who knew the Paris Indianist curriculum in the mid-1890s could reasonably associate Lévi with Buddhist textual instruction, while the Mauss recollection adds the specific Pāli/Buddhist-translation component.

The distinction remains important:

- Abhidharma-kośa / northern Buddhism ≠ Pāli Buddhism;
- but together with Mauss's testimony they establish a broad Buddhist philological teaching repertoire.

---

## 6. The mission creates exactly the interruption needed to explain 1898–99

The IV-Section report for 1897–98 explicitly states that Lévi, away on his India mission, **could direct no conference during the school year**.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1899_num_1_1_2200

This inserts a one-year break between the pre-mission training repertoire and Lovejoy's arrival.

Then, after Lévi returned, the recovered 1898–99 programme/report shows a changed formal configuration:

- Lévi: Bhagavad-Gītā and Indian literature in the IV Section;
- Foucher: Nyāya/Vedānta and Manu in the V Section;
- no recovered advertised Pāli course.

The corrected Collège de France poster independently shows the Foucher substitution under Lévi's Sanskrit chair being cancelled as the 1898–99 year opened.

The sequence is therefore no longer mysterious:

`pre-mission Lévi/Finot repertoire includes Pāli + Buddhist translation`

→ `1897–98 mission interrupts Lévi's teaching`

→ `1898–99 Lévi returns, but formal advertised programme is reorganized and contains no Pāli course`

→ `Lovejoy reportedly expected Pāli Buddhism, found it unavailable, and shifted to occasional private sacred-text reading with Lévi`.

Only the last arrow remains dependent on the Wilson-mediated Lovejoy letters.

---

## 7. Historiographical consequence: programme silence is now positively interpretable

Before this batch, the absence of Pāli from the 1898–99 programme could be used only negatively:

`we found no Pāli course that year`.

Now it can be placed against a recent positive pedagogical precedent:

> **The interesting fact is not simply that Pāli was absent in Lovejoy's year. It is that Pāli/Buddhist translation belonged to Lévi and Finot's recent teaching repertoire, was interrupted by Lévi's mission, and did not reappear in the recovered formal programme immediately on his return.**

This gives Wilson's account a more precise institutional meaning. Lovejoy may have arrived expecting a recognizable pre-mission form of Indianist training that the post-mission timetable no longer supplied in regular course form.

`may have` is essential. We still do not know how Lovejoy learned of the expected course—Harvard advice, printed programmes, correspondence, scholarly reputation, or prior student networks.

---

## 8. New research question: who transmitted the expectation to Lovejoy?

The problem has shifted from:

`Was a Pāli course with Lévi plausible?`

to:

`How did Lovejoy know to expect it?`

High-value routes are now:

1. **Lovejoy's 17 Oct / 1 Dec / 5 Dec 1898 letters** in JHU MS-0873: does he say who recommended Lévi or what course he expected?
2. Harvard/Lanman correspondence or advice: did Charles R. Lanman recommend Paris teachers/courses?
3. American Paris student network: Stickney, Mauss's international circle, or other recent EPHE students may have circulated the course reputation.
4. contemporary EPHE/Collège printed programmes before departure: identify whether a planned Pāli/Buddhist course was advertised earlier and then altered after Lévi's return.

The fourth possibility is now especially worth testing against draft/corrected course programmes, because the user-supplied Collège poster has already demonstrated that working copies preserved cancelled teaching arrangements.

---

## Evidence cautions

1. Mauss's phrase `l'année suivante` is retrospective and should not yet be assigned to a single academic year solely from memory chronology.
2. Mauss's 1895–97 institutional participation is independently secure, making that the correct narrow window for the remembered sequence.
3. The V-Section annual reports do not exhaust IV-Section language instruction or student-specific work.
4. No claim is made that Lovejoy knew Mauss or obtained the course expectation from him.
5. No claim is made that the sacred texts Lovejoy privately read with Lévi were in Pāli; the language/text remains unresolved.
6. The central gain is a **pedagogical precedent**, not direct transmission evidence.
