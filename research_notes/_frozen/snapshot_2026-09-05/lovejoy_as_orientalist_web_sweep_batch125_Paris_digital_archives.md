# Batch 125 — Paris 1898–99 digital-archive scan map: what can be closed online before an archive visit

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: return to the unfinished Paris side of notebooks 004/005, but restrict this pass to materials whose scans, page images, or structured digital surrogates are already online or are explicitly part of a completed digitization project.

## Core result

The remaining Paris work is less dependent on physical archive visits than the earlier queue implied. Four substantial blocks can now be attacked electronically:

1. **EPHE / Archives nationales institutional records**: the completed 2021–2025 EPHE digitization project explicitly includes selected institutional archives and identifies registration registers and attendance lists as especially important records; the project instrument and scans are online as `FRAN_IR_061975`. The same project includes a selection from **Sylvain Lévi's archive**.
2. **Collège de France course administration**: Salamandre has digitized the collection of **Affiches et programmes de cours du Collège de France, 1688–1900** and the faculty-assembly minute registers covering 1674–1950. These are direct electronic routes into Lévi's 1897–99 leave, substitution, return and advertised teaching.
3. **Marillier facsimile closure**: the exact RHR volumes required by Batch72 are already digitally available. JSTOR exposes Marillier's 1897 article, pp.208–253, as stable `23661047` with Download/XML, while RHR 37 (1898) is likewise digitized and exposes the third and fourth installments. Google Books/BSB also provide digitized volume witnesses. The outstanding p.247, p.250 and pp.230–231 checks are therefore online collation tasks, not archive tasks.
4. **Published contemporary movement controls for Sylvain Lévi**: Persée provides page images/searchable text for EPHE annual reports and Lévi's own mission report. AGORHA gives the first Asian mission as 3 October 1897 to September 1898. These sources can be aligned with autumn 1898 institutional programs and Lovejoy's October/December correspondence claims before any manuscript correspondence is obtained.

The principal hard offline holdouts remain the **JHU MS-0873 transcriptions of Lovejoy's 1898 letters** and the **Söderblom notebooks**. In both cases metadata is online, but no scan of the decisive objects has yet surfaced in the public layer checked here.

## 1. EPHE / Archives nationales: the underlying student-record layer is now a digital target

EPHE announced that the project `L'EPHE dans la recherche française pendant le premier siècle de son existence (1868–1968): inventaire et numérisation des archives scientifiques` was completed in May 2025. The official project notice says the digitized selection spans ministry records, EPHE institutional archives, and three scholar fonds. It specifically singles out **registres d'inscription**, **listes de présence**, and section assembly registers as records capable of measuring the school's scientific influence.

Most importantly for the Paris problem, the three scholar selections include:

- Ignace Meyerson;
- **Sylvain Lévi**;
- Paul Alphandéry.

The project instrument and digitizations are linked under:

`FRAN_IR_061975`

Official EPHE project page:  
https://www.ephe.psl.eu/100-ans-darchives-scientifiques-de-lephe-en-ligne

The separate EPHE archive inventory places the Vᵉ Section institutional archive at `20190568/001-399`, with the **scolarité** block at `20190568/185-368`:

https://www.ephe.psl.eu/inventaire-des-archives-de-la-recherche

### Evidence limit

This does **not** prove that the exact 1898–99 Lovejoy registration or attendance sheet is among the digitized images. It does prove that:

- the relevant institutional fonds is online-described;
- the digitization project deliberately included student-registration/attendance material as a record type;
- digital objects exist within the project instrument;
- a Sylvain Lévi archival selection is separately included.

Therefore the next operation should be component-level resolution, not an onsite request: map every 1897–1900 Vᵉ Section registration/attendance component to its `unitid`/cote and test whether it carries a digital-object link.

## 2. Salamandre creates two new electronic control routes around Sylvain Lévi

The Collège de France's Salamandre portal lists among its digitized collections:

- **Registres des délibérations de l'assemblée des professeurs (16ème–20ème siècles)**, with fifteen minute registers covering 1674–1950;
- **Affiches et programmes de cours du Collège de France**, covering **1688–1900**.

Digital collections page:  
https://salamandre.college-de-france.fr/nos-collections-numerisees

This matters because the unresolved Lévi question is partly administrative and calendrical. We already know from modern reconstruction that Alfred Foucher replaced Lévi at the Collège de France during Lévi's 1897–98 Asian mission. AGORHA dates that mission from 3 October 1897 to September 1898. What remains to be established directly is the precise institutional transition around the opening of the 1898–99 year.

The digitized **course posters/programs** can potentially establish:

- whether Lévi's Sanskrit chair is advertised in autumn 1898 under his own name;
- the exact regular course title(s), day/time, and subject;
- whether any substitute is still named;
- whether there is any Pāli/Buddhist offering matching the course Lovejoy reportedly wanted.

The digitized **faculty assembly minutes** can potentially establish:

- leave and substitution decisions;
- return from mission;
- resumption of teaching;
- course scheduling or administrative changes.

No specific 1898 minute or poster has yet been resolved in this pass. The important change is access status: these are now known digitized collections, so they belong ahead of physical archival work.

## 3. The Sylvain Lévi archive is only partly digital, but the online corpus is larger than a simple photo collection

The Collège de France Library of Indian and Central Asian Studies confirms that it holds a scientific archive for Sylvain Lévi. It further states that a digitization program with Salamandre has already made available part of the phototheque, audiovisual archives, and manuscripts from the Lévi collection.

Official library page:  
https://www.college-de-france.fr/fr/bibliotheque-archives/bibliotheque-etudes-indiennes-et-centrasiatiques

Salamandre's digitized-collections page exposes, among other Lévi materials:

- `Fonds iconographiques Sylvain Lévi`;
- a set of Nepalese manuscripts from the Lévi collection;
- Indian manuscripts from the Lévi collection.

This gives an important boundary. **Digitized Lévi archival material is confirmed; digitized Lovejoy correspondence, student appointment books, or private-reading notes are not.** We should therefore search Salamandre with a digitized-only filter for 1898–99 and teaching/student terms, while keeping the correspondence question unresolved.

A further contextual control is AGORHA's detailed Lévi record, which identifies his first scientific mission as running from **3 October 1897 to September 1898** and documents the Buddhist and Sanskrit manuscript collecting undertaken in India, Nepal and Japan:

https://agorha.inha.fr/detail/759

This makes autumn 1898 a particularly narrow digital target for Collège de France administrative records.

Batch72 left three direct-facsimile checks outstanding:

- RHR 36 (1897), p.250;

These no longer require library or archive access.

JSTOR's volume page for RHR 36 identifies:

- Léon Marillier, `La place du totémisme dans l'évolution religieuse à propos d'un livre récent`, pp.208–253;
- stable item `23661047`;
- Download and XML access points.

https://www.jstor.org/stable/23661047

JSTOR's RHR 37 volume page exposes the third and fourth installments with Download/XML:

https://www.jstor.org/stable/i23657010

Independent full-volume digital witnesses also exist. Google Books has a digitized Volume 36 (1897) and a digitized Volume 37 (1898); BSB/Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek identifies RHR 37 as a digitized object (`bsb11864665`).

### Consequence

The methodological claims in Batch72 can now be upgraded from:

`later quotation / contemporary attribution → proposition control`

to:

`direct 1897–98 facsimile → literal wording and context`

once the three page loci are collated. This is probably the fastest high-value Paris closure available online.

## 5. Persée gives a dense digital primary layer around the 1898–99 teaching environment

Several items already used contextually are in fact page-image digital witnesses, not merely secondary summaries.

### Vᵉ Section, 1898–99

Persée exposes the full report:

https://www.persee.fr/issue/ephe_0000-0002_1898_num_12_8

The `Religions de l'Inde` page is separately exposed and gives the Nyāya teaching already integrated into Batch25:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1898_num_12_8_19504

### IVᵉ Section Sanskrit teaching

The 1899 Annuaire is online and contains `XV. Langue sanscrite`, authored under Sylvain Lévi, Louis Finot and Édouard Specht:

https://www.persee.fr/issue/ephe_0000-0001_1899_num_1_1

This should be extracted at proposition level because the personnel sequence around Lévi/Finot/Specht may help reconstruct who was physically teaching what during the transition from Lévi's mission back to Paris.

### Lévi mission reports

Lévi's own report on the India/Japan mission is fully digitized in Persée:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_1899_num_43_1_71335

His `De Nagasaki à Moscou par la Sibérie` is also digitized:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/geo_0003-4010_1899_num_8_40_6122

Contemporary Académie notices from 1898 report on the progress of the mission. These materials may not yield the exact Paris arrival day, but they can substantially tighten the return window before the autumn course-program comparison.

## 6. The two main offline holdouts remain bounded

### JHU MS-0873

The digital search still only exposes the collection-level description of `Daniel J. Wilson transcriptions of Arthur O. Lovejoy letters`. No public digital object for the three crucial Paris transcriptions dated 17 October, 1 December and 5 December 1898 surfaced in the ArchivesSpace layer checked here.

Correct status:

`metadata confirmed / public scan not surfaced`

rather than:

`undigitized`.

This remains the best candidate for a very small targeted reproduction request rather than an archive trip.

### Söderblom

Alvin marks `Nathan Söderbloms efterlämnade papper` as **Non digital** while exposing detailed metadata and finding aids. The relevant notebook ranges remain:

- 1894–1899;
- 1896–1901;
- 1898–1915.

https://alvin-portal.org/alvin/view.jsf?pid=alvin-record:13019

The correct digital move is therefore to search individually catalogued correspondence and related records under a `digital` filter for Marillier / Paris / EPHE / survivance. The notebooks themselves remain a later targeted digitization request unless a child object unexpectedly appears.

## 8. Historiographical consequence

The remaining Paris problem is now more sharply divided:

`digital institutional trace`

→ EPHE registration / attendance; Collège de France course advertisements; faculty minutes

`digital published research trace`

→ Marillier, EPHE reports, Lévi mission reports

`partially digital scholar archive`

→ Sylvain Lévi collection

`still manuscript-dependent personal trace`

→ Lovejoy's 1898 letters; Söderblom notebooks

This means the current ~80% Paris reconstruction can likely be pushed materially higher before any physical archive visit. In particular, the gap between Wilson's statement that Lovejoy wanted a Pāli-Buddhism course and the actual institutional offering can now be tested against digitized Collège de France course programs and EPHE materials rather than left as a biographical paraphrase.
