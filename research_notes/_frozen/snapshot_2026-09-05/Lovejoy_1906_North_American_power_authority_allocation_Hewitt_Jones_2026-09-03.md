## Core result

The North-American lexical evidence in Lovejoy's 1906 `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` cannot be treated as the place where impersonal-power semantics were generated.

Two immediately upstream texts already carry much of the semantic load:

1. J. N. B. Hewitt, `Orenda and a Definition of Religion` (1902);
2. William Jones, `The Algonkin Manitou` (1905).

Together they already supply mystic potency, broad natural distribution, impersonal/property-like status, causal efficacy, unequal degree/concentration, and broad philosophical/religious generalization.

The Lovejoy residual must therefore be sought **after** these constituent propositions are removed.

## I. Hewitt 1902 — `orenda` already bears the causal-universal load

Object:

J. N. B. Hewitt, `Orenda and a Definition of Religion`, *American Anthropologist*, n.s. 4.1 (Jan.–Mar. 1902): 33–46.

JSTOR stable 658926 is openly accessible; Wiley confirms the article metadata.

Hewitt's wording is independently preserved in direct quotation by Durkheim and later source-critical scholarship:

- surrounding bodies possess `mystic potence`;
- the potency is a `property of all things`;
- it is regarded as the `efficient cause of all phenomena` / activities of the environment;
- beings and phenomena possess it in differing quantities/intensities.

Thus Hewitt already supplies:

`UNIVERSAL DISTRIBUTION`
+
`MYSTIC POTENCY`
+
`EFFICIENT CAUSALITY`
+
`VARIABLE QUANTITY`.

Lovejoy does not own these propositions merely because the 1906 synthesis uses analogous language.

## II. Jones 1905 — `manitou` already becomes impersonal cosmic property

Object:

William Jones, `The Algonkin Manitou`, *Journal of American Folk-Lore* 18.70 (July–Sept. 1905): 183–190.

Current controls:

- eHRAF indexed record/abstract;
- later quotation preserving Jones's p.190 formulation;
- issue/bibliographic metadata.

Jones describes manitou as:

- a cosmic, mysterious property existing throughout nature;
- capable of being conceived impersonally;
- obscured/confused when the property is identified with a concrete object;
- a common mystic virtue shared by many agencies;
- differently manifested by function and degree of possession.

Thus:

`OBJECT A / B / C`
share
`COMMON MYSTIC PROPERTY M`

while differing by

`FUNCTION + DEGREE OF M`.

This is extremely close to components of Lovejoy's 1906 diffused-energy model.

Therefore:

`IMPERSONAL PROPERTY`
+
`COSMIC DISTRIBUTION`
+
`VARIABLE DEGREE`

are strongly Jones-owned in the immediately pre-1906 North-American evidence environment.
