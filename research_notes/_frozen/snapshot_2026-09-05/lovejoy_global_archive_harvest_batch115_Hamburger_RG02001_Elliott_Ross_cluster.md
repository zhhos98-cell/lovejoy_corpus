# Archive-side Batch 115 — old Hamburger repository citations to RG-02-001, and the Elliott-MSS Ross-case cluster around Lovejoy's 15 April 1901 letter

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: Batch114 was occupied concurrently by a parallel archive-side run, so the additional provenance controls developed immediately afterward are separated into Batch115 rather than appended under the same batch number. This pass deepens two already-defined retrieval problems without creating another broad name-search wave: the current custody of the Hopkins–Bertrand Russell invitation packet, and the current physical context of canonical `GLA0056`, Lovejoy to Orrin L. Elliott, 15 April 1901.

## Core result

Batch115 adds **no new GLA component** and **no new GAL lead**. It changes retrieval weighting and supplies two new controls.

First, the historical label `Ferdinand Hamburger, Jr. Archives of The Johns Hopkins University` can now be connected to a **specific record-group path** in published archival citations. Modern scholarship on J. B. Watson cites multiple Watson/Frank J. Goodnow letters under that historical repository name while giving the locator:

`Record Group 02.001 / Office of the President / Series 1 / File 115 / Department of Psychology, 1913-1919`.

The current JHU catalogue independently confirms `RG-02-001` as the **Office of the President records**. This does not identify BRACERS `121121` or canonical `GLA0030` / `121122`, and File 115 must never be projected from Psychology onto Philosophy. But it is the first concrete control showing that an object described under the old Hamburger repository label can map into the current presidential record-group domain. `RG-02-001` is therefore no longer merely a generic institutional fallback; it is a historically demonstrated destination for at least some material carrying exactly that old repository label.

Second, exact archival citations in *Advocacy & Objectivity* show that the **Elliott MSS** held a tight Ross-case correspondence cluster immediately around `GLA0056`:

- Orrin L. Elliott -> Charles Henry Hull, 6 Mar 1901, cited to **Hull and Elliott MSS**;
- Hull -> Elliott, 13 Mar 1901, cited to **Hull and Elliott MSS**;
- George Lincoln Burr -> Elliott, 9 Apr 1901, **Elliott MSS**;
- Arthur O. Lovejoy -> Elliott, 15 Apr 1901, **Elliott MSS** (`GLA0056`).

This does not prove that these items occupy one current Stanford folder, nor that the Elliott witnesses are originals. It does prove that `GLA0056` belongs to a recoverable archival problem-space richer than a single surname hit: `Ross controversy | Elliott | March-April 1901 | professional academic correspondence`.

A third control comes from the National Archives NHPRC catalogue, which confirms a completed **David Starr Jordan Papers microfilm edition**, **187 reels with a 31-page guide**. The guide is now a potentially valuable series-resolution aid for Stanford, but project-level microfilming is not item-level evidence that `GLA0056`, the incoming-copy letterpress system, or every SC0058 volume was filmed.

## 1. JHU: a historical repository label now has a demonstrated current record-group mapping

Batch112 established that `Ferdinand Hamburger Jr. Archives` was a historical name for the Johns Hopkins University Archives rather than the Ferdinand Hamburger Jr. personal papers, MS-0738. Batch114 then separated four current search domains: Goodnow MS-0003, Office of the Secretary RG-10-001, Office of the President RG-02-001, and the temporally later Philosophy Department RG-04-120.

Batch115 supplies a missing bridge between the historical repository label and one modern record group.

The published archival apparatus for the Watson/Goodnow correspondence repeatedly uses the form:

`Ferdinand Hamburger, Jr., Archives of The Johns Hopkins University`

followed by:

`Record Group 02.001 / Office of the President / Series 1 / File 115 / Department of Psychology, 1913-1919`.

Examples include Watson -> Goodnow, 31 Dec 1918, and several 1919 Watson/Goodnow letters; the same scholarship also cites Goodnow -> Watson within the file.

The significance is archival, not thematic. Watson has no necessary relation to the Russell invitation packet. The control demonstrates something narrower and more useful:

`historical Hamburger repository citation`

`can coexist with`

`explicit RG 02.001 / Office of the President / departmental-subject-file custody`.

That makes `RG-02-001` a concretely evidenced **repository-label translation path**.

Current JHU control: `https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/834`  
Secondary exact-locator control: Hall P. Beck et al., “Finding Little Albert,” archival references to JHU Record Group 02.001.

## 2. What this changes for BRACERS 121121

BRACERS `121121` is still securely described as:

`Woods -> Frank J. Goodnow`

concerning whether Bertrand Russell should lecture at Hopkins on *The Principles of Social Reconstruction* and criticism of war as an institution.

BRACERS names the historical Ferdinand Hamburger Jr. Archives as its source.

The first recipient-side test remains **Goodnow MS-0003**, because its current arrangement rule is mechanically useful: correspondence is filed by writer and copies of Goodnow replies are attached. The filing key is therefore `Woods`.

But the institutional test is now better constrained. Because other historical Hamburger citations demonstrably map into `RG-02-001`, the current retrieval programme should search **both**:

`MS-0003 / Woods writer file`

and

`RG-02-001 / 1916-17 presidential departmental or subject files`.

Batch114's `RG-10-001 / Office of the Secretary` remains a separate high-value administrative domain because it begins exactly in 1916 and contains correspondence and memoranda. The point is not to rank one institutional record group absolutely above the other before inspection. It is to distinguish why each is plausible:

`RG-10-001` -> temporal + functional fit;

`RG-02-001` -> institutional fit + demonstrated historical-Hamburger-to-record-group mapping precedent.

A document found in both would still require object-level comparison before calling one a copy or duplicate manifestation.

## 3. What this changes for canonical GLA0030 / BRACERS 121122

`121122` remains:

`Lovejoy, Department of Philosophy -> recipient unresolved`

on the proposed Russell lecture. Lovejoy favors a lecture in technical philosophy but rejects a war lecture because Russell's position is described as having an `ultra-pacifist character`.

Nothing in Batch115 resolves its recipient, date, modern fonds, box, folder, or physical form.

The Watson control does, however, change the search architecture. For an institutional Lovejoy document known only to have been in the historical University Archives, `RG-02-001` now deserves a direct bounded test rather than treatment as a merely generic fallback.

The correct route is:

`1916-17 -> Office of President RG-02-001 -> Department of Philosophy / Russell / Lovejoy / lecture / visiting appointment`

in parallel with:

`1916-17 -> Office of Secretary RG-10-001 -> same administrative vocabulary`.

MS-0003 remains conditional for `121122`: only an independent demonstration that Goodnow was the recipient licenses a Lovejoy-writer-file prediction.

The Watson File 115 locator must **not** be copied over. `Department of Psychology, 1913-1919` is a control showing a filing pattern, not the folder for a Philosophy transaction.

This yields a general rule:

> **A precise old locator may establish a repository-to-record-group mapping without establishing a homologous file number for another department or document.**

## 4. The current Philosophy Department record group remains temporally excluded as a presumed original filing path

JHU's current `RG-04-120` begins in 1922; its Administrative Records begin in 1942. JHU's corporate history separately states that Philosophy, Psychology, and Education split into separate departments around 1915.

These statements coexist without contradiction:

`Department of Philosophy existed as an organizational unit in 1916`

but

`the current RG-04-120 described holdings do not begin until 1922`.

Therefore a 1916 Lovejoy document can be a Department of Philosophy document without the present `RG-04-120` being its continuous original filing location. It may have entered presidential records, secretarial records, another predecessor filing system, or survived as a later copy. No such path is assumed until an object proves it.

Current control: `https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/863` and JHU corporate-authority history for the Department of Philosophy, Psychology, and Education.

## 5. Stanford: GLA0056 sits inside a date-tight Elliott-MSS Ross-case cluster

The most useful Stanford advance in this batch is not another series label. It is a cluster of exact historical citations.

*Advocacy & Objectivity* cites the following materials around the Stanford/Ross controversy:

`6 Mar 1901 | Elliott -> Hull | Hull and Elliott MSS`

`13 Mar 1901 | Hull -> Elliott | Hull and Elliott MSS`

`9 Apr 1901 | George Lincoln Burr -> Elliott | Elliott MSS`

`15 Apr 1901 | Lovejoy -> Elliott | Elliott MSS | GLA0056`.

The March pair is particularly important because the publication explicitly cites both **Hull and Elliott MSS**. That could reflect two physical manifestations, copies, reciprocal papers, or a citation convention covering materials in both archives. It cannot be resolved from the note alone.

The 9 April Burr letter is only six days before Lovejoy's letter and is explicitly in Elliott MSS. Together, these citations support a much better physical request strategy:

`Orrin Leslie Elliott papers`

`Ross affair / Ross controversy`

`March-April 1901`

with exact dates and correspondents supplied.

This is stronger than asking Stanford simply for `Lovejoy`.

It does **not** license the following claims:

- the four letters occupy one folder;
- the current collection preserves the historical order implied by dates;
- Burr and Lovejoy letters are both received originals;
- Hull/Elliott duplicate citations prove two extant manifestations;
- a folder titled `Ross Affair` exists in the current public finding aid.

The exact current Elliott box/folder remains unresolved.

Secondary control: *Advocacy & Objectivity*, archival notes around the Ross case.

## 6. Stanford letterpress and microfilm are two different derivative questions

Batch113 established from the official SC0058 history that incoming letters and loose carbons/drafts of outgoing letters were copied into letterpress books. Batch114 then showed that the descriptive landscape is directional and heterogeneous: received examples appear in Series I-A in 1906, outgoing Jordan letterbooks in Series I-AA, while near-date 1901 scholarship uses labels such as `Private Letterbook` and `Stanford Business Correspondence`.

Batch115 adds the official NHPRC publication-project layer.

The National Archives describes the **David Starr Jordan Papers** microfilm edition as:

- Stanford University Libraries;
- 187 reels;
- 31-page guide;
- including correspondence and material relating to Stanford University among other subjects.

Official control: `https://www.archives.gov/nhprc/projects/catalog/david-starr-jordan`.

This is a later derivative domain. It must remain distinct from:

`Elliott-MSS witness`

`SC0058 loose correspondence`

`contemporaneous letterpress copy`

`later microfilm image`.

The microfilm guide may solve a descriptive problem even if it does not solve the object problem. If the guide tells us which early letterbooks or correspondence series were filmed, it may identify the series/volume to request. But until the guide or reel is checked, there is no basis for saying `GLA0056 is on microfilm`.

The new rule is:

> **A collection-level reproduction project is a coverage layer, not proof of reproduction of a named item.**

## Evidence rules added

> **A historical repository label can be translated through a known old record-group locator without transferring that locator's file number to another document.**

> **A date-tight thematic citation cluster is a retrieval aid, not evidence of current folder adjacency.**

> **A citation to two manuscript repositories may indicate multiple witnesses, but physical object count must be established before deduplication or manifestation claims.**

> **A collection-level microfilm edition is a derivative coverage domain, not item-level reproduction evidence.**

> **Improved retrieval probability does not upgrade archival-presence status.**
