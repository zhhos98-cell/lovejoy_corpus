# Lovejoy / JHI canonical reclassification, 1987–2026 — self-history, virtual archive, and global turn

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT ISSUE / OFFICIAL SELF-DESCRIPTION / STRONG BLOG-CALIBRATION CONTROL / NO LINEAR GENEALOGY**

## Core result

Later JHI history does not present a simple sequence:

`LOVEJOY ORTHODOXY -> CONTEXTUALIST REPLACEMENT -> PRESENT GLOBAL FIELD`.

The journal and adjacent intellectual-history scholarship repeatedly **reclassify the historical unit called `Lovejoy`**, while JHI also increasingly turns its own archive into an object of retrospective reconstruction.

The relevant historical sequence is better represented as:

`FOUNDER / PROGRAMME`
-> `GREAT CHAIN / FIFTY-YEAR OBJECT`
-> `METHOD LEGACY AMONG COMPETING HISTORIOGRAPHIES`
-> `PRECEPT vs PRACTICE / INSTITUTIONAL HISTORY`
-> `NINETEENTH-CENTURY HISTORY-OF-PHILOSOPHY CONTINUITY`
-> `CULTURAL-HISTORY REASSESSMENT`
-> `LONG-RANGE METHOD REHABILITATION`
+
`JHI ARCHIVE ITSELF -> RE-CURATED IN NEW CURRENT CONTEXTS`.

This strongly supports the blog's existing claim that a scholarly career is arranged more than once and that later carriers make different arrangements easier to see.

It does **not** prove that later venues caused the original classification, nor that current JHI is the endpoint or fulfillment of Lovejoy's own programme.

## I. 1987: JHI makes Lovejoy / Great Chain an explicit retrospective object

JHI 48.2 (Apr.–Jun. 1987) opens with a concentrated Lovejoy cluster.

Direct JSTOR issue:

https://www.jstor.org/stable/i346291

Contents include:

- Daniel J. Wilson, `Lovejoy's The Great Chain of Being after Fifty Years`, 187–206;
- Gladys Gordon-Bournique, `A. O. Lovejoy and the "History of Ideas"`, 207–210;
- Edward P. Mahoney, `Lovejoy and the Hierarchy of Being`, 211–230;
- Francis Oakley, `Lovejoy's Unexplored Option`, 231–245;
- Melvin Richter, `Begriffsgeschichte and the History of Ideas`, 247–263.

The issue subsequently includes Pocock and other alternative historiographical/material objects.

Secure carrier-level result:

> **By 1987 JHI was not merely preserving a founder. It explicitly converted Lovejoy, Great Chain, and `history of ideas` into objects of retrospective comparison and reassessment alongside alternative historiographical programmes.**

Do not infer unanimity among the contributors.

https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/the-history-of-ideas-precept-and-practice-1950-2000-and-beyond/

The available abstract describes JHI's mid-century position as a new field occupying an unusual space between more technical historical/philological journals and wider culture.

Palti metadata:

https://philpapers.org/rec/ELETPO-3

Diggins metadata / abstract control:

https://www.researchgate.net/publication/236754237_Arthur_O_Lovejoy_and_the_Challenge_of_Intellectual_History

### Historical significance

The issue architecture itself is revealing:

`JHI / HISTORY-OF-IDEAS SELF-HISTORY`
+
`LATIN AMERICAN CRITIQUE / BEYOND HISTORY-OF-IDEAS`
+
`LOVEJOY REASSESSMENT`

appear within one contemporary carrier.

Therefore:

`LATER JHI = SIMPLE LOVEJOYIAN PROGRAMME`

is untenable as a description of the 2006 issue architecture.

Equally, the issue does not amount to `Lovejoy rejected`. He remains an explicit historical object while the field's units and methods are being revised.

## III. 2010 JHI / Catana: programme declaration separated from historiographical practice

Leo Catana, `Lovejoy's Readings of Bruno: Or How Nineteenth-Century History of Philosophy Was "Transformed" into the History of Ideas`, JHI 71.1 (2010), 91–112.

Direct bibliographic/abstract controls:

https://philpapers.org/rec/LEOLRO

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20503633/

Catana's abstract argues:

- Lovejoy made grand methodological statements in *Great Chain*;
- these can be understood as rhetorical declarations helping establish history of ideas as institutionally distinct from history of philosophy;
- they are not adequate descriptions of his actual historiographical practice;
- the Bruno practice is better contextualized within nineteenth-century general histories of philosophy.

This is extremely important for the present project because it independently licenses:

`PROGRAMME STATEMENT != PRACTICE DESCRIPTION`.

It also supplies a longer prehistory:

`NINETEENTH-CENTURY HISTORY OF PHILOSOPHY`
-> `LOVEJOY HISTORIOGRAPHICAL PRACTICE`

without treating the 1930s programme declaration as a zero point.

Do not use Catana to collapse all of Lovejoy into one inherited nineteenth-century method; the direct claim is about the Bruno case and methodological self-presentation.

## IV. 2014 Wickberg: Lovejoy can be reclassified as cultural historian

Daniel Wickberg, `In the Environment of Ideas: Arthur Lovejoy and the History of Ideas as a Form of Cultural History`, *Modern Intellectual History* 11.2 (2014), 439–464.

Direct Cambridge Core record:

https://doi.org/10.1017/S1479244314000080

Wickberg's title and framing make a distinct reclassification move: Lovejoy is reconsidered not merely as atomizing `unit-idea` methodologist but in relation to cultural history and the `environment of ideas`.

Current use:

`LOVEJOY AS UNIT-IDEA FOIL`
!=
`LOVEJOY AS CULTURAL-HISTORY RESOURCE`.

This does not itself settle which classification is historically superior. It demonstrates that later historiography can change the operative Lovejoy-unit being discussed.

## V. 2023 Mouton: a previously abandoned / caricatured long-range Lovejoy becomes defensible again

Nico Mouton, `An Apologia for Arthur Lovejoy's Long-Range Approach to the History of Ideas`, *History and Theory* 62.2 (2023), 272–295.

Open publisher version:

https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/hith.12298

Institutional version:

https://forskning.ruc.dk/en/publications/an-apologia-for-arthur-lovejoys-long-range-approach-to-the-histor

Mouton explicitly says Lovejoy's long-range approach is little appreciated / largely abandoned, reconstructs a long list of later charges against it, and argues that the approach remains viable and valuable.

This creates another reception state:

`CANONICAL METHOD`
-> `CRITICIZED / LARGELY ABANDONED METHOD`
-> `OBJECT OF REHABILITATION`.

Again, this is not a return to an identical original Lovejoy. Rehabilitation itself selects and redescribes particular features.

## VI. JHI's current official description: regional / chronological / methodological plurality

Current University of Pennsylvania Press journal page:

https://www.pennpress.org/journals/journal/journal-of-the-history-of-ideas/

The journal currently states that since 1940 it has served intellectual history across many fields and is committed to diversity in:

- regional coverage;
- chronological range;
- methodological approaches.

It defines intellectual history expansively/ecumenically across:

- philosophy;
- literature and the arts;
- natural and social sciences;
- religion;
- political thought;
- intersections with cultural history, book history, visual culture, etc.

This is a **present self-description**, not evidence that 1940 execution was already regionally or methodologically symmetrical.

Current formulation should be coded as:

`PRESENT JHI JURISDICTION CLAIM`.

Do not retroject it into the founding era.

## VII. 2014/15 JHI Blog launch: expansion is explicitly narrated as continuity

Penn Press launch page:

https://www.pennpress.org/blog/introducing-jhiblog/

The launch presents contemporary intellectual history as broadly conceived and describes Lovejoy and successors as having shown the field to be ecumenical/expansive. It also claims the widest regional, chronological and methodological range.

This is useful precisely as a **retrospective continuity claim** to be historicized, not taken at face value for every earlier period.

Current coding:

`PRESENT / 2014 SELF-NARRATION OF CONTINUITY`
!=
`DIRECT MEASURE OF 1940–60 ARTICLE DISTRIBUTION`.

The repo's existing regional-object census remains the appropriate control on execution.

## VIII. 2022 Virtual Issues: JHI makes carrier reclassification an explicit editorial operation

JHI Blog, `Reimagining the History of Ideas – The JHI in the Early Cold War – Virtual Issue 3.1`:

https://www.jhiblog.org/2022/11/21/3-1-the-jhi-in-the-early-cold-war/

The editorial framing is unusually direct. It says:

- JHI's >80-year archive is often representative of the history of intellectual history, its trends/priorities/methods;
- some work appeared only occasionally and therefore did not receive visibility or relevant context;
- Virtual Issues **recall earlier articles** fitting a theme and **place them in a new and current context**;
- the editors acknowledge the limits of JHI's coverage and use the archive to recover surprising, obsolete, or newly suggestive configurations.

This is direct present-day actor evidence for an operation the blog currently describes more generally:

`ARCHIVAL ITEM ALREADY PRESENT`
+
`LATER THEMATIC SELECTION`
+
`NEW CONTEXT`
->
`CHANGED VISIBILITY / INTERPRETABILITY`.

Very strong calibration rule:

> **A later carrier can change the visibility and analytical role of already-present material without literally rediscovering an absent object.**

This is almost an institutional self-description of `known but differently classified`.

Do not use it as proof that the same mechanism caused Lovejoy's Buddhist work to become peripheral. It is an analogous and directly documented later JHI operation.

## IX. The 2022 Early Cold War issue also politicizes JHI self-history

The same Virtual Issue proposes interpreting early JHI within liberal anti-totalitarian / anti-communist intellectual culture and describes the journal, in part, as an `anti-communist laboratory`.

Use status:

`CURRENT JHI-BLOG HISTORIOGRAPHICAL INTERPRETATION OF EARLY JHI`.

It is not a neutral institutional fact about every article in 1940–59.

But it establishes that current JHI self-history is willing to reconstruct the journal politically, not only as a method lineage.

This matters to the larger repo because Lovejoy's own political classifications and public liberal/anti-communist commitments cannot be treated as separable by default from the institutional history of history of ideas.

## X. 2016–25 global-intellectual-history debate: expansion without a settled endpoint

### 2016 JHI Blog

`Towards a Global Intellectual History?`

https://www.jhiblog.org/2016/02/08/towards-a-global-intellectual-history/

The post describes rapid growth of global intellectual history while explicitly stressing the absence of methodological consensus and asking about:

- who counts as an intellectual;
- how ideas spread and are received across languages/cultures;
- how transformations occur during travel;
- relation between social and intellectual history.

Thus:

`GLOBAL TURN`
!=
`SINGLE GLOBAL METHOD`.

### 2021 JHI execution: Translation in Action

JHI 82.3 contains a cluster introduced by Lisa Hellman and Birgit Tremml-Werner, `Translation in Action: Global Intellectual History and Early Modern Diplomacy`, 453–467.

Direct issue contents:

https://www.jstor.org/stable/e27069771

JHI Blog issue listing:

https://www.jhiblog.org/2021/07/23/jhi-issue-82-3-now-available/

The cluster includes:

- multilingual Stockholm diplomacy;
- Central Asian borderlands;
- translation of friendship across time/space;
- early modern Japanese concepts of rulership/political geography.

The introduction's abstract emphasizes translators beyond official diplomats, asymmetrical power among actors/languages/media, and translation as a local/global encounter rather than transparent idea transfer.

This is concrete journal execution of a substantially different cross-cultural relation from many early `influence` / comparative uses.

### 2021 JHI Blog decolonizing critique

Milinda Banerjee, `Decolonize Intellectual History! An Agenda for the Capitalocene`:

https://www.jhiblog.org/2021/05/19/decolonize-intellectual-history/

This is an authored intervention, not official JHI policy. It criticizes a historically Euro-American/elite-white-male intellectual-history canon and argues that adding elite non-Western canonical figures is itself insufficient.

It demonstrates that the journal's public-facing ecology contains explicit critique of intellectual history's inherited object hierarchy.

### 2025 self-critique of the global turn

Veronica Lazăr, `The Repressed Political Economy of Global Intellectual History`:

https://www.jhiblog.org/2025/11/24/the-repressed-political-economy-of-global-intellectual-history/

This post argues that global intellectual history has greatly expanded discussion of empire, coloniality, race and epistemic injustice but may reproduce asymmetries between a Western center of knowledge production and an extended plurality of non-Western objects.

Again, this is an authored critical position rather than settled journal doctrine.

It provides a crucial anti-triumphalist control:

`MORE GLOBAL OBJECTS`
!=
`SYMMETRICAL KNOWLEDGE PRODUCTION`.

## XI. Canonical reclassification states

Current evidence permits the following reception map without pretending each state replaces the previous one completely:

1. `LOVEJOY = ACTIVE FOUNDER / PROGRAMME ACTOR`;
2. `LOVEJOY = GREAT CHAIN / UNIT-IDEA CANON`;
3. `LOVEJOY = TARGET / FOIL IN CONTEXTUALIST CRITIQUE`;
4. `LOVEJOY = JHI SELF-HISTORY OBJECT`;
5. `LOVEJOY = PRACTITIONER WHO MAY DIFFER FROM HIS PROGRAMME RHETORIC`;
6. `LOVEJOY = CONTINUATION / TRANSFORMATION OF NINETEENTH-CENTURY PHILOSOPHY HISTORIOGRAPHY`;
7. `LOVEJOY = POSSIBLE CULTURAL HISTORIAN`;
8. `LOVEJOY = LONG-RANGE METHOD AVAILABLE FOR REHABILITATION`.

These classifications overlap.

Therefore:

`LATER CANONICAL LOVEJOY`
should never be treated as one stable object.

## XII. Blog consequence

The blog's closing claim is now stronger:

> `A scholarly career is arranged more than once.`

This is no longer supported only by the contrast between notebook/article/*Great Chain*/bibliography.

JHI's own later history supplies repeated examples of Lovejoy being rearranged as:

- book anniversary object;
- method legacy;
- institutional disciplinary predecessor;
- precept/practice problem;
- nineteenth-century historiographical continuation;
- cultural-history resource;
- rehabilitation target.

And JHI's Virtual Issues explicitly describe the editorial operation of taking already-existing archive material and placing it into new/current contexts to alter visibility.

Potential publication-facing strengthening:

> **The field that Lovejoy helped institutionalize has itself repeatedly made different Lovejoys visible.**

This is attractive but should enter the blog only if one compact supporting reference can be carried without opening a long JHI historiography excursus.
