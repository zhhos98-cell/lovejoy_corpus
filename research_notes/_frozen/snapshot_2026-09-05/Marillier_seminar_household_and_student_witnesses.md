# Marillier's research workshop: seminar, household, papers, and student witnesses

## Status

This note combines four independent witness types that substantially refine how the Marillier → Lovejoy transmission should be modeled:

1. **N. W. Thomas (1896)** — first-person description of Marillier's EPHE seminar as student research, presentation, teacher commentary and peer discussion.
2. **Ida M. Tarbell (retrospective autobiography; based on her Paris years 1891–94)** — eyewitness description of Léon Marillier physically spreading papers out after dinner for inspection and of Jeanne Marillier acting as his amanuensis, copy/proof reader and adviser.
3. **Arnold Van Gennep (1912)** — first-person recollection that reading Marillier's French translation of Andrew Lang caused him to enroll in Marillier's course and radically change both the documents and method of his research.
4. **Marcel Mauss (1902, presently controlled through later quotation pending direct primary text capture)** — acknowledges Marillier's training in the “critique des faits” while criticizing his limited systematization and psychological explanatory framework.

Together with RAI MS 99, these witnesses show that Marillier's intellectual production was not a simple lecturer-to-student transmission. It was a **paper-based, household-supported, seminar-discussed research workshop** in which comparative dossiers circulated between personal working papers, assigned student research, oral discussion, publication, translation and editorial work.

---

## 1. N. W. Thomas: seminar research was interactive, not lecture dictation

Primary source: N. W. Thomas, “The École Pratique des hautes Études,” *Folk-Lore* 7 (1896), 200–201.

Thomas states the EPHE principle as **“work by the pupils, directed by the lecturer.”** In Marillier's course, students could be asked to write an essay on a defined point; the essay would be read in the class, commented on by Marillier, and discussed by other students.

This is direct first-person evidence from a Marillier student. It explains why Lovejoy notebook 005 combines:

- course headings and dates;
- reading lists;
- case clusters;
- analytical taxonomies;
- inserted slips;
- apparent independent objections/extensions.

Such a notebook could contain the residue of **assignments, seminar reports, teacher comments, peer presentations and later revision**, rather than simply transcribed lectures.

---

## 2. Ida Tarbell: the Marillier household was part of the research infrastructure

Primary eyewitness source: Ida M. Tarbell, *All in the Day's Work* (1939), recalling her Paris research in the early 1890s.

Tarbell describes Léon Marillier and Jeanne Le Braz Marillier as a scholarly household. She says Jeanne was her husband's **amanuensis, copy and proof reader, and general adviser**. She also recalls Léon Marillier spreading papers out after dinner for her inspection, with Charles Borgeaud present and eager to examine them.

This evidence matters for the fate and production of Marillier's papers:

- his working papers were physically used in domestic scholarly exchange;
- Jeanne participated directly in copying/proofing/advising;
- visitors were invited to inspect documentary material;
- the household linked Marillier to historians and scholars beyond the formal EPHE seminar.

Tarbell's 1896 *Madame Roland* preface provides a contemporary control for the same relationship. She says Léon and his wife directed her to manuscripts and gave her extensive family letters/cahiers for examination. This corroborates a household practice of controlled scholarly access to papers.

### Provenance implication

When Léon and Jeanne died in 1901, any reconstruction of the posthumous paper transfer must consider **both spouses' documentary roles and the wider family/intellectual network**, not just a formal EPHE office. The current RAI MS 99 provenance hypothesis should therefore test:

- N. W. Thomas / Anthropological Institute;
- Anatole Le Braz and the Le Braz family;
- Cécile Marillier and the Marillier/Roland family;
- Jean Réville / *Revue de l'histoire des religions* editorial network;
- Charles Borgeaud / Seignobos circle;
- possible executors or estate handlers after the 1901 deaths.

No source yet proves which route carried MS 99 to the RAI.

## 3. Van Gennep: an independent student testimony to method transmission

Primary source: Arnold Van Gennep, contribution in *Folk-Lore* 23 (1912), p. 376 (Wikisource scan).

Van Gennep recalls that while he was a student at the École des Langues Orientales Vivantes, the appearance of Marillier's translation of Andrew Lang's *Myth, Ritual and Religion* was one of the decisive causes of his changing field. He writes that he then enrolled in Marillier's course and **“modif[ied] radically the nature of the documents and the direction of method”** of his research.

This is a second first-person student witness, independent of Thomas and Lovejoy.

It establishes that Marillier's course was perceived by a later major ethnographer as changing not merely subject matter but:

- what counted as a relevant document;
- how evidence should be organized;
- what methodological direction research should take.

This strongly supports treating the 005 transformation as pedagogical/methodological transmission rather than only topical borrowing.

## 4. Mauss: explicit debt to Marillier's “critique des faits,” followed by theoretical rupture

Primary target: Marcel Mauss, “L'enseignement de l'histoire des religions des peuples non-civilisés à l'École des Hautes Études. Leçon d'ouverture … (27 janvier 1902),” *Revue de l'histoire des religions* 45 (1902), 36–55, JSTOR stable `23661805`.

The full 1902 primary text has not yet been extracted into this repository. Current wording is therefore controlled through later editions/secondary quotation.

Pascal Le Maléfan reproduces Mauss's later collected formulation:

> “Avant tout, M. Marillier nous apprit la critique des faits.”

The same reconstruction characterizes Marillier's work, following Mauss, as extensive **examination and classification** of traveller/ethnographer reports, with the observer's position scrutinized, alongside critical examination of theories and interpretations. Mauss's criticism is that Marillier did relatively little systematization/invention, classifying facts more than explaining them, and ultimately reducing religious facts to universal psychological laws.

This is crucial for separating inherited practice from later Lovejoy abstraction:

### Inherited from Marillier with high probability

- criticism of ethnographic reports and observer position;
- source provenance/contact criticism;
- careful classification of heterogeneous facts;
- resistance to inferring common origin/function from outward resemblance;
- comparison across regional/monographic cases;
- psychological/causal mechanism as explanatory target.

### Lovejoy's likely distinctive move

Lovejoy's 1906 paper does what Mauss says Marillier did comparatively little of: **systematizes and abstracts** a recurrent mechanism into a general causal category — impersonal, transferable, quasi-mechanical efficacy / “Primitive Energetics.”

This makes Mauss's criticism unexpectedly useful for the Lovejoy genealogy. It provides a contemporary diagnostic distinction between:

> **Marillier: critique + examination + classification of facts**
>
> **Lovejoy: abstraction of one causal pattern from that classified field**

This remains a working formulation until Mauss 1902 is directly collated page by page.

## 5. RAI MS 99 now fits this workshop model

RAI MS 99 preserves Marillier-attributed thematic dossiers on:

- origin of death / abode of the dead;
- survivance;
- totemism;
- human sacrifice;
- marriage;
- myths;
- flood;
- taboo;
- cult of the dead / sorcery;
- bibliography.

These are not merely the topics of his published articles. They reproduce the structure of his EPHE teaching over roughly 1889–1901.

Thomas's description suggests a practical use for such dossiers: they could serve as the teacher's source apparatus for defining seminar problems, evaluating student essays and supplying commentary. Tarbell's account shows that Marillier also handled and displayed research papers in a domestic scholarly setting.

Therefore the highest-value collation is now not simply `Marillier MS99 ↔ Lovejoy 005 wording`, but a more granular test:

1. **shared bibliography/order** — possible teacher dossier/assignment;
2. **shared example but divergent classification** — possible seminar debate/student reworking;
3. **Lovejoy-only objections or taxonomy** — possible student extension;
4. **MS99-only examples** — control against overfitting;
5. **later sources in 005 absent from MS99** — evidence of post-seminar accretion.

## 6. New provenance questions after Tarbell

The death sequence is unusually disruptive: Jeanne Le Braz Marillier died in the September 1901 maritime disaster involving the Le Braz family; Léon Marillier survived the wreck but died on 15 October 1901. Anatole Le Braz later recalled that during Marillier's final illness, Marillier discussed unfinished works and gave explicit instructions concerning older published notes.

This demonstrates that Marillier was conscious of incomplete scholarly work and capable of giving literary instructions shortly before death, but no currently located source states what happened to the main research dossiers.

Priority searches should now include:

- wills / estate records for Léon and Jeanne Marillier;
- correspondence of Anatole Le Braz immediately after September–October 1901;
- Jean Réville / RHR editorial correspondence;
- Cécile Marillier family papers;
- N. W. Thomas / Anthropological Institute correspondence and accession records;
- any posthumous publication plan prompted by the public call to publish Marillier's accumulated materials.

---

## 7. Current production model

The evidence now supports a richer chain:

> **Marillier personal thematic dossiers (now at least partly represented by RAI MS 99)**
> ↕
> **household documentary labour (Jeanne as amanuensis/proof reader/adviser; scholarly visitors inspecting papers)**
> ↕
> **EPHE assignments + student essays/source research**
> ↕
> **student oral presentation + Marillier criticism + peer discussion**
> ↕
> **Lovejoy notebook 005 / other student notebooks**
> ↓
> **Lovejoy's c.1900 conceptual abstraction**
> ↓
> **1906 “Primitive Energetics” first published chapter**

This model is materially testable once RAI MS 99 images and another student's notebooks become available.
