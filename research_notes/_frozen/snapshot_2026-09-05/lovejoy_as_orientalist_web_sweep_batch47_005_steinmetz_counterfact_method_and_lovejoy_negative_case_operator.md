## Research move

A closer pass through Mauss’s 1896–97 analytical description of Steinmetz requires a historiographical correction to the way the `Endokannibalismus` line has been framed in earlier batches.

Steinmetz should **not** be treated as a simple classifier who grouped outwardly similar practices and then generalized from resemblance. Mauss’s contemporary account presents a much more disciplined method:

- contrary facts are to be recorded and explained;
- variation is as important as concordance;
- statistical comparison should measure how far a proposed law or theory actually applies;
- deviations may be more instructive than cases matching the expected type;
- *Endokannibalismus* itself, especially §§16–22 / offprint pp.36–47, is named by Mauss as an example of this method of exposition.

The stronger distinction between Steinmetz and 005 therefore lies one level later: **what counts as an explanatory variable, what category receives a case, and what evidentiary warrant permits a universal primitive reconstruction.**

## 1. Mauss’s contemporary account of Steinmetz’s comparative method

In the analytical first half of `La religion et les origines du droit pénal d’après un livre récent`, Mauss describes Steinmetz’s method as explicitly designed against selective comparison.

Mauss reports Steinmetz’s requirement that all contrary facts be brought forward and accounted for. He then describes the statistical method as one that records and quantifies both variations and concordances in order to measure the applicability of a law or theory and build systems progressively closer to the evidence.

Most important, Mauss says in substance that ethnology has more to learn from **deviations** than from mere coincidences with the typical phenomenon.

A footnote then identifies Steinmetz’s recent *Endokannibalismus*, §§16–22, offprint pp.36–47, as displaying this method of exposition.

This is direct contemporary evidence that the exact source written into Lovejoy 005 p.62 already carried a **counterexample-sensitive comparative apparatus**.

## 2. This corrects an overly simple Steinmetz → Lovejoy contrast

Earlier batches usefully contrasted Steinmetz’s strong primitive-origin conclusion with Lovejoy’s mechanism pluralism. That remains valid, but the intermediate methodological picture must be sharpened.

Steinmetz already possessed:

- motive coding;
- source-reliability assessment;
- systematic presentation of contrary evidence;
- interest in variation rather than bare concordance;
- a statistical ambition to determine the range of a law or theory.

Therefore Lovejoy’s distinctive move is not simply `introducing exceptions` or `discovering multiple motives`.

The stronger transformation is:

> **counterfact-sensitive comparison → finer causal and categorical redivision → stricter limits on what may be inferred from distribution or recurring form.**

## 3. The tension inside Steinmetz is methodologically productive

The contemporary evidence now shows a real tension rather than a crude opposition.

### At the level of evidence management

Steinmetz is unusually scrupulous. W. I. Thomas independently confirms that *Endokannibalismus* tabulated peoples, motives and reliability of information. Mauss adds the insistence on contrary facts, deviations and variation.

### At the level of final reconstruction

Thomas reports a strong developmental claim in which hunger / omnivory precedes later magical or religious motives. Keane preserves Steinmetz’s conclusion at pp.59–60 that endocannibalism could with high probability be regarded as a regular custom of primitive humanity and lower peoples.

Thus the tension is:

> **plural and counterfact-sensitive empirical apparatus**
>
> → **strong universalizing primitive reconstruction.**

The methodological question for the 005 paper becomes: **how far can disciplined comparison legitimately license a universal origin claim?**

## 4. Mauss attacks not the existence of counterfacts but the level of the rubric

This correction also makes Mauss’s critique more intelligible.

Mauss does not complain that Steinmetz ignores all anomalous evidence. Instead he says that some of Steinmetz’s statistical rubrics are too broad and that he sometimes grants too much authority to interpretations supplied by informants or ethnographers.

In the mortuary field Mauss asks why Steinmetz did not distinguish more systematically between:

- funerary customs;
- cults of the dead proper;
- deified ancestors versus ordinary dead;
- sacrifice versus a terminal episode of mourning.

The defect is therefore **classification before counting**. A table may contain contrary cases and still compare unlike mechanisms under one heading.

This is extremely close to the later operation of 005.

## 5. 005 repeatedly uses negative cases to break proposed classifications

The later notebook does more than accumulate alternative motives. It repeatedly uses a case that *should* fit a theory, but does not, to narrow or reject the theory.

### PDF pp.106–107 — ceremoniality is not diagnostic

Repeated, public or solemn cannibalism may still fail to establish a religious or sacramental origin. Ceremonial regularity is treated as insufficient evidence.

### PDF p.110 — substitution theory has too few secure demonstrations

The claim that animal sacrifice universally replaces earlier human sacrifice is rejected because convincing cases are limited. The comparative distribution does not support the developmental law at the required strength.

### PDF p.112 — circumcision is not partial human sacrifice

Bodily injury and blood do not suffice to place the rite under sacrifice; initiation, incorporation and social status explain the evidence more directly.

### PDF p.117 — adult married men as a negative case

If even adult married men may be beaten in initiation contexts, bodily injury itself cannot establish the alleged connection with human sacrifice. This is a particularly clean negative-case test.

These moves are best described as a **negative-case operator**:

> predicted diagnostic feature appears → proposed genus is tested → counterexample breaks the inference → case is reassigned by mechanism / relation / historical layer.

## 6. A revised genealogy of comparative operation

The strongest sequence is now more nuanced than `Steinmetz generalizes; Lovejoy distinguishes`.

### Steinmetz

- exhaustive comparison;
- motives and source reliability;
- contrary facts and deviations explicitly matter;
- statistical variation used to test applicability;
- nevertheless strong primitive-origin reconstruction remains possible.

### Mauss

- accepts the evidentiary seriousness of Steinmetz;
- attacks broad rubrics and overconfident source interpretation;
- insists on distinctions among funerary custom, cult, ancestor status, sacrifice and mourning;
- reclassifies `endocannibalisme` relationally and religiously.

### Marillier

- in 1897 explicitly allows a theory to be `partiellement exacte` without being the sole explanation;
- rejects categories based only on `ressemblances extérieures`;
- permits parallel but analogous classes;
- attacks reduction of sacrifice to a single original sacramental type.

### Lovejoy 005

- retains counterexample-sensitive comparison;
- uses negative cases to invalidate diagnostic shortcuts;
- reassigns cases by causal mechanism and historical relation;
- increasingly separates transferable efficacy from sacramental interpretation.

The workshop genealogy is therefore not the invention of pluralism from scratch. It is a **progressive tightening of the relation between evidence, category and causal inference**.

## 7. Implication for `Primitive Energetics`

This also improves the 1906 endpoint.

If 005 repeatedly extracts one causal relation from cases that fail broader ritual categories, then `Primitive Energetics` can be read as the later stabilization of one such cross-cutting mechanism family:

- direct efficacy;
- intrinsic efficacy;
- transmissibility / communicability;
- contact;
- impersonal or quasi-mechanical causation.

The abstraction emerges not from ignoring exceptions but from **reclassifying cases that break inherited genera**.

This remains a developmental interpretation, not a claim that the mature 1906 terminology already existed in 1898–99.

## 8. Strongest safe formulation

> **Steinmetz’s comparative method was already unusually sensitive to contrary facts, variations and source reliability; Mauss explicitly cited *Endokannibalismus* as an example of this evidentiary discipline. The methodological difference visible in Lovejoy’s 005 notebook therefore lies less in discovering exceptions than in what is done with them. Negative cases repeatedly invalidate a proposed ritual genus and force reclassification by causal mechanism, social relation or historical layer. The Paris genealogy is consequently one of tightening evidentiary warrant: from counterfact-sensitive comparison to mechanism-sensitive classification.**
