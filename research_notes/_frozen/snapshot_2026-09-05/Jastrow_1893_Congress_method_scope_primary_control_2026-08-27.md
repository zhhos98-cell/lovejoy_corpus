# Morris Jastrow Jr. 1893 Congress paper — direct method/scope primary control

Date incorporated: 2026-08-27
Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / OPT-001 RESOLVED**

Source returned by user: OCR of C. Staniland Wake, ed., *Memoirs of the International Congress of Anthropology* (Chicago, 1894), Morris Jastrow Jr., `The Scope and Method of the Historical Study of Religions`, printed pp. 287–297.

## Direct results

Jastrow explicitly distinguishes the historical study of religions from dogmatic and philosophical approaches. His historical method is an investigation of religious facts interpreted through the conditions that give rise to them; it deliberately refuses to begin by unifying the phenomena under a prior system of thought.

He makes recourse to original sources and original research a *conditio sine qua non* of historical study. Because no investigator can gather the whole field first-hand, Jastrow proposes a jurisdictional solution: independent original investigation in some section of the field is indispensable for learning how to use the researches of specialists in other sections. This is a direct formulation of the source-specialist / general-comparative distinction previously reconstructed from his 1893 `Recent Movements` paper and 1899 article.

The paper then assigns different auxiliary disciplines different functions. Anthropology bears on race and the limits of religious development; the `new psychology` bears on physiological/psychological processes and individual religious temperaments; folklore bears especially on customs and survivals. Jastrow explicitly warns that folklore/comparison can `prove too much`: wide observation may establish analogies and coincidences without licensing the consequences that initially seem obvious. He says this danger is especially acute in the study of primitive culture.

The institutional section makes the architecture explicit. Jastrow praises the Paris EPHE religious-studies section for assigning the sources of distinct religions to source-specialist chairs while the Collège de France supplies the general/comparative level; he argues that a complete department should additionally mobilize anthropology, psychology, and folklore.

His museum plan repeats the same layered structure: general, special, and comparative sections; primitive and `civilized` religions remain explicitly staged in the special section; comparative charts map agreements/divergences in historical-developmental order.

## Interpretive payoff

This closes MF/OPT-001 and sharpens the pre-Lovejoy American field control:

`local source competence / original research`
→ `disciplined use of other specialists`
→ `auxiliary disciplinary explanation`
→ `comparative synthesis under an explicit anti-overclaim warning`.

At the same time, Jastrow combines that fine-grained methodological discipline with explicit racial-developmental hierarchy. He writes of race as limiting religious development and structures museum/history language around primitive/civilized stages.

Therefore the strongest field-level result is not `source specialization defeated totalization`. It is the opposite and more useful proposition:

> **Late-nineteenth-century source specialization, comparative restraint, psychological explanation, and civilizational/racial totalization could coexist inside the same declared historical method.**

This directly supports the repo's broader rule that decomposition does not imply anti-totalization.
