# Skinner 1970–1974: Mew counterexample -> claim-scope revision; Hobbes -> Locke case substitution

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **SKINNER 1974 SELF-ATTRIBUTED REVISION DIRECT / 1970 ORIGINAL CLAIM PAGE-SPECIFIC CONTROL / MEW 1971 METADATA DIRECT BUT FULL TEXT GATED / CASE-SUBSTITUTION DIRECT**

## Core result

Skinner's 1974 `Some Problems in the Analysis of Political Thought and Action` supplies one of the project's strongest direct examples of a criticism producing an explicitly acknowledged change in claim scope.

Skinner states that he **accepts Peter Mew's criticism**, based on counterexamples, of his earlier attempt to argue that it must *invariably* be necessary to invoke social as well as linguistic conventions in order to decode the intended force of certain utterances. He then says he continues to defend the argument in a **revised and extended form**.

This is stronger evidence than a draft-reader acknowledgment.

It licenses:

`MEW COUNTEREXAMPLES`
-> `SKINNER AUTHOR-ACKNOWLEDGED REVISION OF UNIVERSAL SCOPE CLAIM`.

But it does not license:

`MEW CAUSED EVERY CHANGE IN SKINNER'S LATER METHOD`.

The same article gives a second, distinct mechanism. Skinner says his Hobbes evidence may be controversial and therefore switches to a simpler independent Locke example to substantiate the **same two central methodological conclusions**.

Thus:

`CASE SUBSTITUTION`
!= `METHOD-CLAIM CHANGE`.

Together the two mechanisms distinguish:

1. **claim revision under counterexample pressure**;
2. **case replacement / robustness testing while retaining the claim family**.

## I. 1970 original claim: communication depends on social + linguistic convention

Quentin Skinner, `Conventions and the Understanding of Speech Acts`, *The Philosophical Quarterly* 20.79 (April 1970), 118–138, DOI 10.2307/2218084.

Direct publisher/JSTOR metadata are secure.

The full article remains access-restricted in the current public route, but p.137 is repeatedly reproduced in later scholarship. The page-specific formulation says the success of communication necessarily depends on speaker and audience mutually grasping a complex of conventions, **social as well as linguistic**, concerning what can be stated, what meanings/allusions can be understood without explicit statement, and what criteria for applying a concept count in a given situation and society.

Evidence status:

`1970 PAGE-SPECIFIC SECONDARY QUOTATION OF PRIMARY + DIRECT METADATA`.

Do not call this direct-full inspection.

The contemporaneous claim is nevertheless sufficiently controlled to identify the stronger conventionalist architecture against which the 1974 self-revision is framed.

## III. 1974: Skinner explicitly accepts the criticism

Skinner's 1974 *Political Theory* response directly states that he does not pretend the extension of his argument is difficulty-free.

He then explicitly says he **accepts the criticism Mew made by means of ingenious counterexamples** against his attempt to argue that it must invariably be necessary to invoke social **and** linguistic conventions to decode intended force.

This is a rare evidence state:

`CRITIC IDENTIFIED`
+
`CRITICISM IDENTIFIED`
+
`TARGET CLAIM IDENTIFIED`
+
`AUTHOR SAYS I ACCEPT THE CRITICISM`.

Therefore the usual firewall can be relaxed for this specific proposition.

Normally:

`CRITICISM / DRAFT COMMENT != SPECIFIC REVISION CAUSE`.

Here, for the stated scope claim:

`AUTHOR'S EXPLICIT SELF-ATTRIBUTION`
licenses
`CRITICISM -> CLAIM-SCOPE REVISION`.

The evidentiary license is local, not global.

---

## IV. What exactly changes: universal necessity is weakened, core conclusions retained

Skinner describes the post-Mew argument as **revised and extended** but says it still sustains two principal conclusions.

The directly exposed 1974 text retains:

1. grasping the historical meaning / intended force of a text is a necessary condition for understanding it;
2. this cannot in general be achieved by studying the text alone, because historically relevant conventions/context enter the recovery of what an author was doing.

The Mew correction therefore does not amount to abandonment of the whole contextualist/speech-act programme.

The local transformation is better represented as:

`UNIVERSAL CLAIM: SOCIAL + LINGUISTIC CONVENTIONS MUST INVARIABLY BE INVOKED`
->
`REVISED CLAIM FAMILY: CONTEXT / CONVENTIONS REMAIN NECESSARY TO HISTORICAL MEANING, WITHOUT THE SAME ACROSS-THE-BOARD SOCIAL+LINGUISTIC FORMULA`.

Do not over-specify the exact logical form of the revised proposition beyond what the 1974 article states.

Core rule:

`COUNTEREXAMPLE TO UNIVERSAL FORM`
!= `REFUTATION OF EVERY NARROWER CLAIM IN THE PROGRAMME`.

---

## V. This is a direct example of proof-burden repair

The original stronger formulation carried a universal burden:

`FOR EVERY RELEVANT UTTERANCE, BOTH SOCIAL AND LINGUISTIC CONVENTIONS MUST BE INVOKED`.

A counterexample is sufficient to defeat that universal form.

Skinner's response reduces the burden by retaining methodological conclusions that do not require the same universal quantifier.

Thus:

`COUNTEREXAMPLE`
-> `SCOPE RESTRICTION / BURDEN REDUCTION`
while
`CORE METHOD CLAIM FAMILY SURVIVES`.

This is exactly the sort of proposition-level transformation that generic influence/citation histories miss.

---

## VI. Hobbes -> Locke: disputed case is replaced, method claim held fixed

The same 1974 article gives a different operation.

Parekh and Berki had treated *Leviathan* as an especially strong example of a canonical text capable of speaking to transhistorical audiences without requiring detailed historical context.

Skinner answers with historical claims about Hobbes but concedes that his alleged discoveries and/or interpretation of the Hobbes case may themselves be controversial.

He therefore says he will **revert to a simpler example**: Locke.

The Locke example is used to substantiate the same two central conclusions about historical meaning and the inadequacy of text-alone interpretation.

Therefore:

`HOBBES CASE CONTESTED`
-> `LOCKE CASE SUBSTITUTED`
while
`METHOD CLAIM FAMILY HELD CONSTANT`.

This is not claim revision in the Mew sense.

It is a robustness / independent-exemplification strategy.

---

## VII. Case substitution changes the evidentiary unit, not necessarily the proposition

The historical unit supporting the method claim changes:

`HOBBES / LEVIATHAN`
-> `LOCKE / POLITICAL TEXT`.

The target propositions do not change merely because the case does.

Thus:

`CASE UNIT CHANGE`
!= `CLAIM UNIT CHANGE`.

And:

`ONE CASE'S CONTESTABILITY`
!= `METHOD FAILURE`.

A second case can test whether the method claim depends on peculiarities of the first.

However:

`TWO SUPPORTIVE CASES`
!= `UNIVERSAL VALIDITY PROVEN`.

The evidentiary gain is robustness against a case-specific objection, not proof of universality.

---

## VIII. Two distinct response mechanics inside one article

Skinner 1974 is therefore unusually useful because it contains both:

### A. Revision mechanic

`MEW COUNTEREXAMPLE`
-> `ACCEPTED CRITICISM`
-> `UNIVERSAL SCOPE WEAKENED / ARGUMENT REVISED`.

### B. Case-control mechanic

`HOBBES SUPPORT DISPUTED`
-> `LOCKE SUBSTITUTE CASE`
-> `SAME CENTRAL METHOD CLAIMS RETESTED`.

These should not be collapsed into one generic category such as `criticism influenced Skinner`.

The unit/relation distinctions are different:

- Mew changes the **claim scope / proof burden**;
- Locke changes the **supporting case unit** while the central claim is held fixed.

## X. Relation to Parekh–Berki / unit-selection dialogue

The Mew revision and Hobbes/Locke substitution extend the Round-9 rule:

`UNIT ADEQUACY = QUESTION + CLAIM + PROOF BURDEN`.

They add:

> **PROOF-BURDEN REPAIR MAY OCCUR EITHER BY REVISING THE CLAIM OR BY REPLACING THE SUPPORTING CASE; THESE ARE DIFFERENT OPERATIONS.**

Parekh–Berki 1976 distinguish article-unit from actor/oeuvre-unit.

Skinner 1974 now distinguishes:

- universal method proposition from narrower revised proposition;
- one contested historical case from a substitute case;
- method claim from exemplifying case.

This gives a more complete research-design model:

`QUESTION`
-> `CLAIM`
-> `UNIT`
-> `PROOF BURDEN`
-> if challenged:
   - `REVISE CLAIM?`
   - `CHANGE SUPPORTING CASE?`
   - `CHANGE ANALYTIC UNIT?`
   - `HOLD / ABANDON?`.
