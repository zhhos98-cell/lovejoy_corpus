## Evidence status / firewall

This is the first direct repository read of the **archival Wilson transcription witness** for the France 1898–99 letters. It closes the earlier `letters themselves still required` retrieval gate at the level of Wilson's archival transcription.

It is **not** direct inspection of Lovejoy's autograph letters. Therefore:

- actor-level wording is much stronger than Wilson's later biographical paraphrase;
- Wilson's editorial omissions, summaries, `[sic]`, missing-page notices and transcription choices remain part of the witness;
- publication claims depending on punctuation, spelling, or exact manuscript wording should still be checked against the autograph if available.

The packet itself records at least two incompletenesses in the underlying letters: a missing page in the 20 Oct 1898 letter and an unfinished/missing final page in the 1/5 Dec 1898 letter.

## Executive delta

The new packet materially strengthens the Paris/Orientalist branch in four ways.

1. **Comparative religion was Lovejoy's stated prospective field before the Paris lectures began.** In the 9 Oct 1898 letter to G. H. Howison, written from Auxerre, Lovejoy says the Paris lectures begin in November and that he hopes for `an interesting year's work there in philosophy and comparative religion`.

2. **Lovejoy himself formulates the philology/philosophy jurisdiction problem.** In the 20 Oct 1898 letter to W. W. Lovejoy he proposes `to elucidate the theoretical presuppositions and doctrines of Buddhism from a philosophical point of view`. His diagnosis is explicit: philologists had expounded Buddhist literature while caring little for philosophy; philosophers had reconstructed Buddhist conceptions while neglecting `the technicalities of the texts`. This is direct actor evidence for the exact source/analysis split that the project had previously reconstructed indirectly.

3. **Sylvain Lévi was selected as the intended solution to that jurisdiction problem.** Lovejoy calls Lévi, at the École des Hautes Études, the man he wants `to study under for this purpose`. The same passage links Buddhism and Hebrew Wisdom literature through the problem of evil, while noting that his fellowship required him to keep his principal work within what was ordinarily understood as philosophy.

4. **The December letter identifies actual practice after the formal Pāli course failed to materialize.** Lovejoy states that there were `no courses in Pali Buddhism given this year`, but that he would probably go to Lévi's house and `read a little of the Petakas with him occasionally`. Wilson's typescript clearly reads **`Petakas`**. Normalize only cautiously as likely `Piṭakas`; do not silently replace it with `Jātakas`. The same letter documents Maurice Vernes on Psalms for Lovejoy's `Wisdom work`, Jean Réville on the Fourth Gospel, access to the Faculty of Theology library for Wisdom-book commentaries, and a trial of Sabatier on Acts that Lovejoy abandoned because it lay outside that year's line of work and presupposed too much patristic/extracanonical knowledge.

These are direct actor-level statements of **research intention, disciplinary problem, teacher selection, course failure, compensatory private reading, and adjacent Hebrew/Christian textual work**.

## Critical loci

### A. 9 Oct 1898 — philosophy + comparative religion before Paris coursework

The Howison letter is unusually important because it was written **before** the Paris lecture programme began. The letter's main body is philosophical rather than Orientalist: Lovejoy reports how advanced Harvard students discussed Royce, Howison and pluralism; asks about relations among monads, numerical plurality, and the function of God in a pluralistic scheme; and treats monism/pluralism as constructive problems rather than simple school labels.

At the close he states that the Paris lectures do not begin until November and that he hopes for work in **philosophy and comparative religion**.

Warrant:

> comparative religion was not a later accidental by-product of whatever Paris course he happened to enter; Lovejoy named it prospectively as part of the year's work.

Limit:

> the sentence does not by itself specify Buddhism, Marillier, or a course list. Those are supplied by later letters and independent institutional evidence.

### B. 20 Oct 1898 — actor-level formulation of the philology/philosophy problem

The letter opens the relevant surviving page in the middle of a discussion of biblical Wisdom literature. Lovejoy then proposes a second task: a philosophical elucidation of Buddhist theoretical presuppositions and doctrines.

His diagnosis has three parts:

1. **philological achievement:** Buddhist literature had been expounded;
2. **philosophical failure:** philosophers reconstructed Buddhist conceptions `more or less out of their own heads`;
3. **jurisdiction gap:** philologists neglected philosophy while philosophers neglected the textual technicalities.

This substantially strengthens the current project model. It is actor-level evidence that Lovejoy was explicitly trying to combine **technical textual control** with **philosophical reconstruction**, rather than moving unreflectively from one disciplinary milieu into another.

This passage should now outrank later Wilson paraphrase whenever the claim is about Lovejoy's own formulation of the problem.

### C. Buddhism + Wisdom = problem of evil, but fellowship boundary = philosophy

The continuation on p.11 states that Buddhism and Wisdom literature both finally attempt to deal with the **problem of evil**; Ecclesiastes can read like Buddhist literature. Lovejoy immediately limits himself: he must not do too much that `savors of Comp Religion` because his fellowship obliges him to study principally what is ordinarily understood by philosophy.

This is unusually useful disciplinary-boundary evidence:

- the objects and questions cross philosophy / comparative religion;
- Lovejoy knows the boundary;
- the fellowship imposes an institutional allocation of effort;
- comparative religion is therefore neither absent nor identical with his official principal field.

This directly supports a **distributed formation** model and resists later retrospective career labels.

### D. 1/5 Dec 1898 — course failure and private substitute pedagogy

The December letter supplies the missing practice layer.

Lovejoy reports:

- no Pāli Buddhism course that year;
- intended occasional reading at Sylvain Lévi's house;
- Wilson typescript: `read a little of the Petakas with him occasionally`;
- Maurice Vernes, Psalms, explicitly to get `en rapport` for Lovejoy's Wisdom work;
- residual Hebrew becoming useful;
- Jean Réville, Fourth Gospel, as a `parergon`;
- authorization to use the Faculty of Theology library because it held better Wisdom-book commentary resources than the BnF;
- Sabatier on Acts tried once, then rejected as both technically over-demanding and outside the year's line of work.

This is a compact primary dossier of how Lovejoy **selected, ranked, and abandoned** possible lines of work. Formal programme, private reading, library infrastructure and relevance-to-project are visibly different categories.

### E. `Petakas`, not `Jātakas`

Direct image control of PDF p.13 shows Wilson typed:

`read a little of the Petakas with him occasionally`

Repository rule:

- diplomatic witness: `Petakas`;
- probable normalization: `Piṭakas`;
- confidence in normalization: medium-high, but still editorial;
- **do not write `Jātakas` unless an autograph or independent witness establishes it.**

This corrects any previous loose paraphrase of the private reading as generic `sacred texts` or as `Jātakas`.

### F. Hebrew Wisdom was a coequal live research line

The packet makes `Wisdom work` concrete rather than vague.

- Israel Lévi / new Hebrew fragment of Ecclesiastes appears in the BnF context.
- Maurice Vernes's Psalms course is chosen for relation to the Wisdom project.
- Faculty of Theology library access is valued for commentaries on Wisdom books.
- Buddhism and Ecclesiastes are explicitly joined through pessimism / problem of evil.

This matters because the Paris year should not be reconstructed as a single-track Buddhist apprenticeship. A more accurate actor-level model is:

`philosophical pluralism / metaphysics`
+
`Buddhist theoretical reconstruction grounded in text`
+
`Hebrew Wisdom / Ecclesiastes`
+
`comparative religion / Marillier`
+
`adjacent Christian textual and ecclesiastical observation`.

### G. Howison letter as philosophical baseline

The 9 Oct Howison letter preserves the philosophical baseline from which the Buddhist project should be read. Lovejoy is already preoccupied with:

- pluralism versus monism;
- whether pluralism can be positively and constructively conceived rather than merely used against monism;
- real relations and operations among self-active centres;
- the numerical / `City of Souls` problem;
- the role of deity in a pluralistic universe;
- the distinction between religious adequacy and logical coherence.

This cautions against treating the Paris Buddhist turn as a departure from philosophy. The 20 Oct project statement instead suggests an attempt to bring a textually disciplined Buddhist case into an already active philosophical problem-field.

### H. Religion, politics, and observation

The letters also contain a substantial observational layer on contemporary Christianity and French politics:

- Anglo-Catholic revival and Prayer Book practices;
- Irvingism/Theosophy comparisons;
- Catholicism, Protestantism, `Americanisme`, Emerson/New England Transcendentalist influence, Sabatier and Brunetière;
- Notre-Dame ritual aesthetics versus personal will/responsibility;
- Higher Criticism and moral development in biblical literature.

Some passages contain period racialized/antisemitic characterizations of Jewish figures. Preserve them as actor evidence if the political/social-attitude branch requires them, but they are not necessary to establish the current Buddhist/comparative-method argument and should not be naturalized as neutral description.

## Consequence for the JHI argument

No automatic Blog expansion is required. The current Blog can remain on production hold. But the evidentiary ceiling rises materially.

The strongest possible future use is not simply `Lovejoy studied Buddhism`. It is:

> before and during the Paris year, Lovejoy explicitly described a research problem produced by the separation of philological textual expertise from philosophical reconstruction, and sought a form of study that could join the two.

That actor-level formulation closely matches the repository's independent reconstruction from notebooks, 1898 publication selection, Paris institutional controls and later classificatory work. It therefore strengthens the argument **without requiring a genealogy claim** from a single teacher or discipline.
