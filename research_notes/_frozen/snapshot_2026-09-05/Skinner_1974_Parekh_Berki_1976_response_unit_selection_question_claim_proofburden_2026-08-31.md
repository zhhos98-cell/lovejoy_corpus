# Skinner 1974 / Parekh–Berki 1976: response-unit selection as question + claim + proof burden

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **SKINNER 1974 OPENING/SCOPE DIRECT-CONTROLLED + PB1976 FULL DIRECT / TWO-SIDED ANALYTIC-UNIT DIALOGUE LOCKED**

## Core result

The Skinner / Parekh–Berki exchange now supplies a rare two-sided explicit discussion of **how the object of analysis should be selected**.

Skinner's 1974 symposium response begins by distinguishing two possible response units:

1. pursue Jonathan Wiener's Hobbes criticism by supplying more historical/contextual information about Hobbes;
2. say more about Skinner's **general approach to the study of political theory**, of which his Hobbes work had mainly served as an example.

He explicitly chooses the second.

A footnote simultaneously narrows Charles Tarlton's wider critique: Tarlton had also discussed the methodological writings of John Dunn and J. G. A. Pocock, but Skinner says he confines his remarks to the sections concerned with **his own work**.

Parekh and Berki's 1976 communication then explicitly explains their own unit selection. If their aim had been to trace the development of Skinner's thought or reconstruct his current position, one article would have been insufficient. Their actual aim, they say, was to assess a specific methodological approach philosophically; Skinner's 1969 article articulated that approach sufficiently well to serve as the representative analytic unit.

Together the exchange yields:

> **ANALYTIC UNIT ADEQUACY IS RELATIVE TO THE QUESTION, CLAIM, AND PROOF BURDEN.**

The two sides disagree substantively, but they share the deeper structure that a historical individual, one text, one case, one method programme, and one group/school are **different possible units** which cannot be substituted without argument.

---

## I. Skinner 1974 explicitly chooses between CASE and METHOD PROGRAMME

Quentin Skinner, `Some Problems in the Analysis of Political Thought and Action`, *Political Theory* 2.3 (August 1974), 277–303.

Direct SAGE/JSTOR metadata; the opening text is directly exposed in current searchable carriers.

Skinner says he could answer Wiener by adding historical information about Hobbes's political thought, especially the social pressures around Hobbesian absolutism.

He instead chooses to discuss his **general approach** to political theory.

Crucially, he describes his Hobbes scholarship as having been intended mainly to serve as an **example** of that general approach.

This gives an explicit relation:

`HOBBES CASE`
!= `SKINNER METHOD PROGRAMME`.

And:

`CASE CAN EXEMPLIFY METHOD`
without becoming
`THE SAME ANALYTIC UNIT AS METHOD`.

The choice is a carrier act: the response article decides what kind of thing the symposium debate will continue to treat as its object.

---

## II. The same response narrows a GROUP critique to an INDIVIDUAL-WORK unit

Skinner's note 1 states that Tarlton's critique is also concerned with John Dunn and J. G. A. Pocock, but Skinner confines his remarks to those parts of Tarlton's article concerning his own work.

Therefore:

`TARLTON GROUP / PROGRAMME CRITIQUE`
-> `SKINNER SELF-SCOPED INDIVIDUAL-WORK RESPONSE`.

This is analytically important.

A critique may construct a plural unit such as:

`POCOCK + DUNN + SKINNER / REVISIONIST OR CONTEXTUAL HISTORICISM`.

A respondent may refuse that aggregate unit and answer only:

`SKINNER'S OWN CLAIMS / WORK`.

Thus:

`CRITIC'S UNIT`
!= `RESPONDENT'S ACCEPTED UNIT`.

Disagreement about method can therefore include **disagreement over the admissible object of controversy**.

Do not infer that Skinner thereby denied every methodological affinity with Dunn or Pocock; other evidence directly establishes acknowledged overlap.

`RESPONSE SCOPE NARROWING`
!= `HISTORICAL INDEPENDENCE`.

## III. Skinner's opening also distinguishes historical reconstruction from general methodological defense

The two possible response strategies are not merely two topics.

They impose different proof burdens.

### Hobbes historical case

Would require evidence about:

- intellectual context;
- social pressures;
- historical audience;
- the character of Hobbesian absolutism;
- particular historical claims.

### General methodological approach

Requires defense/clarification of:

- what counts as understanding a text;
- necessary versus sufficient conditions;
- historical meaning;
- intentions/actions;
- relation of internal argument analysis to historical context.

Therefore:

`ONE CARRIER RESPONSE`
can explicitly choose
`METHOD CLAIM`
over
`CASE RECONSTRUCTION`.

Carrier architecture does not dictate the analytic unit automatically; the author selects it.

---

## IV. Skinner explicitly states an object-history in the body

In the directly exposed body, Skinner answers Tarlton's claim that his objects were vaguely specified by saying that his concern had in fact been with the familiar `select few books` elevated to classic status.

He says his original aim was to analyse conditions necessary, and perhaps sufficient, for understanding any one of these texts.

This establishes a further layer:

`CLASSIC TEXT AS OBJECT`
-> `CONDITIONS OF HISTORICAL UNDERSTANDING`
-> `METHOD PROGRAMME`.

The method does not erase the classic-text unit in this 1974 self-description; it changes what must be established about that unit.

This is useful against a simple story in which contextualism merely replaces `TEXT` with `CONTEXT`.

Rather:

`TEXT REMAINS AN OBJECT`
+
`RELATIONAL PROOF BURDEN AROUND THE TEXT EXPANDS`.

That distinction can later be compared with global/contextual approaches where the historical unit itself changes rather than merely its proof requirements.

## V. Parekh–Berki 1976 make the inverse unit defense

Direct full communication:

R. N. Berki and Bhikhu Parekh, `On Quentin Skinner's “Some Problems in the Analysis of Political Thought and Action”`, *Political Theory* 4.2 (May 1976), p.235.

They distinguish:

### Historical/biographical question

`How did Skinner's thought develop?`

or

`What is Skinner's current position?`

Unit required:

`ACTOR ACROSS AN OEUVRE / TIME SERIES`.

One 1969 article would be inadequate.

### Philosophical/methodological question

`Is this specific approach to history of ideas adequate?`

Unit accepted:

`A REPRESENTATIVE METHOD POSITION EMBODIED IN ONE ARTICLE`.

They say Skinner's 1969 article articulated the approach well enough to be used in this way.

Thus:

`ARTICLE REPRESENTATIVE OF METHOD CLAIM`
!= `ARTICLE EXHAUSTIVE OF ACTOR`.

This is not only their defense against a fairness objection. It is an explicit theory of unit adequacy.

## VI. The exchange is symmetrical at a deeper level

Skinner 1974 and Parekh–Berki 1976 are often read simply as substantive opponents.

At the level of research design, however, both explicitly perform the same operation:

1. enumerate possible analytic units;
2. specify the question being asked;
3. choose one unit as adequate for that question;
4. reject leakage from a different unit/proof burden.

### Skinner

`HOBBES CASE`
vs
`GENERAL METHOD`;
chooses general method for the response.

`TARLTON'S DUNN+POCOCK+SKINNER UNIT`
vs
`SKINNER'S OWN WORK`;
chooses own work for reply scope.

### Parekh–Berki

`SKINNER AS DEVELOPING HISTORICAL INDIVIDUAL`
vs
`1969 ARTICLE AS REPRESENTATIVE METHOD POSITION`;
chooses the article because the question is philosophical/methodological.

Therefore the deepest shared rule is:

`UNIT ADEQUACY = FUNCTION OF QUESTION + CLAIM + PROOF BURDEN`.

This is stronger than saying `different methods use different units`.

The same actors can require different units depending on the proposition under test.

## IX. Relation to Lovejoy/global-history architecture

Backstage, this directly helps differentiate two kinds of methodological transformation:

1. **same unit, harder/different proof burden**;
2. **change of historical unit itself**.

Skinner 1974 self-description often belongs to the first pattern:

`CLASSIC TEXT REMAINS OBJECT`
+
`HISTORICAL-MEANING / ACTION / CONTEXT PROOF BURDEN EXPANDS`.

Haddock's 1979 Homer case can belong to the second:

`INDIVIDUAL HOMER UNIT`
-> `POETIC NATIONS / LAYERED TRADITION / COLLECTORS`.

Lovejoy's notebooks contain both possibilities at smaller scale: a term may remain the unit while relation type changes, or a classification/reconstruction may alter what counts as the relevant historical object.

Do not convert this structural comparison into genealogy.
