# Batch 52 — 004 → 005: operator migration, not method origin

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: source-controlled synthesis

## Core correction

The current 005 paper architecture needs one causal correction. It is too strong to say that Lovejoy simply **learned decomposition in Paris**. Notebook 004 and the associated 1898 Buddhist article already document, before the Marillier year, a mature evidentiary operation: a formal or genealogical resemblance cannot by itself establish semantic identity, present function, or systematic relation.

The stronger historical claim is therefore:

> **Paris did not create Lovejoy's practice of decomposition ex nihilo. It moved an already visible philological operator into a new evidentiary ecology — from terms and textual configurations to ethnographic reports, rites, social relations, and causal mechanisms — and thereby widened its scale and objects.**

This changes the role assigned to Marillier. Marillier is not best treated as the origin of an analytic disposition. His seminar becomes the site where an existing discipline of evidentiary separation was **retooled, stressed, and generalized through comparative-religion research practice**.

## 1. Chronological control: the operator is demonstrably pre-Paris

Notebook 004 is materially Harvard (`Harvard Cooperative Society`) and feeds directly into Lovejoy's article `The Buddhistic technical terms upadana and upadisesa`, printed under his Harvard affiliation. The article's first pages already do something methodologically specific: they refuse to let the occurrence of `upādāna` inside `upādānakkhandha` establish that the two are semantically identical, and they test meaning by canonical usage before allowing lexical recurrence to support a historical claim.

The 004 paper-thesis file has already formulated the rule accurately:

> **formal recurrence cannot carry genealogical weight until semantic/functional identity has been independently established.**

This is not a vague preference for careful reading. It is a division of evidentiary burden between:

- provenance / filiation;
- lexical or formal resemblance;
- semantic identity;
- causal or configurational role.

The newly local Senart–Jacobi–Oldenberg sources make the controversy even clearer. Senart himself already practices term filiation and stratification; Oldenberg supplies `Denk-atmosphäre` and `Familienähnlichkeit` as controls against over-strong derivation; Lovejoy's increment is therefore not decomposition itself but the restriction placed on what one type of evidence may prove about another relation.

## 2. 004's characteristic move: lexical recurrence does not entail functional identity

The best 004 hinge remains the nidāna problem.

Lovejoy can grant all of the following at once:

- the formula is historically composite;
- some terms are pre-Buddhist or drawn from existing intellectual materials;
- objective and subjective categories are mixed;
- the sequence is not homogeneous in strict temporal order.

Yet he denies that these facts make the formula unintelligible or accidental. The notebook says, with some verbal uncertainty, that a **discoverable technical system** gives the arrangement.

Thus two propositions that Senart's argument tends to draw closely together are separated:

`historical compositeness` ≠ `absence of present organization`.

The same separation appears at the lexical level. Competing etymologies for `upādi/upadhi` do not settle contextual meaning; Lovejoy turns to usage. In the published article, derivational relation and semantic function are treated as distinct evidentiary questions.

The 004 operator can therefore be compressed as:

> **same word / same root / same formal position does not yet mean same semantic or causal job.**

## 3. 005 performs an isomorphic operation on rites rather than words

In 005 the empirical objects change, but the logical operation remains strikingly similar.

The notebook repeatedly confronts outwardly similar acts:

- killing;
- eating human flesh;
- shedding or applying blood;
- burying body parts in foundations;
- offering food to the dead;
- circumcision and initiation wounds;
- communal or ceremonial performance.

It then refuses to let the visible form determine the classification. A killing can be punishment, provision for the dead, propitiation, magical action, fecundative sacrifice, or sacramental sacrifice. Eating flesh can answer famine, revenge, affection, acquisition of qualities, sympathetic efficacy, propitiation, or communion. Blood can mark kinship, incorporation, protection, life-transfer, medicine/magic, or sacrifice.

The 005 operator is therefore:

> **same outward rite / same material act does not yet mean same causal, social, or religious job.**

This is the ethnographic analogue of the 004 semantic rule.

The parallel is not merely verbal:

| 004 | 005 |
|---|---|
| lexical/formal recurrence | ritual/morphological recurrence |
| etymology or filiation | ethnographic label or outward form |
| contextual semantic function | causal/social mechanism |
| composite textual formula | syncretic ritual complex |
| usage tests identity | negative cases / motive distinctions test identity |
| provenance does not exhaust configuration | historical origin does not exhaust present ritual function |

This is the clearest current evidence for **operator migration across object domains**.

## 4. The second shared rule: compositeness is compatible with organization

The 004 and 005 notebooks also share a second, deeper inference rule.

In 004, inherited or borrowed terms can be brought together into a later configuration that remains technically intelligible. Historical layering does not make a system mere debris.

In 005 p.65, Lovejoy explicitly says that several originally distinct sacrificial motives may later be mingled through **syncretism**, and that delicate analysis is required to associate the different ritual elements. Later inserted sheets likewise warn that a present ritual complex may contain elements with different histories.

Thus the same two-stage analysis appears in both notebooks:

1. **decompose historical provenance**;
2. **reconstruct the later configuration/function without assuming that provenance alone determines it.**

This is stronger than saying that Lovejoy simply liked plural explanations. The notebooks distinguish two temporal questions:

- how did the components arrive here?
- what explanatory work do they perform together now?

## 5. What Paris changes: object, scale, and evidentiary ecology

If the operator already exists in Harvard work, what does the Marillier year contribute?

Three changes are documentable and historically consequential.

### A. Change of object

004 works primarily with printed texts, technical vocabulary, canonical passages, etymology, and system configuration.

005 works with ethnographic testimony, descriptions of ritual acts, missionary reports, regional comparisons, social institutions, and material practices.

The evidentiary unit shifts from **term-in-text** toward **reported practice-in-context**.

### B. Change of scale

004 asks whether a limited set of Buddhist categories can bear specific semantic and genealogical claims.

005 compares many societies and many superficially similar practices. The same evidentiary discipline now has to survive large-scale comparison: a repeated form cannot become a universal type merely because it recurs geographically.

Negative cases therefore acquire greater methodological force. A ceremonial killing that is not sacrifice, or an ingestion that is not communion, strips the visible feature of diagnostic authority.

### C. Change of research ecology

004 is reconstructable mainly as a source-transformation dossier around published Indological arguments.

005 sits inside a documented EPHE research workshop: Marillier's active auditors, thematic seminar problems, bibliography/course-note circulation to other students, source packets, student redivision, and live controversy. The operation is no longer only exercised against published philology; it is embedded in a **collective documentary workflow**.

This is the strongest way to preserve the importance of Paris without making an origin claim.

## 6. Recasting Marillier: not inventor, but domain-shifter and amplifier

The causal language should therefore change from:

> `Marillier taught Lovejoy to decompose similar rites by mechanism.`

To something closer to:

> **Lovejoy arrived in Paris with a demonstrable habit of separating provenance from present function in philological problems. Marillier's comparative-religion workshop placed that habit in a new documentary environment, where outwardly similar practices had to be decomposed by testimony, motive, social relation, causal mechanism, and historical layer.**

Or, even more compactly:

> **Harvard supplied a discipline of evidentiary separation; Paris expanded its empirical jurisdiction.**

This is not a national replacement story. Batch 50 already established the transatlantic asymmetry: the American setting supplied distributed philological and Orientalist infrastructures, while Paris concentrated comparative-religion work institutionally. The new point is methodological: the two infrastructures exercised related operations on different kinds of evidence.

## 7. Consequence for the Mauss comparison

This correction also improves the Mauss–Lovejoy argument.

If Marillier is treated as the single source of Lovejoy's method, the absence of direct Mauss–Lovejoy transmission becomes an awkward gap. If instead Marillier is treated as a **shared problem-and-document environment**, the evidence fits much better.

Mauss documents Marillier's circulation of bibliography and course notes and later summarizes Marillier's teaching as `la critique des faits`. Mauss and Lovejoy can therefore share:

- source criticism;
- resistance to over-broad rubrics;
- reclassification of apparently similar practices;
- skepticism toward single-origin arguments;

without requiring either that Mauss taught Lovejoy or that Marillier originated every analytic move visible in Lovejoy.

The historically interesting question becomes **how different analysts transform a shared research grammar**. The endocannibalism case is exemplary: Mauss can classify transfer-through-ingestion as communion; Lovejoy can place analogous transfer under sympathetic/transferable efficacy. Shared environment produces a comparable problem, not an identical answer.

## 8. A three-stage developmental model that is now safer than an influence narrative

The current evidence supports a three-stage model:

### Stage 1 — Harvard / 004: semantic-configurational separation

`term/form → provenance/filiation → independent test of semantic function → present configuration`

Key restriction: formal recurrence or etymological relation cannot decide semantic identity.

### Stage 2 — Paris / 005: ethnographic-mechanistic separation

`reported form → source provenance/contamination → motive/social relation/causal bearer → historical layering/syncretism`

Key restriction: outward ritual form cannot decide religious mechanism or origin.

### Stage 3 — 1906: cross-domain causal abstraction

The repeated distinctions among personal agency, indwelling spirit, intrinsic efficacy, transferable power, contact, and quasi-mechanical action are abstracted into a more general account of `primitive energetics`.

This final step should still be described as a strong developmental inference rather than a directly documented self-report. But it no longer requires the teleological claim that the 1898–99 seminar supplied a ready-made theory.

## 9. Strong historiographical formulation

The paper can now make a more precise intervention:

> **The importance of Lovejoy's Paris year lies less in the acquisition of a method than in the migration of an inference rule across domains. In his Harvard Buddhist work, lexical recurrence, etymological filiation, and historical borrowing were insufficient to determine semantic or configurational identity. In Marillier's comparative-religion environment, the same discipline was applied to ritual morphology: killing, blood, eating, ceremoniality, and sacrifice could not be classified until their source context, motive, causal bearer, social relation, and historical layer had been separated. The result was not the transmission of a doctrine but the expansion of an evidentiary operator from philology into comparative ethnology.**

This is stronger than `Paris influenced Lovejoy`, but safer than `Paris taught Lovejoy decomposition`.

## 10. Drafting consequences for the 005 paper

Revise the paper architecture in four places:

1. Replace `learning to decompose` with **`extending/retooling an already visible practice of evidentiary decomposition`**.
2. Add a short Harvard/004 control before the Marillier seminar section; no full 004 detour is needed.
3. Make the transition from 004 to 005 explicit as **term-in-text → rite-in-report**.
4. Treat 1906 as an abstraction of a cross-domain causal operator rather than the simple maturation of a Paris-taught doctrine.

The 005 article remains viable as a one-notebook documentary microhistory. 004 functions as a small chronological control that prevents the causal story from becoming too strong; it does not need to become a second primary object.

## Evidence status

### Secure

- 004 is a Harvard working object and its central philological problems pass into Lovejoy's Harvard-affiliated Buddhist article.
- 004 separates composite provenance from present systematic intelligibility.
- Lovejoy's published Buddhist argument separates etymological/filiational evidence from contextual semantic evidence.
- 005 repeatedly separates outward ritual form from motive/mechanism and explicitly treats syncretism as the later combination of historically distinct elements.
- Lovejoy's active participation in Marillier's EPHE work supplies a documented institutional bridge between the two research phases.

### Strong interpretation

- the 004 and 005 procedures are best understood as the same evidentiary operator migrating from philology into comparative-religion ethnography;
- Paris amplified and generalized rather than originated the operation.

### Hold

- do not claim Lovejoy consciously described this as one transferable method;
- do not claim Marillier explicitly noticed or deliberately cultivated Lovejoy's pre-existing philological operator;
- do not infer direct Mauss→Lovejoy transmission from the methodological parallel;
- do not treat the 1906 abstraction as a mechanically predetermined endpoint.
