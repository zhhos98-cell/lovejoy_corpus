## Core result

A safe sequence is visible without making any argument from the absence of Buddhism in those texts:

`1948 PERSON / PHILOSOPHER / HISTORIOGRAPHER / TEACHER / ESSAYS`
-> `1973 EPISTEMOLOGY / UNIT-IDEA / HISTORY-OF-IDEAS METHOD`
-> `1987 GREAT CHAIN / HIERARCHY / HISTORY-OF-IDEAS LEGACY`.

This does not show that JHI `forgot Buddhism`.

It shows something historically cleaner:

> **At different memorial dates JHI organized Lovejoy through different explicit analytical and biographical units.**

## I. 1948 — `Arthur O. Lovejoy at Seventy-Five: Reason at Work`

JHI 9.4 (Oct. 1948) is explicitly titled:

`Arthur O. Lovejoy at Seventy-Five: Reason at Work`.

Direct JSTOR issue:

https://www.jstor.org/stable/i327270

The dedicated opening cluster is:

- p.403, `Arthur O. Lovejoy at Seventy-Five: Reason at Work`;
- George Boas, `A. O. Lovejoy as Historian of Philosophy`, 404–411;
- Maurice Mandelbaum, `Arthur O. Lovejoy and the Theory of Historiography`, 412–423;
- W. P. Montague, `My Friend Lovejoy`, 424–427;
- Marjorie Nicolson, `A. O. Lovejoy as Teacher`, 428–438;
- Theodore Spencer, review of *Lovejoy's Essays in the History of Ideas*, 439–446.

The issue architecture itself therefore constructs a plural commemorative actor:

`HISTORIAN OF PHILOSOPHY`
+
`THEORIST OF HISTORIOGRAPHY`
+
`FRIEND / PERSON`
+
`TEACHER`
+
`AUTHOR OF ESSAYS IN HISTORY OF IDEAS`.

No claim is made about what each article does or does not mention beyond its controlled title/object.

### Mandelbaum unit

Daniel J. Wilson's later annotated bibliography summarizes Mandelbaum 1948 as discussing Lovejoy's historiographical method and the basic theoretical views characterizing his approach to past/knowledge; it highlights causal relationships and Lovejoy's pluralism about the past.

Current internal Library control: Wilson annotated bibliography, entry 377.

This provides a contemporary method-memory unit alongside the issue's other roles.

## II. 1973 — Wiener centenary: method / epistemology becomes the explicit commemorative center

Philip P. Wiener, `Towards Commemorating the Centenary of Arthur O. Lovejoy's Birthday (October 10, 1873)`, JHI 34.4 (1973), 591–598.

Direct JSTOR issue/metadata:

https://www.jstor.org/stable/i346237

Daniel J. Wilson's annotated bibliography, entry 433, characterizes Wiener's piece as commemorating Lovejoy's hundredth birthday and briefly discussing/defending:

- Lovejoy's **epistemological emphasis in philosophy**;
- his **conception of the unit-idea**;
- his **methods in the history of ideas**.

Internal Library control:

`Arthur O. Lovejoy: An Annotated Bibliography`, Wilson, entry 433.

Thus the 1973 centenary carrier can safely be coded:

`LOVEJOY -> EPISTEMOLOGY + UNIT-IDEA + HISTORY-OF-IDEAS METHOD`.

This is a later secondary annotation of the 1973 article rather than direct full-text control; if publication-facing detail beyond the general unit is needed, direct article text should be acquired.
