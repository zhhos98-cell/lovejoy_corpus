# Lovejoy — genealogy vector: out-of-sample controls from 1902 and 1904

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **CANONICAL CORRECTION TO ONE-DIMENSIONAL GENEALOGY LADDER**

## Why this note exists

The 1933 comparison between `Monboddo and Rousseau` and `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism` initially suggested a one-dimensional evidence ladder from resemblance to serial transmission (`G0–G5`). Earlier primary cases show that this is too simple.

Lovejoy can accept that a **direct transmission edge exists** while simultaneously denying that the edge bears much explanatory/causal weight because the relevant material was also available through a broader tradition. Conversely, he can reject a proposed historical transmission even when conceptual affinity is strong because internal dialectic or independent availability is sufficient.

Therefore genealogy must be represented by several independently varying questions.

Current analyst model:

> **genealogy vector = edge existence × route alternatives/exclusivity × causal dependence/weight × propagation × confidence**

This is analyst notation, not Lovejoy's actor vocabulary.

---

## I. 1902 — `Religion and the Time-Process`: affinity without transmission

Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Religion and the Time-Process`, *American Journal of Theology* 6.3 (1902), 439–472.

Lovejoy considers an alleged Eastern influence upon Aristotle and refuses the genealogical inference where the internal dialectic of the Platonic tradition is sufficient to explain the relevant development.

The relation architecture is:

`conceptual affinity / resemblance`
+
`possible cultural source`
≠
`historical transmission`.

A local/intellectual mechanism internal to the available tradition can defeat the need for an external borrowing hypothesis.

### Vector coding

- content/affinity: present;
- direct edge: unproved;
- alternative route: strong (internal dialectic/tradition);
- causal dependence on proposed Eastern source: unsupported;
- confidence in transmission: low/rejected.

This is an early failed-influence case well before the 1933 Monboddo control.

## II. 1904 — `The Dialectic of Bruno and Spinoza`: direct edge without exclusive causal dependence

Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Dialectic of Bruno and Spinoza` (1904).

This is the strongest correction to a linear influence ladder.

Lovejoy accepts that Spinoza read Bruno and therefore that similarities may **in part** be attributed to direct influence. But he immediately limits the explanatory weight of that route. Both Bruno and Spinoza had access to methods, terms, and fundamental concepts circulating in the continuous Neo-Platonic tradition. Even without Bruno as intermediary, Spinoza could have obtained substantial parts of the relevant conceptual material.

Thus:

`direct transmission edge exists`
≠
`source is exclusive`
≠
`recipient is causally dependent on that intermediary`
≠
`all similarity should be assigned to direct influence`.

### Vector coding

- content similarity/affinity: present;
- direct edge: **yes** (reading/contact relation accepted);
- alternative routes: **yes**, strong tradition-level reservoir;
- source exclusivity: low;
- causal dependence/weight of Bruno edge: limited / partial;
- broader historical availability: high;
- confidence that *some* direct influence occurred: positive;
- confidence that Bruno explains the whole conceptual resemblance: negative.

This is an analytically crucial case because it separates **edge existence from edge importance**.

## III. Revised genealogy model

The earlier G0–G5 ladder remains useful as a sequence of evidentiary checks, but it must not be treated as one scalar measuring `strength of influence`.

At least five dimensions now need independent coding.

### E — edge existence

Did A actually reach B through a particular carrier/intermediary?

Values might be:

- no evidence;
- possible;
- probable;
- documented/accepted.

### X — route exclusivity / alternatives

Could B obtain the relevant content through:

- common sources;
- broad tradition-level availability;
- independent recurrence;
- shared scientific/empirical evidence;
- another intermediary?

A documented edge can coexist with low exclusivity.

### D — causal dependence / weight

How much explanatory work should the edge perform?

Counterfactual question:

> If the specific edge were absent, would the recipient probably still have had access to the relevant idea/method?

Lovejoy does not formulate this as modern counterfactual causation, but his 1904 Bruno case effectively asks this kind of historical question.

### P — propagation / downstream operation

Does the item or representation travel further through:

- reuse;
- transformation;
- citation;
- teaching;
- translation;
- public controversy;
- institutional practice?

This dimension is especially visible in the China-garden case.

### C — confidence/calibration

What does Lovejoy actually claim?

- resemblance only;
- possible source;
- probable influence;
- influence in part;
- direct influence;
- origin;
- uncertainty/cannot determine.

Confidence should be coded independently from the analyst's retrospective evaluation.

## IV. Four benchmark cases under the vector model

| Case | Edge (E) | Alternatives/exclusivity (X) | Dependence/weight (D) | Propagation (P) | Lovejoy conclusion (C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1902 alleged Eastern influence → Aristotle | unproved | strong internal Platonic route | proposed external edge unnecessary | not established | transmission rejected/gratuitous |
| 1904 Bruno → Spinoza | accepted | strong Neo-Platonic reservoir | partial/limited | not main object | some direct influence possible/accepted, not full explanation |
| 1933 Rousseau → Monboddo | possible; reading known | strong classics + shared evidence + independent recurrence | indeterminate | no unique serial chain | cannot determine with certainty |
| 1933/48 `China` → English aesthetic reform | multiple documentary carrier edges | alternatives exist, but Chinese actor-attribution/reuse directly documented | substantial at reception/argument level; node weights revisable | strong serial uptake | positive influence/origin thesis at representation-history level |

The matrix validates the core anti-leakage principle across decades while showing why one-dimensional `influence strength` language is inadequate.

## V. New relation distinction: carrier edge vs conceptual reservoir

The 1904 case suggests a particularly useful distinction.

### Carrier edge

A historically specific path:

`Bruno text → Spinoza reading`.

### Conceptual reservoir

A broader historically available repertoire:

`continuous Neo-Platonic tradition → multiple possible carriers`.

The existence of the first does not erase the second.

This matters for mature history-of-ideas narratives because an idea can recur through:

- identifiable dyadic transmission;
- diffuse disciplinary/traditional availability;
- multiple redundant carriers;
- independent rediscovery/recombination.

A history of an idea should therefore distinguish **network reachability** from **specific-edge dependence**.

Again, `network reachability` is analyst vocabulary, not Lovejoy's.

## VI. Connection to the China revision

The 1933→1948 revision of `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism` can now be described more precisely.

New evidence does not merely add an earlier node. It changes the genealogy vector:

- Montaigne edge: still historically real as reception/reuse;
- exclusivity/priority: reduced;
- causal weight as originator: reduced;
- earlier missionary/voyager carrier network: increased weight;
- propagation model: becomes more distributed.

Thus Lovejoy's revision converts a **great-author-centered edge model** into a more distributed **carrier-reservoir model** when earlier evidence becomes available.

This is an unusually strong case of evidence-responsive causal reallocation.

## VII. Relation to the broader `scale → relation → warrant` architecture

Genealogy now slots cleanly into the governing model.

### Scale

What causal object is under study?

- word;
- proposition;
- idea;
- motif;
- whole complex;
- representation;
- movement;
- carrier network.

### Relation

What is claimed?

- resemblance;
- availability;
- reading/contact;
- borrowing;
- partial influence;
- direct influence;
- common source;
- recurrence;
- diffusion/propagation.

### Warrant

What supports the claim?

- chronology;
- documentary access;
- citation;
- distinctive textual reuse;
- actor acknowledgment;
- carrier trace;
- elimination/retention of alternatives;
- serial downstream evidence.

The key rule remains:

> **A relation established at one scale does not automatically upgrade to a stronger genealogical relation.**

## VIII. Consequence for the 1898 → mature Lovejoy problem

Notebook 004 already operates in a field where:

- diagnostic resemblance may support historical adoption only under stronger controls;
- one doctrinal feature does not prove a whole mature system existed;
- borrowing/provenance does not settle present function;
- field-level availability complicates direct-source attribution.

The 1902 and 1904 cases demonstrate that closely related causal problems appear explicitly in Lovejoy's own historical-philosophical writing only a few years later.

This shortens the documentary gap between the Buddhist notebook and mature genealogy practice without proving that one technique simply migrated unchanged.

Strong safe formulation:

> **By 1902–04 Lovejoy was already treating conceptual resemblance, direct textual contact, tradition-level availability, and causal dependence as separately warrantable historical relations.**
