Date: 2026-08-26  
Status: synced  
Scope: notebooks `MS38_004_001_061_004` and `MS38_004_001_061_005`, with 1898 and 1906 publications as endpoint controls.  
Chronological rule: ordinary forward tracing stops at **1910**; later material enters only for explicit Buddhism / India / Sanskrit nodes.

## Core result

Batch 211 proposed that the political and epistemic consequences of comparison depend not only on **what is compared** but on **where the resulting abstraction is made to terminate**. Batch 212 tests that proposition directly inside notebooks 004 and 005.

A four-part endpoint distinction is useful:

1. **ACTOR-OWNED** — a meaning/category is explicitly treated as belonging to the historical actors or native speakers;
2. **SYSTEM-OWNED** — a relation is reconstructed as a functional property of a specific doctrinal/ritual configuration, without claiming that actors explicitly formulated the analytic category;
3. **ANALYST-OWNED** — Lovejoy supplies a comparative rubric, historical division, or causal classification used to sort heterogeneous evidence;
4. **OPEN/HOLD** — the notebook deliberately withholds final assignment or records competing possibilities.

The main result is asymmetrical.

### Notebook 004

Lovejoy’s strongest successful interpretations usually terminate at the **specific textual or doctrinal system**. He reconstructs what `upādāna`, `upādisesa`, `viññāṇa`, or the nidāna formula does inside Buddhism while leaving etymology, textual history or genealogy partially open. Analyst-owned historical rubrics exist (`original / derived elements`), but they are not normally reattributed to a generalized `Indian mind`.

### Notebook 005

Lovejoy uses substantially more **analyst-owned comparative rubrics**: naturalistic / ritual / social / moral determinants, propitiatory / alimentary / sacramental mechanisms, fetish versus charm, intrinsic versus spirit-mediated efficacy, etc. Yet the notebook repeatedly keeps those rubrics **case-relative**, subjects them to negative examples, and often leaves genealogy or classification open.

### 1906

A real endpoint shift occurs when one analyst-owned comparative category — impersonal / transferable / quasi-mechanical efficacy — is promoted into a fundamental category and reattributed at collective scale to `primitive philosophy` / `savage thought` / `most savages`.

Therefore:

> **1906 is not merely a larger version of 005. It changes the endpoint of abstraction: a provisional comparative rubric becomes a generalized actor-level philosophy.**

This makes the scale jump more precise than `notebook → theory`.

## 1. Endpoint codes

### AO — ACTOR-OWNED

Use when Lovejoy treats the category or distinction as one explicitly possessed by the actors or recoverable from native/textual usage.

Examples:

- a Pāli term’s meaning established from Buddhist textual use;
- a native power term whose actual semantic range is argued from indigenous lexical applications.

### SO — SYSTEM-OWNED

Use when Lovejoy reconstructs a relation as objectively doing work in a specific system even if the historical actors do not themselves formulate the analyst’s vocabulary.

Examples:

- `upādāna` as chronic volitional attachment performing a causal function in rebirth;
- `viññāṇa` temporally antecedent yet logically included in `nāmarūpa`;
- a rite working by propitiation rather than communion in one local case.

### AN — ANALYST-OWNED

Use when Lovejoy supplies a comparative or historical organizing category.

Examples:

- `Original Elements / Derived Elements`;
- naturalistic / ritual / social / strictly moral determinant sequence;
- sacrificial mechanism classes;
- `Primitive Energetics` at the moment it is first coined as a comparative label.

### H — HOLD / unresolved

Use when the notebook preserves alternatives, questions the diagnostic value of evidence, or explicitly says the matter cannot yet be settled.

The codes describe **where the analytical result terminates**, not authorship of every word on the page.

## 2. Notebook 004: interpretation tends to terminate at a specific system

### PDF p.17 — Sāṃkhya comparison

Lovejoy receives Rhys Davids’s historical problem and asks what would count as a genuinely discriminating Sāṃkhyan feature. Generic eternity is insufficient; thorough-going dualistic separateness is more diagnostic.

Endpoint:

- the diagnostic criterion itself = **AN**;
- any resulting claim that a passage is genuinely Sāṃkhyan = **SO / historical-system assignment**;
- whole-system priority remains partly **H**.

The abstraction is not attributed to a generalized Indian cognitive type.

### PDF p.18 — Buddhist `agnosticism`

A refusal to answer can look like philosophical agnosticism. Lovejoy blocks that label and reconstructs the refusal as an objection to the form/presuppositions of the questions within a salvation doctrine based on insight.

Endpoint: **SO**.

The point is not `Buddhists have the category question-form critique` as an explicit native theory. The point is that the Buddhist system makes the apparent silence intelligible in a particular way.

### PDF p.22 and 1898 — `upādāna`

Warren’s `identity` between desire and attachment is rejected through textual comparison. Lovejoy reconstructs a differentiated role for attachment and later stabilizes it as a chronic condition of will related to rebirth.

Endpoint: primarily **SO**, supported by AO-level textual usage.

### PDF p.42 — `viññāṇa / nāmarūpa`

`Viññāṇa is temporally (?) antecedent to nāmarūpa and logically a subdivision of it.`

Endpoint: **SO**.

This is Lovejoy’s analytic formulation of relations inside the Buddhist configuration. It is not presented as a universal Indian mental category.

### PDF pp.49–52 — composite nidāna formula

Historical heterogeneity is granted while present technical intelligibility is reconstructed.

Endpoint: **SO + H**.

- present configuration/systemic relation = SO;
- exact historical derivation of every component = partly H.

### PDF pp.63–66 / 1898 — `upādisesa`

Usage supports an ethical/systemic reading while etymology and contradictory textual witnesses remain unresolved.

Endpoint:

- semantic/function claim = **AO/SO**;
- etymological/text-historical totalization = **H**.

This is perhaps the clearest example of a successful local interpretation coexisting with unresolved higher-level history.

### PDF p.68 — `Original / Derived Elements`

Lovejoy classifies flux / Three Characteristics and dependent origination as original, psychology / perception / khandhas as derived.

Endpoint: **AN**.

This is an analyst-owned historical stratification of the Buddhist system. Importantly, it is not then elevated into a generalized philosophy of `the Indian mind`.

## 3. 004 endpoint pattern

A compact formula is:

> **textual usage → relation reconstruction → specific Buddhist/Sāṃkhya system → historical surplus left open.**

The analyst supplies discriminations, but successful interpretation generally returns to a **bounded system**.

This helps explain the 1907 Vedānta review. Lovejoy can compare Rāmānuja, Śaṅkara, Green and other idealists without turning the comparison into one collective Indian or Eastern cognitive type. The endpoint remains system/problem-relative.

## 4. Notebook 005: analyst-owned rubrics become much more prominent

The evidentiary object changes radically. Lovejoy is no longer primarily interpreting one textual/doctrinal configuration. He is handling large heterogeneous sets of ethnographic reports.

That requires a comparative coding layer.

### PDF pp.3–20 — survivance determinants

The notebook sorts apparent future-life rewards/punishments by:

- terrestrial/status continuation;
- ritual/manual qualification;
- social/clan relation;
- manner of death;
- transitional / `half-moral` cases;
- strictly moral desert.

After Batches 203–207, the components and much of the transition grammar are known to be workshop-shared rather than Lovejoy inventions.

Endpoint nevertheless remains largely **AN**: these are comparative divisions used to code cases.

The local actor/system mechanism behind each case can then be SO, while the general determinant table is an analyst rubric.

### Suicide cluster

Lovejoy tests moral guilt, clan solidarity, taboo, social injury, death-type and imported moralization.

Endpoint: frequently **H → provisional SO**, not AO.

The point is precisely that the observer’s or analyst’s first moral label may fail.

### PDF pp.24–28 — historical layers

Older journey/trial machinery, later moral use, imported influence and surviving naturalistic elements are arranged into historical morphology.

Endpoint: primarily **AN historical reconstruction**, constrained by local case evidence.

### Sacrifice classification

Funerary, provisionary, propitiatory, alimentary, expiatory, magical, fecundative, social/incorporative and sacramental mechanisms are separated.

Endpoint:

- comparative taxonomy = **AN**;
- mechanism in a particular rite = **SO**;
- uncertain origin or mixed cases = **H**.

The notebook does not ordinarily say that the taxonomy itself belongs to `the savage mind`.

## 5. 005’s most characteristic move is AN → SO or H, not AN → collective AO

This is the main new finding.

Lovejoy begins with an analyst-owned comparison:

> `could this be moral punishment / communion / sacrifice / fetish / magic?`

Then he tests local evidence.

The result is usually one of two things:

### AN → SO

A specific mechanism is assigned to the case:

- clan/social consequence;
- manner-of-death selection;
- propitiation;
- direct transferable efficacy;
- provision for the dead;
- incorporation.

### AN → H

The evidence remains insufficient for:

- moral desert;
- sacramental communion;
- one historical origin;
- substitution theory;
- a universal developmental sequence.

What is comparatively rare in the notebook is:

### AN → generalized collective AO

> `this analyst category is the fundamental concept possessed by primitive peoples as a whole.`

That move becomes much more explicit in 1906.

## 6. Fetish / charm is a clean endpoint example

In 005 the visible object may be magical/sacred, but Lovejoy distinguishes:

- fetish: efficacy through an inhabiting/present spirit;
- charm/amulet: efficacy intrinsic to the object or otherwise not requiring an indwelling spirit.

The pair is an **AN** distinction used to recover the operative local relation, which becomes **SO** in an individual case.

The notebook does not yet need to claim that all `primitive thought` is fundamentally organized by one of these two categories.

In 1906, however, the intrinsic/impersonal side is abstracted and ranked above personality as a fundamental category of primitive philosophy.

The same distinction therefore changes endpoint:

> **005: comparative tool → local mechanism**  
> **1906: comparative tool → universalized collective philosophy**.

## 7. p.103 chronology veto shows the importance of HOLD

The proposed agricultural/sacrificial developmental sequence is rejected as circular or chronologically unsupported when agriculture/domestication evidence fails to fit the theory.

Endpoint: **H / rejected AN genealogy**.

This is important because 005 does not merely produce ever finer classifications. It preserves failure to close.

Batch 179 already showed that blank spaces, question marks, overlapping columns and unresolved classifications are part of the working archive.

The published 1906 article is therefore selected from a notebook ecology in which many possible reattributions never reach closure.

## 8. 1906: one analyst category crosses the endpoint boundary

The 1906 causal category begins in recognizably AN form:

> Lovejoy correlates heterogeneous familiar data and proposes a category more fundamental than personality.

He then attempts to give it AO warrant through native lexical evidence:

- `wakonda`;
- `manitou`;
- `mana`;
- Bantu power vocabulary.

The evidence is designed to show that the abstraction is not merely the analyst’s convenient classification but corresponds to a conception genuinely present in the thought/language of the populations studied.

Finally the address broadens:

> `Primitive Energetics`  
> `primitive philosophy`  
> `savage thought`  
> `most savages` / many or most races.

Thus the endpoint chain is:

> **AN → lexical AO warrant → collective AO reattribution.**

This is the scalar transformation that distinguishes 1906 from most notebook classifications.

---

## 9. Why the lexical evidence matters politically

The collective reattribution is not presented as an arbitrary Western imposition. Lovejoy’s strongest methodological effort is precisely to prove actor-level warrant.

Batch 77 showed the granularity of the procedure:

- missionary custom report may be retained;
- theological translation may be rejected;
- native word application may be retained;
- abstract semantic claim requires stronger linguistic evidence;
- independent specialist studies are recruited when older sources reach their proof ceiling.

This makes the political problem more interesting, not less.

The historical operation is:

> **construct an analyst category carefully enough that it can be returned to heterogeneous actors as their own common fundamental conception.**

The issue is therefore not simply `Western category imposed without evidence`. It is the more difficult question of how evidence of local lexical/ritual relations is made to support a generalized collective philosophical subject.

## 10. Comparison with 1907 Vedānta closes the contrast

The 1907 review also uses analyst categories and cross-tradition comparisons, but its endpoint is different.

Lovejoy does **not** infer:

> Śaṅkara + Rāmānuja + other systems → one fundamental Indian philosophy.

He instead insists that the broad label `Vedānta` has concealed internal difference and calls for comparative analysis by reasons/problems.

Endpoint:

> **AN problem-space → differentiated SO systems.**

This negative control strengthens the 1906 finding. Collective actor reattribution is not an automatic consequence of Lovejoy’s comparative method. It is a particular closure used in the `primitive philosophy` argument.

## 12. Larger implication: publication does not merely simplify; it can change ownership of an abstraction

Manuscript-to-print analysis often focuses on compression:

- cases disappear;
- caveats are shortened;
- prose becomes cleaner;
- a working dossier becomes a thesis.

The present endpoint audit reveals another transformation:

> **the ownership/address of an analytic category can change.**

In 005, impersonal/direct efficacy is mainly one analyst-controlled mechanism class tested against local cases.

In 1906, the same mechanism-family is supplied with lexical actor-level warrant and returned as a fundamental category of generalized primitive thought.

Thus publication does not simply reduce evidence. It can **reassign epistemic ownership**.

This may be one of the strongest ways to historicize the relation between notebooks and published theory without treating the notebook as a literal draft.

---

## 13. Political consequence: the key question is `who owns the abstraction?`

The early corpus now suggests a useful political diagnostic.

When a comparative category remains analyst-owned, its status is visibly provisional and contestable.

When it becomes system-owned, the claim is local and bounded by the configuration being reconstructed.

When it becomes actor-owned, the historian claims to have recovered the actors’ own conceptual world.

When it becomes **collective actor-owned**, a much larger entity appears:

> `primitive philosophy`, `savage thought`, `most savages`.

The political stakes rise with the scalar jump because heterogeneous populations can be unified through a category reconstructed from selected common relations.

This gives a more exact question than `is primitive ideological?`:

> **At what point does a scholar’s useful analytical category become a claim about what a generalized class of people themselves fundamentally think?**

The 005 → 1906 transition provides direct material for answering that question historically.

## 14. Evidence discipline

- AO/SO/AN/H are project analytical codes.
- A reconstructed system-property is not identical to an explicitly actor-formulated concept; therefore AO and SO must remain separate.
- Notebook 005 is longitudinal and composite; a classification on one page should not be treated as a fully stabilized Lovejoy theory.
- 1906 is not a literal publication of 005; it draws on overlapping problem/mechanism fields and later reading.
- Lovejoy’s linguistic evidence is genuinely designed to establish actor-level warrant; critiques of whether it succeeds belong to a separate historiographical evaluation.
- The 1907 Vedānta review is a negative control for comparative closure, not proof that Lovejoy consciously maintained two epistemic regimes.

## 15. Strongest current paper formulation

> **The difference between Lovejoy’s notebooks and the 1906 `Primitive Philosophy` essay is not simply that the latter generalizes more. It changes the endpoint of the abstraction. In notebook 004, Lovejoy’s successful analytical distinctions generally terminate in a bounded Buddhist or Sāṃkhyan configuration, while genealogy can remain unresolved. Notebook 005 requires more analyst-owned comparative rubrics, but these normally lead back to local mechanisms or remain on hold when the evidence cannot sustain a stronger conclusion. In 1906 one mechanism class crosses a further boundary. Lovejoy first constructs impersonal, transferable efficacy as a comparative category, then uses indigenous lexical evidence to give it actor-level warrant, and finally reattributes it at collective scale as the fundamental concept of `primitive philosophy`. Publication therefore performs an epistemic reassignment: an analyst’s cross-case mechanism becomes a claim about what a generalized class of people fundamentally think.**

Compact project rule:

> **After comparison, ask who owns the resulting category.**

That is project vocabulary, not Lovejoy’s.
