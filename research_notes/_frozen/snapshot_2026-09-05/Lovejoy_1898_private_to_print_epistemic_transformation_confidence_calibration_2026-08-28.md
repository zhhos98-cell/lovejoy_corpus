# Lovejoy 1898 — private-to-print epistemic transformation: from notebook question marks to public proof calibration

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **CANONICAL PRIVATE/PUBLIC CONFIDENCE CONTROL / DIRECT MANUSCRIPT–PUBLICATION PAIR**

## Core result

Notebook 004 and the 1898 JAOS article provide a rare direct pair for studying how Lovejoy transforms **working uncertainty** into **published epistemic calibration**.

The publication does not simply remove uncertainty markers and present a confident finished doctrine.

Instead two transformations occur simultaneously:

1. **scale-selective pruning** — broad genealogical, chronological, system-level, and metaphysical hypotheses are more likely to be omitted, bracketed, or softened;
2. **metalinguistic calibration** — local semantic/textual/causal claims that survive into print receive more explicit verbal grading of evidentiary strength.

Working shorthand:

> **private question mark / branching → public claim selection + explicit proof-status language**.

This supplies direct 1898 evidence for the current `confidence by relation` model.

---

## I. Notebook 004: uncertainty is local, not global

The notebook contains many coexisting epistemic statuses.

The clearest compact example is the relation between `viññāṇa` and `nāmarūpa`:

`Viññāṇa is temporally (?) an antecedent of nāmarūpa, and logically a subdivision of it.`

The question mark attaches to the **temporal** relation while the **logical/classificatory** relation is stated without the same marker.

Thus even at notebook level:

`C_temporal`
≠
`C_logical/classificatory`.

The page should not be paraphrased as Lovejoy being `uncertain about the relation` in general. He is uncertain about one coordinate of a multi-relation mapping.

Other notebook forms of calibration include:

- competing etymological branches;
- unresolved bibliographic/source alternatives;
- stronger assertion of local classificatory distinctions;
- question-form chronology;
- provisional historical stratification;
- strong semantic/function judgments alongside open genealogy.

The epistemic texture is proposition-specific.

---

## II. Publication: uncertainty becomes explicit proof-language

The 1898 article presents itself as a **preliminary study** and repeatedly labels evidentiary strength.

The `upādisesa` section is the best public control.

Lovejoy states, in substance, that:

- Oldenberg's citations `come near to being conclusive` for a prevailing interpretation/use, without making them universally decisive;
- competing derivations are both `etymologically possible`;
- therefore meaning should be controlled by actual textual use rather than settled by etymology alone;
- several points are `relevant but not necessarily conclusive`;
- resistant/contrary passages remain;
- the question `cannot be regarded as finally settled`.

These phrases are not decorative modesty. They distinguish different **proof statuses** within one argument.

The publication therefore converts manuscript uncertainty into a public vocabulary of:

- near-conclusiveness;
- possibility;
- relevance without sufficiency;
- residual counterevidence;
- provisional closure.

---

## III. Claim selection and confidence grading work together

The manuscript/publication comparison shows an asymmetric survival pattern.

### More likely to disappear or soften

- broad Sāṃkhya/Buddhism chronology;
- priority of whole philosophical systems;
- large historical-stratification reconstructions;
- full original/derived-elements model;
- stronger global Nirvāṇa/metaphysical conclusions;
- broad psychological/taxonomic diagrams not required for the paper's local burden.

### More likely to survive and sharpen

- `upādāna` ≠ `upādānakkhandha`;
- skandhas as objects/conditions of attachment rather than attachment itself;
- karma and `upādāna` performing different causal jobs;
- strict temporal sequence distinguished from a broader causal architecture;
- etymology distinguished from semantic usage;
- borrowed terminology/components distinguished from original `arrangement and application`;
- provisional semantic conclusion explicitly graded against counter-passages.

Observed gradient:

> **the larger and more genealogical/metaphysical the claim, the higher the publication burden; the more locally controllable the semantic/textual/causal claim, the more confidently it can be retained.**

This is an observed publication asymmetry, not evidence that Lovejoy consciously formulated a general editorial law.

---

## IV. Private `?` does not simply become public `probably`

The transformation is more structural than a one-to-one lexical replacement.

Three routes appear.

### Route A — local uncertainty becomes verbal qualification

Notebook branching/questioning survives as published language such as:

- possible;
- relevant but not conclusive;
- nearly conclusive;
- not finally settled.

### Route B — broad uncertain proposition is deleted from the article's burden

The full system-genealogy question may simply disappear rather than be published with a modal hedge.

### Route C — exploratory complexity is compressed into a narrower high-confidence proposition

Large notebook diagrams and multi-axis mappings can yield a local published distinction whose warrant is much better controlled.

Therefore the publication operation is:

`exploratory claim space`
→ `select scale/relation`
→ `set publication burden`
→ `grade surviving evidence`
→ `assert only what passes locally`.

---

## V. This is epistemic transformation, not merely shortening

Calling the article a shorter version of the notebook misses the asymmetry.

Publication changes:

- which proposition is being asked;
- which scale is allowed to carry the argument;
- which relation receives the burden of proof;
- how uncertainty is verbalized;
- which counterevidence must remain visible.

The best description is therefore:

> **publication transforms the epistemic architecture of the research object.**

The notebook preserves a large field of candidate relations and hypotheses. The article redistributes confidence by restricting its burden to a smaller set of controllable propositions.

This helps explain why archive and publication should not be treated as interchangeable evidence for `Lovejoy's method`.

## VI. Relation to 1902

The pre-existing 004→1898→1902 control shows a further shift.

### 1898

The dominant public calibration question is:

> how strongly does witness/evidence X support local reading Y?

### 1902

The dominant control becomes more explanatory:

> if two historical genealogies can explain the affinity, which one is actually required by the evidence?

Thus explicit proof grading recedes somewhat while **explanatory competition / genealogical redundancy** becomes more prominent.

This is a transformation in epistemic style, not the disappearance of warrant control.

## VII. Relation-specific confidence now has a direct early control

The broader branch can now state securely:

- relation-specific confidence is visible in the private notebook;
- it survives publication in transformed, explicit metalinguistic form;
- publication is selective by claim scale/type;
- later Lovejoy continues to separate secure edges from uncertain causal weights and secure analytical implications from uncertain historical operation.

This strengthens the person-level recurrence of **confidence jurisdiction** without proving that one consciously articulated method persisted unchanged.

Strong safe formulation:

> **Already in 1898, Lovejoy could attach different confidence levels to temporal, logical, semantic, etymological, causal, and genealogical propositions concerning the same textual material; publication made that local grading more explicit while pruning larger claims.**

## VIII. Consequence for the JHI/archival argument

The historical value of notebook 004 is not merely that it contains `more information` than the article.

It reveals:

- rejected scales;
- suspended genealogies;
- unresolved alternatives;
- provisional relation assignments;
- the raw distribution of confidence before publication selection.

The article reveals the second operation:

- which claims Lovejoy judged publishable;
- which were narrowed;
- which were omitted;
- how evidentiary strength was publicly encoded.

Thus:

> **the notebook preserves the pre-publication confidence topology; the article records its transformation into public warrant.**

`confidence topology` is analyst vocabulary.
