## 1. Harvard advanced Pāli enrollment — CLOSED, not unresolved

The 27 Aug gate states that exact enrollment in Pāli course 4 or 5 was not demonstrated. That statement is superseded.

Direct primary evidence now closes the point:

- Lovejoy's Harvard graduate record card records `Sanskrit 5`, grade A, in 1896–97;
- the contemporaneous 1896–97 course announcement identifies Indo-Iranian course 5 as Charles Rockwell Lanman's **advanced Pāli** course, `The Sacred Books of Buddhism`, reading the *Dhamma Pada*, *Sutta Nipāta*, and *Majjhima Nikāya*.

Publication-safe state:

> **In 1896–97 Lovejoy was formally enrolled in Lanman's advanced Pāli course and received an A.**

Causal firewall:

> enrollment does not prove that Lanman assigned, supervised, or caused Lovejoy's 1897 AOS communication, notebook 004, or 1898 JAOS article.

## 2. MS-0873 France 1898–99 — actor-level problem statement added

JHU supplied the 18-page France 1898–99 packet from Daniel J. Wilson's MS-0873 transcriptions. This is an archival transcription witness, not Lovejoy autograph correspondence.

Publication-bearing results:

- 9 Oct 1898: before Paris lectures, Lovejoy prospectively names `philosophy and comparative religion` as the year's work;
- 20 Oct 1898: Lovejoy explicitly formulates a gap between philologists' textual expertise and philosophers' reconstruction of Buddhist conceptions, and identifies Sylvain Lévi as the scholar he hopes to study under for this purpose;
- 1/5 Dec 1898: Lovejoy reports that no Pāli Buddhism course is available and expects occasional private reading at Lévi's house instead.

Strongest safe publication formula:

> **By October 1898 Lovejoy could explicitly describe the gap between philological control of Buddhist texts and philosophical reconstruction.**

Chronology firewall:

- this wording does not originate the problem;
- technical Buddhist work is already directly documented by the April 1897 AOS communication and notebook/publication chain;
- JAOS 19.2 is presently fixed only to the July–December 1898 half-year, so exact article-release order relative to 20 Oct 1898 remains unresolved.
