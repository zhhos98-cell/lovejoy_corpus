## Core result

Modern historiography of the academic study of religion changes how the late-1890s Lovejoy environment should be described.

The old field history often emphasized the emergence of a new `science of religion`, comparative method, philology and institutional differentiation.

Masuzawa and Chidester show that this same process also **constructed and ranked the objects being compared** through:

- language-family classification;
- `Aryan / Semitic` schemes;
- `world religion` taxonomy;
- savagery / civilization distinctions;
- imperial / colonial source infrastructures;
- metropolitan, colonial and indigenous mediation.

Therefore:

> **DISCIPLINARY FORMATION AND OBJECT FORMATION OCCUR TOGETHER.**

This is a crucial background control for Lovejoy's Buddhist and comparative-religion work.

## I. Masuzawa 2005 — `world religions` as a modern classificatory construction

Tomoko Masuzawa, *The Invention of World Religions: Or, How European Universalism Was Preserved in the Language of Pluralism* (University of Chicago Press, 2005).

Official/public descriptions emphasize that `world religions` is not merely a descriptive multicultural list. Masuzawa reconstructs it as:

- a logic of classification;
- a form of knowledge;
- an outgrowth of comparative theology / religion;
- deeply linked to comparative philology and language classification;
- a framework in which Buddhism and Islam acquired distinctive significance through opposition between `Aryan` and `Semitic` religion/language formations;
- compatible with continuing forms of European / Christian universalism.

Official University of Chicago / Google Books controls:
https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/I/bo3534198.html
https://books.google.com/books?id=OMku6YC9VPwC

### Lovejoy calibration

Do not describe Lovejoy's 1898 Buddhist work as simply one `world religion` entering a neutral comparative arena.

The arena already sorted objects through historically specific relations among:

`LANGUAGE`
+ `TEXTUAL CANON`
+ `RACE / PEOPLE`
+ `RELIGION`
+ `CIVILIZATIONAL STATUS`.

The fact that Buddhism could become an object of exact philological scholarship and a major comparative religion is itself historically produced.

## II. Chidester 2014 — comparative religion as imperial/colonial/indigenous mediation

David Chidester, *Empire of Religion: Imperialism and Comparative Religion* (University of Chicago Press, 2014).

Official abstract:
https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226117577.001.0001

Chidester reconstructs the academic study of religion between the 1870s and 1920s by tracing how knowledge was:

- produced;
- authenticated;
- circulated

through imperial, colonial and indigenous relations.

This deliberately counters field histories that narrate comparative religion mainly as a sequence of metropolitan theories.

## III. Max Müller becomes a distributed knowledge node, not solitary founder

Chidester's `Classify and Conquer` chapter uses Max Müller as a central case.

It argues that Müller's classification of religions by language was both supported and destabilized by material from South Africa and reconstructs relations among:

- imperial theorists;
- colonial experts;
- indigenous informants;
- metropolitan comparative classification.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226117577.003.0003

This supplies a powerful general rule for the Lovejoy repo:

`METROPOLITAN THEORETICAL CLAIM`
!=
`SOURCE PRODUCTION AT METROPOLITAN CENTER`.

The sources that enable comparison have their own colonial/local chains.

This is structurally similar to the Brinton/Pawnee work now being reconstructed:

`LOWER LOCAL/ETHNOGRAPHIC WITNESSES`
-> `SYNTHESIS CARRIER`
-> `COMPARATIVE THEORY`.

## IV. Chidester's Jastrow chapter is directly relevant to American context

The closing `Enduring Empire` chapter explicitly shifts to the United States and identifies **Morris Jastrow Jr.** as important in the emergence of an academic study of religion in America.

Source:
https://academic.oup.com/chicago-scholarship-online/book/16049/chapter-abstract/171033966

Chidester argues that the American study of religion retained a structural division between:

`SAVAGERY`
vs
`CIVILIZATION`,

with Native American and African American religions cast as proximate `savage` objects while ancient civilizations, especially the Ancient Near East, occupied different scholarly roles.

This is a major historiographic calibration for the repo because Jastrow is already a direct contextual actor in Lovejoy's 1890s environment.

## VI. Buddhism's position becomes historically more specific

Masuzawa shows why Buddhism had an unusually important role in European comparative religion:

- it challenged simple Christian/pagan binaries;
- it entered philological reconstruction through Sanskrit/Pāli textual corpora;
- it was entangled with `Aryan` language/race narratives;
- it became a `world religion` under a classificatory system whose apparent pluralism did not eliminate European hierarchy.

Thus Lovejoy's Buddhist work sits at an intersection:

`SPECIALIST TEXTUAL / PHILOLOGICAL OBJECT`
+
`COMPARATIVE-RELIGION OBJECT`
+
`LANGUAGE/RACE/CIVILIZATION CLASSIFICATION FIELD`.

This is stronger and more historically precise than simply calling him `an Orientalist`.
