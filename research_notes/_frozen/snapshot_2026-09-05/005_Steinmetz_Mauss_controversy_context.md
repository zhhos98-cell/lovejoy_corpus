Consolidated: 2026-08-26

## Core result

Lovejoy's early 005 `survivance` block belongs inside a named live controversy, not merely a generic Marillier topic.

Marillier's official EPHE report for 1898–99 says the Monday conference explicitly returned to his `Survivance` problem in order to **refute objections made by S. R. Steinmetz** to the thesis that early eschatology is amoral and that the other life is continuation rather than compensation, reparation or punishment. The same report says new supporting facts were added especially from the two Americas and lists Lovejoy among auditors who took an active part.

Marcel Mauss's 1896 review of Steinmetz independently reconstructs the same dispute and repeatedly uses Marillier against Steinmetz. Mauss is therefore a near-contemporary public **controversy witness**, not evidence that Lovejoy necessarily read his article.

The historical sequence is:

> **Marillier `Survivance` problem → Steinmetz objection → Mauss public rebuttal using Marillier → Marillier 1898–99 seminar explicitly reopens the dispute → Lovejoy active participant → 005 preserves the same source-critical/selector problem field.**

## 1. The seminar target is explicit

The 1898–99 EPHE report identifies:

- study of beliefs concerning `la survivance de l’âme`;
- the claim that no moral element intervenes at the origin of non-civilized eschatology;
- explicit refutation of Steinmetz's objections to Marillier's earlier memoir;
- defence of the other life as continuation rather than compensation/reparation/punishment;
- addition of new facts, especially from the two Americas;
- Lovejoy among auditors taking an active part.

This is stronger than a later thematic resemblance. It places Lovejoy in a research setting whose argumentative opponent and evidentiary burden are known.

---

## 2. Steinmetz object

The dispute concerns S. R. Steinmetz, *Ethnologische Studien zur ersten Entwickelung der Strafe: nebst einer psychologischen Abhandlung über Grausamkeit und Rachsucht* (Groningen: Noordhoff, 1894–).

For the present project, the relevant Steinmetz move is his rejection of a genuinely amoral/continuation stage in post-mortem belief and his tendency to read differentiated future destinies as evidence against that thesis.

The project does not need a general history of Steinmetz here; the controlled role is **opponent in the survivance/retribution controversy resumed by Marillier's seminar**.

---

## 3. Mauss makes source criticism central to the rebuttal

Mauss's critique says, in effect, that critical reading of texts yields different results from simply registering their gross/general content.

The dispute therefore turns on **how a report is entered as evidence**, not only on which examples are collected.

Mauss rejects apparent counterexamples where historical contact may have introduced later categories:

- Christian influence in several American/Oceanic cases;
- Hindu/Buddhist influence in Indonesian/Indic-adjacent cases;
- Sanskrit `naraka` as a borrowing marker.

Thus:

`reported heaven/hell or good/bad distinction`

cannot automatically become:

`indigenous primitive moral retribution`.

Its provenance and historical layer must first be controlled.

Direct Marillier 1893/94 recovery now independently confirms that contact/borrowing and superposition are Marillier's own operations. Mauss remains valuable because he shows those operations were publicly mobilized in the Steinmetz controversy before Lovejoy's Paris participation.

---

## 4. Differential destinations do not prove retribution

Mauss argues, using Marillier, that differentiated afterlives remain compatible with continuation when the selector is:

- persistence of terrestrial inequality/status;
- performance of rites;
- manner/kind of death.

The complete Marillier primary now directly controls a still broader selector field, so this point is no longer evidence for a Lovejoy-created taxonomy.

Its contextual value is different:

> **the seminar controversy itself required participants to disaggregate moral-looking outcomes by operative selector.**

Lovejoy 005 participates in that exact argumentative burden.

---

## 5. 005 correspondence

The early notebook repeatedly performs controversy-relevant operations:

- missionary/contact chronology is checked;
- Hindu/Buddhist influence is separated from older local strata;
- drowning, childbirth, suicide and other kinds of death are treated as possible non-moral selectors;
- ritual and bodily qualification are distinguished from moral merit;
- differential destinations are not admitted as evidence of morality until the operative relation is specified.

The current originality verdict, however, is narrower than the original Batch20 formulation. Lovejoy does **not** uniquely sharpen a simple Marillier binary into a serial determinant taxonomy; direct Marillier and Söderblom controls show both the determinant field and serial redivision were already available in the workshop environment.

The Lovejoy delta lies in his particular redivision, dense repeated same-case reallocation, cross-domain migration and later 1906 reaggregation.

---

## 6. The American-evidence line

The 1898–99 report says the seminar added new facts especially from the two Americas. This is relevant to the heavy North-American material in 005.

A contemporaneous related object is Elizabeth Laetitia Moon Conard's work on Algonquian future-life beliefs, begun during Paris study and later translated/published by Marillier. This is a contextual route, not evidence that Conard supplied the exact `new facts` used in Lovejoy's notes unless a direct documentary bridge is recovered.

North-American source circulation should therefore be handled source-by-source, not as a blanket Conard→Marillier→Lovejoy chain.

---

## 7. Strongest contextual formulation

> **The first thirty pages of 005 preserve work inside a live EPHE dispute over how ethnographic reports should be criticized before they are classified. The issue was not simply whether non-European peoples possessed moral retribution. The seminar problem required heterogeneous reports to be decomposed historically and causally: imported Christian/Indic categories separated from earlier strata, status/ritual/death-type selectors separated from moral valuation, and apparently differential destinies admitted as moral only after the operative relation was identified. Lovejoy's document is one working redivision of this controversy field.**

## 8. Evidence discipline

### Secure

- Marillier's 1898–99 conference explicitly names Steinmetz as an opponent to be refuted;
- Lovejoy is listed among active auditors;
- Mauss 1896 publicly uses Marillier in the same dispute;
- source criticism, contact contamination, ritual qualification and manner-of-death selectors are central to the controversy;
- direct Marillier recovery independently confirms the underlying selector/source-critical operations.

### Strong inference

- the Steinmetz dispute is part of the immediate argumentative environment of Lovejoy's early 005 block;
- the notebook's source-critical interruptions are best understood as work inside that active dispute rather than isolated marginalia.

### Unproved

- Lovejoy personally read Mauss 1896;
- a particular 005 sentence records Marillier's spoken rebuttal to Steinmetz;
- Conard supplied a specific seminar datum used by Lovejoy;
- exact source of every `new fact` from the Americas.
