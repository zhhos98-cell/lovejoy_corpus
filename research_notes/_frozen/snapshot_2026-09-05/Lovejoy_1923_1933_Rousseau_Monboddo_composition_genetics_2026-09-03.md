## I. 1923 — Rousseau object already reclassified before Monboddo enters the comparison

Primary:

Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Supposed Primitivism of Rousseau's "Discourse on Inequality"`, *Modern Philology* 21.2 (Nov. 1923): 165–186.

Direct journal primary is publicly exposed through JSTOR / University of Chicago metadata and page images.

Lovejoy's starting target is a received macro classification:

`ROUSSEAU SECOND DISCOURSE = GLORIFICATION OF STATE OF NATURE / PRIMITIVISM`.

He calls this one of the persistent historical errors and says confusion partly arises from failure to distinguish the senses of `state of nature`.

The 1923 operation includes:

- split ambiguous `state of nature` usages;
- reconstruct multiple pre-civil stages rather than one undifferentiated primitive state;
- separate the first animal/solitary stage from later savage/social stages;
- deny that Rousseau's ideal lies simply in the earliest condition;
- identify an intermediate stage as normatively favored;
- recast the historical significance of the *Second Discourse* as an early contribution to an **evolutionary conception of human history**.

Thus by 1923 Lovejoy already owns the proposition:

`ROUSSEAU = NOT SIMPLY PRIMITIVIST`

and, more strongly,

`ROUSSEAU SECOND DISCOURSE`
→ `DEVELOPMENTAL / EVOLUTIONARY HUMAN HISTORY`.

This is the Rousseau-side module later reused in 1933.

## II. 1933 explicitly declares the 1923 Rousseau analysis as a prior module

Primary:

Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Monboddo and Rousseau`, *Modern Philology* 30.3 (1933): 275–296; direct English reprint in *Essays in the History of Ideas*, open through Project MUSE.

The article explicitly says:

- in the preceding essay Lovejoy had already shown that what was original and historically significant in Rousseau's *Second Discourse* was contrary to the usual primitivist classification;
- Rousseau's novelty lay in a form of **social evolutionism**;
- the current article will show that what had been observed about Rousseau is also true of Monboddo;
- the Rousseau–Monboddo influence question will be treated only incidentally but explicitly.

This is direct self-reuse, not retrospective reconstruction by the project.

The composition relation is therefore actor-explicit:

`1923 ROUSSEAU RESULT`
→ `1933 APPLY / TEST PARALLEL ON MONBODDO`.

## III. The six-thesis frame is a recombination device

In 1933 Lovejoy says that in Monboddo's main work, as in Rousseau's *Discourse*, six theses can be found, all unusual and some historically novel in the third quarter of the eighteenth century.

The most consequential later theses include:

1. distinctively human psychology lies not in fixed original attributes but in a **capacity for gradual development** of higher faculties (`perfectibilité`);
2. human history begins at a stage in which man is, in one sense, not yet fully human;
3. history is not simply decline from primitive perfection but a slow and painful ascent from animality toward rational/social life;
4. a new historical science is required to reconstruct successive stages of intellectual/social development;
5. living `savage` peoples can be used as evidence for stages through which ancestors of civilized peoples are supposed to have passed.

Lovejoy then says directly that he had already shown all these theses can be traced in Rousseau; he will now show them in Monboddo.

Thus the six-thesis structure is best read as a **comparison grid** built from a prior Rousseau analysis and then populated with Monboddo evidence.

It is not simply a summary of Knight 1900.

## IV. What is not Lovejoy-1933-owned — developmental Monboddo was already field-visible

William Angus Knight's *Lord Monboddo and Some of His Contemporaries* (1900) already presents:

- language as acquired rather than natural;
- speechless individuals/peoples as evidence;
- the `Political State as necessary for the invention of Language`;
- political society as preceding language;
- man placed between solitary/gregarious and political/non-political animal categories;
- uncivilized peoples used to reconstruct the origins of society.

The 1911 *Encyclopaedia Britannica* entry likewise describes Monboddo as:

- gradually elevating man from animal condition;
- tracing the rise to social state;
- treating language as consequent upon social life;
- using savage peoples as evidence for civilization.

Therefore Lovejoy 1933 does **not** own:

`MONBODDO HAD A DEVELOPMENTAL / STADIAL ACCOUNT OF MAN`.

The correct source-ownership distinction is:

`DEVELOPMENTAL MONBODDO = FIELD-AVAILABLE`

while

`LOVEJOY 1933 = SIX-THESIS SELECTION / PARALLELIZATION / RECLASSIFICATION / GENEALOGY TEST`.

## V. 1904 supplies a second Lovejoy-owned module inside 1933

Lovejoy's 1904 `Some Eighteenth Century Evolutionists` had already audited Monboddo under a different question:

`DID MONBODDO ACTUALLY HOLD BIOLOGICAL TRANSFORMISM?`.

That article separates:

- language evolution;
- man–orang ancestry;
- species identity;
- common descent;
- transformism.

In 1933 Lovejoy explicitly cites the 1904 essay when reopening whether Monboddo was an evolutionist in biological and anthropological senses.

Thus 1933 has at least two direct Lovejoy-owned prior modules:

`1923 ROUSSEAU / PRIMITIVISM RECLASSIFICATION`

and

`1904 MONBODDO / BIOLOGICAL-EVOLUTION AUDIT`.

The 1933 paper combines them around a new comparison object.

## VI. 1933 does not merely merge old conclusions — it performs new operations

The paper adds several operations not already closed by either earlier module.

### A. Parallelization

Lovejoy asks whether the same six-thesis complex exists in both Rousseau and Monboddo.

`ROUSSEAU MODULE`
+
`MONBODDO MATERIAL`
→ `SHARED SIX-THESIS CONFIGURATION`.

This is a new comparative object.

### B. Anti-primitivist reclassification of Monboddo

The article argues that historians who simply call both men `primitivists` miss the more important element.

For Monboddo, occasional praise of natural physical condition is separated from ethical/intellectual primitivism; Lovejoy emphasizes arts/sciences, rational-political virtue, perfectibility and social development.

Thus:

`PRIMITIVIST LABEL`
→ split component meanings
→ retain limited physical/natural-health retrospectivism
→ reject macro classification
→ reclassify developmental complex as anti-primitivist in its historically important direction.

### C. Biological inference is reopened and partly strengthened

Using the same 1773 Knight letter and tail packet already handled in 1904, Lovejoy makes a stronger inference about common anthropoid ancestry/species transformation.

Authority:

`research_notes/Lovejoy_Monboddo_1904_1933_same_evidence_new_claim_reallocation_2026-09-03.md`.

### D. Genealogy becomes a distinct Gate-B problem

After establishing the content configuration, Lovejoy separately asks:

`WHY ARE ROUSSEAU AND MONBODDO SO SIMILAR?`.

He keeps open:

- direct Rousseau influence;
- independent/spontaneous recurrence;
- common classical/Epicurean sources;
- common contemporary anatomy;
- common travel/ethnographic evidence.

Even with Rousseau priority + Monboddo reading + admiration + close passages, direct influence cannot be closed with certainty.

Thus:

`CONFIGURATIONAL SIMILARITY / CONTENT IDENTITY`
!=
`GENETIC CAUSATION`.

## IX. Relation to `primitive`

The 1923→1933 sequence is especially useful because it shows `primitivism` itself being treated as an overbroad umbrella category.

1923:

`ROUSSEAU = PRIMITIVIST`
→ split `state of nature` senses / stages / valuations
→ macro label downgraded.

1933:

`ROUSSEAU + MONBODDO = PRIMITIVISTS`
→ compare six developmental propositions
→ retain limited retrospectivist/natural-health elements
→ reclassify main historical significance toward evolutionism / anti-primitivism.

This is a mature case of:

`SHARED LABEL`
!=
`SHARED UNDIFFERENTIATED HISTORICAL OBJECT`.

But the larger project should not infer from this later critique that Lovejoy's own 1906 use of `primitive philosophy` escaped totalization. The two operations must be held together rather than harmonized.
