## I. Meylan 2017: Lovejoy as a category-maker inside the history of religion

Nicolas Meylan, *Mana: A History of a Western Category* (Brill, 2017), reconstructs the migration of the Oceanic term `mana` into Western religious-studies and anthropological theory.

Official Brill metadata:
- *Mana: A History of a Western Category*, Supplements to Method & Theory in the Study of Religion 10;
- Brill, 2017;
- chapters include `The First Century of Mana` and `Bringing Mana Within Theory`.

Official pages:
- https://brill.com/display/book/9789004349247/B9789004349247_003.xml
- https://brill.com/display/book/9789004349247/B9789004349247_004.xml

A searchable full-text witness of the book's `Domestication of Mana` discussion characterizes Lovejoy 1906 as follows:

- Lovejoy attacks Marett's treatment of mana as the `supernormal` / supernatural and thereby as a primarily religious category;
- Lovejoy instead stresses `the efficacious, the powerful, the productive` and reconstructs mana as a primitive conception of causal efficacy/power;
- he equates mana with orenda, manitou, ngai and related terms;
- Meylan reads Lovejoy as remaining broadly within a Tylorian intellectualist paradigm, but as shifting the question from religion/supernormality toward causality;
- Meylan further argues that Lovejoy's cross-cultural equation had a selective boundary: he did not simply incorporate every analogous impersonal-power notion, especially analogues drawn by Hubert and Mauss from more prestigious textual cultures.

Searchable witness:
https://dokumen.pub/mana-a-history-of-a-western-category-supplements-to-method-amp-theory-in-the-study-of-religion-10-9004348700-9789004348707.html

## II. Why this matters for the existing Primitive Energetics gate

The repo already established through Leuba 1912 / Lovejoy 1914 that the minimum identity core of `primitive energetics` is thinner than Lovejoy's rich 1906 formulation:

`IMPERSONAL POWER / POTENCY`
!= necessarily
`STRICT MECHANICAL REGULARITY`.

That correction makes corpus selection more, not less, important.

If Lovejoy's type core is thin, the question becomes:

> **Why are some impersonal-power analogues admitted as tokens of `primitive philosophy` while other apparently analogous power concepts are not?**

Meylan's observation suggests that the missing boundary may be located not only in semantic content but in the **historical status assigned to the source population/tradition**.

Possible structure to test:

`THIN POWER-EFFICACY SIMILARITY`
+
`ETHNOGRAPHIC / "PRIMITIVE" SOURCE STATUS`
-> admitted token

while

`THIN POWER-EFFICACY SIMILARITY`
+
`PRESTIGIOUS / TEXTUAL HISTORICAL TRADITION`

This would be a major correction to any purely semantic reconstruction of the category.

## V. Later afterlife: Lovejoy's 1906 formulation did circulate beyond history-of-ideas historiography

Meylan's history also demonstrates that the 1906 article had a disciplinary afterlife as a contribution to mana/dynamism theory, rather than surviving only in Lovejoy bibliography.

Other public witnesses show:

- Irving King, *The Development of Religion* (1910), cites Lovejoy's Monist article directly in its account of impersonal potency and develops the category while criticizing older religion classifications;
- Leuba 1912 names `manitouism` and explicitly compares his `dynamism` to Lovejoy;
- later C. G. Jung appropriates `primitive energetics` into a psychology-of-energy genealogy;
- Meylan 2017 reconstructs this longer transformation as part of the Western history of mana.

This creates a second specialist-afterlife pattern parallel to the 1898 Buddhist paper:

`LOVEJOY OBJECT`
-> `SPECIALIST DISCIPLINARY AFTERLIFE`
while
`LOVEJOY HISTORIOGRAPHY`
-> often organizes the actor around other canonical objects.

Do not collapse the two specialist routes; Buddhist philology and mana/religion-theory are distinct carriers.

## VI. Historiographic significance

Modern history of religious studies changes the question from:

`WAS LOVEJOY RIGHT ABOUT MANA?`

to:

`HOW DID LOVEJOY PARTICIPATE IN MAKING MANA INTO A WESTERN COMPARATIVE CATEGORY, AND WHAT SOURCE HIERARCHIES MADE THAT COMPARISON POSSIBLE?`

This shift aligns with wider historiography of religion that treats categories such as `world religions`, `mana`, `Semitic`, `Aryan`, `primitive`, and `religion` itself as historical scholarly constructions rather than transparent descriptions.

It also exposes a crucial political/epistemic layer:

`RELATION-SPECIFIC CARE IN LOCAL ANALYSIS`
can coexist with
`ASYMMETRICAL PROMOTION OF SOME POPULATIONS/TRADITIONS INTO THE ANALYTIC CATEGORY "PRIMITIVE"`.

The project should preserve both propositions.
