# MS38_004_001_061_005 and Léon Marillier's EPHE conferences, 1898–1899

## Status

This note records a major evidentiary upgrade. The 1898–1899 report of the École pratique des hautes études, Section des sciences religieuses, explicitly lists **Lovejoy** among the auditors who “took an active part in the work” of Léon Marillier's conferences on *Religions des peuples non civilisés*. The two reported conference sequences correspond strikingly to the two dominant blocks of notebook `MS38_004_001_061_005`.

**Current judgment:** the core of notebook 005 is very likely Lovejoy's working notebook from, or immediately around, Marillier's 1898–1899 EPHE conferences. Later inserted slips and later bibliography show that the notebook continued to accrete after the initial course context, so the whole physical object should not be dated uniformly to 1898–1899.

## 1. Direct institutional evidence: Lovejoy is named as an active auditor

Primary source: École pratique des hautes études, Section des sciences religieuses, *Rapport sommaire sur les conférences de l'exercice 1898-1899 et le programme des conférences pour l'exercice 1899-1900*, p. 33, section “Religions des peuples non civilisés,” L. Marillier.

The report names regular students and then states:

> “Auditeurs ayant pris une part active aux travaux : MM. Lovejoy, W. S. Andrews et Lorette.”

This is direct documentary evidence that Lovejoy studied with Marillier in this exact course environment. It also corroborates Lovejoy's own 1906 statement that his conclusions were strongly influenced by his studies under the late Léon Marillier.

## 2. Monday conference ↔ notebook pp. 1–30: survivance and amoral eschatology

The 1898–1899 report describes Marillier's first conference as a study of beliefs concerning survival of the soul. Its essential thesis was that **no moral element enters primitive eschatology at the origin**. Marillier defended his earlier memoir, *La survivance de l'âme et l'idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés*, arguing that the other life is conceived as **continuation**, not compensation, repair, or punishment, of terrestrial life. New evidence was drawn especially from the Americas.

This maps almost exactly onto notebook 005 pp. 1–30:

- future life begins as continuation of terrestrial status and condition rather than recompense for virtue;
- manner of death, ritual conditions, social rank, magical capacity, suicide, drowning, childbirth, etc. determine destiny independently of moral desert;
- universal happiness can still be “naturalistic” if it merely intensifies this-worldly existence;
- moral reward/punishment appears as a later or infiltrated element;
- missionary/contact influence is repeatedly treated as a source-critical problem;
- the dead world can preserve rank, occupation and bodily-like needs;
- origin-of-death myths and second-death/limited-survival conceptions are collected comparatively.

The earlier Marillier report for 1893–1894 is even closer to notebook pp. 7–30: death is described as accidental rather than natural; myths explain how death entered the world; the land of the dead reproduces the world of the living; rank there is determined by terrestrial rank rather than moral merit; afterlife is continuation, not compensation.

## 3. Tuesday conference ↔ notebook pp. 46–119: sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy

The 1898–1899 report describes Marillier's second conference as a continuation of research on **human sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy**. It gives the following analytical program:

- special study of magical and religious practices among Bantu peoples of southern Africa;
- determination of the reasons for using human victims in distinct types of sacrifice: **expiatory, funerary, anthropophagic, and magical**;
- comparison with Guinea-coast Africans, North American Indians, and non-Aryan populations of India;
- parallel study of bloody animal sacrifice, with emphasis on **agrarian rites and fecundating sacrifices**.

That is almost a synopsis of notebook 005 pp. 46–119. The notebook develops:

- funerary/alimentary sacrifice;
- expiatory/scapegoat sacrifice;
- magical sacrifice acting directly on forces of nature;
- anthropophagy and selected-body-part consumption;
- southern African/Bantu materials, Callaway, Grout and related sources;
- Guinea/West-African comparisons;
- North American and Indian comparisons;
- human versus animal sacrifice;
- agricultural and fecundative rites;
- blood as curative, rejuvenating, protective and transferable efficacy;
- criticism of attempts to reduce sacrifice to one communion theory;
- cannibalism decomposed into distinct causal mechanisms.

Notebook p. 117's synthetic conclusion — alimentary and expiatory/propitiatory sacrifice are common, sacramental union comparatively exceptional — is best understood inside this Marillier course problem rather than as an isolated Lovejoy invention.

## 4. The p. 29 slip is now interpretable: “Marillier — Survivance — 12 June”

Notebook PDF p. 29 contains an inserted slip transcribed as:

`Marillier — Survivance — 12 June`

Previously this was only a suggestive internal clue. In light of the EPHE report, it is now strongly contextualized: “survivance” was the explicit subject of Marillier's 1898–1899 conference attended actively by Lovejoy. Since Lovejoy was in this course in the academic year 1898–1899, **12 June 1899 is a strong candidate** for the slip's date, but the year is not written on the slip and must remain an inference.

## 5. Relation to Marillier's longer teaching trajectory

Marillier's teaching before and after Lovejoy's documented attendance shows that notebook 005 sits inside a sustained research program rather than a one-off lecture series.

- 1893–1894: origin of death, land of the dead, rank and non-moral afterlife; published memoir on *survivance* and justice.
- 1897–1898: funerary, expiatory, anthropophagic and magical human sacrifices in West Africa.
- 1898–1899: survivance/amoral eschatology + human sacrifice/ritual anthropophagy; **Lovejoy documented as active auditor**.
- 1899–1900: agrarian sacrifices among non-Aryan Indian peoples, especially the Khonds; magical/fecundating power of blood; expiatory sacrifice and expulsion of evils; critical study of Frazer; origin of death, disease, amoral eschatology, Christian interpolations in American myths.
- 1900–1901: theories of totemism; ritual anthropophagy and human sacrifice in America.

Several of the later inserted strata of notebook 005 — especially agricultural sacrifice, blood as fecundating power, Frazer criticism, totemism, origin-of-death/source-critical material — align with Marillier's 1899–1901 continuation. This does **not** prove Lovejoy attended those later sessions; he is not listed as an active auditor in the 1899–1900 report. It does show that his notebook continued to track questions central to Marillier's research program.

## 6. Link to the 1906 *Monist* article

Lovejoy's 1906 “The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy” states in a footnote on p. 381 that its conclusions were reached in **1900**, independently of several later writers, and that they were “much influenced” by his studies under Léon Marillier.

The institutional evidence now supplies the missing documentary bridge:

1. Lovejoy is formally documented as actively participating in Marillier's 1898–1899 conferences.
2. Notebook 005 reproduces the two principal conference problems with exceptional structural fidelity.
3. The notebook itself contains a `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June` slip.
4. Lovejoy later says the 1900 conclusions behind the 1906 article were strongly influenced by Marillier.
5. The 1906 article announces a larger analysis of customs — sacrifice, agriculture, initiation, taboo, eschatology — precisely the domains worked through in notebook 005.

The most defensible production sequence is therefore now:

> **Marillier EPHE course / research program (documented Lovejoy attendance, 1898–1899) → notebook 005 comparative ritual/es\-chatology workshop → Lovejoy's conceptual formulation by 1900 → 1906 published “first chapter” on impersonal transferable energy / Primitive Energetics.**

## 7. Evidential cautions

**Secure:**
- The EPHE 1898–1899 report explicitly names “Lovejoy” as an active auditor in Marillier's conferences.
- The conference topics match the dominant structures of notebook 005.
- The notebook contains a `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June` slip.
- Lovejoy's 1906 article explicitly acknowledges strong influence from studies under Marillier and dates its conclusions to 1900.

**Strong inference:**
- The core notebook is course-derived or course-adjacent working material from Marillier's 1898–1899 EPHE teaching.
- The p. 29 date is probably 12 June 1899.

**Not yet secure:**
- That every page of 005 was written during 1898–1899.
- That Lovejoy attended Marillier's 1899–1900 or 1900–1901 conferences.
- Exact dating of later inserted slips and their route of transmission.
- Whether Marillier directly supplied particular bibliography or whether Lovejoy independently extended it.

## Research significance

The key shift is from an intellectual-influence hypothesis to a documented pedagogical and production context. Notebook 005 can now be read not merely as evidence that Lovejoy happened to share Marillier's interests, but as a material trace of **active participation in Marillier's comparative-religion seminar**, followed by Lovejoy's own abstraction of that evidentiary practice into a theory of impersonal efficacy.
