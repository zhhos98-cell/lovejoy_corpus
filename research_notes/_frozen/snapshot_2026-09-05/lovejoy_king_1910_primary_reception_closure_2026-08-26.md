# Lovejoy 1906 → Irving King 1910 — direct primary reception closure

Date: 2026-08-26
Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / CLOSES FORMER KING 1910 PRIMARY HOLD**

Primary object directly checked:

Irving King, *The Development of Religion: A Study in Anthropology and Social Psychology* (New York: Macmillan, 1910), Internet Archive/Cornell scan `cu31924031018876`.

## Core revision

The former reception status

`Lovejoy 1906 → King 1910 = SECONDARY-CONTROLLED / PRIMARY HOLD`

is superseded.

The edge is now **DIRECT PRIMARY**.

King does not merely mention Lovejoy in a bibliography. Chapter VI, `The Mysterious Power`, makes several proposition-level uses of Lovejoy's 1906 `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`.

## 1. Printed p.135 — King imports Lovejoy's comparative formulation

King describes the mysterious potency as communicable property / mobile invisible substance / latent transferable energy, then reproduces Lovejoy's formulation of a `distinct and rather abstract conception` of a diffused, all-pervasive, invisible, manipulable and transferable life-energy/universal force. The footnote identifies:

A. O. Lovejoy, `The fundamental concept of the primitive philosophy`, *The Monist* XVI, pp.365, 376.

This is a strong reception edge because Lovejoy's comparative abstraction enters the opening construction of King's chapter, not just a closing bibliography.

Analytical verdict:

> **King directly incorporates Lovejoy's comparative category-description into his own `Mysterious Power` chapter.**

This does not mean King adopts Lovejoy's whole causal or religious interpretation.

## 2. Printed p.139 — King uses Lovejoy against Fletcher's volitional reading of `wakonda`

In discussing Siouan/Omaha `wakonda`, King says that blood and the menstruant woman are `wakonda`, carrying danger and fecundating energy, and adds that Lovejoy may help explain sex taboos and the `nudity charm`.

More important, King's footnote states that, as Lovejoy points out — and William Jones agrees — Alice C. Fletcher interprets `wakonda` in terms of will, whereas will/volition was probably only a special case of `wakonda`.

Thus King uses Lovejoy for a **semantic / interpretive correction of an ethnographic authority**.

Reception operation:

`Fletcher observer gloss = will`
→ `Lovejoy/King correction`
→ `will is only one special case of a broader potency field`.

This is directly relevant to the project's proof-warrant architecture: one source may remain evidentially useful while its conceptual gloss is rejected or narrowed.

## 3. Printed p.146 — Lovejoy as evidence résumé / bibliographic-semantic relay

King writes that there is definite evidence that the impersonal-force conception is held by other races and explicitly says:

`Lovejoy gives a good résumé of some of this evidence.`

He then summarizes Lovejoy's Polynesian, Bantu and Masai material, including the Bantu `mulungu` discussion and Lovejoy's grammatical/semantic argument against personality.

This establishes a second reception function:

> **Lovejoy 1906 is used as an evidence dossier and relay into source material.**

King is not only taking a theory; he is treating the article as a controlled comparative source packet.

## 4. Printed p.163 — King retains the power field while reallocating its religious status

After a long chapter on the impersonal/quasi-mechanical potency field, King concludes that it is widespread and should be explicitly considered in discussions of magic, primitive religion, customs and morality.

He then states that it cannot be held to be `in itself a religious concept`; it is instead a point of view/theory of the world that may be used by religion or magic and can enter later religious consciousness as raw material.

This is a **component-wise transformation**, not simple assent or dissent.

King retains:

- broad comparative power field;
- impersonal / quasi-mechanical characterization at substantial strength;
- Lovejoy as evidence and semantic authority.

King reallocates:

- the category's place in the definition/genesis of religion.

Thus:

`Lovejoy comparative category = RETAIN / REUSE`

while

`religious-status proposition = KING RECLASSIFICATION`.

This provides a direct 1910 control for the later Leuba pattern of proposition-level grading.

## 5. Implication for the American reception sequence

The early American chain can now be stated more strongly:

`Lovejoy 1906`
→ `King 1910 directly incorporates Lovejoy's category-description, uses him to correct Fletcher, and treats the article as an evidence résumé`
→ `Leuba 1910 review of King (existence secure; exact content still primary HOLD)`
→ `King 1911 reply/restatement explicitly names Leuba's review`
→ `Leuba 1912 directly reads King and Lovejoy together, retains their evidence field while contesting Lovejoy's strong mechanical-regularity claim`
→ `Lovejoy 1914 reviews Leuba (review existence secure; exact corrected proposition still direct-primary HOLD)`.

The ecology is therefore no longer merely triangular through a secondary King locator. King is a directly controlled internal node.

## 6. JHI-facing consequence

A short article should not narrate all reception details. The useful larger claim is:

> **The 1906 argument entered an American religious-science/psychology field in decomposable form. King could take Lovejoy's comparative category and source criticism while reclassifying its religious status; Leuba could later retain the evidence dossier while rejecting a stronger causal characterization. Reception proceeded proposition by proposition rather than as wholesale acceptance or rejection of a theory.**

This gives a publication-level analogue to the notebooks' recurring rule that stronger propositions require separate warrants.

Do not claim conscious methodological borrowing from Lovejoy unless actor-level evidence appears.
