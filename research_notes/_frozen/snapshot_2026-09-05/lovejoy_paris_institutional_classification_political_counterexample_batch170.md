# Batch 170 — Paris institutional classification, workshop inheritance, and Marillier's political counterexample

Date: 2026-08-23
Status: source audit complete; item-level retrieval and proof limits remain explicit below.

## Question

How did the Paris institutions Lovejoy entered in 1898–1899 distribute textual traditions, living populations, research methods, and scholarly authority? Can the politics of those arrangements be reconstructed without treating an individual's political commitments as a transparent proxy for his classificatory practice?

## Institutional distribution is visible in the contemporary course architecture

The EPHE Section des sciences religieuses report for 1898–1899 arranges its chairs in an instructive sequence. Léon Marillier appears under `I. Religions des peuples non civilisés`, p. 33; Léon de Rosny under `II. Religions de l'Extrême-Orient et de Amérique indienne`, p. 34; Sylvain Lévi and Alfred Foucher under `III. Religions de l'Inde`, p. 35; and André Berthelot and Jules Toutain under `VIII. Religions de la Grèce et de Rome`, pp. 38–39. Other chairs separately cover Egypt, Israel and the western Semites, rabbinical Judaism, and Islam.

The titles are contemporary institutional evidence, not neutral analytical descriptions. India and classical antiquity are organized as historically and philologically bounded traditions; the populations assigned to Marillier are aggregated under a civilizational condition rather than a geographical or textual tradition. De Rosny's grouping of the Far East and Indigenous America introduces another non-identical classificatory principle. The useful claim is therefore not that all non-European material occupied one subordinate slot, but that distinct administrative categories distributed comparable religious material in different ways before any individual argument began.

Primary source: EPHE, *Rapport sommaire sur les conférences de l'exercice 1898–1899 et le programme des conférences pour l'exercice 1899–1900*, section reports, pp. 33–39: https://www.persee.fr/issue/ephe_0000-0002_1898_num_12_8.

## Lovejoy's placement has unequal evidentiary strength

The Marillier report names Lovejoy among the auditors who took an active part in the work. Wilson's letter-based reconstruction places him with Maurice Vernes and Jean Réville, and the contemporary programs independently establish the relevant Psalms and Fourth Gospel courses. Sylvain Lévi's regular Sanskrit offerings and Foucher's Nyāya instruction are institutionally documented, as is Joseph Trumbull Stickney's Sāṃkhya series based on Garbe. Lovejoy's attendance at those particular public courses remains unverified. Wilson's account of private reading with Lévi must remain distinguished from enrollment in any named course until the underlying correspondence is inspected.

## Marillier is a political counterexample, not an escape from classification politics

EPHE's official prosopographical notice describes Marillier as committed to an evolutionary conception of religion and as contrasting so-called savage thought with the thought of civilized populations. It also identifies him as a contributor to the Protestant, Dreyfusard newspaper *Le Signal* and as an advocate for Armenians, the protection of Indigenous rights, peace, and popular education.

These facts resist a simple inference from classificatory evolutionism to a single personal political position. They also prevent the reverse inference: advocacy for Indigenous rights does not dissolve the institutional assignment of living populations to a category defined through their purported lack of civilization. The historical question concerns how humanitarian commitments, documentary criticism, universal psychology, evolutionary chronology, and unequal evidentiary placement could coexist within the same scholarly formation.

The same official notice locates Marillier at the junction of multiple institutions: philosophy training, physiological psychology, liberal Protestant biblical criticism, the EPHE, women's teacher education, popular education, and the *Revue de l'histoire des religions*, which he co-directed with Jean Réville from 1896. These institutional connections help explain why Lovejoy's documented interests in religious psychology, ethnographic testimony, biblical criticism, and historical comparison converged in Paris without being reducible to a single disciplinary lineage.

Official institutional source: Patrick Cabanel, `Léon Marillier`, *Dictionnaire prosopographique de l'EPHE*: https://prosopo.ephe.psl.eu/l%C3%A9on-marillier.

## Workshop continuity and explanatory rupture

Mauss's opening lecture of 27 January 1902 explicitly attributes to Marillier the critical treatment of evidence and describes exhaustive collective bibliography, common critical exercises, and seminar reading of ethnographic documents. His subsequent EPHE reports identify analytical criticism of particular reports and student participation. At the same time, Mauss contests the explanatory priority Marillier assigned to general psychological laws and develops a sociological account.

This permits a stronger reconstruction than either an uninterrupted transmission narrative or a clean school-versus-school opposition. What persists is an institutionalized procedure for collecting, comparing, criticizing, and reclassifying reported facts; what changes is the explanatory warrant thought sufficient for turning those facts into historical or social knowledge. Lovejoy's 1906 `primitive energetics`, Marillier's religious psychology, and Maussian collective authority should accordingly be compared proposition by proposition, not treated as interchangeable outcomes of a common Parisian education.

## Material-paper question

The page-level paper techniques in Lovejoy's notebook must be located within this documentary workshop without assuming that any specific graphic was copied from Marillier or shared by Mauss. Facing-page distributions, lists, branching lines, brackets, deletion, and cross-references can be compared with independently surviving student notes if they become accessible. The best current external target is Nathan Söderblom's Paris study notebooks, 1894–1901, precisely identified in the Uppsala catalogue. Marillier's own RAI MS 99 files provide an independent teacher-side control, especially files 1, 2, 4, and 11.

Evidence limit: shared topical interests or common seminar attendance do not establish identical diagrams, direct transmission of a paper technique, or the order in which a given page was composed.
