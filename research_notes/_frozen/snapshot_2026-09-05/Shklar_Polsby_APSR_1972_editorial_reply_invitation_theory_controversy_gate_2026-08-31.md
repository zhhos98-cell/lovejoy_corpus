# Shklar–Polsby / APSR, December 1972: editorial reply invitation and theory-controversy ceiling

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **LETTER DATE/LOCUS DIRECTLY IDENTIFIED THROUGH HARVARD-CITING SCHOLARSHIP / POLSBY APSR MANAGING-EDITOR ROLE DIRECT / REPLY-INVITATION CONTENT SECONDARY-ARCHIVAL LOCK / CRITICAL ITEM IDENTITY + PRINT STATUS HOLD**

## Core result

A late-1972 Judith Shklar / Nelson W. Polsby correspondence edge adds a different kind of evidence to the APSR carrier-capacity model.

Harvard holds the relevant Shklar papers as `HUGFP 118`. Recent scholarship citing the original archive identifies:

`Judith N. Shklar -> Nelson W. Polsby, 19 December 1972, HUGFP 118, Box 1`.

Andreas Hess's archival reconstruction states that Polsby, then editor of the *American Political Science Review*, asked Shklar whether she wished to respond to a severe criticism of claims made in *After Utopia*. Shklar's reply distinguished disagreement with ideas from intolerance toward them and rejected the implication that arguing strongly against positions meant wishing to suppress opponents.

The exact original letter has not been directly read in this repository.

Therefore the current secure relation is:

`APSR MANAGING EDITOR POLSBY`
-> `INVITATION TO SHKLAR TO RESPOND TO A CRITICISM OF AFTER UTOPIA`
-> `SHKLAR PRIVATE REPLY, 19 DEC 1972`.

This is evidence for **editorially mediated theory controversy / reply opportunity** inside APSR's communications ecology.

It is not yet evidence that either the critical item or Shklar's response appeared in print.

Core firewall:

`EDITORIAL INVITATION TO REPLY`
!= `PUBLISHED REPLY`
!= `PUBLISHED CRITICAL ARTICLE`.

## III. Hess's archival reconstruction identifies the editorial problem

Hess's account, based on the Shklar papers, describes the incoming Polsby request as asking whether Shklar wished to respond to a harsh criticism of the theses of *After Utopia*.

The critic allegedly treated Shklar's attack on certain ideas as evidence of intolerance. Shklar's response rejects that inference, distinguishing disagreement from suppression.

For present purposes the important operation is not Shklar's mature liberal doctrine but the carrier relation:

`CRITICAL TEXT / MANUSCRIPT ABOUT SHKLAR`
-> `APSR MANAGING EDITOR`
-> `INVITED TARGET-AUTHOR RESPONSE`.

That is a controversy-management mechanism.

But Hess's narrative does not, by itself, identify the critic or establish publication status.
