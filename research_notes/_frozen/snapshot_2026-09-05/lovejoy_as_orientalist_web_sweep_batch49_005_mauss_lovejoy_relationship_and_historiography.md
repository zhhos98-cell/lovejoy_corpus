# Batch 49 — Lovejoy 005 only: Mauss–Lovejoy relationship, Marillier mediation, and historiographical visibility

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: relationship of Marcel Mauss to Arthur O. Lovejoy as it bears on `MS38_004_001_061_005`, with historiographical audit.

## Research move

The question is not simply whether `Mauss influenced Lovejoy`. The present evidence does **not** support a direct personal or textual influence claim. No meeting, correspondence, direct Lovejoy citation of Mauss, or Mauss citation of Lovejoy has yet been recovered in the current audit.

What the evidence does support is historically more precise:

> **Mauss and Lovejoy were successive participants in the same Léon Marillier-centered comparative-religion problem field at the EPHE. Marillier supplied Mauss with bibliographies and course notes; Mauss converted that research apparatus into a critique of Steinmetz and, with Hubert, into a social theory of sacrifice and magic. Lovejoy entered Marillier’s seminar immediately afterward, worked on the same Steinmetz/sacrifice/anthropophagy problem field, and later explicitly credited Marillier for the conclusions published as `Primitive Energetics`. The two trajectories overlap methodologically but diverge in how they abstract efficacy: Hubert/Mauss increasingly socialize it; Lovejoy increasingly causalizes it as impersonal, transferable, quasi-mechanical power.**

This relation has been **partly noticed in the historiography**, especially in histories of `mana`, but the documentary Marillier-mediated relation and the proposition-level 005 comparison appear not to have been reconstructed.

## 1. Institutional chronology: adjacent cohorts, not proven classmates

The EPHE prosopographical dictionary gives Mauss’s training sequence clearly:

- agrégation in philosophy, 1895;
- EPHE study scholarship, 1895–1897;
- courses with **Léon Marillier**, as well as Meillet, Foucher, Israël Lévi, Sylvain Lévi and Finot;
- Henri Hubert as condisciple and friend;
- mission in the Netherlands and England, 1897–1898;
- appointment at the EPHE from 1901 in the line of `Religions des peuples non civilisés`.

Lovejoy’s securely reconstructed Marillier participation is 1898–1899, when the official programme lists him as an active auditor in the conference. Thus Mauss and Lovejoy are best described as **successive / adjacent cohorts around Marillier**, not as documented classmates.

This matters because direct encounter is unnecessary for documentary continuity: Marillier himself is the stable institutional node.

## 2. The strongest material bridge is Marillier’s own paper apparatus

Mauss explicitly acknowledges, in the 1896–97 Steinmetz critique, that Marillier communicated to him:

- `sa bibliographie`;
- `les notes de ses différents cours`.

He credits these materials for a substantial third part of his research. The same essay also preserves narrower acknowledgments of references taken from Marillier’s course material.

This gives a concrete sequence:

> **Marillier working bibliography / course notes → Mauss research text.**

Lovejoy’s case must remain more cautious. We do not yet have a receipt or statement that Marillier handed Lovejoy the same papers. But 005 belongs to the later Marillier programme, contains the exact Steinmetz title `Endokannibalismus`, a dated `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June` slip, source packets, inserted notes, and sustained reclassification work.

The relation is therefore documentary at the level of **shared infrastructure and problem transmission**, not yet sheet identity.

## 3. Mauss learned from Marillier but also broke with him

The Mauss–Marillier relation is itself not a simple influence line.

Later Mauss explicitly said of his former teacher:

> `Avant tout, M. Marillier nous apprit la critique des faits.`

Modern reconstruction of Mauss’s 1902 assessment emphasizes that Marillier trained him in:

- severe criticism of ethnographic reports and observer position;
- examination and classification of facts;
- criticism of theories and interpretations;
- extensive comparative reading.

But Mauss then criticizes Marillier’s explanatory endpoint. In Mauss’s reconstruction, Marillier too often treated a religious fact as explained when reduced to a universal psychological law; Mauss instead moves toward the social/institutional explanation of religion.

This is crucial for Lovejoy. The common inheritance need not be doctrine. The most stable transferable object may have been **critical operations over facts**, which different students then attached to different explanatory ontologies.

## 4. Micro-level divergence: kin anthropophagy and `communion`

Mauss’s 1896–97 Steinmetz critique provides the cleanest local comparison with 005.

Mauss narrows Steinmetz’s `endocannibalisme` to `l’anthropophagie rituelle des parents`. In the reported logic of the rite, eating the relative transmits the `vertus du mort`; a hair, bodily fragment or drop of blood can represent the person. Mauss then classifies the practice explicitly as:

> `un simple acte de communion`.

Lovejoy’s 005 later works with almost the same transfer grammar but refuses to make it diagnostic of communion:

- PDF p.108: acquisition of strength/courage/intelligence through flesh or organs may belong to sympathetic magic rather than sacrifice/communion;
- PDF p.117: sacramental union is comparatively exceptional;
- PDF p.119: head/brain → intelligence; heart → courage/life; sexual organs → sexual power; transferable efficacy rather than sufficient evidence for sacramental cannibalism.

Thus the local relation is **not agreement**:

> **Mauss: transfer-through-kin-ingestion can constitute communion.**
>
> **Lovejoy: transfer-through-ingestion is not in itself evidence of communion; it may instantiate a separate efficacy mechanism.**

This is a proposition-level reclassification event inside a shared problem field.

## 5. Macro-level convergence: against a universal communion genealogy of sacrifice

The local disagreement should not be confused with their larger method.

Hubert and Mauss’s 1899 `Essai sur la nature et la fonction du sacrifice` explicitly attacks Robertson Smith’s genealogical derivation of sacrifice from original totemic communion. Their introduction stresses that the theory had grouped facts genealogically by perceived analogies instead of first analyzing the complexity of sacrificial systems. They treat their own theory as provisional and emphasize coordinated analysis of selected facts.

Most important for the Marillier link, a footnote identifies **Léon Marillier, `La place du totémisme dans l’évolution religieuse` (1897–98)** as one of the strongest critics of Robertson Smith.

This produces a striking 1897–99 triangle:

> **Marillier anti-unilateral critique → Hubert/Mauss explicitly cite it in their sacrifice theory**
>
> **Marillier seminar 1898–99 → Lovejoy’s 005 works through sacrifice/anthropophagy and repeatedly rejects formal resemblance as proof of common origin**.

At the macro level, therefore, Mauss/Hubert and Lovejoy converge in refusing a single communion genealogy, even though they may classify a particular transfer case differently.

This distinction is analytically useful:

- **micro taxonomy:** divergence;
- **macro evidentiary rule:** convergence.

## 6. 1904 versus 1906: a genuine fork in the abstraction of efficacy

The relation becomes clearer when Hubert/Mauss 1904 is compared with Lovejoy 1906.

### Hubert/Mauss

In `Esquisse d’une théorie générale de la magie`, mana and analogous forms of efficacy are mobilized to solve the problem of magical efficacy. Their theoretical destination is sociological:

- magic is a social fact;
- belief and efficacy are grounded in collective states / collective life;
- mana becomes a category enabling magical judgments and a representation produced within social classification.

### Lovejoy

In `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` (1906), Lovejoy’s destination is different:

- efficacy is impersonal and quasi-mechanical;
- power is communicable / transferable;
- the primitive conception works according to regular and intelligible laws;
- the abstraction becomes `primitive energetics` / `manitouism`;
- Lovejoy explicitly says the underlying conclusions had been reached in 1900 and strongly shaped by study under **Marillier**.

The cleanest formulation is therefore:

> **same Marillier/Codrington/mana problem region → different explanatory abstraction.**
>
> **Hubert/Mauss socialize efficacy; Lovejoy causalizes it.**

This is a heuristic contrast, not an assertion that either trajectory had one exclusive source or remained internally unchanged.

## 7. Historiography: yes, the theoretical adjacency has been noticed

It would be wrong to claim that no one has connected Lovejoy with Mauss/Hubert.

### Nicolas Meylan, 2017 — the strongest prior notice

Nicolas Meylan’s *Mana: A History of a Western Category*, especially chapter 3 (`Bringing Mana Within Theory`) and the transition into chapter 4, places Mauss/Hubert and Lovejoy in the same genealogy of mana.

Meylan does several things directly relevant here:

1. reconstructs Mauss’s training under Marillier and the likely Marillier/Codrington route by which mana entered Mauss’s research field;
2. treats Hubert/Mauss 1904 as an attempt to establish the **social nature** of magic and its efficacy;
3. then treats Lovejoy 1906 as a reconfiguration of mana into a primitive theory/praxis of **causality**;
4. explicitly notes that Lovejoy equated mana with orenda/manitou/ngai etc. but did **not** universalize across prestigious textual cultures in the same way as Hubert/Mauss;
5. characterizes Lovejoy as remaining more strongly within an intellectualist/Tylorian explanatory paradigm.

This is the closest existing secondary scholarship currently found to our question.

But Meylan’s object is the **history of mana as a Western scholarly category**. He does not reconstruct Lovejoy’s 005 notebook, Lovejoy’s active Marillier seminar participation, the Steinmetz dossier, or the Marillier note-circulation chain into Mauss.

### Earlier/later broad reception

Older histories of magic and `dynamism` already placed Lovejoy and Hubert/Mauss in the same general theoretical constellation. Hutton Webster’s 1948 *Magic: A Sociological Study* belongs to this reception history. Later, Jungian histories of mana likewise list Lovejoy, Mauss/Hubert, Lehmann, Söderblom and others as the literature from which `mana`/energy theory was assembled.

These are **genealogies of a concept**, not histories of a relationship.

## 8. Historiographical gap on the Lovejoy side

Daniel J. Wilson’s standard 1980 biography reconstructs Lovejoy’s 1898–99 Paris year as a period devoted chiefly to a historical approach to comparative religion. It names:

- Maurice Vernes;
- Jean Réville;
- Sylvain Lévi;
- the Bibliothèque Nationale and other libraries.

But the Paris reconstruction does not identify the Marillier EPHE seminar node that the archival programme and 005 now establish.

This helps explain why the Mauss adjacency has remained invisible in Lovejoy scholarship: if Marillier disappears from the standard Paris story, Mauss is almost impossible to see as a neighboring trajectory.

The current archival work therefore does not invent a previously unknown theoretical resemblance; it **changes the historical infrastructure beneath a resemblance already noticed by mana scholarship**.

## 9. Current direct-relationship audit

The current web/archive audit has **not yet located**:

- Mauss–Lovejoy correspondence;
- a documented meeting;
- a direct Lovejoy citation of Mauss in the 1898–1906 material currently collated;
- a direct Mauss citation of Lovejoy.

This is an archival negative, not proof of non-contact. Cataloguing of correspondence can be incomplete and citations may be buried in unpublished notes or reviews.

Therefore avoid:

> `Lovejoy learned X from Mauss.`

Prefer:

> `Lovejoy and Mauss occupied successive positions in a Marillier-centered problem field; the same controversies and documentary apparatus were being reworked into different explanatory forms.`

## 10. Strongest paper-level formulation

> **The relationship between Marcel Mauss and Arthur Lovejoy is presently best understood not as a demonstrated line of personal influence but as a Marillier-mediated adjacency. Mauss studied with Marillier in 1895–97, explicitly used Marillier’s bibliographies and course notes in his critique of Steinmetz, and with Hubert cited Marillier’s anti-unilateral critique in the 1899 theory of sacrifice. Lovejoy entered Marillier’s conference in 1898–99 as an active participant; his notebook 005 records the same Steinmetz and sacrificial problem field, but reclassifies some of its central cases. At the micro level Mauss could call kin endocannibalistic transfer `communion`, whereas Lovejoy separated bodily transfer from communion as sympathetic efficacy; at the macro level both trajectories resisted deriving heterogeneous sacrifices from a single primordial communion. By 1904–06 their abstractions diverged further: Hubert and Mauss grounded efficacy in collective life, while Lovejoy—who explicitly credited Marillier for the conclusions reached around 1900—abstracted transferable efficacy into `primitive energetics`. Historians of mana, above all Nicolas Meylan, have noticed the theoretical adjacency; what appears new is the archival reconstruction of the Marillier-mediated documentary and seminar infrastructure linking the two trajectories.**

## 11. Evidence limits and next tests

1. No direct meeting/correspondence claim until a letter, diary or registration/attendance record proves it.
2. No direct reading claim until Lovejoy cites Mauss/Hubert or a manuscript source-order/textual match establishes use.
3. Audit the full Lovejoy correspondence index / JHU MS38 for `Mauss`, `Hubert`, `Durkheim`, `Marillier`, `Bouglé` and `L’Année sociologique`.
4. Search Mauss correspondence and review corpus for `Lovejoy`, especially after the 1906 *Monist* article.
5. Search *L’Année sociologique* bibliographic reviews for Lovejoy’s 1906 article or adjacent American `mana` / `manitou` literature.
6. Date the appearance and Paris availability of *L’Année sociologique* vol. II closely enough to determine whether the Hubert/Mauss sacrifice essay was physically available during the later part of Lovejoy’s 1898–99 stay. Availability would still not prove reading.
7. Recover Marillier’s own RHR 34 (1896), pp.113–115 review of Steinmetz to establish the missing intermediate classification.
