# Batch 139 — Irving Babbitt as a concrete Harvard-to-Lévi Pāli transmission control before Lovejoy

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced; corrected against the existing 004 source-uptake map  
Scope: test how Lovejoy could have known to expect Pāli/Buddhist study with Sylvain Lévi in Paris, and whether a resident Harvard figure had recently followed that route before Lovejoy's graduate years.

## Core result

Irving Babbitt is the first concrete Harvard-based transmission control that fits the chronology closely enough to matter.

Harvard University Archives states that Babbitt, after Harvard and two years teaching classics in Montana, went to Paris for postgraduate study in **Pāli literature and Buddhist traditions at the Écoles des Hautes-Études**. He returned to the United States, received a Harvard A.M. in 1893 in classical literature and ancient Eastern languages, and returned to Harvard in **1894** as an Instructor of French. A Presses de l'Inalco scholarly reconstruction makes the teacher edge explicit: Babbitt spent a year at EPHE **under Sylvain Lévi**, studying **Pāli and Sanskrit**.

This overlaps Lovejoy's Harvard graduate period in a way Stickney's chronology does not. By 1895–97, when Lovejoy was doing Sanskrit/Buddhist work under Charles R. Lanman, Babbitt was already a resident Harvard instructor who had himself recently gone to Paris for Pāli/Buddhist study in the Lévi network.

The bounded conclusion is:

> **A concrete Harvard-to-Lévi Pāli transmission route existed on campus before Lovejoy left for Paris.**

No current document shows that Babbitt recommended Lévi to Lovejoy, that Lovejoy took Babbitt's course, or that Babbitt formally advised him. Network topology is established; the interpersonal transmission event is not.

## 1. Harvard's archive gives the Pāli/Buddhist Paris trajectory

The current HOLLIS finding aid for **Papers of Irving Babbitt, HUG 1185** states that Babbitt left Montana to pursue postgraduate studies in `Pali literature and Buddhist traditions` at the Écoles des Hautes-Études in Paris. It then records his return, Harvard A.M. in 1893, and return to Harvard in 1894 as Instructor of French.

Source:
https://hollisarchives.lib.harvard.edu/catalog/hua10004

HOLLIS itself does not name Lévi in this biographical paragraph. The specific teacher edge currently comes from a Presses de l'Inalco scholarly chapter, which states that Babbitt studied Pāli and Sanskrit at EPHE under Sylvain Lévi:

https://books.openedition.org/pressesinalco/43480?lang=en

The route is therefore:

`American/Harvard classicist → EPHE Paris → Lévi → Pāli/Sanskrit → return to Harvard`.

The teacher edge should remain `strong scholarly reconstruction` until a contemporary Babbitt note, Paris registration record or letter naming Lévi is directly recovered.

---

## 2. Babbitt was actively teaching at Harvard during Lovejoy's period

A contemporary Harvard Crimson announcement for 1895–96 lists **Irving Babbitt, instructor in French** and says he would conduct **French A**:

https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1895/6/8/radcliffe-elective-pamphlet/

Thus Babbitt was not merely an alumnus somewhere in the American scholarly field. He was physically/institutionally back at Harvard from 1894 and teaching during Lovejoy's graduate period.

This is only co-presence. There is no current evidence that Lovejoy enrolled in French A or that the two discussed Paris.

---

## 3. Lanman's Harvard environment already joined Pāli, Sanskrit, Buddhism and Sāṃkhya

On **29 April 1896**, Lanman gave the first of three public Harvard lectures on Buddhism. The Crimson says its abstract came from Lanman's outline with bibliographical notes. The lecture explicitly covered:

- Buddhism in relation to Vedism/Brahmanism;
- **Sāṃkhya and Vedānta**;
- northern and southern Buddhist literature;
- **Sanskrit and Pāli**;
- Tipiṭaka, Five Nikāyas, Pali Text Society and the Siamese edition.

Source:
https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1896/4/29/lecture-on-buddhism-the-first-of/

This closely matches the problem field later visible in notebook 004. Lovejoy's attendance at this public series remains unproved; its evidentiary value here is environmental/curricular.

---

## 4. Warren 1896 is stronger than an environmental control: Lovejoy actually used it

Also in 1896, Henry Clarke Warren's *Buddhism in Translations* appeared as **Harvard Oriental Series III**, edited by Lanman, translated from original Pāli materials and dedicated to Lanman as Warren's friend and teacher.

Primary digital witness:
https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Translations

Full-volume control:
https://books.google.com/books/about/Buddhism_in_Translations.html?id=CeIOAAAAYAAJ

Crucially, the existing repo work already proves this was not merely part of Lovejoy's surroundings. `MS38_004_source_uptake_map_v7.csv` identifies **direct Warren uptake in notebook 004**:

- notebook p.65: Warren Chapter II introduction is part of the exact source field for Lovejoy's discussion of Buddhist refusal of speculative questions and the `purely practical aim` problem;
- notebook pp.71–105: Warren is among the major mediated sources in the term-by-term dependent-origination apparatus;
- Warren pp.115–116 supply a highly specific upstream `patchwork` hypothesis: the full dependent-origination formula appears composite because the sentient being is generated twice, and older formulas may have been combined or modified.

Therefore the Harvard Pāli-Buddhist production network entered Lovejoy's **actual research apparatus**. This is a materially stronger statement than `a relevant book was published around him`.

The source map also imposes the correct caution: not every decomposition or compositeness claim in 004 is Lovejoy's invention, and not every related proposition should be attributed uniquely to Warren. The source-transformations are already mapped separately.

## 6. Babbitt's Pāli project was durable

Babbitt's posthumous *The Dhammapada: translated from the Pāli with an essay on Buddha and the Occident* appeared in 1936, while the Harvard archive preserves its manuscript predecessor. This shows that his Paris Pāli interest persisted as a long-running intellectual project rather than disappearing after a study-abroad year.

That fact increases the plausibility that Babbitt's Paris training remained part of his recognizable scholarly profile at Harvard. It still tells us nothing by itself about a conversation with Lovejoy in the 1890s.

---

## 7. The transmission question is now finite

Before this batch, possible explanations for Lovejoy's expectation of Pāli study with Lévi were diffuse: Lanman advice, printed Paris programmes, American students already in Paris, or general scholarly reputation.

Babbitt supplies a concrete campus route:

`Babbitt studies Pāli/Buddhism at EPHE/Lévi`

→ `returns and teaches at Harvard from 1894`

→ `Lovejoy enters Harvard graduate study in 1895 and directly uses Harvard Pāli scholarship such as Warren 1896`

→ `Lovejoy later goes to Paris expecting Pāli/Buddhist study with Lévi`.

Only the first, second and fourth institutional/source nodes are established. The Babbitt→Lovejoy transmission arrow remains unproved.

The highest-value searches are now:

1. Lovejoy's Harvard course/adviser records and correspondence for `Babbitt`;
2. Babbitt's student/graduate-study materials for `Lévi`, `Lanman`, Paris and Pāli;
3. Lanman correspondence for Babbitt and Lovejoy within the same advising network;
4. any printed Harvard/EPHE notice that explicitly linked Babbitt's Paris training to Lévi and circulated before Lovejoy left for France.

---

## Paper-level implication

> **Lovejoy did not leave a Harvard world in which Pāli Buddhism and Sylvain Lévi were remote names. Babbitt, back on Harvard's faculty from 1894, had himself pursued Pāli and Buddhist study at the École pratique des hautes études and is identified by institutional scholarship as a student of Lévi; Lanman's Harvard programme publicly connected Sanskrit/Pāli Buddhism to Sāṃkhya and Vedānta; and Warren's new Harvard Pāli volume entered Lovejoy's notebook directly. This does not establish who recommended Lévi to Lovejoy, but it converts the route by which such an expectation could circulate from an abstract possibility into a concrete Harvard network problem.**

## Evidence cautions

1. HOLLIS directly confirms Babbitt's Pāli/Buddhist EPHE study; the specific `under Lévi` edge currently rests on scholarly reconstruction.
2. Babbitt and Lovejoy had Harvard co-presence, but no 1890s contact/advising edge has been found.
3. Babbitt taught French; do not assume Lovejoy took his course.
4. Lanman's 1896 public lecture series is environmental unless attendance evidence appears.
5. **Warren 1896 is a confirmed Lovejoy source in 004**, not merely an environmental control.
6. HUG 1185 exposes no digital content and requires donor permission.
