# Haddock 1974 in *Political Theory*: `history of ideas` as a live method-aggregation problem after the Skinner symposium

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT ARTICLE METADATA + DIRECT REFERENCE ARCHITECTURE + OPENING TEXT CONTROL / FULL ARGUMENT STILL PARTLY PAYWALLED**

## Core result

A new venue-level control sharply weakens any chronology in which the new journal *Political Theory* simply institutionalized Skinnerian contextualism after displacing an older `history of ideas` field.

Only three months after *Political Theory* 2.3 opened with its dedicated August 1974 symposium on Quentin Skinner, volume 2.4 (November 1974) published:

B. A. Haddock, `The History of Ideas and the Study of Politics`, pp.420–431.

The article's direct reference architecture places in one methodological problem-space:

- Maurice Mandelbaum 1965, `The History of Ideas, Intellectual History and the History of Philosophy`, *History and Theory*;
- John Passmore 1965, *History and Theory*;
- John Dunn, `The Identity of the History of Ideas`;
- J. G. A. Pocock 1962 + *Politics, Language and Time*;
- Quentin Skinner, `Social Meaning and the Explanation of Social Action`;
- Bhikhu Parekh and R. N. Berki's 1973 JHI critique of Skinner;
- Collingwood, Wittgenstein, Winch, Taylor and others.

Thus, in the immediate aftermath of the Skinner symposium, the new political-theory venue treated `history of ideas` not as a dead predecessor label but as a **live jurisdictional and methodological problem** to be reconstructed across several competing traditions.

The safe institutional conclusion is:

`SKINNER SYMPOSIUM`
+
`HISTORY-OF-IDEAS METHOD ARTICLE`
within the same journal/year
=
`METHOD AGGREGATION / CONTINUING DISPUTE`,
not
`METHOD REPLACEMENT`.

The issue itself juxtaposes multiple styles of political thought/history rather than presenting one authorized method.

This continues the journal's inaugural architecture of conceptual analysis, political discourse, normative theory and historical political thought within one venue.

---

## II. Haddock begins from a jurisdictional crisis in politics

The recoverable opening states that recent years have cast a `cloud of methodological confusion` over the study of politics.

He describes an older uneasy compromise between:

- study of political institutions;
- study of selected classic texts;

then says sociological techniques made that compromise harder to sustain. The result was a widespread view that `politics` might lack disciplinary unity and amount to the application of several disciplines to an ambiguous subject-matter.

This framing is important because Haddock's problem is not narrowly:

`WHICH TEXTUAL INTERPRETIVE METHOD IS BEST?`

It is:

`WHAT GIVES THE STUDY OF POLITICS INTELLECTUAL / DISCIPLINARY UNITY?`

and what role can history of ideas play in that unity.

This aligns closely with CSPT's 1967 institutional crisis and Richter's later claim that political theory lacks an uncontested definition, without proving direct causal dependence.

## IV. Parekh/Berki remain visible inside the new venue's methodological discourse

This is important for the canonical-compression branch.

Parekh/Berki 1973 is often less visible in later Skinner anthologies, but Haddock's 1974 article directly cites their JHI critique while formulating the relation between history of ideas and politics.

Thus in the mid-1970s their critique had already travelled beyond JHI into the new *Political Theory* venue.

That means later reduced visibility cannot be explained by immediate contemporaneous irrelevance.

The trajectory is:

`JHI 1973 PAREKH/BERKI`
-> direct use in `POLITICAL THEORY 1974 HADDOCK`
+
`SKINNER PT 1974 RESPONSE`
-> `BERKI/PAREKH PT 1976`.

This strengthens the claim that the debate ecology was reciprocal and cross-venue before later anthology canonization narrowed its portable critic set.

## V. Relation to Richter/CSPT

No direct evidence currently proves Richter commissioned or influenced Haddock's article.

However, institutional context is now unusually dense:

- Richter co-founded CSPT in 1967;
- *Political Theory* emerged from the Columbia CSPT chapter ecology;
- Richter served on *Political Theory*'s editorial board from 1972;
- the journal explicitly sought to build bridges among political-philosophy approaches;
- Haddock 1974 aggregates history-of-ideas / Cambridge / H&T / JHI method debates;
- Richter's own later work performs increasingly explicit methodological translation among these traditions.

Safe formulation:

`HADDOCK ARTICLE FITS THE BRIDGE-VENUE ARCHITECTURE`.

Hold:

`RICHTER CAUSED / COMMISSIONED THE ARTICLE`.

## VI. Formal-role correction for Richter

Reliable institutional/biographical controls list Richter as a *Political Theory* editorial-board member beginning in 1972.

Some later obituaries/retrospectives use looser language suggesting he helped found the journal, but the formal founding editors are Robert D. Lamb and V. Walter Odajnyk.

Therefore use:

`CSPT COFOUNDER + EARLY POLITICAL THEORY EDITORIAL-BOARD MEMBER`

rather than

`FORMAL FOUNDING EDITOR OF POLITICAL THEORY`.

This is another relation-specific institutional control:

`INSTITUTIONAL GENEALOGY / NETWORK ROLE`
!=
`FORMAL EDITORIAL OFFICE`.
