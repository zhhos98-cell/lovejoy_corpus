# Batch 50 — 005: Mauss–Marett–Lovejoy silent convergence and the social/causal fork

## Research question

Can the relation between Lovejoy's 1906 `primitive energetics` and the Mauss/Année sociologique mana programme be made historically sharper without claiming a direct Mauss–Lovejoy contact that we still cannot document?

## New primary-source control: L'Année sociologique, tome XI (1906–1909)

A complete public-domain scan of *L'Année sociologique*, tome XI (covering 1906–1909; publication metadata 1910) is available via Wikimedia Commons / Internet Archive:

- Wikimedia Commons: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:L%27ann%C3%A9e_sociologique,_tome_11,_1906-1909.djvu
- Internet Archive identifier: `lannee-sociologique-1906-1909`
- Searchable OCR used for rapid full-volume checking: https://fr.scribd.com/document/45924385/Annee-sociologique-1906-09

The searchable OCR returns **no match** for `Lovejoy`, `The Monist`, or `Primitive Energetics`. This is useful negative reception evidence, but only in a restricted sense: it shows no obvious indexed/reviewed appearance of Lovejoy's 1906 article in this volume. It does **not** prove that Mauss or another collaborator had never seen the article; OCR noise and editorial selectivity remain real controls.

## The key positive evidence: Marett reviewed in exactly the conceptual zone

In the same volume, the religion section reviews R. R. Marett, *The Threshold of Religion* (London, 1909). The review is signed `M. M.` and belongs to the sequence of religion reviews conventionally attributable to Marcel Mauss.

The review says that Marett treats mana as approximately equivalent to the idea of **`pouvoir, d'efficace`**, and as a kind of **`catégorie subconsciente de la philosophie primitive`**. It then states: **`Nous nous félicitons de constater cette parfaite concordance avec les théories que nous professons.`** The qualification immediately following is decisive: Marett does not attach sufficient importance to the relation of mana/orenda to **`valeur et d'autorité sociale`**.

This creates an unusually clean comparative control for Lovejoy 1906. Lovejoy had already defined the mana-family in terms of efficacy/power/productivity and made it the central category of a `primitive philosophy`, but his explanatory endpoint was causal regularity: an impersonal/quasi-mechanical, communicable efficacy working according to regular and intelligible laws — `primitive energetics`.

So the overlap is not merely a retrospective similarity between two vocabularies. Both Lovejoy and the Année/Mauss review can be placed against a **shared third interlocutor, Marett**, and both focus on the same semantic cluster: mana = power / efficacy + primitive philosophy. The divergence is then explicit rather than inferred:

- **Lovejoy:** efficacy is stabilized as a causal category; the analytic pressure falls on regularity, transmissibility, quasi-mechanical operation, and the genealogy of causation.
- **Mauss / Année:** efficacy is accepted, but the analytic pressure falls on `valeur` and `autorité sociale`; the force-category requires a social explanation.

This is the strongest form yet of the paper's proposed macro-fork: **Lovejoy causalizes efficacy; Mauss socializes efficacy.**

## A second, immediately adjacent Mauss control: the Wundt review

The Marett notice directly follows a long review of Wilhelm Wundt's *Völkerpsychologie* (volumes on myth and religion), also signed `M. M.`. The transition is analytically important.

The reviewer criticizes Wundt for failing to grasp the `vagues énergies`, the `forces anonymes et impersonnelles` that constitute the substance of religious life. Wundt, as represented in the review, treats such forces as insufficiently religious because they differ little from forces studied by science and manipulated by secular technique — therefore as something like `prodromes de science`. The reviewer rejects that move: the fact that religious conceptions later helped form scientific conceptions does not make them nonreligious in origin.

This puts the difference from Lovejoy in almost laboratory conditions. Lovejoy's 1906 article uses the same family of impersonal, quasi-mechanical efficacy to reconstruct a primitive theory/praxis of causation behind what later differentiates into religion and science. The Année review, by contrast, explicitly resists allowing the science-like quality of impersonal force to remove it from religion and relocates its explanatory ground in collective/social life.

## What this does to the Mauss–Lovejoy relationship problem

We still have no documentary warrant for `Mauss read Lovejoy`, `Lovejoy read Mauss`, correspondence, or a meeting. The best relationship model remains **Marillier-mediated adjacency**, not direct exchange.

But the absence of direct contact is now less damaging analytically, because the comparison can be triangulated through a common live problem and a common interlocutor:

`Marillier workshop / mana problem` → `Lovejoy 1906` ↘

`Marett's mana / preanimism` → competing receptions → **causal regularity (Lovejoy)** vs **social value/authority (Mauss)**

This is stronger than saying that two scholars independently used the word `mana`. It reconstructs a **shared problem-space with a discriminating proposition**: what kind of efficacy is mana, and what ultimately explains that efficacy?

## Reception asymmetry: useful but must remain cautious

The same Année volume visibly reviews Marett in detail, while its searchable OCR contains no obvious `Lovejoy` or `The Monist`. That suggests a reception asymmetry: Marett was a visible interlocutor inside the Année review network while Lovejoy was not, at least in tome XI. This may help explain why the later historiography of mana often places Lovejoy beside the Durkheimian line retrospectively without a documented contemporaneous exchange.

Do **not** convert this into a claim of deliberate omission or ignorance. The correct wording is: **no visible reception in this volume has yet been found**.

## Historiographical control

Nicolas Meylan, *Mana: A History of a Western Category* (2017), independently confirms the theoretical contrast: Lovejoy attacks Marett's `supernormal` emphasis, defines mana as the efficacious/powerful/productive, and develops `primitive energetics` as a universal causality category. Meylan places this against Hubert–Mauss and later Durkheimian treatments. The documentary novelty here is therefore not the abstract contrast itself, but the combination of:

1. the Marillier-mediated paper/workshop genealogy established in Batches 43–49;
2. the proposition-level reclassification evidence in 005;
3. the **shared-Marett triangular control** from *L'Année sociologique* XI;
4. the negative reception check for Lovejoy in the same review volume.

## Paper-ready compression

> Lovejoy and Mauss need not be made direct interlocutors to recover their divergence. In 1906 Lovejoy redescribed mana as the efficacious and productive principle of a primitive causal philosophy, `primitive energetics`. A few years later, reviewing Marett's *Threshold of Religion*, the Année sociologique accepted almost the same description of mana as `pouvoir`, `efficace`, and a category of primitive philosophy, but immediately insisted on its relation to `valeur et autorité sociale`. The fork is exact: Lovejoy made efficacy legible as causal regularity; Mauss made efficacy legible as social authority. The same Année volume has no visible indexed notice of Lovejoy, so this should be treated as a structured convergence within a shared problem-space rather than as direct exchange.
