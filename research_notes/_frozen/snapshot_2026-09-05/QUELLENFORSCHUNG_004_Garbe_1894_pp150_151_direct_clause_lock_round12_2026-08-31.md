## Core result

The notebook's opening extended `Garbe p.150` passage is not merely a general paraphrase of Sāṃkhya epistemology. Its argumentative spine follows Garbe 1894 pp.150–151 very closely and should be coded as a **close English translation/excerpt of Garbe**.

The first-edition table of contents explicitly places `4. Die Methode` at p.150.

Garbe p.150 begins by saying that all Indian systems show genuinely philosophical sense by accounting for their accepted sources of knowledge (`pramāṇa`). He then says that the orthodox systems show the greatest agreement in their treatment of inference: terminology, definitions, details and examples are nearly everywhere the same because Vaiśeṣika–Nyāya developed this field to its highest Indian form and the other schools adopted it.

At the p.150→151 break Garbe then writes, in substance:

- where Sāṃkhya texts discuss inference at length, this is a `fremdes Element`;
- Indian scholarly taste required the discussion;
- a **European exposition of Sāṃkhya philosophy need take notice of it only insofar as it is important for the method of the system**;
- Sāṃkhya recognizes three sources of knowledge: perception, inference, reliable communication/testimony.

These are precisely the propositions compressed into Lovejoy's notebook opening.

## Further first-edition control

The 1894 preface is itself source-critically relevant. Garbe says:

- he used the surviving source material insofar as it mattered for understanding the system and its history;
- he avoided European philosophical analogies because they risk obscuring the characteristic individuality of the Indian system;
- he kept close to the wording of sources where compatible with clarity;
- his **arrangement of the material is entirely non-Indian**, because the original works did not provide a model for an orderly exposition.

This should be treated as a source-environment control, not automatically as Lovejoy uptake beyond the documented pp.150–151 excerpt.

It is nevertheless striking for Quellenforschung because Garbe explicitly distinguishes:

`SOURCE WORDING`
from
`SCHOLARLY ARRANGEMENT`.
