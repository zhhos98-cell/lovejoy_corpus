# Quellenforschung Round 15 addendum — Marillier 1898–99: prospective programme ≠ retrospective reported schedule

Date: 2026-08-31  
Status: **CORRECTS ROUND-15 MATRIX ROW 005-04 AND ALL EARLIER “INDEPENDENT TIMETABLE ALIGNMENT” LANGUAGE**

## Core correction

A hostile recheck of the EPHE annuals reveals a documentary discrepancy that earlier repo prose flattened.

The **prospective programme for the academic year 1898–1899**, printed in the preceding annual, lists Marillier's courses as:

- `Les croyances relatives à la survivance de l'âme` — **Wednesdays at 5 p.m.**;
- `L'anthropophagie rituelle et les sacrifices humains` — **Fridays at 9:30 a.m.**.

Source: *Programme des conférences pour l'année 1898-1899*, EPHE Section des sciences religieuses, programme printed in the report for 1897–98.

By contrast, the **retrospective report on the conferences actually given in exercise 1898–1899** labels them:

- `Conférence du lundi` — survivance / amoral eschatology;
- `Conférence du mardi` — human sacrifice / ritual anthropophagy.

The same retrospective page names Lovejoy among `Auditeurs ayant pris une part active aux travaux`.

Notebook dates:

- 29 May 1899 = **Monday**;
- 6 June 1899 = **Tuesday**;
- 12 June 1899 = **Monday**.

Therefore the notebook dates align with the **retrospective reported days**, not with the previously announced programme.

This is a better documentary result than the earlier simplified statement, but it must be narrated correctly.

---

## 1. Why the discrepancy should not be silently harmonized

The two EPHE documents have different documentary functions:

`PROSPECTIVE PROGRAMME`
= intended timetable before/during the coming academic year;

`RETROSPECTIVE COMPTE RENDU`
= report of the conferences after the exercise.

A philological reconstruction must preserve the disagreement rather than selecting one and suppressing the other.

Possible explanations include:

- timetable changes after the programme was printed;
- rescheduling during the year;
- a reporting convention or error in one of the annuals.

The currently available documents do not prove which mechanism produced the discrepancy.

Thus:

> **planned schedule ≠ reported actual schedule**

is secure;

> **the timetable was formally changed on date X**

is not established.

---

## 2. Comparative control from the following academic year

The discrepancy is not unique to 1898–99.

The prospective **programme for 1899–1900** lists:

- cult of the dead / condition of souls — Wednesday at 5;
- human sacrifice / ritual anthropophagy — Tuesday at 9:30.

The later retrospective **report for exercise 1899–1900** instead labels:

- first conference — **Monday**, human sacrifice / ritual anthropophagy, especially agrarian sacrifice, magical/fecundating blood, expiatory rites, Frazer;
- second conference — **Wednesday**, afterlife/death, amoral eschatology, Christian interpolations in American myths.

This second programme/report mismatch makes it more plausible that the retrospective report records a timetable that differed from the original announcement rather than the 1898–99 discrepancy being a unique accidental anomaly. It still does not by itself tell us when or why the timetable changed.

Do not use:

> `The notebook dates independently confirm the published timetable.`

because there is no single consistent published timetable.

## 4. Consequence for the 005 reading-path reconstruction

The three dates remain important:

- Monday 29 May — survivance/moral-eschatology material;
- Tuesday 6 June — `Sac. June 6.` and sacrifice/Africa material;
- Monday 12 June — explicit `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June` slip.

Their topic/day pairing matches the retrospective 1898–99 conference report strikingly well.

The correct historical inference is therefore:

`DATED NOTEBOOK TOPIC + WEEKDAY`
↔
`RETROSPECTIVE EPHE CONFERENCE DAY + TOPIC`
+
`LOVEJOY NAMED ACTIVE AUDITOR`
→ **SESSION-LINKED STRATUM STRONGLY SUPPORTED**.

But:

`SESSION-LINKED`
≠
`VERBATIM LECTURE NOTES`
≠
`EVERY SURROUNDING PAGE DATED TO THAT SESSION`
≠
`PROSPECTIVE PROGRAMME WAS FOLLOWED AS PRINTED`.
