# Lovejoy — genealogy jurisprudence: `Monboddo and Rousseau` vs `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism`, 1933–1948

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **CANONICAL POSITIVE/BORDERLINE GENEALOGY CONTROL / INFLUENCE-THRESHOLD RECONSTRUCTION**

## Governing question

The identity-jurisprudence branch has established that Lovejoy can judge two formulations conceptually the same or sufficiently similar while leaving their historical/genetic relation open. The next question is therefore:

> **What additional evidence moves Lovejoy from content similarity to a positive historical influence/origin claim?**

Two essays published in 1933 provide an unusually clean contrast:

- `Monboddo and Rousseau`: strong content similarity, chronological priority, access/reading, and actor acknowledgment are present, yet Lovejoy refuses to close the direct-influence question;
- `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism`: Lovejoy makes a positive transmission/origin argument by reconstructing documentary carriers, uptake, repetition, and downstream propagation of a representation of Chinese aesthetic practice.

The contrast suggests that Lovejoy's actual genealogy threshold is significantly higher than `similarity + priority`.

The current best reconstruction is:

> **content similarity + chronological availability are only candidate-generation devices; positive genealogy requires evidence of a historically operative carrier or pathway, uptake/use, and enough control of competing routes to make the causal attribution probative.**

This is an analyst reconstruction from Lovejoy's case law, not a formal criterion stated by Lovejoy.

# I. Borderline case — `Monboddo and Rousseau`

Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Monboddo and Rousseau`, *Modern Philology* 30.3 (1933), 275–296.

## Evidence Lovejoy possesses

Lovejoy identifies a connected group of theses in both Rousseau and Monboddo. The similarities are substantial enough for a genuine intellectual-identity comparison.

The causal case also contains several facts which could tempt a historian into an influence claim:

1. **chronological priority** — Rousseau's *Second Discourse* precedes Monboddo's mature treatment;
2. **access / reading** — Monboddo had read Rousseau before writing the relevant work;
3. **close textual/content similarity** — some passages and connected doctrines are highly similar;
4. **actor acknowledgment** — Monboddo explicitly recognizes agreement with Rousseau and praises Rousseau;
5. **plausible direction** — Rousseau is temporally prior.

Yet Lovejoy still says the causal question cannot be answered with certainty.

## Why he refuses closure

Alternative causal routes remain live:

- independent/spontaneous recurrence of the same thoughts;
- common ancient/classical sources (Epicurean accounts, Lucretius, Cicero, Horace, etc.);
- common exposure to contemporary comparative anatomy;
- common travel/ethnographic materials;
- broader shared intellectual environment.

Thus:

`similarity`
+ `priority`
+ `availability`
+ `known reading`
+ `actor acknowledgment`
↛ **direct influence**

when alternative routes remain historically adequate.

### Critical inference

Lovejoy does not use actor acknowledgment as a magic genealogical key. An actor can acknowledge agreement while claiming independent discovery, and the historian still has to test causal alternatives.

This is a high-value negative/borderline influence control.

# II. Positive case — `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism`

Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism`, *Journal of English and Germanic Philology* 32.1 (1933), 1–20; revised and expanded in *Essays in the History of Ideas* (1948), 99–135.

## Clarify the causal object

The essay should not be read as primarily proving the ethnographic authenticity of a timeless `Chinese aesthetic` from Chinese-language primary materials.

The causal object is more precise:

> **European representations of China and Chinese gardening, carried through travelers, missionaries, translations, essays, aesthetic discourse, and garden theory, and then used by European historical actors as evidence/authority for irregular or non-geometrical beauty.**

The relevant historical genealogy is therefore **carrier-indexed reception history**.

This distinction matters because:

`actual cultural/source authenticity`
≠ `historical efficacy of a representation`.

A representation can be inaccurate as ethnography and still be causally effective if historical actors accepted it as Chinese and mobilized it in argument or practice.

---

# III. The positive transmission chain

The exact chain is complex and should not be reduced to one named great thinker.

The 1933/1948 essay reconstructs, among other things:

- early European voyager/missionary reports about China;
- translations and republication across European languages;
- seventeenth-century European admiration for Chinese government/civilization;
- Sir William Temple's explicit presentation of Chinese gardening as an alternative to European symmetry/order;
- Temple's `sharawadgi` passage and the contrast between geometrical order and an irregular beauty not easily reducible to visible plan;
- Joseph Addison's later description, which Lovejoy/related contemporary scholarship identifies as drawing heavily and nearly literally from Temple without acknowledgment;
- Alexander Pope and other early eighteenth-century participants in the anti-regular gardening discourse;
- later explicit use of `Chinese` irregularity and `sharawadgi` by figures such as Horace Walpole;
- Sir William Chambers as a major later propagandist of Chinese gardening;
- later modification and eventual decline of the Chinese garden as an arbiter of taste even while the irregular/picturesque aesthetic survived in altered configurations.

The structure is therefore not:

`China resembles English romanticism`
→ `China caused romanticism`.

It is closer to:

`representation/document`
→ `translation/circulation`
→ `explicit actor attribution to China`
→ `uptake in European aesthetic argument`
→ `textual reuse / reformulation`
→ `propagation through additional writers/practitioners`
→ `changed aesthetic norm and new configurations`.

This is a much thicker historical relation than mere conceptual resemblance.

# V. A new firewall: source authenticity ≠ historical efficacy

The China case adds a relation-type distinction absent from a simple influence model.

The historical proposition can be:

> European actors believed or represented practice X as Chinese, and that attribution influenced their aesthetic argument.

This can be true even if a later scholar finds that:

- `sharawadgi` is not actually Chinese in origin;
- reports distorted Chinese gardens;
- the aesthetic abstraction was substantially European;
- the purported `Chinese` model was already mediated through European categories.

Thus:

`ethnographic/source authenticity`
≠ `actor attribution`
≠ `historical causal efficacy`.

For a history of European ideas, actor attribution plus demonstrable uptake can be historically causal even when external source attribution is false or distorted.

This is closely aligned with the branch's broader rule:

> **do not allow one established relation to settle another without a new warrant.**

# VI. 1933 → 1948 revision: genealogy is evidence-responsive

Nico Mouton's direct comparison of the 1933 and 1948 versions identifies a particularly important methodological revision.

## 1933 formulation

Lovejoy gives Montaigne a strong nodal role in the European vogue for admiration of China, writing that Montaigne `began the fashion` shortly after the establishment of the Jesuit mission in Peking.

## 1948 revision

After additional evidence, Lovejoy removes this strong Montaigne-first formulation and emphasizes earlier reports by voyagers and missionaries. He notes that admiration for Chinese political institutions was apparently already commonplace by around 1590 and then treats Montaigne's posthumously published China paragraph as a response to a missionary report that had circulated in translation.

### Methodological consequence

New evidence changes not merely the quantity of background detail but the **allocation of causal weight within the chain**:

`Montaigne as originator/node`
→ downgraded to
`Montaigne as a visible receiver/reuser of earlier circulating reports`.

This is a rare direct revision-case showing Lovejoy's genealogy as revisable under new carrier evidence.

It supports a historical model in which causal nodes are not fixed by canon or fame; anonymous or lower-status carriers can replace a canonical author when documentary chronology demands it.

# VII. Reconstructed influence threshold — provisional

From the two 1933 cases, a positive Lovejoyian genealogy appears to require several distinct upgrades.

## G0 — conceptual resemblance

A and B share content, motif, proposition, pattern, or function.

Status: **candidate only**.

## G1 — chronological possibility

A or its carrier exists early enough to affect B.

Status: **necessary control, not proof**.

## G2 — availability/access

B could plausibly encounter A, and preferably did encounter the relevant carrier.

Status: **stronger possibility, still not sufficient**.

## G3 — diagnostic uptake

B's text/practice shows evidence of reception, reuse, distinctive borrowing, explicit attribution, or transformation of A/carrier.

Status: **substantially stronger genealogical evidence**.

## G4 — alternative-route control

Common sources, independent recurrence, and other plausible routes are tested rather than silently ignored.

Status: **required before strong direct-influence closure**.

## G5 — serial historical operation

The element/representation can be followed through additional carriers, transformations, and downstream uses.

Status: **supports a broader history of influence/circulation rather than an isolated dyadic borrowing claim**.

This G0–G5 ladder is an analyst reconstruction. Lovejoy does not present these numbered stages.

# VIII. Relation to Skinner's later influence test

The branch already records that Skinner's 1969 discussion of influence demands, roughly:

- genuine similarity;
- inability to obtain the doctrine from another source;
- low probability of independent/random generation.

The Monboddo/Rousseau case is structurally striking because Lovejoy explicitly refuses direct-influence closure for precisely the kinds of reasons Skinner later formalizes: common sources and independent recurrence remain viable.

The China case adds a positive side which Skinner's minimalist conditions do not by themselves exhaust: **carrier, reception, reuse, and serial propagation**.

Do not claim Skinner derived this from Lovejoy. The comparison is structural unless direct transmission evidence is found.
