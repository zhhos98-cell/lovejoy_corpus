## Why this closure exists

The remaining live question was whether the Baltimore Lovejoy–Carnap encounter on 31 December 1935 might coincide with, or even trigger, Carnap's technical liberalization of verificationism. The last research round closes that issue sufficiently for the current Lovejoy project.

Current result:

> **Carnap's liberalization and the key technical move from definability/translation to non-eliminative reduction were already underway before Baltimore and had become explicit by the Paris Unity of Science congress in September 1935. Baltimore is therefore a public boundary encounter inside an already developing architecture, not the origin of Carnap's shift.**

The exact internal composition chronology of every paragraph of `Testability and Meaning` remains reconstructible in principle, but it is no longer thesis-bearing for the original JHI article or for the current Lovejoy methodological control.

## 3. 28 June 1935 — plural verification conditions, but still a strong translation/equipollence framework

Primary text:

Rudolf Carnap, `Les Concepts Psychologiques et les Concepts Physiques Sont-ils Foncièrement Différents?`, *Revue de Synthèse* 55.2 (1935), 43–53; online publication date 28 June 1935; DOI 10.1163/19552343-05502004.

Direct modern text reproduction:

https://www.phil.cmu.edu/projects/carnap/editorial/latex_pdf/1935-3.pdf

### 3.1 Physicalism still framed through translation/equipollence

Carnap says physicalism should be made more precise by replacing `traduisible` with `équipollent`:

> for every scientific statement there is an equipollent statement in the language of physics.

He distinguishes logical equipollence from physical equipollence, the latter allowing scientifically accepted synthetic laws in reciprocal derivation.

So at the end of June the public framework still has a relatively strong relation:

`statement S1`
↔ via logical + accepted physical laws ↔
`statement S2`.

### 3.2 But the old single verification-method criterion is already explicitly broadened

Carnap then criticizes talk of **`la méthode`** of verification. One should instead speak of:

- the **class of possible methods of verification**, or better;
- the **verification conditions** of a statement.

He says a statement can generally be checked in several ways and defines a verification condition as a test formula deducible from the statement with the aid of scientifically accepted laws.

He explicitly says Wittgenstein's meaning criterion therefore has to be **broadened**, so that a statement's meaning is determined by the class of its verification conditions.

### 3.3 Same proposition / different verification routes

Carnap gives examples in which:

- one observer can verify directly and indirectly;
- another can verify only indirectly;
- this does not constitute a fundamental logical difference between the statements.

He then gives the ordinary scientific analogy of current intensity being measurable through many different physical procedures while science still treats this as **one statement**, not several meanings.

Direct-primary consequence:

> **By June 1935 Carnap already explicitly rejects `one proposition = one verification route`. Multiple verification methods belong to one class of verification conditions and need not split proposition identity.**

This directly overlaps Lovejoy's own 1930 objection that heterogeneous verification routes do not automatically create heterogeneous meanings.

Historiographical consequence:

> **verification-route plurality is field-level by 1935; it cannot function as a uniquely Lovejoyian analytical marker.**

### 3.4 Why June 1935 is still a transitional rather than final point

Despite that liberalization, the June paper still ties sameness of meaning rather strongly to coincidence of verification-condition classes and still states physicalism in an equipollence/translation framework.

Thus the June text is best coded as:

`pluralized verification conditions`
+
`strong equivalence / translation architecture`

rather than the later non-eliminative reduction architecture.

## 4. September 1935 Paris — the technical break is explicit: definition is not the only method of concept introduction

The decisive primary text is Carnap's Paris Unity of Science congress paper:

Rudolf Carnap, `Über die Einheitssprache der Wissenschaft. Logische Bemerkungen zum Projekt einer Enzyklopädie`, delivered at the Paris 1935 congress; published in *Actes du Congrès International de Philosophie Scientifique*, fasc. II, *Unité de la science* (Paris: Hermann, 1936), 60–70.

Direct text:

https://www.phil.cmu.edu/projects/carnap/editorial/latex_pdf/1936-4.pdf

The paper's own section structure makes the transition explicit:

1. unified concepts;
2. **reduction of concepts**;
3. consequences for positivism and physicalism;
4. conceptual system of the encyclopedia.

### 4.1 Carnap explicitly rejects definition as the sole introduction method

Carnap begins the technical section by saying that rigorous methodology had usually divided signs into primitive signs and signs introduced by **definition**. Definition permits elimination/retranslation of the defined term.

He then says directly:

> one cannot restrict oneself to definition as the only procedure for introducing new signs.

He introduces another procedure:

> **Reduktion**.

### 4.2 The solubility example produces the reduction sentence

The attempted explicit definition of `x is soluble in water` fails because an object never placed in water would vacuously satisfy the conditionalized definition.

Carnap replaces it with a conditional relation of test circumstance, disposition term, and response:

`if x is in water at t, then x is soluble iff x dissolves at t`.

He names this a **Reduktionssatz**.

### 4.3 The key non-eliminability result

This is the decisive version-historical difference from the earlier translation/definition ideal.

Carnap states that a term introduced by definition can generally be eliminated everywhere. By contrast:

> **a sign introduced by reduction generally cannot be eliminated; sentences containing it are generally not re-translatable into sentences containing only the previously given signs.**

Carnap calls the distinction between definition and reduction **fundamental**.

This is direct-primary evidence for the technical architecture later elaborated in `Testability and Meaning`.

Compactly:

### earlier ideal

`new term`
= explicit definitional equivalent
→ eliminable
→ fully retranslatable.

### Paris 1935

`new term`
introduced conditionally by reduction sentence
→ empirically testable under specified conditions
→ **not generally eliminable**
→ **not generally fully retranslatable**.

This is the key technical liberalization.

### 4.4 Carnap himself links Paris reduction to the forthcoming `Testability and Meaning`

The paper closes with an explicit cross-reference:

> for the procedure of reduction and its significance for empirical testability, see `Testability and Meaning`, forthcoming in 1936.

That gives direct actor-level evidence that Paris and `Testability and Meaning` belong to one developing technical project.

## 5. Paris also separates truth from confirmation and relation-types more generally

The Paris congress trio also includes:

- `Von der Erkenntnistheorie zur Wissenschaftslogik`;
- `Wahrheit und Bewährung`.

In `Von der Erkenntnistheorie zur Wissenschaftslogik`, Carnap explicitly says earlier epistemology, including some earlier Vienna-Circle work, mixed psychological and logical components and must be decomposed into distinct problem-types. He insists that one must specify whether a purported epistemological question is logical or psychological.

In `Wahrheit und Bewährung`, he explicitly requires a distinction between:

- definition of truth;
- criterion of confirmation/acceptance.

Thus by Paris 1935 Carnap himself is already operating with a thick relation-jurisdiction discipline:

`logical ≠ psychological`

`truth ≠ confirmation`

`definition ≠ reduction`

`language convention ≠ empirical truth within the adopted language`

`reduction ≠ full eliminative translation`.

This further controls the Lovejoy branch against any residual narrative in which Carnap represents an undifferentiated verificationist foil.

## 6. December 1935 Baltimore — boundary encounter after the technical turn, not before it

Existing direct-primary chronology remains:

- 9 Dec. 1935: Carnap sends the Baltimore paper/manuscript to Quine for English correction;
- 31 Dec. 1935: Carnap reads the Baltimore paper;
- Lovejoy intervenes at length after a programme change;
- Carnap's same-day diary summarizes Lovejoy's objection as concerning conventionalism and the positivist entitlement to declare metaphysics meaningless;
- Carnap replies at length.

The Paris evidence now fixes the interpretation:

> **By the time Lovejoy encounters Carnap in Baltimore, Carnap has already publicly distinguished reduction from definition and has already abandoned unrestricted eliminative translation as the general model of empirical concept introduction.**

Therefore do not claim:

- Lovejoy caused the reduction-sentence move;
- Lovejoy caused Carnap to abandon strict definability;
- Baltimore initiated Carnap's broad liberalization of verificationism.

The event remains historically important as a **public dispute over what Carnap's already liberalizing framework licenses with respect to metaphysics and conventionalism**.

## 7. 1936–37 `Testability and Meaning` — systematic elaboration rather than Baltimore-originated reversal

Published `Testability and Meaning` makes explicit the architecture already visible in the pre-Baltimore trajectory:

- older verifiability requirement is rejected;
- confirmation replaces conclusive verification;
- confirmability and testability are distinguished;
- reduction sentences are formally developed;
- reducibility is distinguished from definability;
- earlier Vienna-Circle claims that all scientific descriptive terms are definable by perception terms are explicitly corrected;
- empirical meaningfulness is treated relative to the empiricist/scientific sense of `meaning`, not as a theory of every possible psychological sense of meaning.

Carnap states directly that earlier positivists, including his own 1928 work, failed to distinguish reducibility from definability; he now accepts reducibility while rejecting unrestricted elimination and retranslation.

The correct developmental model is therefore:

`May 1934 — liberalization already conceptually underway`

→ `spring 1935 — first English TM version in composition`

→ `28 Jun. 1935 — plural verification conditions + strong equipollence framework`

→ **`Sept. 1935 Paris — explicit non-eliminative reduction + truth/confirmation + logical/psychological partition`**

→ `9 Dec. 1935 — Baltimore version sent to Quine`

→ `31 Dec. 1935 — Lovejoy public challenge`

→ `spring 1936 — final TM version`

→ `Oct. 1936 / Jan. 1937 publication`.

This is sufficient version-historical resolution for the present project.

## 8. Consequence for Lovejoy 1938 — sharpened adversarial reading

The final bridge reconstruction now gives a controlled way to read Lovejoy's 1938 `On the Criteria and Limits of Meaning`.

Lovejoy's positive point remains strong:

> a criterion suited to empirical testability cannot silently acquire jurisdiction over every broader notion of thinkability or philosophical meaning.

But Carnap's own development now makes the scope issue unavoidable:

- June 1935 Carnap already pluralizes verification methods;
- Paris 1935 Carnap rejects full eliminative definability;
- 1936–37 Carnap explicitly restricts the discussion to meaning **in the empiricist/scientific sense** and distinguishes confirmability from testability;
- Carnap also separates framework choice/convention from factual truth inside a chosen language.

Lovejoy's 1938 critique becomes most accurate when read as:

> **a challenge to whether Carnap's restricted technical criterion is entitled to the broader philosophical consequence of excluding metaphysical questions as meaningless.**

It becomes less accurate if paraphrased as:

> `Carnap simply identifies all conceivable meaning with a single actual verification procedure.`

Carnap had already moved well beyond that formulation before Baltimore.

This confirms the branch's adversarial protocol:

`A ≠ B`

must be followed by reconstruction of the actor's actual bridge between A and B before judging illicit transfer.
