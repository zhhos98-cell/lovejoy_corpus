# Lovejoy 1940 — Coleridge: recipient prior state, confirmation, source plurality, and logical justification

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **MATURE RECIPIENT-PRIOR-STATE CONTROL / LOVEJOY PRIMARY CLAIMS CURRENTLY MEDIATED THROUGH WILSON + PARTIAL LATER QUOTATION / DIRECT PRIMARY LOCK OPEN**

Primary: Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Coleridge and Kant's Two Worlds`, *ELH* 7 (1940), 341–362; reprinted in *Essays in the History of Ideas* (1948).

## Why this case matters

The genealogy branch added `R = recipient prior state` after stress-testing Lovejoy's 1920 Schiller → Schlegel argument against later scholarship. That initially made `R` look like a corrective supplied by later historians.

The 1940 Coleridge essay changes that status.

Daniel J. Wilson's annotated bibliography, quoting/summarizing Lovejoy's primary article, records Lovejoy's claim that Coleridge gained from German philosophy:

> a feeling — **or the confirmation of a feeling to which he was already predisposed** — of the moral indispensability of individual freedom, together with a means of logically justifying that belief through a Kantian two-worlds scheme.

This is high-value actor-level evidence that mature Lovejoy himself distinguished:

`source introduces disposition/content`
from
`source confirms / articulates / justifies a pre-existing disposition`.

The direct article text should still be locked before publication-grade quotation. The conceptual result is secure enough for branch-level use through Wilson's explicit summary and quotation.

---

## 1. Lovejoy's stated object is both nature and source

Wilson quotes Lovejoy as saying that to understand Coleridge it is necessary to understand the **nature and interrelations** of philosophical ideas that were vital in his life.

Lovejoy then asks about the **precise nature and source** of Coleridge's ideas concerning freedom/determinism and the distinctions between:

- phenomenal / noumenal worlds;
- Reason / Understanding.

The object is therefore not a simple source hunt.

It is:

`identify philosophical content`
+ `reconstruct interrelations`
+ `locate sources`
+ `explain what the received material did for the recipient`.

This already fits the branch's `T → relation → genealogy` architecture.

---

## 2. Source plurality is actor-explicit

Wilson records Lovejoy's judgment that Coleridge's distinction was learned:

- **partly from Kant**;
- **more from Jacobi and Schelling**.

Thus `German influence` is not treated as one undifferentiated edge.

Lovejoy distinguishes source contributions within a shared intellectual field.

Analytically:

`A_Kant`
≠ `A_Jacobi`
≠ `A_Schelling`

and their causal weights need not be equal.

This is a mature counterpart to 1904 Bruno/Spinoza, where a direct edge and a wider tradition-level reservoir were separately assessed.

---

## 3. The crucial distinction: introduction versus confirmation

The most important phrase is Lovejoy's alternative formulation:

`a feeling`
**or**
`the confirmation of a feeling to which he was already predisposed`.

This explicitly keeps two causal models open.

### Model I — introduction

The external source gives Coleridge a disposition/belief he did not previously possess.

### Model II — confirmation / articulation

The recipient already has the relevant moral disposition; external philosophy:

- confirms it;
- gives it conceptual articulation;
- supplies a framework in which it can be defended;
- changes its epistemic/philosophical status.

The historical delta under Model II is not creation of content ex nihilo.

It is:

`prior disposition`
→ `contact with philosophical scheme`
→ `confirmation + logical articulation/justification`.

This is precisely the causal distinction the current genealogy vector needed.

---

## 4. Logical justification is itself a distinct delta

Lovejoy says the German/Kantian scheme gave Coleridge not merely the feeling of freedom's moral indispensability, but **a means of logically justifying that belief**.

Therefore the source can alter one relation-level without creating another:

`moral commitment / disposition already present`
+
`new logical warrant/framework`
→ `philosophically articulated position`.

This is a powerful example of typed causal effect.

The source may contribute:

- content;
- confidence;
- conceptual vocabulary;
- inferential structure;
- justification;
- systematic placement;

in different degrees.

`influence` is therefore underspecified unless the historian states **what changed**.

---

## 5. Recipient prior state becomes actor-level Lovejoy evidence

The current genealogy vector is:

`T × R × A × E × X × D × P × C`.

After the 1940 case, `R` is no longer justified only by later criticism of Lovejoy's Schiller/Schlegel causal allocation.

Mature Lovejoy himself recognizes that a recipient may be `already predisposed` before the source encounter.

The correct causal question is therefore:

> **What difference did contact with this source make relative to the recipient's prior intellectual state?**

Possible values of `D` include:

- introduction;
- confirmation;
- acceleration;
- revaluation;
- logical justification;
- systematic articulation;
- terminological supply;
- recombination;
- little/no detectable delta.

This vocabulary is analyst reconstruction. The introduction/confirmation/logical-justification distinction is directly grounded in Lovejoy's reported wording.

## 6. Comparison with 1920 Schiller → Schlegel

### 1920 case

Lovejoy strongly emphasizes Schiller as an instrument of Schlegel's aesthetic conversion. Later scholarship argues that important components of the recipient's later position were already forming before the relevant reading.

This exposed a possible over-allocation of causal weight.

### 1940 case

Lovejoy explicitly formulates the recipient-prior-state alternative himself:

`new feeling`
**or**
`confirmation of prior predisposition`.

The mature case therefore shows a more differentiated causal vocabulary than the 1920 case.

Do not infer a simple developmental story from two cases alone. The difference could reflect object/evidence differences as much as chronological methodological maturation.

But it blocks any claim that recipient prior state is foreign to Lovejoy's mature practice.

---

## 7. Comparison with 1904 Bruno → Spinoza

1904 separates:

- direct reading/contact;
- shared tradition-level availability;
- degree of dependence.

1940 adds a more recipient-centered distinction:

- prior predisposition;
- source confirmation;
- source-supplied logical justification.

Together the cases show that Lovejoy can analyze causal history on both sides of the edge:

### Source side

What materials/channels were available?

### Recipient side

What was already present, and what changed after uptake?

This is more precise than a simple transmission model.

## 8. Relation to typed relations / jurisdiction

The Coleridge case also shows why logical and historical relations must not collapse.

Lovejoy can say that a source supplied a **logical justification** while separately asking whether it supplied the underlying **moral disposition**.

Thus:

`logical warrant of belief`
≠ `psychological/moral motivation for belief`
≠ `historical source of belief`
≠ `historical source of its justificatory framework`.

This is exactly the kind of relation partition made explicit in 1941's logical / psychological / historical-genetic triad.

The 1940 essay therefore provides a strong immediate bridge into the 1941 mature taxonomy.

## 10. Current conclusion

> **By 1940 Lovejoy explicitly recognizes a recipient-prior-state problem: an external philosophy may introduce a conviction, or may instead confirm a disposition already present while supplying new logical justification. The historical effect of a source must therefore be typed by what changed, not inferred from post-contact resemblance alone.**

This strengthens the genealogy branch without idealizing Lovejoy's consistency across cases.
