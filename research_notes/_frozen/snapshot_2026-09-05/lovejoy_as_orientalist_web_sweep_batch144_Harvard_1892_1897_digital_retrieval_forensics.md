2. ## 3. Radcliffe 1896–97 digital series supplies a parallel official witness

Radcliffe ListView root:

`https://listview.lib.harvard.edu/lists/drs-34299933`

Persistent archival identifier:

`RAD.ARCH:5310447`

The root includes an **1896–1897** child and continuous neighboring years.

The historical significance is strengthened by the Harvard Crimson's 8 June 1895 report on the Radcliffe elective pamphlet for 1895–96. It states that in Indo-Iranian languages **Professor Lanman would have charge of the Sanskrit courses that had been conducted the previous year by Paul Elmer More**. The same notice lists Irving Babbitt as an instructor in French at Radcliffe.

This is useful for two reasons:

1. it independently confirms the Lanman–More Sanskrit teaching succession in the institutional environment immediately before Lovejoy's documented 1896–97 Harvard work;
2. it explains why Radcliffe can be a parallel witness for course descriptions/instructor assignments even though Radcliffe enrollment cannot prove Lovejoy's Harvard enrollment.

Do not merge the Radcliffe and Harvard student bodies or treat the Radcliffe course as Lovejoy's course without page-level concordance.

## 4. Contemporary 1896 Lanman Buddhism lecture gives the exact public source environment

The Harvard Crimson, 29 April 1896, printed an outline from Lanman's bibliographical notes for the first of three public lectures on Buddhism. The outline includes:

- Buddhism in relation to antecedent Vedism/Brahmanism;
- relation to contemporary Sāṃkhya and Vedānta;
- northern and southern Buddhist literature;
- source languages **Sanskrit and Pāli**;
- Tipiṭaka and its Vinaya, Sutta and Abhidhamma divisions;
- the five Nikāyas;
- the Pali Text Society;
- the King of Siam's edition.

This is now the clearest contemporary Harvard-side institutional control for the technical world visible in notebook 004.

### Evidentiary boundary

It proves that this material was being publicly taught by Lanman at Harvard in April 1896, immediately before the academic year in which Wilson places Lovejoy's Sanskrit/Buddhist-text study under Lanman.

It does **not** prove:

- Lovejoy attended the public lecture;
- Lovejoy's formal course had exactly this syllabus;
- Lovejoy acquired any particular notebook proposition from this lecture.

The proper use is `environment / instructor repertoire`, not `direct source transmission`.

## 5. Harvard's current departmental history clarifies the administrative vocabulary

Harvard's Department of South Asian Studies historical page states that Charles Lanman, arriving in 1880, was the first to preside over the department then called **Indo-Iranian Languages** and that he founded the Harvard Oriental Series in 1891. It further says that by 1902, with Avesta relinquished and Pāli and Prākrit added, the department was renamed **Indic Philology**.

For the Lovejoy years, therefore, searches should prioritize the historical label:

> `Indo-Iranian Languages`

as well as:

> `Sanskrit`, `Lanman`, `Buddhist`, `Pali/Pāli`.

The 1902 administrative change must **not** be used to infer that no Pāli material was taught earlier: Lanman's 1896 public outline explicitly discusses Pāli. It only warns against searching the 1896–97 catalogue under the later departmental label `Indic Philology`.

## 6. Parallel source: 1896–97 Graduate Handbook now identified as a finite full-view object

A separate inter-university series, `Graduate Courses: A Handbook for Graduate Students`, was compiled by the Graduate Club of Harvard in cooperation with other graduate clubs during the 1890s. Bibliographic/search records identify a volume for **1896–97**, published in 1897, about 208 pages; Google Books exposes full-view editions in this series.

This is potentially valuable because it was designed specifically to summarize graduate courses by institution/instructor, and later volumes demonstrably list instructors, course titles, frequency and duration.

### Use hierarchy

The handbook can help identify:

- Lanman course titles/levels;
- whether Everett/Toy offerings were presented as graduate work;
- course frequency and duration;
- likely course numbers/titles to search in Harvard's annual report.

But it does **not** replace:

- Harvard's official FAS announcement for exact institutional wording;
- Harvard President's Report for enrollment;
- Harvard Archives `list of courses taken by Lovejoy` for individual enrollment.

At present, the full 1896–97 handbook text has not been extracted by the automated browser, so no course wording is claimed from it yet.
