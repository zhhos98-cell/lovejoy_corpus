# I. Governing correction

Do **not** write:

`Lovejoy studied Buddhism -> invented unit-ideas -> founded JHI`.

The evidence now supports a more complex history:

1. Lovejoy's 1898 Buddhist/Pali work required separate judgments about semantic identity, logical/classificatory inclusion, temporal antecedence, causal relation, and textual derivation.
2. His 1906–1914 comparative-religion work turned `primitive` into an aggregation and bridge-license problem: local evidence, type identity, chronology and population representativeness had different burdens.
3. Interwar `history of ideas` institutionalized some of these problem forms through the Hopkins History of Ideas Club and ACLS planning, especially around `primitivism`.
4. JHI from 1940 was not a fixed Lovejoyian orthodoxy but an institutional field of methodological argument over decomposition, continuity, causation, context, transmission and cultural process.
5. After Lovejoy, Wiener/Boas/JHI and the International Society expanded the infrastructure internationally while regional intellectual-history traditions and area-studies networks also developed partly independently.
6. International/network expansion and canonical symmetry did not move at the same speed: the 1973 *Dictionary of the History of Ideas* had meaningful non-Western entries but remained retrospectively acknowledged as primarily Western-centered.

Strongest conceptual formula:

`PROGRAMME != PRACTICE != INFRASTRUCTURE != CANON`.

# IV. JHI founding and governance

## 1940 programme

Lovejoy's `Reflections on the History of Ideas` explicitly names `primitivism` among exemplary broad JHI subjects, together with evolution, progress, views of human nature, nationalism, racism, etc.

JHI's founding programme emphasizes crossing disciplinary/national/language boundaries and tracing antecedents/consequents rather than confining inquiry to departmental boxes.

## JHI was not simply a Hopkins Press imprint

Strong institutional evidence:

- `Journal of the History of Ideas, Inc.` existed from the founding era;
- IRS/nonprofit data give exemption ruling in August 1940;
- 1941 advertising already directs subscriptions to `Journal of the History of Ideas, Inc.`, City College, New York;
- this corporate/editorial infrastructure persists through later decades.

By 1973 masthead/governance visibly distinguishes:

- executive/editorial roles;
- Board of Editors;
- Corresponding Editors;
- Board of Directors.

Interpretive payoff:

JHI was a portable corporate/editorial institution, not institutionally locked to Hopkins after founding.

Do not conflate `Journal of the History of Ideas, Inc.` with later references to a `Society for the History of Ideas` unless legal/organizational continuity is directly proven.

# V. Early JHI as methodological argument, not orthodoxy

## Spitzer–Lovejoy 1944

The dispute is best coded as a disagreement over proof burdens:

`idea presence/content similarity`
!=
`historical continuity`
!=
`causal explanation of a movement`.

Important new control from Lovejoy's reply:

Lovejoy presents major claims of his method as `working hypotheses` requiring textual/historical testing, rather than as methodologically guaranteed ontology.

This is a major bridge to the early relation-specific proof discipline, but still do not claim one consciously stable method from 1898 to 1944.

## Mandelbaum 1948

Near-contemporary JHI reconstruction emphasizes:

- Lovejoy's causal concerns;
- pluralism of past reality;
- need to distinguish continuous ideas linked by historical transmission from merely recurrent/similar ideas produced independently.

This is especially important because the comparison to anthropological `diffusion vs independent invention` makes `similarity != transmission` an explicit JHI-era proof problem.

## Roy Harvey Pearce 1947–1953

This is currently the strongest successor line.

Sequence:

- 1945 History of Ideas Club: `The Indian as Noble Savage`;
- 1947 captivity narratives: already emphasizes form + impact + milieu + content;
- 1948 JHI `A Note on Method in the History of Ideas`: analytical decomposition was necessary pioneer work, but the next stage should ask how ideas function inside specific cultural processes; `primitivism + progress` is a worked methodological example;
- 1952 JHI on the Indian/Puritan mind;
- 1953 *The Savages of America*: Idea -> Symbol -> Image -> thought/action;
- later retrospective: `From the History of Ideas to Ethnohistory`.

Governing line:

`LOVEJOYIAN ANALYSIS`
-> `PEARCE SYNTHETIC / CULTURAL-PROCESS EXTENSION`
-> `LATER ETHNOHISTORICAL SELF-CRITIQUE`.

This demonstrates that contextual/synthetic development occurred inside the JHI lineage well before Skinner's 1969 canonical critique.

# IX. Regional infrastructures and globalization

Do not write a smooth `JHI became global` story. The evidence now shows different regional pathways.

## A. China / Chinese Thought — strongest convergence

Sequence:

`AHA Chinese Reactions to Imported Ideas`
-> `ACLS / Mortimer Graves encouragement`
-> `Committee on Chinese Thought`
-> `AAS / Rockefeller support`
-> Fairbank / Wright / Nivison / Schwartz / de Bary etc.
-> JHI articles and symposia
-> JHI editorial governance.

1951 JHI `Chinese Reactions to Imported Ideas` marks an important role shift:

China is no longer only a source influencing Europe; Chinese actors become recipients/rejecters/adapters of imported ideas.

Arthur F. Wright's 1960 JHI intervention is a key disciplinary-interface target: modern Sinology is pushed from Orientalist/philological isolation toward historical and social-scientific reconstruction.

Direct page lock still desirable.

## B. Transpacific / Nakamura — very strong convergence

Sequence:

`Japanese comparative thought project, 1945–46`
-> `Rockefeller`
-> `Stanford interdisciplinary seminar`
-> `Committee on Chinese Thought circulation`
-> `UNESCO`
-> `East-West Center`
-> `Nakamura + Wiener + Arthur Wright`
-> 1964 *Ways of Thinking of Eastern Peoples*
-> JHI review/editorial governance.

Key actor-level methodological shift in the 1964 edition:

- reject monolithic `East` / `Oriental mind`;
- use Buddhism as a moving tradition whose selection/adaptation/modification differs across India, China, Tibet and Japan;
- reduce innate/national/racial explanation, increase socio-cultural/environmental explanation.

Wiener explicitly says this work broadened his own interests in history of ideas.

This is a major second-generation bridge back to Buddhism, but not a direct Lovejoy transmission claim.

## C. Latin America / Zea — institutional convergence, not simple export

Sequence:

`Comite de Historia de las Ideas en America`
-> Pan-American / regional institutional planning
-> national histories of ideas / philosophy
-> Rockefeller and publication infrastructure
-> 1962 Mexico meeting with International Society
-> Zea as JHI Corresponding Editor by 1973
-> JHI engagement with Zea's *Positivism in Mexico*.

Key correction:

Wiener/JHI encountered an already institutionalized Latin American `historia de las ideas`; this was convergence between traditions, not unilateral diffusion from the US.

## D. South Asia / Indic

Strong area infrastructure existed independently in ACLS planning from the interwar period.

JHI execution includes direct internal-history objects such as `The Hindu Philosophy of History` (1955), and Nakamura later brings Buddhism/Indic material into the Wiener reference network.

Current state: infrastructure -> article -> later canon/interface is visible, but no China-like single committee-to-JHI bridge has yet been reconstructed.

## E. Arabic / Islamic

Strong ACLS training/area infrastructure exists from the 1930s–40s.

JHI execution includes Islamic philosophy/history-of-science cases; the 1973 DHI gives internal conceptual headwords such as `Causation in Islamic Thought` and includes contributors such as Michael Marmura and Seyyed Hossein Nasr.

Current state: infrastructure -> article -> canon is strong; editorial/Society governance convergence is less fully reconstructed.

## F. Africa / African-American — strongest parallel-path control

ACLS Negro Studies was a serious 1940s field-building project, not an absence of infrastructure.

Critical 1940 ACLS conference findings:

1. ACLS situates Negro Studies alongside its other field-development committees, including History of Ideas and Asian/Islamic studies.
2. Alain Locke / Mortimer Graves discuss whether a specialized journal could worsen disciplinary isolation rather than solve it.
3. Frazier/Bunche/Herskovits debate African survivals with a proof rule structurally close to the later influence problem: observed similarity is insufficient; historical connection/contact must be demonstrated.

Thus `similarity != transmission` and `specialization vs liaison` problems existed simultaneously in a parallel ACLS field-building experiment.

Later African intellectual history appears to appropriate history-of-ideas methods mainly through African Studies venues rather than converging as strongly with JHI governance/canon.

Do not call Africa absent from history of ideas; call the current pattern **parallel appropriation / weaker JHI institutional convergence** pending more evidence.

Major open mediator:

**Mortimer Graves** may connect Negro Studies, Chinese Thought, and History-of-Ideas planning. Next round should trace him first.

# X. Dictionary of the History of Ideas as canon snapshot

The 1973 DHI is crucial because it is a reference architecture, not merely a journal sample.

Original DHI contains meaningful non-Western/internal entries, including:

- `Buddhism` — Hajime Nakamura;
- `Causation in Islamic Thought` — Michael Marmura;
- Islamic intellectual-life material — including Seyyed Hossein Nasr;
- China/Asia-in-Western-thought entries.

This shows non-Western ideas had entered the reference canon by the Wiener generation.

But later JHI governance explicitly described the old DHI as confidently centered on the Western tradition, with non-Western traditions appearing only marginally. The 2004 *New Dictionary* explicitly expanded scope to Africa, Asia, Latin America and the Middle East.

Therefore:

`NETWORK GLOBALIZATION`
!=
`REFERENCE-CANON SYMMETRY`.

This is one of the strongest large-scale results of the sweep.
