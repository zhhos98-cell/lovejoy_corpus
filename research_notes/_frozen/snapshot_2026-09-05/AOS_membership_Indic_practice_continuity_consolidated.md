## 1. 1897 — entry into the American Oriental Society is primary-secure

Primary AOS proceedings for the Baltimore meeting, 22–24 April 1897, establish two separate facts.

First, Lovejoy's Buddhist paper appears on the meeting programme as paper no. 30, `Mr. Arthur O. Lovejoy, Harvard University`, on the Buddhist technical terms later published as `upādāna and upādisesa`. The residual group including paper no. 30 was **read by title**, with or without a brief statement of contents, as the meeting approached adjournment.

Second, at the final session persons recommended by the Directors were elected, and under `As CORPORATE MEMBERS` appears `A. O. Lovejoy, Oakland, Cal.` The revised 1897 member list gives the fuller entry `ARTHUR ONCKEN LOVEJOY (Harvard University), Oakland, Cal. 1897.`

Safe formulation:

> **Lovejoy had a Buddhist paper formally in the 1897 AOS programme, it was read by title, and at the same meeting he was elected a corporate member. His own physical attendance is not established.**

The attendance question must remain separate. The relevant residual papers were described as being `for the most part` by members not present, but that wording does not identify Lovejoy individually.

## 2. 1898–1899 — membership continues while recorded meeting activity drops out

The revised May 1898 member list still includes Lovejoy, now at `1689 Cambridge St., Cambridge, Mass.` His finished eleven-page technical article appears in *JAOS* 19 (1898), pp.126–136.

The 1898 Hartford proceedings list members present and meeting papers; Lovejoy does not appear in either list. Because he was already a member by then, the correct bounded statement is:

> **AOS member + active JAOS specialist author, but no recorded Hartford attendance and no recorded 1898 meeting paper.**

The December 1899 member roll still includes `ARTHUR ONCKEN LOVEJOY, 1689 Cambridge St., Cambridge, Mass. 1897.` The 1899 Cambridge proceedings contain a complete attendance list and a numbered paper programme; Lovejoy appears in neither.

Thus by 1899:

> **corporate affiliation persisted, but sustained annual-meeting participation is not documented.**

This matters because the 1899 Cambridge meeting contained a substantial Historical Study of Religions / Indic ecology; geographical and thematic proximity must not be converted into evidence of Lovejoy's attendance.

## 3. 1900–1901 — later primary rolls revise the old exit window

Batch186 recovered two later AOS corporate-member lists that supersede the older Batch69–70 lower bound.

### December 1900 roll

The list explicitly headed `REVISED, DECEMBER, 1900` contains:

`ARTHUR ONCKEN LOVEJOY, 5 Rue Rollin, Paris, France. 1897.`

This is a contemporary institutional correspondence record. It does **not** prove Lovejoy was physically present in Paris in December 1900; the address could be retained from an earlier correspondence state.

It is, however, an independent AOS record linking Lovejoy's member file to the Paris address also relevant to the notebook environment.

### December 1901 roll

The list explicitly headed `REVISED, DECEMBER, 1901` again retains Lovejoy:

`ARTHUR ONCKEN LOVEJOY, [address desired.] 1897.`

Therefore Lovejoy remained a corporate member through at least **December 1901**.

This changes the old asynchronous narrative. Wilson's bibliography records a dedicated four-lecture Washington University Association syllabus, `The Philosophy of Buddhism`, in 1901. The primary syllabus itself is not yet independently recovered, so its exact course contents remain Wilson-mediated; nevertheless, the chronology is now clear:

> **dedicated Buddhist teaching and formal AOS membership coexisted in 1901.**

Do not repeat the older claim that the 1901 teaching may have followed an AOS departure.

## 4. January 1903 — hard negative terminus

The AOS corporate-member list explicitly revised **January 1903** no longer contains Lovejoy. In the alphabetic L-section the sequence passes from Jacob W. Loch to Percival Lowell, where Lovejoy would necessarily occur if still listed; full-volume search likewise yields no Lovejoy entry.

The current institutional exit interval is therefore:

> **after the membership roll revised December 1901 and before the membership roll revised January 1903.**

No exact resignation/removal date is established.

The Society's bylaws allowed Directors to drop a corporate member after two years of unpaid assessment, but no Lovejoy-specific record currently proves nonpayment. Formal resignation is likewise unproved.

Use the bylaw only as an administrative possibility demonstrating why disappearance from the roll need not encode intellectual or ideological withdrawal.

## 5. Membership is affiliation evidence, not a professional-identity proxy

The AOS's own membership notice states that it was **not necessary to be a `professed Orientalist`** in order to become a member.

That actor-level statement provides the correct evidentiary firewall.

AOS membership can establish:

- formal corporate affiliation;
- institutional availability to the Society's scholarly life;
- in Lovejoy's case, conjunction in 1897 with a specialist Buddhist paper.

Membership alone cannot establish:

- professional employment as an Orientalist;
- a stable self-identity as an Orientalist;
- sustained meeting attendance;
- continuous specialist publication.

Conversely, disappearance from the member roll establishes institutional non-affiliation at that date, not disappearance of Buddhist/Indic knowledge or competence.

Project rule:

> **institutional affiliation chronology and intellectual-practice chronology must be tracked separately.**

## 6. The practice chronology continues beyond the institutional exit

The strongest directly controlled sequence is:

| date | institutional / publication state | Buddhist / Indic practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1897 | elected AOS corporate member; Buddhist paper no.30 read by title | specialist Buddhist technical-term work |
| 1898 | member; no recorded Hartford attendance/paper | technical *JAOS* article, pp.126–136 |
| 1899 | member; no recorded Cambridge attendance/paper | no new specialist AOS paper located |
| Dec. 1900 | member; AOS record gives `5 Rue Rollin, Paris` | institutional record only; do not infer physical residence from retained address |
| 1901 | member through December | Wilson records four-lecture `The Philosophy of Buddhism` syllabus |
| 1902 | exact post-Dec-1901 membership change unresolved | `Religion and the Time-Process` explicitly reuses the 1898 Buddhist analysis |
| Jan. 1903 | absent from revised member roll | institutional non-affiliation only |
| 1907 | no AOS affiliation currently evidenced | direct Vedānta review shows current Indic-philosophical field judgment |

The 1902 self-citation is especially important: Lovejoy himself turns the 1898 specialist Buddhist paper into argumentative infrastructure for a larger comparative history of categories.

The 1907 review is separately primary-controlled in `lovejoy_1907_deussen_vedanta_primary_review_batch201.md`. It shows a changed scholarly mode: not fresh Pāli philological work, but current field-state judgment, differentiation of Śaṅkara and Rāmānuja, reception-history criticism, knowledge of recent translation, and a programme for treating Indian systems as technical philosophy through their reasons and arguments.

Safe larger formulation:

> **The specialist institutional locus changed before Indic competence disappeared. Lovejoy did not sustain a career of professional Buddhological publication, but Buddhist/Indic practice continued across teaching, comparative category-history, and philosophical criticism.**

Avoid the stronger identity claim `Lovejoy remained an Orientalist` unless explicitly qualified.

## 7. Contemporary circulation is stronger than a simple publication fact

Batch186 adds two contemporary bibliographic controls that should remain separate from direct intellectual reception.

### 1899 — *Orientalische Bibliographie*

The bibliography for 1898, published in 1899, lists Lovejoy's *JAOS* article by full name and exact pages. This proves that the paper entered an international specialist bibliographic circuit within roughly a year.

### 1903 — Albert J. Edmunds, Pali Text Society bibliography

Edmunds's signed `A Buddhist Bibliography, Based upon the Libraries of Philadelphia` includes Lovejoy's article in a selective specialist bibliography and places it within `Comparative Religion, Psychology, and special criticism` near Lévi/Jacobi/Lanman material.

These controls establish **documented specialist discoverability and classificatory uptake**. They do not prove agreement with Lovejoy's semantics, direct correspondence, or close reading by every named scholar in the same bibliography.

1. **AOS institutional entry is secure:** election + Buddhist programme paper in 1897.
2. **Physical attendance in 1897 is unresolved.**
