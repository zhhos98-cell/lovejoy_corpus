## 1. p.64 — extension of the p.53 Frazer + Marillier recombination, not a fresh theory of mechanical religion

The direct-image-controlled page is W3 for:

- magical sacrifice as `a sort of machine`;
- the operator must remain master of the apparatus;
- `the gods` may exploit lack of technical skill and subject the sacrificer in turn;
- a contrasting kind of magical sacrifice may act not by constraining divine will but directly on natural forces.

Round 23 already established the relevant source field:

- **Frazer (1890)** supplies the general grammar of sympathetic magic as impersonal, necessary, quasi-mechanical efficacy acting directly on nature without personal/spiritual agency;
- **Marillier** supplies sacrifice as a possible magical act/force that constrains gods or otherwise operates through magical efficacy.

The p.64 page therefore should not be used to claim a Lovejoy invention of `religion as machine` or of the contrast between magical force and personal divine will.

What remains locally significant is the page's **internal differentiation within magical sacrifice**:

1. technically hazardous coercive relation with personal gods;
2. direct action on natural forces with no divine will functioning as mediator.

Calibration:

- general mechanical/magical grammar: F/A0;
- sacrifice-as-magical-force: Marillier field A0;
- exact two-branch contrast on p.64: provisional S/A2 as local redistribution/recombination, not priority.

This is best read with p.53 as one continuous mechanism-analysis block.

## 2. p.65 — mixed motives and survivance are field problems; the local page operation is explicit analytical dissociation

The direct-image page is W3 for the proposition that sacrificial forms are rarely found in sharply separated purity; two or more motives/elements are commonly mingled, and only `delicate analysis` can dissociate them. It further says syncretism/fusion may have been preceded by a period in which the sacrifices were distinct in character and function.

### Upstream field control

Marillier's 1892–93 EPHE work on Oceanian taboo explicitly treats institutions, rites and legends as capable of surviving the beliefs that produced them. The broader Marillier formation therefore already contains the historical problem that:

`PERSISTING FORM != PERSISTING ORIGINAL BELIEF / MOTIVE`.

Contemporary sacrifice controversy also rejects single-origin taxonomies when procedures contain heterogeneous operations.

### Local delta

No upstream exact formulation has yet been recovered for the p.65 sequence:

`MOTIVES MINGLED -> DELICATE ANALYSIS -> DISSOCIATE RITUAL ELEMENTS -> POSSIBLE PRIOR DISTINCTNESS IN CHARACTER/FUNCTION`.

Therefore the safe ownership is:

- survivance / mixed historical formation as a problem: F/A0–A1;
- the explicit notebook procedure of **analytical dissociation of presently fused sacrificial elements**: W3 / provisional A2.

The claim should be phrased as a Lovejoy-local analytical operation inside an inherited survivance/syncretism problem, not as the invention of survivance theory.

## 3. p.66 — strongest surviving local relation: ritual type -> social jurisdiction / competent performer

The direct-image page is W3 for the following contrast:

- expiatory sacrifice is `very generally performed` by the whole community, which thereby disembarrasses itself of responsibility for a crime/offence;
- magical sacrifice is performed by priests, specifically priests possessing magical skill in charms.

A focused source sweep found ample upstream support for the **constituents**:

- collective participation can characterize expiatory or kin-responsibility rites;
- magical specialists/priests and charm expertise are commonplace in the ethnographic and comparative-religion field;
- Marillier separately treats expiatory and magical sacrifice as functional types.

What has **not** been recovered is an exact source that makes the notebook's horizontal pairing:

`EXPIATORY SACRIFICE -> WHOLE COMMUNITY`

versus

`MAGICAL SACRIFICE -> TECHNICALLY COMPETENT PRIEST / CHARM SPECIALIST`.

This page therefore remains one of the cleanest 005 Lovejoy-local relations.

### Why it matters

The operator is not merely classificatory. The notebook treats **who is competent, obligated, or socially authorized to perform a rite as evidence about what mechanism the rite instantiates**.

Safe formulation:

> **Lovejoy locally indexes sacrificial mechanism to social jurisdiction: collective expiation and specialist magical technique are distinguished partly by their competent ritual bearer.**

Calibration: W3 / A2 for the relation, with no priority claim for the underlying facts about communal rites or magical specialists.

This is stronger than saying simply `Lovejoy classifies sacrifice by performer` because the page relates performer to responsibility and mechanism.

## 4. p.69 — animal -> human substitution direction is Robertson-Smith-owned

The direct-image-controlled page is W3 for:

- animal sacrifice is `not an evidence of advancement`;
- animal sacrifice may be more primitive than human sacrifice;
- a human victim may later be substituted for a specially respected animal.

Earlier project language treated this as a striking Lovejoy reversal of the common developmental arrow `human sacrifice -> animal substitute`.

Clause-level source control requires a downgrade.

### Robertson Smith's theory already contains this direction

The Robertson-Smith sacrifice genealogy, as summarized explicitly by Hubert and Mauss and later reference literature, states that when the kinship between humans and sacred/totemic animals ceased to be intelligible, **human sacrifice could replace animal sacrifice** because human blood now supplied the direct kin relation sought in the rite.

Thus:

`ANIMAL / TOTEM SACRIFICE -> HUMAN SUBSTITUTE`

is not Lovejoy's independent discovery.

### What p.69 still proves

The page remains valuable because Lovejoy:

- rejects victim species as a universal ladder of cultural advancement;
- actively adopts/tests a local sequence running counter to the familiar humanitarian substitution narrative;
- thereby requires substitution direction to be demonstrated case by case.

Calibration:

- animal->human historical direction: Robertson-Smith field A0;
- notebook use of that direction as an anti-universal evolutionary control: A1–A2 depending on the exact surrounding argumentative syntax;
- no originality claim for bidirectional substitution.

The old formulation `Lovejoy reverses the arrow` should be replaced by:

> **Lovejoy mobilizes an already available Robertson-Smith counter-arrow to block a universal human-to-animal progress sequence.**

## 5. p.70–72 — form/killing/cannibalism/funerary service remain mechanism discrimination, not category invention

The current summaries distinguish:

- punishment of a failed rain-maker from sacrifice;
- cannibalism/medicine/spirit appropriation from sacrifice proper;
- funerary killing as provision of attendants/service/status continuity for the dead rather than communion with a god.

These distinctions live inside a field already crowded with competing functional explanations. Their historical significance lies in **case reclassification and negative controls**, not in invention of the possible mechanisms themselves.

Until original-image wording and immediate source carriers are controlled, keep these pages primarily A1 with possible local A2 seams where Lovejoy explicitly says that a superficially similar killing is `rather` punishment, medicine, service, provision, or another function.

The governing rule is:

`RITUALIZED KILLING / EATING / OFFERING != SACRIFICE BY FORM ALONE`.

## 6. p.73 — Tylor owns the object-soul explanation; Marillier owns the survivance problem; Lovejoy's delta is local explanatory reassignment

The direct-image page is W3 for a sequence in which an inherited funerary practice is first connected with protection against return of the dead and then acquires `another meaning`: complete destruction releases the spirit, while breaking objects can release their spirit/soul so the dead may use them in the other world.

Earlier project language risked treating this as direct evidence that Lovejoy discovered the general principle `same practice, new explanation`.

### Tylor upstream control

*Primitive Culture* already warns that destruction/breaking of funerary goods does not by itself prove one belief. Tylor treats breaking as compatible with ordinary abandonment/destruction, but also records the animistic explanation that killing/breaking an object releases its spirit so it can accompany the dead, analogously to killing attendants or animals for the other world.

Therefore the **object-soul transmission explanation** is Tylor-owned.

### Marillier upstream control

Marillier's EPHE formulation that rites/institutions/legends can survive the beliefs that gave rise to them establishes the larger historical problem of persistence with altered rationale before Lovejoy's notebook.

### Local Lovejoy seam

What can still belong to the notebook operation is narrower and more precise:

`ONE PRACTICE LOCALLY READ AS PROTECTION AGAINST RETURN`
->
`ANOTHER MEANING: RELEASE / TRANSMISSION OF SPIRIT`.

If the direct image indeed marks this as Lovejoy's connective sequence, the A2 object is **explanatory reassignment between two source-available interpretations**, not the theory of survivance or object souls.

Calibration:

- object-spirit explanation: Tylor A0;
- survivance/reinterpretation problem: Marillier/Tylor field A0–A1;
- exact p.73 `another meaning` relation and local succession of interpretations: W3 / provisional A2.

## 8. Consequence for the 005 -> 1906 argument

Rounds 23–24 materially lower the ceiling for any genealogy that treats notebook 005 as the origin of Lovejoy's later `quasi-mechanical` primitive philosophy.

The source field already contained:

- impersonal magic;
- magical sacrifice;
- sacramental/communion sacrifice;
- expiation;
- funerary provision;
- animal/human substitution;
- survivance and reinterpretation.

What the notebook more securely documents is an **operator practice**:

1. refuse classification by surface form alone;
2. separate multiple possible mechanisms;
3. ask which social bearer or competent agent instantiates the rite;
4. distinguish current form from original motive;
5. allow one inherited form to acquire another rationale;
6. test developmental arrows locally rather than globally.

Safe bridge to 1906:

> **The Paris notebook does not supply the component theory of impersonal efficacy from nothing. It shows Lovejoy repeatedly reallocating already available causal and ritual categories across ethnographic cases; the 1906 article later reaggregates one family of those relations at a broader comparative scale.**
