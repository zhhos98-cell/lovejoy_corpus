# Pocock ↔ Skinner, October 1968: `joint manifesto` and pre-canonical programme consciousness

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT LETTER QUOTATION THROUGH HIGH-GRADE PUBLISHED CONTROL / PRIVATE-PAPERS PROVENANCE IDENTIFIED / NO CLAIM OF JOINT AUTHORSHIP OR DRAFT EXCHANGE**

## Core result

A high-value letter places Skinner's methodological project inside an explicitly self-conscious programme network between the April 1968 PSA performance and the 1969 publication of `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas`.

Richard Whatmore's introduction to the Princeton Classics edition of J. G. A. Pocock's *The Machiavellian Moment* quotes a letter from Pocock to Quentin Skinner dated **8 October 1968**, cited to the **private papers of Quentin Skinner**.

Pocock tells Skinner that Skinner appears to be working on the same broad problem — political thought as an activity — from a standpoint `less sociological and more philosophical` than Pocock's, and then asks whether they should consider some kind of a **joint manifesto**.

This is a major pre-publication edge.

It establishes that by October 1968:

- Pocock recognized substantial methodological convergence with Skinner;
- Pocock simultaneously recognized a difference in explanatory orientation;
- the methodological work was self-consciously susceptible to being formulated as a shared programme or manifesto;
- this recognition occurred after Skinner's April 1968 PSA performance and before the 1969 journal publication.

Therefore:

`METHOD PRACTICE / DISCUSSION`
-> `PROGRAMME CONSCIOUSNESS`
precedes
`CANONICAL 1969 MANIFESTO-LIKE PUBLICATION`.

This does **not** prove that Skinner and Pocock ever drafted a joint manifesto or that Pocock saw the exact PSA/H&T manuscript.

## II. Pocock's distinction is itself UNIT × RELATION evidence

The letter does more than announce agreement.

Pocock distinguishes his own work from Skinner's by saying the latter seems more philosophical and less sociological.

The distinction fits later methodological divergence:

- Pocock increasingly privileges political languages/discourses and their historical availability;
- Skinner increasingly privileges linguistic action, intention and what agents are doing in using arguments/concepts.

Thus convergence at the programme level does not require identical preferred historical units or explanatory relations.

Safe formulation:

`SHARED PROBLEM FIELD / PROGRAMME AFFINITY`
!=
`IDENTICAL METHOD`.

This is directly consistent with the project's wider firewall:

`SAME SCHOOL LABEL != SAME UNIT × RELATION CONFIGURATION`.

## III. The letter sits inside reciprocal unpublished-work circulation

Whatmore's account also says new work circulated before publication among Pocock, Dunn and Skinner.

Pocock sent Dunn and Skinner a `last bunch of scripts`, most of which later became material in *Politics, Language, and Time* (1971), because he wanted to show what he had been doing toward a model of political thought as an activity.

This proves a broader prepublication ecology:

`UNPUBLISHED SCRIPTS`
<-> `COLLEAGUE READING / DISCUSSION`
<-> `PROGRAMME COMPARISON`.

However, preserve directionality:

`POCOCK SENT SCRIPTS TO SKINNER/DUNN`

is directly reported,

while

`SKINNER SENT THE 1968 PSA/H&T MANUSCRIPT TO POCOCK`

is not yet proven merely from this evidence.

Skinner 1969 separately thanks Pocock among readers who commented on earlier drafts, which **does** prove Pocock read some earlier Skinner draft. What remains open is whether that draft is identical with the 4 April 1968 conference typescript and when Pocock read it.

This gives a powerful combined edge:

`POCOCK ↔ SKINNER PREPUBLICATION TEXT/COMMENT ECOLOGY = DIRECT`

but

`EXACT VERSION IDENTITY = HOLD`.

## IV. Skinner's 2025 JHI retrospective independently confirms the correspondence corpus

Quentin Skinner's 2025 JHI article `J. G. A. Pocock: A Life in Letters` is described as a memoir based on his correspondence with Pocock between **1965 and 2020**.

This independently establishes that the private correspondence corpus survives and has been used directly by Skinner for publication.

Thus the 1968 Pocock letter is not an isolated quotation floating without archival context; it belongs to a substantial, surviving correspondence series in Skinner's possession/private papers.

Archive consequence:

`SKINNER PRIVATE PAPERS / POCOCK CORRESPONDENCE, 1965–2020`

is now a concrete S2a/programme-formation corpus.

The immediate high-value request is not generic `Skinner papers`, but specifically:

- Pocock ↔ Skinner letters 1967–69;
- enclosures/scripts accompanying letters;
- references to PSA paper / `Unimportance of the Great Texts`;
- references to `Meaning and Understanding`;
- discussion of Dunn, Laslett, Austin, intentions, languages, political thought as activity;
- any follow-up to the proposed joint manifesto.

## V. `Manifesto-consciousness` should be distinguished from later canonization

The 1969 essay is often retrospectively called a manifesto of Cambridge contextualism.

The October 1968 letter lets us distinguish:

### A. actor-level programme consciousness

A participant literally proposes a possible joint manifesto before Skinner's canonical article appears.

### B. published manifesto-like intervention

Skinner 1969 provides a powerful, portable polemical taxonomy and methodological argument.

### C. retrospective manifesto canonization

Later anthologies, histories and anniversary volumes stabilize `Meaning and Understanding` as a foundational manifesto.

Thus:

`MANIFESTO CONSCIOUSNESS`
!=
`MANIFESTO TEXT`
!=
`CANONIZED MANIFESTO STATUS`.

This distinction is valuable for the larger canon-formation argument.

---

## VI. Stronger anti-founder model

The correspondence pushes the early Cambridge story even further away from a one-author origin model.

Secure strands now include:

- Laslett's contextual Locke edition as a major earlier model;
- Dunn ↔ Skinner multi-year discussion;
- Dunn 1968 uses Austin; Skinner later credits Dunn's recognition of Austin's relevance;
- Skinner manuscript circulated to multiple readers including Mandelbaum and Pocock;
- Pocock circulated unpublished scripts to Dunn/Skinner;
- Pocock explicitly recognizes methodological overlap and proposes a joint manifesto;
- Skinner 1969 becomes the most canonically successful portable formulation.

Therefore:

`CANONICAL AUTHOR`
!=
`SOLE UNIT OF METHOD FORMATION`.

A better historical unit is:

`RECIPROCAL DRAFT / LETTER / DISCUSSION / PUBLICATION NETWORK`.

This is a direct application of the UNIT × RELATION framework to historiography itself.

## VII. Evidence firewall

Secure:

- Pocock letter date: 8 Oct 1968;
- letter to Skinner;
- methodological comparison (`same thing`, less sociological/more philosophical);
- proposal for some kind of joint manifesto;
- published provenance given as private papers of Quentin Skinner;
- Pocock circulated unpublished scripts to Dunn and Skinner;
- Skinner 1969 acknowledges Pocock as a reader of earlier drafts;
- Skinner 2025 JHI memoir draws on Pocock correspondence 1965–2020.

Open:

- exact Skinner draft Pocock read;
- whether it was before/after April PSA performance;
- whether Pocock's October letter follows reading that exact draft;
- whether any joint-manifesto notes were drafted;
- whether the proposal affected Skinner's 1969 revisions;
- whether H&T editors knew about these discussions.

Do not turn network adjacency into clause-level revision causation.
