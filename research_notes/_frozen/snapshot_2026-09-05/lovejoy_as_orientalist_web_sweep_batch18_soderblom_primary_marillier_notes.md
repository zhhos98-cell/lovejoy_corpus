# Batch 18 — Söderblom 1901 primary text: direct evidence that Marillier circulated his working notes to students

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced

## Research move

A full public-domain plaintext witness of Nathan Söderblom, *La vie future d’après le mazdéisme à la lumière des croyances parallèles dans les autres religions* (Paris: Ernest Leroux, 1901) has now been captured from Internet Archive (`laviefuturedapr00sdgoog`) and searched directly. This produces a major primary-source upgrade for the Marillier workshop model.

The decisive new evidence is not merely that Söderblom cites Marillier. Söderblom states in his **preface** that Marillier **gave him his own notes** for use in the book; later, at printed p.191, Söderblom explains that Marillier allowed him to consult **abundant notes on cosmogony and flood narratives** and that references taken directly from those extracts would be marked with an `M.`. The following pages contain a dense run of bibliographic citations explicitly marked `M.`.

This is direct evidence that Marillier’s personal thematic dossiers circulated into student research outputs.

## 1. Preface: Marillier gave Söderblom his notes

Söderblom thanks Antoine Meillet for years of Avestan teaching and advice, Jean Réville for spending many hours on the manuscript, and Léon Marillier for his support. He then states that Marillier had generously offered him something he would not have thought to ask for: **Marillier’s notes on part of the large comparative field in which Marillier possessed unusual competence**.

Söderblom sends the reader to printed p.191 for the concrete case.

Evidentiary significance:

- this is first-person testimony by a documented Marillier student;
- it shows teacher-to-student transfer of research notes, not merely lectures or published bibliography;
- it supplies a material mechanism by which Marillier’s long-built comparative dossiers could enter a student’s dissertation/book.

This does **not** prove that Lovejoy received the same privilege or the same sheets. It does, however, make such dossier-mediated transmission an observed practice inside the exact seminar environment in which Lovejoy was an active auditor.

## 2. Printed p.191: the notes are identified and their use is documented

At p.191, introducing a comparative study of world-destruction and renewal myths, Söderblom says he was able to traverse the immense comparative domain thanks to Marillier, who allowed him to consult **“ses notes abondantes relatives à la cosmogonie et aux récits de déluge.”**

A footnote then states in substance that notes drawn directly from Marillier’s extracts will be **marked with an `M.`**.

This is unusually strong provenance documentation. It gives us:

1. the subject of one Marillier dossier: cosmogony / flood narratives;
2. its physical/intellectual form: abundant notes/extracts;
3. the student-use procedure: consultation and selective incorporation;
4. an internal siglum (`M.`) identifying borrowed bibliographic extracts.

## 3. The following pages visibly use the `M.` apparatus

The Internet Archive text shows repeated `M.`-marked references immediately after p.191. Examples include sources on Bengal, Greek flood traditions, Peru, New France, Yucatán, Eskimo traditions, Polynesia, North America, California, India and South America.

Among the marked references are, for example:

- N. W. Hunter, *Annals of Rural Bengal*;
- Ternaux-Compans materials;
- Paul Le Jeune, *Relation de la Nouvelle-France*;
- Morillot, Eskimo myths and legends;
- A. Fornander;
- W. W. Gill, *Life in the Southern Isles*;
- *Journal of American Folklore*;
- A. Robinson, *Life in California*;
- von Martius;
- Duflot de Mofras;
- Wilkes and Hale, U.S. Exploring Expedition;
- several additional American/Polynesian ethnographic and travel sources.

The point is not that every one of these sources appears in 005. The important fact is that Söderblom’s book preserves a visible **bibliographic residue of Marillier’s note files**.

## 4. This directly validates the RAI MS 99 dossier model

RAI MS 99 contains a Marillier-attributed folder explicitly catalogued as:

> `Déluge: Asie, Amérique – Océanie`

alongside `Notes bibliographiques et divers` and the other teaching/research folders on survivance, origin of death, sacrifice, totemism, taboo, etc.

Söderblom 1901 independently reports having used Marillier’s abundant **cosmogony/flood notes** and then marks imported references `M.`. This is a striking match to the type of material represented by MS 99 `/8` and `/11`.

The safe formulation is:

> **Söderblom proves that Marillier possessed and circulated thematic bibliographic extract dossiers of the same kind as those represented by RAI MS 99.**

This does not yet prove that the surviving MS 99 `/8` sheets are the exact sheets Söderblom consulted. Image-level handwriting/provenance and bibliography comparison are still required for identity.

## 5. Söderblom’s use of Marillier goes beyond raw bibliography

The early comparative chapter of the book explicitly adopts the Tylor distinction between `continuation theory` and `retribution theory`, notes that **Marillier translated the former as `survivance`**, and retains the terminology `continuation` / `survivance`.

Söderblom’s stated analytic goal is to show that beliefs in continuation are not, in their original form, specifically moral or properly religious. At printed p.33 he then says that Marillier had already analyzed the reasons why the dead receive different destinies, and that he will take up Marillier’s study **with different divisions and from a somewhat different point of view**.

The same page contains an explicit note:

> Söderblom says he owes Marillier’s book many facts about the religion of “savage peoples” and followed Marillier’s 1898–99 EPHE conferences on the subject with great profit.

This gives a particularly useful model of student production:

- inherit a problem and source field;
- acknowledge the teacher’s prior analysis;
- rearrange the divisions;
- change the point of view;
- produce a distinct argument.

That is exactly the kind of transformation we should test in 005 rather than searching only for copied wording.

## 6. The `mana` passage is now primary, not secondary

The full text confirms the previously secondary-reported passage around printed p.37. Söderblom is discussing why some dead succeed in dangerous post-mortem journeys. The decisive factor is personal vigor, intellectual and bodily strength, and ability to perform extraordinary acts. He summarizes this with what he calls **the characteristic expression used by Marillier after the Melanesians: `le mana de l’homme`**.

The passage is therefore securely primary evidence that, by 1901, a Marillier student publicly attributed a generalized `mana` formulation to Marillier.

This strengthens the contextual genealogy:

> Codrington / Melanesian `mana` → Marillier’s comparative vocabulary and dossiers → same-seminar student Söderblom (1901) and, by a different analytical route, Lovejoy’s later theory of impersonal/transferable efficacy (1906).

It still does not establish that Lovejoy copied the term from Marillier; 005 has no secure `mana` reading.

## 7. Söderblom explicitly differentiates himself from Marillier

Later in the same chapter Söderblom introduces a section titled **“Comparaison avec le point de vue de M. Marillier.”** He says Marillier classifies differences in future destiny according to their relation to morality, while Söderblom wants a somewhat different analytical division.

He then engages the Steinmetz critique of Marillier in detail. Söderblom defends Marillier’s effort to judge indigenous conceptions by the moral or quasi-moral standards of the peoples studied, while criticizing attempts to redescribe strength, wealth, skill, manner of death, or burial provision as moral deserts merely to save a universal theory of post-mortem retribution.

The methodological point is especially important: **do not project the analyst’s categories backward into the actors’ world merely because the empirical arrangement looks irrational from the analyst’s standpoint.**

That operation is very close to the analytic habit visible in 005: separate outwardly similar future-life distinctions according to whether they arise from status, ritual qualification, magical efficacy, social membership, mode of death, or strictly moral guilt.

## 8. Revised material model of the Marillier seminar

Before this primary capture, our workshop reconstruction rested on:

- N. W. Thomas: student essays/presentations + Marillier criticism + peer discussion;
- Tarbell: Marillier physically spreading papers for inspection; Jeanne as amanuensis/proof reader/adviser;
- RAI MS 99: surviving thematic note/extract dossiers;
- Van Gennep: course changed documents and method;
- Lovejoy 005: course-linked working notebook.

Söderblom now supplies the missing direct mechanism:

> **Marillier handed/loaned his own notes to a student, who incorporated their bibliography into a research book and marked direct extracts with a source siglum.**

This makes the most plausible production ecology:

> Marillier thematic dossier / extracts
> ↕
> seminar problem + oral criticism
> ↕
> student notebook / essay / specialist research
> ↕
> revised publication with acknowledged or unacknowledged reclassification

For 005 the next high-value test is therefore not generic thematic comparison. It is **bibliographic sequence/provenance comparison**: distinctive source order, page numbers, clusters and abbreviations between MS 99 and 005.
