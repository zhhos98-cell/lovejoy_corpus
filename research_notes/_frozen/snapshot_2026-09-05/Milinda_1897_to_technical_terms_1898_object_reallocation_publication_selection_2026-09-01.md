# 1897 Milinda -> 1898 technical terms: object reallocation and publication selection

Date: 2026-09-01  
Status: **DIRECT PUBLICATION-TEXT CONTROL + STRONG DOCUMENTARY RECOMPOSITION HYPOTHESIS**

## 1. Research question

The AOS documentary chain now contains two distinct 1897 descriptions of Lovejoy's Buddhist communication:

1. **10 Apr 1897 Final Circular** — `Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`;
2. **22–24 Apr 1897 Baltimore Proceedings, No. 30** — `On the meaning of the Buddhist technical terms ...`;
3. **1898 JAOS publication** — `The Buddhistic technical terms upādāna and upādisesa`.

The remaining question is not simply whether the 1897 communication preceded the published article. That is now secure at topic/title level. The more specific question is:

> **What role does the Milinda Pañha actually play inside the 1898 published argument, and what does that tell us about the apparent shift from a Milinda-centered announced object to a technical-term publication?**

This dossier does not claim manuscript identity between the 10 Apr announcement, the late-April AOS communication, and the 1898 published article. It reconstructs only the documentary/publication structure that can be directly observed.

## 2. 1898 article: the published object is not the Milinda Pañha as a whole

Lovejoy's published article opens by defining its problem as the meaning of **upādāna** and the kindred **upādi-**, with consequences for the `paṭicca-samuppāda`, Buddhist psychology, rebirth, and Nirvāṇa.

The article explicitly describes itself as a **preliminary study** directed toward establishing the meanings of these terms by examining their use in the Piṭakas.

The published burden is therefore lexical / semantic / doctrinal and corpus-based:

`competing interpretation of a technical term`

`-> textual usage across canonical witnesses`

`-> relation among upādāna, skandhas, karma, rebirth / causation`

`-> separate analysis of upādi / upādisesa and Nirvāṇa`.

This is materially different from the 10 Apr announcement's object-description, `Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`.

## 3. Every explicit Milinda citation in the 1898 article is local witness evidence

A complete text search of the 1898 article yields three explicit Milinda loci, all in **Section I (`upādāna`)** and all before Section II (`upādi / upādisesa`) begins.

### 3.1 Milinda Pañha p. 65 — supports the karma side of a causal distinction

Lovejoy distinguishes the causal functions of **karma** and **upādāna** in rebirth:

- karma, in the special usage he reconstructs, explains the **particular condition** in which a person is reborn;
- upādāna explains the **fact of rebirth itself**.

The first proposition carries a footnote to Samyutta material translated by Warren **and Milinda Pañha p. 65**.

Milinda therefore functions here as one textual witness among others for a **specific causal allocation**, not as the overarching object of interpretation.

### 3.2 Milinda Pañha p. 33 — supports the upādāna side of the same distinction

The immediately paired claim — that upādāna is the cause of rebirth *per se* — is footnoted to previously cited passages and **MP p. 33**.

Again, the Milinda is evidentiary support for a proposition about a technical term's causal role.

### 3.3 Milinda Pañha p. 60 — supports a local place in the nidāna analysis

Later in Section I, Lovejoy reconstructs the middle section of the `paṭicca-samuppāda` as a psychological analysis of sensation and volition. He says the analysis reaches the concrete fact of sensation, with pleasure/pain characteristics, before desire and then upādāna arise.

That local proposition is footnoted to **Milinda Pañha p. 60** together with Warren.

Thus the third Milinda use is again a **local witness to a proposition inside a larger causal/psychological reconstruction**.

## 4. Milinda disappears when the article turns to upādisesa

Section II begins when Lovejoy turns to the meaning of `upādi-` in `upādisesa`. The evidentiary field changes to:

- Childers;
- Rhys Davids;
- Oldenberg;
- Sutta Nipāta;
- Itivuttaka and related canonical passages;
- etymological and scribal problems involving `upādi / upadhi`;
- Sanskrit Nyāya and Sāṃkhya controls.

No explicit Milinda citation appears in this second half of the article.

This matters because the eventual published title contains **two** technical objects, `upādāna` and `upādisesa`, while the Milinda Pañha functions only inside the first object's proof apparatus.

The published article therefore cannot be described accurately as a `Milinda paper` merely under a new title.

## 5. Documentary sequence: primary object -> witness? Strong hypothesis, not manuscript fact

The observed sequence is unusually suggestive:

### Documentary state A — 10 Apr 1897

Primary object named in title:

`Milinda-pañha`

Task named in title:

`critical summary of the argument`.

### Documentary state B — late Apr 1897 Proceedings

Primary object named in title:

`Buddhist technical terms ...`

Task named in title:

`meaning`.

### Documentary state C — 1898 publication

Primary objects:

`upādāna + upādisesa`.

Methodological burden:

`usage / semantic relation / causal-doctrinal function / competing interpretation`.

Role of Milinda:

`three local supporting witnesses, all inside the upādāna section`.

The strongest interpretive hypothesis is therefore an **object reallocation**:

> **A text that appears as the announced primary object on 10 April is, in the eventual publication, no longer the object whose argument is summarized. It has become one member of a larger evidentiary corpus used to prove local propositions about a technical term.**

This is a strong documentary/publication hypothesis, not a demonstrated manuscript genealogy. A different paper could have replaced the 10 Apr item before the meeting. Until a submitted manuscript, abstract, or correspondence is recovered, use `apparent reallocation / recomposition`, not `Lovejoy transformed the same paper`.

## 6. Relation to canonical 004 publication-selection result

The canonical dossier `004_proof_architecture_publication_selection_consolidated.md` already establishes a manuscript-to-print asymmetry:

- notebook 004 contains broad Sāṃkhya/Buddhism genealogies, taxonomies, metaphysical possibilities and system-identification problems;
- the 1898 publication selects narrower semantic/textual/causal claims that can be locally controlled;
- broad genealogy is omitted or softened while operators of diagnostic specificity and causal allocation survive.

The AOS title history now supplies an **independent public documentary trace** consistent with that archival result.

The observed shift is:

`whole named text / argument-summary object`

`-> technical-semantic object`

`-> local textual witnesses allocated to proposition-specific burdens`.

This is not merely a shorter paper. It is a change in **what counts as the primary historical object of the argument**.

### Strong combined formulation

> **Notebook-to-print comparison had already suggested that Lovejoy's 1898 publication pruned a much broader research space into locally controllable semantic and causal claims. The newly recovered AOS title sequence gives a public chronological analogue of the same selection process: the 10 April announcement foregrounded the Milinda-pañha as an argument to be critically summarized, whereas the final meeting record foregrounded the meaning of Buddhist technical terms, and the 1898 article uses the Milinda only as one textual witness within the upādāna proof structure.**

The phrase `analogue of the same selection process` is safer than claiming both traces derive from one documented editorial decision.

## 7. Relation to proof architecture

The Milinda reallocation is especially important because it illustrates the project's proof-level partition in material form.

In the published article, `Milinda Pañha` is not asked to prove `Buddhism as a whole` or a textual genealogy. Its citations are allocated to specific propositions:

`Milinda witness`

`-> karma / rebirth-condition claim`

`Milinda witness`

`-> upādāna / rebirth-per-se claim`

`Milinda witness`

`-> sensation / nidāna-sequence claim`.

The text is thus handled at **witness/proposition level** rather than as an indivisible doctrinal whole.

This fits the broader 004 rule:

> **stronger propositions require their own evidence; one source does not automatically carry every higher-level inference.**

## 8. What this changes historiographically

The 1898 article should now be placed in a visible pre-publication sequence rather than treated as a single inaugural event.

The strongest chronology is:

`Harvard advanced Pāli, 1896–97`

`-> 10 Apr 1897: Milinda-pañha argument-summary communication announced`

`-> late Apr 1897: technical-terms communication recorded by AOS and read by title in Lovejoy's absence`

`-> 1898: technical-terms article published; Milinda redistributed as local evidence inside the upādāna argument`.

This supports the historical language **research programme / publication pathway / object recomposition**.

It does not establish:

- one continuous manuscript;
- Lovejoy's conscious editorial rationale;
- the exact date on which `upādisesa` entered the project;
- that the late-April missing terms were definitely `upādāna` and `upādisesa` before visual control;
- Lanman's supervisory role.

## 10. Evidentiary statuses

### DIRECT PRIMARY CLOSED

- 10 Apr Milinda-centered announced title.
- late-April technical-terms title at AOS.
- 1898 published technical-terms title.

- all three explicit Milinda loci are in Section I / upādāna.
- each is attached to a local proposition/citation burden rather than an article-wide interpretation of the Milinda.

### STRONG INTERPRETIVE RESULT

- the publication path displays **object-scale narrowing/recomposition** from a text-centered announced object toward proposition-level technical semantics.
- the eventual publication reallocates Milinda from apparent title-level object to local evidentiary witness.
- this public title/citation sequence is strongly consonant with the independent notebook 004 -> 1898 `publication selection` asymmetry.

### HOLD

- whether the 10 Apr paper was withdrawn and replaced rather than transformed;
- exact missing technical-term names in the printed 1897 Proceedings line;
- conscious editorial rationale or supervisory intervention.

## Publication-safe core

> **The April 1897 AOS records reveal a change in the scale of Lovejoy's announced Buddhist object. On 10 April his communication was described as a critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha; the final meeting record instead described a paper on the meaning of Buddhist technical terms. In the 1898 published article, the Milinda Pañha appears only as local textual evidence in the upādāna section: Lovejoy cites it to support specific claims about karma, rebirth, upādāna, and the nidāna sequence, while the separate upādisesa analysis proceeds without explicit Milinda citation. The sequence therefore strongly suggests object recomposition—from a named text as primary object toward technical-semantic propositions for which that text becomes one witness—although the surviving documentation does not yet prove that all three stages were versions of the same manuscript.**
