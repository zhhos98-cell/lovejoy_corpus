## Core result

The one-page 1976 Berki/Parekh communication in *Political Theory* is now directly readable rather than metadata-only.

R. N. Berki and Bhiku Parekh, `On Quentin Skinner's “Some Problems in the Analysis of Political Thought and Action” (Volume 2, No. 3, August 1974)`, *Political Theory* 4.2 (May 1976), p.235, DOI `10.1177/009059177600400209`.

Direct vLex machine-readable text exposes the complete communication.

Its importance is sharper than merely proving that the 1973–74 debate continued.

Berki and Parekh explicitly distinguish **two different possible research units**:

1. Skinner as a historically developing individual thinker, whose development/current position would require reading across multiple works;
2. Skinner's 1969 article as a bounded textual unit selected because, in their judgment, it clearly embodied a specific methodological point of view whose adequacy they wanted to assess philosophically.

Their defense of their 1973 procedure is therefore explicitly unit-relative to the question being asked.

Compact rule:

> **THE LEGITIMATE ANALYTIC UNIT DEPENDS ON THE PROPOSITION / RESEARCH QUESTION.**

And the corresponding ceiling:

> **AN ARTICLE MAY BE TREATED AS REPRESENTATIVE OF A METHOD POSITION WITHOUT BECOMING A SUFFICIENT UNIT FOR THE AUTHOR'S INTELLECTUAL DEVELOPMENT OR PRESENT POSITION.**

This is a direct contemporary analogue of the project's codebook rule:

`UNIT TYPE BELONGS TO CLAIM, NOT ONCE-FOR-ALL TO PHYSICAL OBJECT`.

## I. Direct text: why they selected the 1969 article

The communication opens by saying their attention had only just been drawn to Skinner's August 1974 response.

They acknowledge that Skinner's replies and new points would warrant extended treatment, but choose to answer one accusation: that their earlier critique had been insufficiently fair because it singled out one Skinner article for detailed analysis.

Their answer is explicit.

If their purpose had been:

- to trace the development of Skinner's thought; or
- to outline his present position,

then the objection would have force.

But they say their actual purpose was different:

- to assess the adequacy of a **specific approach** to the history of ideas;
- they judged Skinner's 1969 article to articulate that approach especially clearly;
- consequently they concentrated on that article.

Thus:

`ACTOR-DEVELOPMENT QUESTION`
requires a different evidentiary corpus/unit from
`PHILOSOPHICAL ADEQUACY OF A METHOD-POSITION QUESTION`.

---

## II. Actor vs representative methodological unit

The sharpest formulation in the communication distinguishes Skinner:

- not primarily as a `unique historical individual`;
- but as the representative of a specific methodological point of view embodied in the article.

This should not be paraphrased into a claim that Skinner simply **was** the method position.

The historical operation is narrower:

`1969 ARTICLE`
-> selected as
`REPRESENTATIVE TEXTUAL INSTANCE OF METHOD POSITION`
for one philosophical critique.

The proof burden is therefore asymmetric.

The article may license criticism of the approach **as articulated there**.

It does not, by itself, license claims about:

- Skinner's complete oeuvre;
- the diachronic development of his methodology;
- his 1974 present position;
- all contextualist scholarship;
- later Cambridge-school canonization.

Core firewall:

`REPRESENTATIVE FOR CLAIM X`
!= `EXHAUSTIVE OF ACTOR Y`.

## III. This clarifies the 1973 critique's historical unit

The earlier repo dossier had to infer much of Parekh/Berki's 1973 positive architecture from Skinner's 1974 response because the 1973 full article facsimile was not yet directly controlled.

The 1976 communication now directly clarifies at least one methodological fact about the 1973 article:

> **They understood their own exercise as a philosophical critique of a specific method-position embodied in a selected article, not as an intellectual biography of Skinner.**

This is an important source-state upgrade.

It explains why a one-article focus can be methodologically defensible while still being inadequate for a different proposition about authorial development.

No claim should be made that Skinner accepted this defense.

## IV. The communication also confirms carrier-role compression

The argumentative route is now directly controlled at three carrier states:

1. 1973 JHI — full-length lead critique article, pp.163–184;
2. 1974 PT — three-part Skinner symposium culminating in Skinner's long response, pp.277–303;
3. 1976 PT — one-page `Communications` reply, p.235.

Thus the same controversy changes publication role and scale:

`FULL-LENGTH CRITIQUE ARTICLE`
-> `SYMPOSIUM / EXTENDED RESPONSE`
-> `ONE-PAGE COMMUNICATION`.

But the 1976 text explicitly says the larger dispute would deserve extended treatment and then deliberately takes only one point.

Therefore:

`SHORTER CARRIER ROLE`
!= `ARGUMENT EXHAUSTED OR SETTLED`.

Carrier compression is not substantive closure.

---

## V. Relation to Skinner's 1974 objection

Skinner's 1974 response treats Parekh/Berki's one-article focus as insufficiently attentive to the evolution/qualification of his position.

Berki/Parekh's 1976 answer shows that the disagreement is partly a **unit-choice dispute**:

- Skinner evaluates their article against a diachronic actor/oeuvre unit;
- Berki/Parekh defend a bounded article/method-position unit because their proposition was philosophical rather than historico-biographical.

This does not automatically establish that either unit choice is correct for every question.

It shows that the adequacy of a unit must be assessed relative to the proposition.

Thus:

`UNIT ADEQUACY`
is a function of
`QUESTION + CLAIM + PROOF BURDEN`,
not a fixed property of `ARTICLE` or `AUTHOR`.
