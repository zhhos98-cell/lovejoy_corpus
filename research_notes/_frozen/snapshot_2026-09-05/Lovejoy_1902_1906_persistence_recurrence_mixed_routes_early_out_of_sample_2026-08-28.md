# Lovejoy 1902–1906 — persistence and recurrence as claim-level warrant routes: early out-of-sample control

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **EARLY OUT-OF-SAMPLE CONTROL / TWO-ROUTE MODEL REVISED / DIRECT PRIMARY + REPO PRIMARY CONTROLS**

Primary controls:

- Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Religion and the Time-Process`, *American Journal of Theology* 6.3 (1902), 439–472;
- Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`, *The Monist* 16.3 (1906), 357–382.

## Core result

The mature distinction reconstructed as:

- `persistence through historical transformation`;
- `recurrence without demonstrated lineage`

is already useful for Lovejoy's early practice, but it needs an important correction.

These are **not two intrinsic kinds of idea**.

They are two **claim-level warrant routes** that can apply to different pairings or historical segments involving the same candidate invariant.

The 1902 `otherworldliness` inquiry is mixed:

- at the broad Western/Indian comparative level, otherworldliness functions as a recurrent conceptual type without requiring a single transmission lineage;
- within the Western sequence, Lovejoy traces a historically continuous Platonic/Neo-Platonic/Christian career in which inheritance, internal dialectic and transformation matter.

The 1906 `primitive energetics` inquiry is much closer to a pure recurrence/type case:

- historically independent North-American, Oceanic and African fields are correlated through a shared lexical/causal mechanism;
- their independence blocks a simple borrowing story but **strengthens the claim that the same causal type recurs independently**.

This yields a major correction:

> **historical independence is relation-sensitive evidence: it weakens genealogy while potentially strengthening recurrence/type robustness.**

---

# I. 1902 otherworldliness — one invariant, two historical routes

Lovejoy's 1902 inquiry treats the difficulty of religion with time/becoming as a broad historical problem and follows forms of `otherworldliness` in both Western and Indian civilizations.

At this widest comparative scale the relevant invariant is thinner than a complete doctrinal system.

It concerns, approximately:

- location of ultimate reality/value beyond temporal becoming;
- depreciation or relativization of the temporal world;
- aspiration toward an eternal/perfect order;
- the conceptual difficulty of assigning positive worth to process/change as such.

The comparative fact that Indian and Western formations can instantiate this structure does not itself establish historical borrowing.

Thus one route is:

`different civilizational/doctrinal settings`
→ `diagnostic conceptual/presuppositional convergence`
→ `comparative recurrence/type`.

No common direct lineage is required merely for the type judgment.

---

# II. Inside the Western sequence, the route changes to persistence

Lovejoy then gives the Western history a much more continuous architecture.

Platonism supplies a major basis for Western otherworldliness; Neo-Platonic developments and Christian appropriation continue/reconfigure it.

The historical object is no longer merely:

`another independent token of otherworldliness`.

It is also:

`Platonic conceptual complex`
→ `internal dialectical development`
→ `Neo-Platonic elaboration`
→ `Christian reception/transformation`.

Here historical continuity itself contributes warrant for identifying a career of the presupposition/motive across changed configurations.

This anticipates the later persistence route:

> **large changes in doctrinal configuration can be tolerated when an historical path of inheritance/rearticulation is visible.**

---

# III. Eastern influence is therefore a separate proposition

The crucial early firewall is that cross-cultural affinity does not automatically become genealogy.

Lovejoy rejects or resists proposed Eastern influence where the internal dialectic of the Platonic tradition is sufficient to account for the relevant result.

Thus three propositions must be separated:

1. `Indian formation I and Western formation W instantiate a comparable otherworldly structure`;
2. `W belongs to a historically continuous Platonic/Neo-Platonic development`;
3. `I historically caused or transmitted the relevant structure to W`.

Lovejoy can accept (1) and (2) while withholding (3).

This is a particularly clean early demonstration that **type recurrence and historical persistence can coexist without cross-civilizational transmission**.

---

# IV. 1906 primitive energetics — historical independence becomes positive recurrence evidence

The 1906 paper is built differently.

Lovejoy correlates materials from:

- North America (`manitou`, `oki`, `wakonda`, etc.);
- Oceania (`mana` and related usage);
- African/Bantu vocabularies and practices.

He reconstructs a causal-semantic profile involving:

- impersonal or less-than-personal efficacy;
- localization/concentration of power;
- communicability/transfer;
- direct effect through contact;
- regulation, insulation and practical manipulation;
- quasi-mechanical causal expectation.

At p.376 the independence of observers/fields is itself emphasized. The fields are not presented as descendants of one common historical institution.

The inference is therefore approximately:

`historically independent fields A, B, C`
+
`diagnostically convergent lexical/causal structure M`
→ `M is a recurrent comparative type`.

This is recurrence without genealogy by design.

---

# V. Independence has opposite evidentiary valence depending on relation type

This is the strongest new result.

## For genealogy / transmission

Independence means:

`no known carrier / common descent`
→ weakens `A influenced B`.

## For recurrence / comparative type

Independence can mean:

`same diagnostic structure appears despite lack of transmission`
→ strengthens robustness/generality of the type.

Therefore the same historical fact can have opposite inferential effects under different relation predicates.

Project shorthand:

> **evidence has no relation-independent valence.**

This is an unusually clean example of the wider jurisdiction thesis.

---

# VI. Recurrence cases require aggregation warrant, not genealogical warrant

1906 helps specify why recurrence-route identity can be difficult even without transmission questions.

The historian still must show:

- that the local lexical/ritual evidence has been correctly interpreted;
- that the regional mechanisms are diagnostically similar enough;
- that the invariant has not been made too thin;
- that family resemblance has not been silently converted into one concept;
- that an analyst's comparative type may legitimately be reattributed to historical actors as a `fundamental concept` or `primitive philosophy`;
- that sampling supports the breadth of the demographic/civilizational predicate.

Lovejoy explicitly acknowledges evidentiary incompleteness and procedural provisionality, but that does not itself solve these aggregation problems.

Thus recurrence does not have a lower burden than persistence. It has a **different burden**.

---

# VII. Persistence cases can borrow warrant from the transformation path

In 1902's Western sequence, historical continuity supplies evidence unavailable to the 1906 cross-cultural comparison:

- predecessor/successor relation;
- inherited conceptual vocabulary;
- identifiable doctrinal tradition;
- internal dialectic;
- reception/rearticulation.

This can allow a thinner invariant to remain historically trackable because the transformation path supplies part of the identity account.

But continuity is not enough by itself. A historical lineage can contain major conceptual rupture, and the historian must still say what persists.

Provisional hypothesis strengthened by this early control:

> **persistence-route identity may tolerate greater conceptual transformation than recurrence-route identity because documented historical continuity contributes independent warrant; recurrence-route identity must compensate through greater diagnosticity of the invariant.**

This should be tested case by case rather than assumed as a universal law.

---

# VIII. The two routes can coexist inside one history

The 1902 essay prevents a binary classification of whole works.

A single historical inquiry can contain:

- recurrence between independently developed/culturally separated formations;
- persistence within one historical tradition;
- rejected transmission hypotheses connecting the two;
- later recombination of inherited and independently available materials.

Therefore code the **edge/pair/claim**, not the book, as:

- `PERSISTENCE`;
- `RECURRENCE`;
- `MIXED`;
- `UNCERTAIN`.

This is methodologically important for the Great Chain as well, which contains both continuous careers and independent returns/revivals of conceptual structures.

---

# IX. Revised Gate-A architecture

The current identity procedure should now read:

### A0 — object type
What kind of candidate unit is this?

### A1 — identity route
For this pair/segment, is the claim:
- persistence through lineage/transformation;
- recurrence without lineage;
- mixed;
- uncertain?

### A2 — invariant selection
What feature is held fixed?

### A3 — invariant thickness / diagnosticity
How much content is required to count as the same type?

### A4 — tolerated transformation
Which changes are non-identity-destroying?

### A5 — aggregation/representativeness
How are multiple tokens promoted into a type, tradition, movement or collective actor-level category?

Only then proceed to Gate B:

- logical relations;
- psychological relations;
- causal mediation;
- historical/genetic relations;
- causal weight/propagation.

---

# X. Relation to `logicalian` vs literal logic

This early comparison reinforces the original correction that governs the branch.

The operation is `logician-like` in the sense that Lovejoy gives the same datum different inferential force depending on the relation under test.

But the relevant historical work is not formal logic.

The classification objects are:

- comparative religious motives;
- philosophical traditions;
- ethnographic mechanisms;
- cross-cultural semantic/causal types.

Formal logic later provides one explicit technical arena for relation-space and inferential restriction. It does not supply the historical aggregation criteria used here.
