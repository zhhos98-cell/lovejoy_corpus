# Lovejoy 1906 -> 1914 — Brinton, Leuba, priority claims, and causal reallocation

Date: 2026-09-03  
Status: **BRINTON 1897 DIRECT PRIMARY / LOVEJOY 1906 DIRECT-READ PROJECT CONCORDANCE / LEUBA 1912 DIRECT PRIMARY / LOVEJOY 1914 DIRECT PRIMARY / PRIORITY CLAIM SOURCE-OWNERSHIP RECUT**

## Core result

Lovejoy's 1914 review of James H. Leuba is a decisive actor-level retrospective on what Lovejoy thought his 1906 `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` had accomplished.

It also exposes a priority dispute that must be separated proposition by proposition.

The strongest source-critical result is:

> **Lovejoy did not discover the existence of impersonal power-beliefs, native power vocabularies, pre-/non-animistic power, or cross-cultural power comparison. What is most defensibly distinctive in his 1906 configuration is a reallocation of causal priority: he moves the explanatory center away from psychic/volitional, supernormal, or magic-specific interpretations and toward efficacious, quasi-mechanical, transferable causation; he carries that causal relation across the magic/religion boundary, ranks it above personality, and makes it architectonic for `primitive philosophy`.**

Lovejoy's own 1914 claim that his paper was the `original formulation` of the hypothesis and its principal evidence is therefore historically important as an **actor priority claim**, but cannot be accepted at broad resolution after upstream controls.

## I. Brinton 1897 already contains impersonal power + linguistic proof

Direct primary:

Daniel G. Brinton, *Religions of Primitive Peoples* (1897).

Brinton's general causal theory is explicitly psychic/voluntarist.

He says the universal postulate of religion is:

`CONSCIOUS VOLITION`
-> `ULTIMATE SOURCE OF ALL FORCE`.

Behind the phenomenal world stands `Mind`, `conscious Will`, or `Intelligence` analogous to human mentality.

Yet Brinton's own developmental account also says that the primitive divine influence is initially:

- `vague`;
- `impersonal`;
- `undefined`;

before becoming differentiated and personified.

He then appeals explicitly to language as `conclusive evidence`, choosing geographically separated examples:

- Dakota `wakan`;
- Peruvian `huaca / wakan`;
- South-Sea `mana`.

He says these terms need not denote personality and can designate power present in persons, animals or things.

Thus, before Lovejoy 1906, Brinton already supplies:

`IMPERSONAL / UNDEFINED POWER`
+
`NATIVE POWER TERMS`
+
`CROSS-REGIONAL LINGUISTIC COMPARISON`
+
`LATER PERSONIFICATION`.

What remains decisive is Brinton's causal interpretation:

> impersonal power itself is generated from the subconscious / human sense of will and ultimately belongs inside a theory in which conscious volition is the source of force.

Public direct control: Project Gutenberg, *Religions of Primitive Peoples*, esp. Lecture II, printed pp.47–63.

## II. Lovejoy 1906 reallocates the same problem to efficacy / causation

Direct-read project authority:

`research_notes/lovejoy_as_orientalist_web_sweep_batch12_1906_primary_concordance.md`.

Lovejoy begins by attacking Brinton, Guyau, Tylor, Spencer and Grant Allen for giving personality / anthropomorphism foundational status.

His positive category is:

- impersonal;
- quasi-mechanical;
- diffused / interconnected;
- unequally localized;
- communicable / transferable;
- manipulable through contact, insulation and ritual procedure.

He says it is a category **more fundamental than personality**.

He also rejects Frazer's allocation:

`MECHANICAL CAUSATION -> MAGIC`

versus

`PERSONAL RELATION -> RELIGION`.

Instead quasi-mechanical efficacy can structure material conventionally called religious as well as magical.

The paper's lexical demonstration is organized through North-American, Oceanic and Bantu power terms and repeatedly asks whether ethnographic translations such as `god` or `spirit` have incorrectly personified what indigenous usage presents as power / efficacy.

Thus the strongest Brinton -> Lovejoy contrast is not:

`PERSONALITY`
vs
`IMPERSONALITY`.

Brinton himself already has an impersonal phase.

It is:

### Brinton

`HUMAN / SUBCONSCIOUS WILL`
-> `IMPERSONAL UNDEFINED POWER`
-> `DIFFERENTIATION / PERSONIFICATION`.

### Lovejoy

`IMPERSONAL QUASI-MECHANICAL EFFICACY`
-> `CAUSAL CATEGORY MORE FUNDAMENTAL THAN PERSONALITY`
-> personal / spirit interpretations become secondary, partial or sometimes mistranslated allocations.

This is a **causal-priority reallocation**.

## III. Leuba 1912 reproduces the dispute and partially agrees with Lovejoy

Direct primary:

James H. Leuba, *A Psychological Study of Religion: Its Origin, Function and Future* (1912), esp. pp.74–84.

Leuba directly discusses Lovejoy 1906 while treating non-personal power.

He quotes Lovejoy's criticism of Marett:

- the basic idea is not primarily the unpredictable / abnormal / portentous;
- it is force conceived as working through regular and intelligible laws and capable of study/control;
- Lovejoy proposes `Primitive Energetics` for the belief-family.

Leuba dissents from Lovejoy on the strength of `regular and intelligible laws`, arguing that wakonda/manitou/mana remain unpredictable and magical rather than mechanical.

But Leuba then develops his own `dynamism` around active impersonal power and states that generic terms formerly translated as personal divinity often designate force/power.

Therefore Leuba's position is not simple rejection of Lovejoy.

The disagreement is partly about:

- degree of regularity / law-likeness;
- terminology;
- the religious/non-religious boundary.

## IV. Lovejoy 1914 gives an actor-level retrospective definition of his 1906 claim

Direct primary:

Arthur O. Lovejoy, review of James H. Leuba, *A Psychological Study of Religion*, *International Journal of Ethics* 24.2 (1 Jan. 1914): 216–220, DOI `10.1086/intejethi.24.2.2376508`.

On pp.218–19 Lovejoy explicitly corrects Leuba's presentation of the 1906 argument.

Lovejoy first concedes that the first important expression of the **general non-animistic tendency** was Marett's 1900 paper, and adds that accumulating ethnographic evidence was independently forcing the same general view upon many students.

This is an important actor-level field-ownership concession:

`GENERAL NON-ANIMISTIC TURN`
!=
`LOVEJOY PRIORITY`.

Lovejoy then narrows his ownership claim.

He says the theory Leuba accepts concerning the **origin and content of the primitive idea of impersonal power** is essentially the theory Lovejoy had advanced in 1906.

He identifies his own labels as:

- `primitive energetics`;
- `manitouism`.

He says he tried to establish the wide diffusion and importance of the conception chiefly through:

> examination of the **mode of use of the principal native terms** expressing the idea.

Most strikingly, he describes the 1906 paper as the **`original formulation` of a hypothesis (and of the principal evidence for it)** of which Leuba makes extensive use.

He then says Leuba still makes less use of the hypothesis than the facts justify, because mana/manitou interpenetrates other early beliefs and significantly affects the development of religion.

This is the strongest actor-level retrospective statement currently recovered for what Lovejoy believed he owned in the 1906 intervention.

## V. Lovejoy's 1914 priority claim must itself be decomposed

The 1914 sentence should not be accepted or rejected globally.

### Priority claim A — general non-animistic / impersonal-power problem

Lovejoy himself concedes Marett 1900 priority for the first important general expression.

Brinton 1897 additionally already contains impersonal, non-personal power vocabulary and cross-regional linguistic comparison.

Therefore:

`GENERAL IMPERSONAL / NON-ANIMISTIC PROBLEM`
!=
`LOVEJOY ORIGINAL`.

### Priority claim B — native power terms as evidence

Brinton 1897 already uses Dakota `wakan`, Peruvian `wakan/huaca` and Polynesian `mana` as linguistic evidence for non-personal power.

Hewitt 1902 and the American ethnological field further develop `orenda` / native power terminology.

Therefore:

`NATIVE POWER-TERM EVIDENCE`
!=
`LOVEJOY ORIGINAL`.

### Priority claim C — impersonal potency as causal category

Hewitt 1902 treats `orenda` as mystic potency / efficient cause.

Hubert–Mauss 1902–03 correlate mana/orenda/other power notions as transmissible force/activity and causal medium.

Therefore:

`IMPERSONAL POWER AS CAUSAL POTENCY`
!=
`LOVEJOY ORIGINAL IN BROAD FORM`.

### Priority claim D — cross-cultural power reaggregation

Hubert–Mauss explicitly correlate Melanesian mana, Huron/Iroquois orenda, ancient Indian neuter brahman and other analogues before 1906.

Therefore:

`MANA / ORENDA / RELATED TERMS -> GENERAL POWER CATEGORY`
!=
`LOVEJOY ORIGINAL`.

### Priority claim E — Lovejoy's exact configuration

The most defensible residual is narrower:

`POWER / EFFICACY`
-> treated as the diagnostic causal relation rather than supernormality / awe / conscious will
-> carried across the magic/religion jurisdiction
-> ranked as `more fundamental than personality`
-> elevated into architectonic `primitive philosophy`
-> supported through a proposition-sensitive rereading of ethnographic / grammatical evidence.

This exact configuration may remain historically distinctive even where its ingredients are upstream.

That is a **configurational / relation-level** claim, not conceptual priority for impersonal power.

## VI. Source criticism sharpens the residual further

The 1906 proof architecture does not simply collect native terms.

Current project controls show Lovejoy repeatedly restricts what a witness can prove:

- early missionary / observer evidence may establish a concrete application without establishing one universal abstract meaning;
- a reporter's personification may be rejected while the underlying custom / native association is retained;
- later Algonkin grammatical evidence is used to test whether personification is encoded in language;
- Hetherwick's Yao noun-class evidence is used as an independent grammatical control against translating `mulungu` as a personal being.

Thus Lovejoy's stronger residual can be formulated as:

`FIELD-AVAILABLE POWER DOSSIER`
-> `RECODE RELATION AS EFFICACY / CAUSATION`
-> `AUDIT REPORTER TRANSLATION / PERSONIFICATION PROPOSITION BY PROPOSITION`
-> `USE GRAMMAR / NATIVE USAGE AS INDEPENDENT SEMANTIC CONTROL`
-> `REAGGREGATE SURVIVING RELATIONS AS PRIMITIVE ENERGETICS`.

Again, source criticism and grammatical semantics themselves are not Lovejoy inventions.

The residual is the **particular reallocation and recombination**.

## VII. A second 1914 methodological statement: definition must not decide the evidence in advance

The same Leuba review ends with another unusually relevant criticism.

Lovejoy says Leuba's general conclusions are confused by failing to distinguish whether a `definition` of religion is:

1. a preferred stipulation for use of the word;
2. a statement of common elements across diverse historical usages;
3. an analytical description of complex observable phenomena at different individual/racial developmental phases.

Lovejoy says the third is what is wanted.

He objects when Leuba concludes that religion `is` or `is not` something merely because the relevant element was excluded by an initial definition.

This is a direct actor-level anti-circularity rule:

`DEFINITIONAL BOUNDARY`
!=
`EMPIRICALLY ESTABLISHED PHENOMENAL BOUNDARY`.

That directly clarifies the 1906 rejection of Frazer-like magic/religion jurisdictional partition:

> a classificatory definition cannot, by itself, determine which causal relations actually occur in practices called `religious`.

This strengthens the interpretation of 1906 as **category-boundary reallocation**, not only a competing theory of mana.

## VIII. Historiographical consequence

The 1914 review produces an unusually useful three-level ownership structure.

### Actor's own ownership map

Lovejoy:

- concedes the general non-animistic field turn to Marett / distributed ethnographic pressure;
- claims the more specific hypothesis + principal evidence as his original formulation.

### Source-critical recut

Pre-1906 primary controls show that many ingredients of the supposedly specific hypothesis were already field-available.

### Residual historical object

The most defensible Lovejoy-level object is therefore not an ingredient but a configuration:

> **causal-priority reversal + boundary crossing + architectonic ranking + proposition-sensitive semantic reanalysis + collective reaggregation.**

This is precisely the kind of case in which:

`ACTOR PRIORITY CLAIM`
!=
`SOURCE-CRITICAL PRIORITY RESULT`.

It also shows that `source ownership` was not merely a retrospective problem imposed by this project. Lovejoy himself was actively contesting ownership and misattribution in 1914.

## IX. Consequence for the larger `primitive` thesis

This result strengthens the disciplinary-technology model.

The same power materials could be organized as:

- Brinton: psychic / voluntarist origin of religion;
- Marett: supernormal / pre-animistic threshold;
- Frazer: law-like magic outside personal religion;
- Hewitt: causal potency / savage philosophy;
- Hubert–Mauss: general magical / collective power category;
- Leuba: dynamism and a religion/non-religion cleavage;
- Lovejoy: quasi-mechanical efficacy as the architectonic causal concept of `primitive philosophy`.

Therefore the historical object is not simply the discovery of a primitive fact.

It is a field in which scholars repeatedly **reassign the same evidence to different explanatory relations and disciplinary jurisdictions**.

That is a stronger model of `primitive` as an operational concept than an invention/priority story.
