## p. 52 / ms p. 143 — the whole enumeration is secondary and composite

This page contains an unusually explicit direct-image formulation. The left page continues the argument that dispositions persist through successive existences and can function as conditions of future existence. Lovejoy then summarizes:

> `But this really means th. t. whole enumeration is secondary as to origin & composite as to character!`

This sentence is image-secure and should be treated as a central control for the notebook's source-critical logic.

Immediately after it he asks:

> `And what of nāmarūpa?`

The remainder tests whether the place assigned to `nāmarūpa` can be explained through affinities with Sāṁkhya. On the right page Lovejoy explicitly resists over-inference from such affinities: a scholastic explanatory correspondence does not warrant treating the whole nidāna theory as a unitary primitive nomenclature or as a theory conceived all at once.

### Interpretive consequence

The direct p. 52 witness makes the notebook's distinction especially clear:

`secondary/composite provenance` does not entail `mere accident`, and `structural affinity` does not entail `single-source borrowing`.

This is precisely the anti-origin firewall needed for the larger argument. Lovejoy himself is already working against a simple source-monism: he reconstructs layers and correspondences while withholding a claim that the whole system was conceived in one act or borrowed wholesale from one prior doctrine.
