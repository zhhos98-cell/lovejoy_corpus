## 1. pp.106–108 — cannibalism-by-mechanism is a Marillier-course problem plus Tylor/Frazer/Trumbull source field

The current summaries distinguish motives for anthropophagy including:

- famine/alimentary use;
- revenge/hatred;
- affection or funerary incorporation;
- acquisition of qualities;
- sympathetic magic;
- sacrifice/propitiation;
- sacramental or mystical union.

These categories are not Lovejoy inventions.

### Marillier course control

The 1898–99 EPHE report explicitly states that Marillier's Tuesday conference continued research on `Sacrifices humains et l'anthropophagie rituelle`, with special Bantu material and comparisons across Guinea, North America and India. The course sought to determine the reasons human victims were employed in multiple sacrifice types: expiatory, funerary, anthropophagic and magical.

Lovejoy is explicitly listed among auditors who took an active part in the work.

Therefore the notebook's terminal cannibalism decomposition sits directly inside a documented Marillier problem field.

### Other source controls

- Frazer and Trumbull supply body-part/quality transfer and sympathetic efficacy;
- Tylor and the broader sacrifice literature supply alimentary, funerary, gift, propitiatory and sacramental distinctions;
- earlier 005 pages already show direct extraction from Cameron, Grout, Trumbull and Kingsley.

### Ownership

pp.106–108 should be treated primarily as **mechanism adjudication inside an inherited course/source field**, A0–A1. A Lovejoy-local claim requires direct rejection, reclassification, ranking, or relation among mechanisms, not the existence of the categories themselves.

## 4. p.112 — strong local source-adjudication seam: circumcision is rejected as partial human sacrifice

The page is high confidence for the proposition that circumcision should **not** be interpreted as a partial human sacrifice substituted for complete human sacrifice. Instead the notebook interprets it through:

- initiation;
- fraternity/blood relation;
- incorporation;
- admission to a social/religious group.

### Competing contemporary theory existed explicitly

By 1891, published anthropology/popular anthropology could state directly that circumcision represented a substitute for human sacrifice or a partial bodily offering. The same literature also related circumcision to blood covenant.

Trumbull's *The Blood Covenant* (1885), already a directly cited source zone in notebook 005, instead gives strong support to circumcision as blood-covenant/incorporation/fraternity.

### Lovejoy-local operation

The significance of p.112 is therefore not that Lovejoy invents initiation or blood incorporation. It is that he **adjudicates between competing available genealogies**:

`PARTIAL-HUMAN-SACRIFICE THEORY`
-> **REJECTED**

versus

`INITIATION / BLOOD-COVENANT / INCORPORATION`
-> **PREFERRED EXPLANATORY FIELD**.

This is one of the cleaner 005 source-critical acts because the rival interpretation is historically real and the notebook explicitly refuses it.

Calibration:

- competing theories: F/A0;
- rejection/preference relation: high-confidence provisional A2, subject only to final direct-image wording check rather than broad source uncertainty.

Safe formulation:

> **Lovejoy does not create the incorporation interpretation of circumcision; he selects it against a contemporary partial-sacrifice genealogy.**

That is a relation-level alternative-mechanism test. If the direct image preserves the connective wording as currently summarized, it may support A2 as a local explanatory fork.
