# UNIT × RELATION: object form, workshop discipline, and historical proof from Lovejoy's notebooks to the global testing of contextualism, 1898–2026

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **CROSS-PROJECT SYNTHESIS / DIRECT LOVEJOY MATERIAL CONTROL + DIRECT MARILLIER WORKSHOP CONTROL + PRIMARY/OPEN RECENT METHOD CONTROLS / NO TRANSMISSION CLAIM ACROSS 1898–2026**

## Core result

The project has repeatedly shown that historical claims fail when distinct **relations** are collapsed:

`identity != chronology != causation != transmission != representativeness`.

The global/contextualism branch now reveals a second, orthogonal problem that should be made explicit:

> **Before asking what relation holds, one must also specify what kind of historical unit is being related.**

A proposition, named author, textual layer, canonical tradition, ritual form, institutional role, interpretive chain, speech act and reproduced practice are not interchangeable historical units. The same relation-word — `meaning`, `influence`, `continuity`, `borrowing`, `context`, `transmission` — imposes different proof burdens depending on the unit.

The project should therefore add a second axis:

`UNIT TYPE × RELATION TYPE`.

This is not a new universal method attributed to Lovejoy, Skinner, Tan, Iqtidar, Jenco or anyone else. It is an analytic reconstruction generated by comparing independently documented historical problem-settings.

Strongest formula:

> **RELATION-SPECIFIC WARRANT requires UNIT-SPECIFIC JURISDICTION.**

Or more compactly:

`WHAT IS RELATED?`
comes before
`WHAT RELATION HOLDS?`.

# I. Why `non-Western objects made the method sophisticated` is the wrong causal story

The current evidence requires three analytically separate sources of methodological complexity in the young Lovejoy.

## A. Object/source form

The Buddhist and comparative-religion materials contain relations that do not line up neatly:

- a term can be logically nested in a classification but temporally antecedent in a causal/sequence account;
- a surviving rite can retain a form after the belief that generated it has changed;
- one ceremonial complex can contain several mechanisms;
- similar myths or rites can have different functions, provenance and historical scope;
- differentiated post-mortem destinies can be selected by death-type, status, rite, taboo, imported moralization or strict moral desert;
- a textual/religious tradition can be historically composite rather than an expression of one contemporaneous doctrine.

These are properties of the evidence/problem field, not merely ideas inside Lovejoy's head.

## B. Training/workshop regime

Marillier's EPHE programme already trained students to:

- distinguish outwardly similar rites by function/mechanism;
- separate surviving forms from original generating beliefs;
- test comparative generalizations against monographic evidence;
- identify missionary/imported layers;
- distinguish causal/religious functions within sacrifice;
- separate moral from non-moral eschatological selectors;
- reconstruct composite systems generated by historical superposition.

Söderblom 1901 even explicitly describes his own work as following Marillier with `d’autres divisions et à un point de vue un peu différent`.

Thus mechanism-sensitive decomposition and student **redivision** were already workshop practices.

## C. Lovejoy's particular working redivision

Lovejoy's documentary delta lies in:

- the exact arrangement of relations on notebook pages;
- unusually dense same-case reallocation;
- carrying the diagnostic burden across Buddhism, eschatology, sacrifice, blood, cannibalism, efficacy and agriculture;
- repeated spatial/diagrammatic hypothesis testing;
- later reaggregation of one causal family into `Primitive Energetics`.

Therefore:

`OBJECT COMPLEXITY`
+
`WORKSHOP DISCIPLINE`
+
`INDIVIDUAL REDIVISION / REAGGREGATION`

must not be collapsed into one causal explanation.

This firewall is essential before making any global comparison.

# II. Lovejoy 004: the smallest clean UNIT × RELATION case

Direct page-image control of notebook 004, PDF p.42 / manuscript p.123, gives the project's clearest local example.

Lovejoy draws a hierarchy in which:

`nāmarūpa -> nāma -> vedanā / saññā / saṅkhārā / viññāṇa`.

Thus `viññāṇa` is spatially/classificatorily nested inside `nāma` and `nāmarūpa`.

Immediately below, his prose describes `viññāṇa` as:

- temporally (?) antecedent to `nāmarūpa`;
- logically a subdivision of it.

The evidentiary lesson is not simply that two relations differ.

It is that **the unit remains the same while the relation changes with the analytical dimension**.

| Unit | Relation dimension | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `viññāṇa` relative to `nāmarūpa` | classification / logical inclusion | subdivision / nested part |
| `viññāṇa` relative to `nāmarūpa` | temporal sequence | antecedent (?) |

Therefore:

`SAME UNIT`
can occupy
`DIFFERENT RELATIONS`
without contradiction.

This is the minimal prototype of the matrix.

Do not inflate it into a mature methodology. It is a directly documented local working operation.

The full material audit of notebook 005 adds a second dimension.

## Object-level decomposition

The notebook repeatedly separates:

- rite/form;
- generating belief;
- present explanation;
- causal mechanism;
- social selector;
- moral selector;
- imported layer;
- chronological sequence;
- historical provenance.

Marillier supplies important upstream workshop precedents for these distinctions.

## Material research form

005 itself is physically accretive:

- pp.29–37 inserted/folded sequence;
- p.46 separate slip;
- pp.49–54 multi-leaf/foldout sequence over an already written spread;
- pp.92–100 another major foldout/insert sequence;
- p.119 another overlaid slip;
- p.44 directly dates one reuse context to `Hist. Relig. — Dec. 20, 1905`.

The notebook is therefore not just intellectually heterogeneous. It is a **composite working source-object** whose layers were inserted, reused and overlaid.

Crucial restriction:

`PHYSICAL COMPOSITENESS`
!=
`CHRONOLOGICAL ORDER OF IDEAS`.

A physical insert establishes accretion, not its date.

This is an important UNIT problem: if the historian treats `notebook 005` as one homogeneous unit, false temporal/genealogical claims become almost inevitable.

Possible units include:

- codex as archival object;
- individual leaf/slip;
- dated entry;
- argument cluster;
- case note;
- later insert;
- source excerpt;
- classificatory schema.

Each supports different historical claims.

# IV. Marillier makes the unit problem explicit at the level of rites and systems

Marillier's comparative-religion programme repeatedly demonstrates that outwardly unitary objects are analytically divisible.

A `ceremony` is not necessarily one mechanism.

A `survival` is not necessarily evidence of the original belief continuing unchanged.

A `religious system` is not necessarily a synchronous doctrinal whole.

A `similar myth` is not necessarily the same historical/significatory unit.

This gives a workshop-level rule:

`PHENOMENAL UNITY`
!=
`CAUSAL / FUNCTIONAL / HISTORICAL UNITY`.

The historian must decide which unit is warranted by the problem rather than inherit the visible form as a natural unit.

This is why `redivision` is so useful an actor-near category for 005.

# VI. Wiener 1961: one relation-word, multiple causal units

The p.537 reconstruction now shows Wiener doing something structurally comparable at the level of `influence`.

He distinguishes at least four possible mechanisms:

1. direct textual/personal relation;
2. mediated, traceable chain through other persons' talk/writing;
3. pupil relation that may or may not amount to discipleship;
4. diffuse `climate of opinion`.

These are not merely stronger/weaker quantities of the same relation.

They involve different **causal units**:

| Influence model | Candidate unit |
|---|---|
| direct contact | individual source / recipient |
| traceable chain | sequence/network of intermediaries |
| pupil/disciple | institutional relation vs doctrinal uptake |
| climate | distributed intellectual field |

Skinner 1969 formalizes one proof problem from this richer field: alternative-source discrimination.

Thus:

`ONE RELATION-WORD: INFLUENCE`
can hide
`MULTIPLE UNIT STRUCTURES + MULTIPLE MECHANISMS`.

# VII. Skinner's characteristic historical unit: linguistic action by an agent

Skinner's intervention was transformative within Anglo-American intellectual history partly because it changed the preferred unit of analysis.

Against histories that could treat doctrines or propositions as moving through time comparatively independently of use, Skinner asks what a writer was **doing** in issuing a particular linguistic act within available conventions.

The characteristic unit becomes approximately:

`AGENT + UTTERANCE/TEXT + LINGUISTIC CONVENTION + INTENDED INTERVENTION`.

That unit is extremely powerful for many historical problems.

The recent global/comparative literature matters because it asks whether this unit has universal jurisdiction.

The answer emerging from the 2021–26 anniversary ecology is not one simple yes/no.

Different domains expose different boundary conditions.

---

# VIII. Tan 2023: the historical unit can be a reproduced tradition, not an identifiable author

Sor-hoon Tan's 2023 *History and Theory* article directly tests Skinner against Chinese and comparative philosophy.

The article began as a contribution to the 2021 British Academy Skinner anniversary conference and underwent significant journal revision.

Tan argues that Skinner's requirement to recover intended meaning through linguistic context has limited application to important ancient Chinese texts because these texts are not best understood primarily as communications by specific historical authors carrying intended messages to readers.

Instead, they function as means through which traditions perpetuate beliefs and practices. Their meanings are dynamically co-created through:

- production;
- reproduction;
- reception/consumption;
- transformation by audiences;
- changing practices that constitute the tradition.

Using the Five Classics and *Analects*, Tan makes the historically relevant unit larger and temporally more distributed than a single authorial speech act. (Sor-hoon Tan, `Texts and Traditions in Chinese and Comparative Philosophy`, *History and Theory* 62.1 [2023], 88–105, DOI 10.1111/hith.12291.)

The important distinction is:

`TEXT AS AUTHORIAL COMMUNICATIVE ACT`
!=
`TEXT AS TRADITION-PERPETUATING / TRANSFORMED PRACTICE`.

This is a **domain-adequacy** argument, not evidence that Chinese thought possesses or anticipates `the Skinner method`.

---

# IX. Iqtidar 2026: the unit can be a long institutional chain of interpretation

Humeira Iqtidar's 2026 chapter, `Meaning, Context, Interpretations in the Islamic Tradition: Provincialising Skinnerian Contextualism`, makes a different intervention.

She argues that context and intention have played central roles in Islamic political and juridical thought from the eighth century, through long-running chains of interpretation linking past, present and future. Practices that became expected in Anglo-American history of ideas from the 1970s had been standard scholarly practices among Islamic learned traditions for centuries. (In Adrian Blau, ed., *Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas and Beyond*, Proceedings of the British Academy 281 [2026], pp.101–116; open access JSTOR.)

This shifts the historical unit toward:

`INSTITUTIONALIZED INTERPRETIVE CHAIN / LEARNED TRADITION`.

The implication is different from Tan's.

Tan asks whether individual authorial intention is an adequate unit for certain classical textual traditions.

Iqtidar asks whether Anglo-American canonical chronology mistakenly confuses:

`FIELD-LOCAL METHODOLOGICAL NOVELTY`
with
`HISTORICAL PRIORITY OF THE INTERPRETIVE PRACTICE`.

Therefore keep:

`TAN CRITIQUE != IQTIDAR CRITIQUE`.

And:

`ANALOGOUS PRACTICE != TRANSMISSION TO SKINNER`.

---

# X. Jenco: historicization itself has multiple institutional genealogies

Leigh Jenco supplies a third kind of control.

Her 2017 study of the early twentieth-century Chinese `reorganization of the national heritage` shows a strict historicist effort to clarify the historical location/context of classical texts. Yet participants did not regard this historicism as deriving simply or mainly from imported Western historiography.

Jenco notes that Hu Shi could draw simultaneously on Deweyan historical thinking and on Chinese historiographical resources such as Zhang Xuecheng and Zheng Qiao; Gu Jiegang's `doubting antiquity` work likewise drew on Chinese scholarly lineages and evidential/text-critical practice. (Leigh Jenco, `How should we use the Chinese past?`, *European Journal of Political Theory* 16.4 [2017], 450–469, DOI 10.1177/1474885117703768.)

This supplies a third firewall:

`HISTORICIZATION`
!=
`ONE WESTERN GENEALOGY OF HISTORICISM`.

Multiple scholarly traditions can develop historically sensitive source criticism, contextualization and genealogical reconstruction under different institutional/problem conditions.

Again:

`STRUCTURAL ANALOGY`
!=
`COMMON ORIGIN`.

# XIII. New three-way distinction: canonical priority / historical priority / domain adequacy

The recent global-contextualism literature supports a useful distinction for the entire project.

### `CANONICAL PRIORITY`

Who becomes remembered inside a particular discipline as the decisive methodological innovator?

Skinner plainly has this status for contextualism in anglophone intellectual history.

### `HISTORICAL PRIORITY`

Where and when did analogous interpretive operations exist historically?

Iqtidar shows that context/intention-sensitive learned interpretation has much older Islamic histories.

### `DOMAIN ADEQUACY`

Does the canonical method's preferred historical unit fit the source/object under study?

Tan argues that identifiable authorial intention is not always the adequate unit for traditional Chinese classics.

These are separate relations:

`CANONICAL PRIORITY`
!=
`HISTORICAL PRIORITY`
!=
`DOMAIN ADEQUACY`.

The same distinction can discipline Lovejoy historiography:

- a later canonical label does not prove first historical occurrence;
- an upstream workshop precedent does not prove Lovejoy merely copied it;
- a method useful for one source-form need not travel unchanged to another.

# XIV. Reframing the Lovejoy-to-Skinner longue durée

The project should not write:

`LOVEJOY DISCOVERS RELATION-SPECIFIC METHOD -> JHI -> SKINNER -> GLOBALIZATION`.

The evidence supports a less teleological architecture:

### 1890s–1900s

Different source/object forms inside Orientalist/comparative-religion scholarship plus EPHE workshop norms generate concrete redivision and proof problems.

### Interwar/midcentury JHI ecology

Different units/relations are institutionalized and argued over through primitivism, recurrence/continuity, cultural process, influence, comparative/cross-regional history.

### Skinner 1960s onward

Authorial linguistic action becomes a powerful corrective unit for a particular history-of-political-thought problem.

### 1980s–2020s canonization

That intervention is packaged, debated, revised and made portable.

### Global/comparative testing

Other textual and learned traditions show both:

- historically prior analogous interpretive practices;
- domains in which a singular authorial speech-act unit is insufficient.

Therefore the longue durée is not a transmission chain but a **series of jurisdictional reallocations of the historical unit**.

Possible master formulation:

> **The history of intellectual-history method can be written as a history of changing jurisdictions over the unit of historical relation: what counts as an idea, a text, an actor, a tradition, a source, a context, a transmission chain, or a practice, and what evidence allows relations among those units to be asserted.**

# XV. HPS payoff: source ontology is methodologically active

A strong HPS implication now emerges.

Historical methods do not operate on neutral `content` after the source object is given.

The ontology/form assigned to the source partly determines the admissible inference.

If the source is treated as:

- one author's communicative act;
- a layered canonical accretion;
- a ritual survival;
- a reproduced practice;
- a juristic commentary chain;
- a bibliographic synthesis;
- a physically composite notebook;

then the historian has licensed different kinds of questions before interpretation begins.

This can be called:

### `SOURCE-ONTOLOGY EFFECT`

> The evidentiary consequences produced by deciding what kind of historical entity a source or object is.

This is not metaphysical realism about documents. It is a practical epistemology of historical inference.

The project's repeated archival lesson is:

`SOURCE FORM`
-> constrains
`UNIT OF ANALYSIS`
-> constrains
`ADMISSIBLE RELATION`
-> determines
`PROOF BURDEN`.

This may be the deepest common denominator across the entire Lovejoy corpus.
