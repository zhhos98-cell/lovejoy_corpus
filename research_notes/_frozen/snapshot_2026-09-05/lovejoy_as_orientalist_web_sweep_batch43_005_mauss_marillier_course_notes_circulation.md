# Batch 43 — Lovejoy 005 only: Mauss directly documents Marillier’s bibliography and course-note circulation

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope lock: **MS38_004_001_061_005 only**.

## Research move

The Steinmetz/Mauss route has now produced a more important documentary result than another thematic parallel. In the critical continuation of Marcel Mauss’s `La religion et les origines du droit pénal d’après un livre récent`, Mauss explicitly says that for the third part of his study he owes much to his teacher Léon Marillier, who had communicated to him **his bibliography and the notes of his different courses**.

The searchable electronic reproduction gives the sentence beginning:

> `Pour toute cette troisième partie de mon travail je dois beaucoup à mon maître, M. Marillier, qui a bien voulu me communiquer sa bibliographie et les notes de ses différents cours...`

This is direct contemporary evidence for a documentary practice already reconstructed from other witnesses: Marillier’s teaching did not circulate only oral exposition. Bibliographies and course-note packets moved into another researcher’s written work.

The significance for 005 is methodological and material. It does **not** prove that Lovejoy received the same sheets. It makes the weaker and more defensible proposition substantially stronger: a student/researcher in Marillier’s immediate orbit could be given bibliographies and course notes as working research material, and those materials could then be reorganized in independent written analysis.

## 1. Why this matters beyond the already-known two-reference borrowing

An earlier pass had already recovered Mauss’s narrower acknowledgment that two Tahitian references from Gaussin and Ellis were borrowed from Marillier’s course on flood myths. That showed transfer of specific references.

The newly isolated sentence is broader. Mauss says Marillier communicated:

- **his bibliography**;
- **the notes of his different courses**;
- and Mauss credits this material for an entire major part of his argument.

This upgrades the documentary ecology from occasional oral recommendation to reusable paper/research infrastructure.

## 2. The material sequence now has multiple independent witnesses

The current evidence for Marillier’s workshop no longer rests on one retrospective statement.

### Mauss, 1896–97

Marillier communicates bibliography and course notes directly into Mauss’s research process. In the same essay Mauss also says that two specific references were borrowed from Marillier’s flood-myth course.

### Söderblom, later publication based on Paris work

Söderblom explicitly states that Marillier gave/loaned him notes and marks extracts from Marillier’s material. This independently confirms note circulation to another student working on another comparative-religion problem.

### N. W. Thomas / EPHE pedagogical controls

The seminar environment is documented as student-work based, with directed essays/presentations, teacher intervention and peer discussion.

### Lovejoy 005

The notebook is a heterogeneous research object containing bibliographic intake, dated Marillier material, source criticism, case sorting, inserted fragments and later reuse. Its form is therefore compatible with a workshop in which bibliographies and note packets circulated, even though identity of the physical sheets remains unproved.

The important point is cumulative: **the circulation mechanism is independently attested around Marillier before we infer anything from Lovejoy’s handwriting.**

## 3. This changes the evidentiary status of the `shared source field` argument

Previously the paper could safely say that Lovejoy, Söderblom and Marillier occupy overlapping source fields, and that Marillier demonstrably lent notes to Söderblom. Mauss now gives a second direct case of Marillier’s documentary transmission practice.

Thus the argument can be sharpened:

> **Marillier’s comparative-religion teaching operated through transmissible bibliographic and note-based research materials. Mauss explicitly credits Marillier’s bibliography and course notes; Söderblom independently records receiving Marillier’s notes; Lovejoy’s 005 notebook was produced within the same institutional programme and contains source clusters and classificatory work continuous with that research ecology.**

The last clause remains an inference about Lovejoy’s access route. The first two clauses are direct evidence.

## 4. Connection to the Steinmetz controversy is especially useful

The sentence occurs inside Mauss’s extended critique of Steinmetz, the very controversy that Marillier’s 1898–99 EPHE report later says he resumed/refuted in the conference Lovejoy attended actively.

So the documentary sequence is now unusually tight:

> **Marillier bibliography/course notes → Mauss’s Steinmetz critique**
>
> **Marillier resumes Steinmetz problem in 1898–99 EPHE conference → Lovejoy is active auditor**
>
> **Lovejoy 005 contains the exact title `Endokannibalismus — Steinmetz` and later reclassifies anthropophagy by motive/mechanism.**

This still does not establish a direct `Mauss text → Lovejoy text` line. It establishes that Marillier’s own documentary apparatus was an active carrier within the controversy before Lovejoy entered the programme.

## 6. Relation to Batch 42: classification and paper circulation can now be joined

Batch 42 isolated a specific classificatory disagreement:

- Mauss: transfer of the dead person’s virtues through kin anthropophagy = `un simple acte de communion`;
- Lovejoy 005: transfer of qualities through selected organs may be sympathetic/transferable efficacy and need not be communion.

Batch 43 adds a material mechanism for how such live classificatory problems circulated around Marillier: bibliographies and course notes were directly communicated to researchers.

The paper can therefore connect **document movement** and **conceptual redivision** without reducing one to the other:

> bibliography / course notes / source packets circulate → comparative cases are reassembled → categories are contested and subdivided → Lovejoy’s notebook records one further redivision.

This is substantially stronger than a generic influence narrative.

## 7. Strongest safe formulation

> **Marcel Mauss explicitly acknowledged that Léon Marillier had communicated to him his bibliography and the notes of his different courses for a substantial section of the 1896–97 Steinmetz critique. Together with Söderblom’s independent testimony that Marillier supplied him with notes, this establishes documentary circulation as a normal research practice in Marillier’s orbit. Lovejoy’s 005 notebook, produced within the later EPHE programme that resumed the Steinmetz controversy, should therefore be read against a demonstrable infrastructure of circulating bibliographies and note packets, while the identity of any particular sheet or direct handoff to Lovejoy remains open.**

## 8. Evidence limits

1. The Mauss sentence proves communication to **Mauss**, not to Lovejoy.
2. No claim is made that Lovejoy read Mauss’s printed article.
3. Exact original RHR print pagination for the Mauss acknowledgment should still be aligned from facsimile before final quotation; the searchable reproduction securely preserves the sentence and article structure.
4. Marillier’s own review of *Endokannibalismus*, RHR 34 (1896), pp.113–115, remains the highest-value missing intermediate text.
5. Scope remains **005 only**.
