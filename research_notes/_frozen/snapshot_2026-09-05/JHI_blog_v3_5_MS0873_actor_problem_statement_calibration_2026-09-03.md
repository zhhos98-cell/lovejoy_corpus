## New source-bearing actor statement

The 20 Oct 1898 letter records Lovejoy proposing to elucidate Buddhism's theoretical presuppositions and doctrines `from a philosophical point of view`. He then diagnoses a disciplinary jurisdiction problem:

- philologists had expounded Buddhist literature but paid little attention to philosophy;
- philosophers had reconstructed Buddhist conceptions `more or less out of their own heads` while neglecting `the technicalities of the texts`;
- Sylvain Lévi was the scholar Lovejoy hoped to study under `for this purpose`.

The 1/5 Dec 1898 letter then records the practice-level consequence:

- `no courses in Pali Buddhism` were offered that year;
- Lovejoy expected occasional private reading at Lévi's house;
- Wilson's typescript reads `Petakas` for the object of reading; likely normalization to `Piṭakas` is editorial and should not be silently inserted into the public prose.

The 9 Oct Howison letter independently shows that before the Paris lectures began Lovejoy prospectively described his year's work as `philosophy and comparative religion`.
