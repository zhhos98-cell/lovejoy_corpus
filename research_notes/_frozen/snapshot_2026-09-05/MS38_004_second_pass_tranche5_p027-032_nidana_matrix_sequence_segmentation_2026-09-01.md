## 1. P. 27 / ms p. 85 — the nidāna chain is redrawn as a six-channel matrix

Lovejoy writes across the page edge:

> `The relation of t. Nidānas 3–8 inclusive may be represented thus:`

The diagram is not a simple vertical list. It spatially reconstructs the sequence as follows:

- `3. Consciousness` is split into six sensory consciousnesses: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind;
- those six lines converge on `4 Namarupa`;
- `Namarupa` fans back out into six `5. Sense-organs`;
- the six channels then remain parallel through `6. Contact`, `7. Sensation`, and `8. Desire`;
- the last two rows use the sensory objects: sight, sound, smell, taste, touch, and `thot or perceptn.`

### Material-form consequence

The notebook page converts a serial doctrinal formula into a **many-to-one / one-to-many / parallel-channel diagram**:

`6 CONSCIOUSNESSES -> NĀMARŪPA -> 6 SENSE ORGANS -> 6 CONTACTS -> 6 SENSATIONS -> 6 DESIRES`.

That geometry matters. It shows that Lovejoy is already refusing to treat the written order of the nidānas as if every adjacent term bore the same simple one-dimensional relation.

The diagram is analytical evidence, not decoration.

## 2. P. 28 / ms p. 87 — consciousness and nāmarūpa at conception

The right page resumes `Nidānas cont.` and defines six forms of consciousness. It then copies the Mahānidāna-Sutta / Oldenberg discussion in which consciousness entering the womb is related to the arising and subsequent growth of `name & corporeal form`.

Lovejoy notes Oldenberg's interpretation that the proposition

> `from consciousness comes name & corp. form`

is envisaged at the moment of conception.

### Consequence

The page gives one concrete causal/embryological reading of the viññāṇa -> nāmarūpa relation. This is important later because p. 42 will place viññāṇa logically inside nāmarūpa while also calling it temporally antecedent. P. 28 supplies part of the concrete textual problem to which that later explicit relation-language responds.

This is a local notebook relation, not proof of a general method.

## 3. Pp. 29–30 / ms pp. 89–91 — viññāṇa and saṅkhāra as unequal links

P. 29's Childers note says that `consciousness` inadequately expresses all that is meant by `viññāṇa`; Lovejoy treats viññāṇa as exceptionally important among the mental khandhas and notes traditions in which destruction of viññāṇa entails destruction of the whole being.

The page then turns to `Conformations` / `Saṅkhāras`. The key analytical sentence is clear in substance:

> `Saṅkhāras = karma; & why they are placed second is clear enough.`

Lovejoy reasons that if existence is due to avijjā, the `predispositions` provide the primary determination of its course and in a sense carry existence over into the new life.

P. 30 continues with rebirth language from the Saṅkhāruppatti Sutta and then turns to `Ignorance`, defined as ignorance of sorrow, its cause, its removal, and the method of removal.

### Consequence

Lovejoy is not treating nidāna positions as semantically interchangeable slots. Individual links receive different explanatory burdens:

- viññāṇa: consciousness / continuity / constitution;
- saṅkhāra: predispositional determination and transfer across rebirth;
- avijjā: ignorance of the truths and head of the series.

The sequence is being decomposed by **function**, not merely copied in order.

## 5. P. 32 / ms p. 95 — explicit non-uniformity inside the sequence

This is the strongest page of the tranche.

The left page gives an abridged paticca-samuppāda sequence from the Milindapañha and discusses Rhys Davids on the first terms. On the right Lovejoy writes, with several opening words still difficult but the relational claims clear:

- form and consciousness are treated as **parallel**;
- their order may be **inverted without making any difference**;
- the subsequent links down to `taṇhā` follow in **fixed sequence**;
- another Milindapañha version is more abridged and makes karma follow directly upon taṇhā.

He then gives a direct structural segmentation:

> `It seems clear fr. th. th. 3–9 form a single indivisible group, while 1–2 may be regarded separately, & 10–12 are simply 3–9 looked at fr. another point of view.`

### Direct-image security

The following are W3 in substance:

1. **parallel relation** for form / consciousness;
2. **order invertibility** for that local relation;
3. **fixed sequence** for subsequent links to taṇhā;
4. grouping `3–9` as a `single indivisible group`;
5. separating `1–2`;
6. treating `10–12` as `3–9 looked at fr. another point of view`.
