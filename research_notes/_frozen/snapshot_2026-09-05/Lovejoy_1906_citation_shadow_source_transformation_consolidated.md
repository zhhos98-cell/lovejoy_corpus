---

## Core result

Downstream reception is best modeled as the movement of **components, source packets and evidentiary functions**, not as linear inheritance of conclusions.

The key sequence is:

> **citation shadow → verdict recomputation → vocabulary de-fingerprinting → epistemic-address shift → quotation architecture → inherited quotation packet.**

Three general rules follow.

1. **A named intermediary can preserve access to a dossier while an earlier author's name disappears.**
2. **A citation edge does not imply assent to the cited source's proposition-level verdict.**
3. **A quotation can become a portable documentary object whose omissions and adjacent interpretive frame travel together.**

This makes reception a history of evidentiary reassignment and documentary transformation rather than a citation census.

## I. Malinowski 1915: citation shadow, not hidden Lovejoy influence

Bronisław Malinowski's 1915 Polish synthesis directly discusses the impersonal-power problem-space:

- mana;
- arungquilta;
- churinga;
- manitu;
- orenda;
- vague / mysterious / non-personal efficacy;
- mechanical causal description at the analyst's level.

The searchable primary text, however, contains no recovered:

- `Lovejoy`;
- `Primitive Energetics`;
- *Monist* XVI reference.

In the relevant dossier Malinowski instead directs readers to named sources including:

- Codrington;
- William Jones;
- Hewitt;
- Spencer and Gillen;
- Marett;
- Hubert and Mauss;
- Preuss;
- Durkheim;
- Irving King, chapter VI;
- James H. Leuba, chapter IV, pp.70–84.

The Leuba chapter is precisely the place where Leuba directly quotes and audits Lovejoy 1906 and retains the Lovejoy paper as a useful evidence summary.

The secure relation is therefore:

`Lovejoy 1906`
→ `Leuba 1912 directly quotes/audits Lovejoy`
→ `Malinowski 1915 explicitly cites Leuba ch.IV`.

But:

`Lovejoy 1906 → Malinowski's specific mechanical-factor sentence`
= **NOT DEMONSTRATED**.

The correct concept is **citation shadow**:

> **Lovejoy can become structurally one step removable from the citation surface because later syntheses provide named gateways to the same dossier.**

This is not a hidden-influence claim.

## II. Prehistory control: King–Leuba exchange predates Lovejoy 1906

Leuba's relation to Irving King's psychology of religion must not be made Lovejoy-centric.

A direct 1905 Leuba review of King's dissertation already places King and Leuba in active professional exchange before Lovejoy's 1906 Monist article.

Therefore later King–Leuba convergence, disagreement, review/reply circulation or source-function partition cannot be assumed to have been generated by Lovejoy.

Safe rule:

> **later relay topology does not erase pre-existing intellectual relations among the relay nodes.**

This is an important negative control on reception genealogy.

## III. Jones → Lovejoy → Leuba → Malinowski: components travel, verdicts are recomputed

William Jones's 1905 `The Algonkin Manitou` is a particularly strong proposition-level control.

Jones supports:

- an impersonal / cosmic mysterious property;
- broad diffusion in nature;
- transferability;
- efficacy;
- grammatical evidence for property/abstraction.

But his own summary explicitly limits the stronger inference: interpretation is not governed by a regular rule and is based more on feeling than knowledge.

### Lovejoy 1906

Lovejoy recruits Jones as unusually high-resolution native linguistic/grammatical evidence and accepts the impersonal/transferable/efficacious packet.

He then strengthens the causal verdict beyond Jones's own limit:

- `quite regular and intelligible laws`;
- study and control;
- `Primitive Energetics`.

Thus:

`Jones evidence architecture` = **ACCEPT / HIGH VALUE**

while

`Jones anti-regularity verdict` = **NOT INHERITED**.

This is receiver-side inferential strengthening.

### Leuba 1912

Leuba retains:

- non-personal power;
- the comparative dossier;
- Lovejoy as a good evidence summary.

But he rejects the stronger mechanical reading because ritual means and effects do not show a sufficiently definite/stable quantitative relation.

This is a rollback of Lovejoy's causal-law strengthening, not rejection of the underlying dossier.

### Malinowski 1915

Malinowski combines:

- anti-rationalist / anti-abstract account of native thought;
- vague / felt mysterious force;
- analyst-level description of manitu as essentially a `mechanical factor`.

Thus the sequence zigzags:

`Jones` → efficacy / transfer, no regular-rule verdict

`Lovejoy` → regular/intelligible causal law

`Leuba` → impersonal power retained, mechanical regularity rejected

`Malinowski` → native abstraction weakened, mechanical descriptor restored at analyst level.

The general rule is:

> **components travel; verdicts are recomputed.**

And:

> **relay preserves access to evidence more reliably than it preserves the interpretation placed upon that evidence.**

## IV. Citation edge ≠ proposition-assent edge

Malinowski directly cites Leuba chapter IV in the relevant impersonal-power dossier.

But Leuba chapter IV rejects Lovejoy's strong mechanical regularity, whereas Malinowski later uses a mechanical descriptor.

Therefore a cited source can function as:

- dossier aggregator;
- bibliographic gateway;
- descriptive source;
- theoretical adversary;
- evidence summary;

without its proposition-level verdict being inherited.

Canonical rule:

> **the cited source may be a dossier source without being the verdict source.**

This is the downstream analogue of Lovejoy's own source-function partition in 1906.

## V. `Mechanical` is distributed field vocabulary, not a Lovejoy fingerprint

A major false-positive control comes from Hubert and Mauss, *Esquisse d'une théorie générale de la magie* (1902–03).

Their primary text already distinguishes several senses of mechanical language.

They say magical force is:

- **not mechanical** in the ordinary causal/ontological sense;
- nevertheless **comparable to mechanical force** because both concepts name the cause of a class of effects.

Mana is treated as a spiritual/magical force that does not simply act mechanically, while other passages can use `mechanical` for direct ritual efficacy without differentiated spiritual intermediaries.

Thus even before Lovejoy, the field contains:

- mechanical force as ordinary physical model;
- analogy to mechanical explanatory function;
- direct/automatic ritual efficacy;
- non-mechanical magical force.

Lovejoy's move is stronger:

> **he converts impersonal efficacy into a claim about regular/intelligible causal law and names a comparative philosophical system `Primitive Energetics`.**

Leuba then treats the mechanical claim as an empirical proof burden.

Saintyves 1914 can escalate mana/force language toward a remote genealogy of modern scientific dynamism.

Malinowski can use `mechanical factor` while denying clear native abstraction.

Therefore:

> **mechanical vocabulary is not a Lovejoy fingerprint.**

## VI. Mechanicality is a spectrum of logical strengths

The field should be coded propositionally.

| Author | Mechanical relation | Logical strength |
|---|---|---|
| Hubert–Mauss 1902–03 | magical force comparable to mechanical force; mana itself not simply mechanical | functional analogy / ontological distinction |
| Jones 1905 | impersonal efficacy and transfer, but no regular-rule interpretation | efficacy without regular mechanics |
| Lovejoy 1906 | quasi-mechanical, regular/intelligible laws, study/control | causal-law assimilation |
| King 1910 | mechanical-force comparison indicated by secondary locator; exact p.139 wording pending | primary HOLD |
| Leuba 1912 | rejects mechanical label without stable means–effects relation | empirical threshold / rollback |
| Saintyves 1914 | mana/dynamism escalated toward genealogy of scientific universal force | secondary-controlled page-specific escalation |
| Malinowski 1915 | vague/felt native force described analytically as mechanical factor | analyst-level mechanics + anti-abstraction |
| Jung 1928 | substantially accepts Lovejoy's energetic regularity but changes epistemic address | causal acceptance + epistemic correction |

The vocabulary travels more readily than the logical strength assigned to it.

## VII. Stronger reception fingerprints require conjunction, not keywords

Weak indicators include:

- `force`;
- `energy`;
- `mechanical`;
- electricity analogies;
- mana / manitou / orenda grouping;
- impersonal power.

All were widely distributed.

A stronger Lovejoy-specific packet would require several of the following together:

1. explicit Lovejoy name or *Monist* XVI locator;
2. `Primitive Energetics` / `manitouism` terminology;
3. `quite regular and intelligible laws`;
4. explicit contrast with Marett's supernormal/anomalous emphasis;
5. efficacy/power/productivity as semantic core;
6. native grammar/language as evidence for abstraction/impersonality;
7. recognizable North America → Oceania → Africa comparative architecture;
8. reuse of Lovejoy's particular evidence grouping/order.

Canonical rule:

> **lexical recurrence is weaker than inferential/documentary configuration.**

## VIII. The category survives by changing epistemic address

Jung 1928 and Malinowski 1915 converge structurally despite very different citation relations.

### Jung 1928 — direct Lovejoy reception

Jung accepts:

- efficacy;
- power;
- productivity;
- much of the regular/manipulable energetic characterization;
- Lovejoy's comparative abstraction as analytically useful.

But he rejects the implication that the modern comparative abstraction exists for indigenous actors as the same detached abstract `concept`.

The analyst may construct the concept; the native datum may instead be a representation / psychic phenomenon bound to object and relation.

### Malinowski 1915 — no visible Lovejoy edge

Malinowski similarly rejects the attribution to `primitive` actors of:

- sharply defined abstract concepts;
- subtle personal/impersonal distinctions;
- clearly formulated magical laws.

For him such powers are more often vague and felt.

Yet he retains an analyst-level comparative field of impersonal power and can use a mechanical descriptor.

The common downstream operation is:

> **the comparative category survives at the analyst's level while being withdrawn from the native actor's level.**

Project shorthand:

> **etic retention / emic de-abstraction.**

More exact:

> **the category survives by changing epistemic owner.**

These are analytical terms, not period vocabulary.

## IX. Epistemic address and causal strength are independent dimensions

Jung and Malinowski agree on one axis but differ on another.

### Jung

- causal regularity: substantially retained;
- native abstract concept: narrowed/rejected;
- analyst abstraction: retained.

### Malinowski

- native conceptual clarity: rejected;
- analyst-level mechanical descriptor: retained;
- direct Lovejoy citation: absent.

Therefore downstream texts must be coded at least by:

`recipient`
× `source`
× `proposition`
× `evidentiary job`
× `verdict`
× `epistemic address`
× `citation visibility`.

Simple assent/dissent is not enough.

## X. Codrington 1891: same-source laboratory for Lovejoy and Durkheim

Codrington's famous mana description is internally mixed.

It states that mana is:

- non-physical / supernatural in a qualified sense;
- power / influence;
- mobile and conveyable;
- manifest through persons and objects.

But it also says:

- spirits possess and impart mana;
- mana essentially belongs to personal beings to originate;
- the surrounding chapter repeatedly ties particular efficacy to spirits/ghosts and personal direction.

The source therefore does not offer a clean autonomous impersonal-energy theory.

Its causal configuration is hybrid:

`impersonal efficacy`
+
`personal/spiritual origination/directorship`
+
`material mediation/transfer`.

This makes Codrington an unusually controlled same-source test.

---

## XI. Lovejoy partitions the Codrington witness

Lovejoy uses Codrington as a major authority for:

- efficacy;
- power;
- transferability;
- distribution through persons and things.

He also keeps adverse clauses visible, including the personal-origin tension.

His operation is:

`mixed witness`
→ decompose propositions
→ retain adverse evidence
→ reweight the semantic center toward efficacy / power / productivity.

This does not make Lovejoy's final abstraction neutral. It means the adverse proposition remains visible inside the source problem rather than being removed from the quoted packet itself.

## XII. Durkheim's marked ellipsis transforms the local warrant packet

Durkheim 1912 quotes the same Codrington definition at a proof-bearing point where Melanesian mana is made the equivalent of North-American wakan/orenda and used to validate an impersonal totemic force.

The quotation retains:

- non-physical force;
- physical manifestation;
- non-fixity;
- conveyability;
- Melanesian religion as acquiring/directing mana.

A **visible ellipsis** falls precisely where Codrington states:

- spirits have and impart mana;
- personal beings essentially originate it.

This must be described carefully.

It is not secret falsification. The omission is openly marked.

But it is propositionally consequential because the omitted clause bears directly on the direction of causation that Durkheim is trying to establish.

Canonical formulation:

> **marked selective quotation / ellipsis-mediated source transformation.**

And more specifically:

> **Durkheim's ellipsis openly signals abbreviation while removing the clause that most directly conflicts with the causal genealogy for which Codrington is being recruited.**

No claim about private intent is warranted.

---

## XIII. Causal-arrow contrast

The source and Durkheim point in different genealogical directions.

### Codrington

`personal/spiritual agents`
→ originate / impart mana
→ mana acts through persons, objects and media.

### Durkheim

`anonymous impersonal religious force`
→ individualizes / concentrates
→ spirits, demons, gods.

Thus the omitted Codrington sentence is not merely a nuance about how `personal` mana is. It contains a directional relation adverse to Durkheim's later causal story.

Safe project term:

> **ellipsis-mediated removal of a directional contradiction.**

Avoid psychological language such as intentional concealment unless independently evidenced.

## XIV. Quotation architecture is a distinct source operation

The same-source comparison expands the repository's source model.

Earlier source-function work gave:

`source × proposition → evidentiary competence`.

The Codrington case adds:

`full source text`
→ `quoted / excerpted proposition set`
→ `evidentiary function`
→ `claim licensed`.

A source can therefore be transformed before the reader even reaches proposition-level interpretation, through selection of which propositions enter the visible quotation packet.

For publication/reception analysis, track:

1. full source presence;
2. proposition extraction;
3. excerpt boundaries / ellipses;
4. source-function assignment;
5. licensed claim;
6. fate of adverse evidence: retained, corrected, omitted, displaced, or reclassified.

This is a stronger diagnostic than shared vocabulary.

## XV. Durkheim → Swain → Hardy: the omission becomes part of the quotation

Batch99 shows that Durkheim's shortened Codrington packet becomes transmissible.

### Pre-Durkheim controls

Contemporary/public reference reproductions in 1892 and 1911 preserve the full personal-origin clause. Therefore the adverse sentence remained publicly quotable before and around Durkheim.

### Swain 1915

J. W. Swain's English translation of Durkheim preserves the same excerpt structure and ellipsis rather than restoring the omitted Codrington clause.

### Alister Hardy 1966

In *The Divine Flame*, Hardy explicitly says his Durkheim quotations come from Swain's translation, then reproduces the same shortened Codrington packet in the same theoretical sequence and follows it with Durkheim's `anonymous and diffused force` / `same impersonality` commentary.

The documentary chain is therefore explicit:

`Codrington full hybrid statement`
→ `Durkheim marked truncation`
→ `Swain preserves truncation in English`
→ `Hardy inherits the truncated packet while presenting Durkheim`.

Canonical result:

> **a selective quotation can become a portable documentary object whose omission travels with it.**

Compact formulation:

> **the omission became part of the quotation.**

## XVI. Quotation–interpretation packets can outlive theoretical destinations

Hardy's theoretical project differs sharply from Durkheim's.

Durkheim recruits the packet into a sociological theory of impersonal religious force.

Hardy uses Durkheim within a much later natural-theological / natural-history-of-religion project.

Yet the documentary form remains stable:

`wakan / common life`
→ `mana equivalent`
→ shortened Codrington
→ `same impersonality`.

Thus:

> **documentary form may be more stable than theoretical use.**

A source packet can survive changes in the destination theory.

## XVII. Excerpt boundaries can carry provenance

A robust quotation-packet fingerprint should use:

1. identical or highly similar start/end boundaries;
2. same ellipsis position;
3. same omitted proposition;
4. same surrounding interpretive sentences;
5. explicit intermediary attribution where available.

Hardy is a high-confidence case because he explicitly names Swain as his Durkheim translation and reproduces the same packet in the same interpretive sequence.

This yields a general method:

> **from lexical fingerprinting to excerpt-boundary fingerprinting.**

And:

> **omissions can function as positive evidence of textual descent.**

This should be used cautiously and only when the documentary configuration is distinctive.

## XVIII. Canonical reception/source-transformation model

The downstream field now supports at least six distinct operations.

### A. Citation shadow

A source remains accessible through an intermediary dossier while its name disappears from the receiving text.

### B. Verdict recomputation

A later author retains evidence but independently changes the conclusion assigned to it.

### C. Distributed vocabulary

Shared terms such as `mechanical`, `force`, `energy`, or `mana` are too common to identify transmission by themselves.

### D. Epistemic-address shift

A comparative abstraction can survive as analyst metalanguage after being withdrawn from the putative native conceptual level.

### E. Quotation architecture

A source is transformed through excerpt boundaries and ellipsis before it performs evidentiary work.

### F. Quotation-packet inheritance

A selective excerpt plus its interpretive frame can become a reusable documentary object transmitted through translation and later quotation.

Together these operations show:

> **citation visibility, source access, proposition verdict, epistemic address, and documentary form are independent dimensions of intellectual transmission.**
