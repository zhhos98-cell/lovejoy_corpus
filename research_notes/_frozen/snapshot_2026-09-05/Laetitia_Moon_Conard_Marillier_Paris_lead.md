# Elizabeth Laetitia Moon Conard as a possible Marillier/Paris control witness

## Status

This is a new lead arising from the search for other students or near-students whose papers might preserve notes from Léon Marillier's teaching.

**Secure facts:** Elizabeth Laetitia Moon (later Conard) studied in Paris in 1897–1898; later biographical notices describe her as studying at the Sorbonne / École des Hautes Études. She completed a University of Chicago doctorate in 1899 on Algonkin ideas of God and future life. Bibliographic records show that Léon Marillier translated her dissertation, *Ideas of the future life held by Algonkin Indians*, into French; the French publication appeared as *Les Idées des Indiens Algonquins relatives à la vie d'outre-tombe*.

**Not yet proved:** that Moon was formally enrolled in Marillier's EPHE conference, attended his lectures, or left a Marillier lecture notebook. Her name has not yet surfaced in the published Marillier course attendance lists checked so far.

## Why the lead matters

The timing and subject are unusually close to the Lovejoy/Marillier problem:

- Paris study: 1897–1898;
- University of Chicago PhD: 1899;
- subject: future life among Algonkin peoples;
- Marillier directly translated the dissertation;
- Marillier's 1898–1899 conference explicitly says that the new evidence for his survivance/justice thesis was drawn especially from the two Americas.

This raises a concrete source-transmission question: did Moon's dissertation research feed Marillier's 1898–1899 course, and therefore some of the North American evidence in Lovejoy notebook 005?

## Archive targets

The current web sweep has not yet located a dedicated public `Laetitia Moon Conard papers` finding aid. Grinnell College has an active Laetitia Moon Conard Legacy Project and recent research on her life, including family interviews and archival work. Smith College archival material also survives (the Grinnell StoryMap reproduces a Smith Archives photograph of Moon).

Priority enquiries/searches:

1. Grinnell College Special Collections / College Archives: ask what primary Conard papers or copies were used for the Laetitia Moon Conard Legacy Project, especially 1896–1901 correspondence, diaries, study notebooks, Paris material, dissertation drafts, or family papers.
2. Smith College Archives: search Elizabeth/Laetitia Moon, class of 1894 / AM 1896–97, alumnae files, Paris correspondence, photographs, diaries, and family deposits.
3. University of Chicago archives: doctoral file/dissertation correspondence in comparative religion, 1897–1899; any correspondence with Marillier or French publication arrangements.
4. Bibliographic/source collation: compare Moon's Algonkin dissertation with Marillier's 1898–1899 course summary and Lovejoy 005 pp. 16–27, which use North American evidence in testing moral versus non-moral eschatology.

## Evidential caution

Do not presently call Moon a `student of Marillier`. The stronger wording supported by current evidence is:

> Moon was in the same Paris/EPHE-Sorbonne comparative-religion environment immediately before Lovejoy's documented Marillier year, worked on precisely the American future-life problem central to Marillier's seminar, and had her dissertation translated by Marillier.

If an enrollment record, correspondence, or notebook links her directly to Marillier's class, she would become an exceptionally valuable third witness alongside Lovejoy and Nathan Söderblom.
