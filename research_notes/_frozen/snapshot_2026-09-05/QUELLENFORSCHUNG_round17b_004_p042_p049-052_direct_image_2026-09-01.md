## 2. p.49 / ms p.137 / split local p.13 — `logical system`, not `technical system`

The immediately preceding clause about the sequence being non-homogeneous / not strictly temporal contains crossing/overwriting and should remain a quotation-level HOLD. The conceptual contrast, however, is already secure:

`COMPOSITE / NOT STRICTLY ONE TEMPORAL SERIES`

is compatible with

`DISCOVERABLE LOGICAL SYSTEM / INTENTIONAL REPETITION`.

## 5. p.52 / ms p.143 / split local p.16 — origin, composition, and scholastic reconciliation separated

The left leaf gives a decisive summary of the historical layer. The key wording is secure in substance and largely in hand:

`the whole enumeration is secondary as to origin & composite as to character.`

Lovejoy then asks:

`And what of nāma-rūpa?`

On the right leaf, the Sāṁkhya-style reconciliation is described as `plainly only a scholastic expedient`.

The methodological importance is the separation of three different questions:

1. **historical origin** — where terms/materials come from;
2. **logical arrangement** — whether the composite series has intelligible relations;
3. **scholastic reconciliation** — how later interpretation makes inherited pieces cohere.

Those are different evidential and ontological levels. The notebook can call the formula composite while simultaneously refusing the inference that its arrangement is merely accidental.

Calibration: **W3** for the three quoted evaluative transitions; **W2** for several compressed middle clauses.

## 6. Direct consequence for the paper-centered argument

The original-page check strengthens the paper’s best formulation and weakens a flatter contradiction story.

The local notebook sequence now directly supports:

`NON-IDENTICAL RELATION TYPES`
+
`COMPOSITE HISTORICAL ORIGIN`
+
`LOGICAL INTELLIGIBILITY OF ARRANGEMENT`

`LATER SCHOLASTIC RECONCILIATION`.

This is not the same as saying Lovejoy solved a contradiction by choosing one level. He keeps levels apart and asks how a system can remain intelligible when historical origin, temporal sequence, logical classification, and later scholastic ordering are non-isomorphic.

The strongest publication-facing sentence can therefore be tightened to:

> Across ms pp.123–143 Lovejoy repeatedly distinguishes temporal from logical relations inside a composite Buddhist formula; on ms p.137 he explicitly rejects the claim that the terms are merely jumbled and instead writes that a `discoverable logical system` governs their arrangement.

This is stronger than `he noticed a contradiction`, but the anti-origin firewall remains unchanged:

- no claim that Buddhist studies caused the mature unit-idea method;
- no linear maturation story;
- no conversion of a local notebook operator into a universal Lovejoy method without later evidence;
