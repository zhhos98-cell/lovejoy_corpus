# Batch 132 — Paris transition zone: Lévi returns from a specifically Buddhist mission as Lovejoy arrives

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: integrate the newly read Collège de France poster/minutes with primary mission records and the already recovered EPHE teaching reports. The question is no longer simply whether Sylvain Lévi was absent and then returned, but **what kind of research mission interrupted his teaching, how the institutional substitution ended, and where Lovejoy's October–December 1898 letters fall inside that transition.**

## Core result

The transition can now be reconstructed as a tightly dated sequence rather than a generic `Lévi was away in Asia` background.

On 30 April 1897 the Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres approved **8,000 francs** for Sylvain Lévi, explicitly so that he could continue **research on Buddhism in the sub-Himalayan regions**. During the 1897–98 academic year the official EPHE report says he could conduct **no conference at all** because of the mission. In Kathmandu in early 1898 he located and copied rare manuscripts; a subsequent Académie notice singled out his acquisition of a manuscript of Asaṅga's *Sūtrālaṅkāra* as a basis for future Mahāyāna research.

The Collège de France course poster independently shows the teaching consequence of that absence: in its spring-1898 printed state Alfred Foucher is explicitly named as Lévi's `remplaçant` under the Sanskrit chair. In the working correction copy prepared for first semester 1898–99, the Foucher replacement block is cancelled. The 6 November 1898 faculty assembly then processes current-year substitutes without a Lévi/Foucher pair.

Most importantly, the return can be fixed before Lovejoy's correspondence window. The EPHE annuaire records Lévi at Vimy on **9 October 1898**, representing the École pratique with Finot at the inauguration of the Abel Bergaigne monument. Wilson's earliest cited Lovejoy Paris letter is **17 October**. The IVe Section begins its 1898–99 conferences on **3 November**. The corrected Collège poster moves the opening of its first-semester programme to **5 December**, the same date as one of Wilson's three Lovejoy letters.

This creates an unusually precise historical configuration:

`funded Buddhist mission`

→ `full-year interruption + Foucher substitution`

→ `Lévi back in France by 9 Oct`

→ `Lovejoy correspondence begins 17 Oct`

→ `EPHE teaching resumes 3 Nov`

→ `Collège substitution cancelled / first-semester programme opens 5 Dec`

→ `realized EPHE teaching = Bhagavad-Gītā + Indian literature, not a regular Pāli-Buddhism course`.

The safest interpretation is therefore stronger than before: **Lovejoy reached Paris during the institutional re-entry of an Indianist who had just returned from a mission explicitly funded for Buddhist research, but whose recovered regular teaching on return did not reproduce the Pāli-Buddhist offering Lovejoy reportedly expected.** The exact private reading remains dependent on Lovejoy's letters.

---

## 1. The mission had an explicit Buddhist research mandate

At the Académie session of 30 April 1897, the Fondation Garnier proposed and the Académie approved a grant of 8,000 francs to Lévi:

`pour lui permettre de continuer ses recherches sur le bouddhisme dans les régions sub-himalayennes`.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_1897_num_41_2_89869

This wording matters. The mission should not be described only as an `India/Nepal/Japan trip`, because the Académie's funded purpose was explicitly connected to Buddhist research in the Himalayan region.

That gives a sharper context for Lovejoy's later reported expectation of Pāli/Buddhist study with Lévi. It does not prove that the mission caused or defined the course Lovejoy expected, but it places Buddhist textual research at the center of Lévi's immediate pre-return activity.

---

## 2. Mission progress generated concrete Buddhist manuscript evidence

In early 1898 Barth reported to the Académie that Lévi had reached Kathmandu in January, had already located rare and little-known manuscripts, intended to acquire or have them copied, and had taken impressions of inscriptions.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_1898_num_42_2_71146

Soon afterward Senart reported that the Nepal stay was ending and highlighted one acquisition in particular: a manuscript of Asaṅga's *Sūtrālaṅkāra*, which he expected to provide a solid basis for future research on the Mahāyāna.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_1898_num_42_2_71154

Thus the mission's Buddhist component is documented at both the funding and object-acquisition levels.

---

## 3. The mission's teaching interruption is exact at EPHE

The IVe Section report for 1897–98 states that Lévi, being charged with the Indian mission, `n'a pu diriger aucune conférence pendant l'année scolaire 1897-98`.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1899_num_1_1_2200

This is a full academic-year interruption, not merely a few missed weeks. Finot maintained the teaching during Lévi's absence.

The sequence therefore has a real institutional break immediately before Lovejoy's year.

---

## 4. The Collège poster now independently proves the substitution and its cancellation

The manually recovered Salamandre course poster is a correction copy. Its spring-1898 printed state contains:

- `M. SYLVAIN LÉVI, professeur`;
- `M. H. FOUCHER, remplaçant`.

In the working state corrected for first semester 1898–99, the Foucher replacement line and associated spring course block are cancelled.

Repo primary-scan analysis:

`research_notes/lovejoy_as_orientalist_web_sweep_batch131_College_de_France_course_poster_1898_1899.md`

This is direct documentary evidence for the transition from mission substitution back toward Lévi's own chair occupancy. It is stronger than relying on a modern institutional chronology alone.

The poster still does not provide a cleanly legible final 1898–99 Sanskrit course title. Therefore it does **not** directly prove that the Collège itself offered no Pāli/Buddhism course. The no-regular-Pāli result remains strongest on the recovered EPHE side until a clean final Collège programme is found.

## 6. Lévi is back in France only eight days before the first Wilson-cited Lovejoy letter

The EPHE annuaire records the Abel Bergaigne monument inauguration at Vimy on **9 October 1898** and explicitly lists Sylvain Lévi and Louis Finot among the École pratique representatives.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1900_num_1_1_2270

This proves Lévi had returned to France and re-entered the EPHE scholarly network by 9 October.

Wilson's Paris reconstruction cites Lovejoy letters dated:

- 17 October 1898;
- 1 December 1898;
- 5 December 1898.

The first is therefore only eight days after the hard Vimy anchor.

Evidence limit: Vimy is not Paris; no inference is made that Lovejoy and Lévi had already met by 17 October. The value is chronological compression.

---

## 7. Two institutional opening dates now bracket Lovejoy's December letters

The IVe Section official programme states that its conferences begin **3 November 1898** at the Nouvelle-Sorbonne, 46 rue Saint-Jacques.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1899_num_1_1_2222

The manually recovered Collège de France correction poster changes the opening date from the spring state to **5 December** for first semester 1898–99.

This creates an important distinction:

- EPHE Sanskrit/Indianist teaching cycle is already underway in early November;
- Collège de France first-semester courses open in early December;
- Wilson's 1 December and 5 December Lovejoy letters fall immediately before/on that Collège opening point.

This may eventually help identify what institution Lovejoy meant when he referred to the unavailable Pāli-Buddhist course. Until the letters are read, do not assume `Pāli Buddhism with Lévi` refers to the Collège de France rather than EPHE, Sorbonne, or a less formal arrangement.

---

## 8. After return, Lévi's realized EPHE programme is Indianist but not the desired Pāli course

The 1898–99 EPHE report records Lévi's two regular conferences as:

- explanation of the Bhagavad-Gītā;
- a continuous survey of Indian literature.

Stickney is named as a particularly active participant.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1900_num_1_1_2251

Therefore there is no contradiction between:

1. Lévi returning from a mission with intense Buddhist research and manuscript collecting;
2. Lovejoy expecting Pāli/Buddhist study;
3. the actual regular teaching being reconfigured around Bhagavad-Gītā and Indian literature.

The archival problem is now one of **course-cycle discontinuity and teaching modality**, not simply absence/presence.

---

## 9. The public mission cycle closes in January 1899

On 27 January 1899 the Académie records Lévi giving an account of the mission he had completed for the Ministry and Académie; the President congratulated him on its success.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_1899_num_43_1_89964

His published report then appeared in the Académie proceedings, pp.71–92.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/crai_0065-0536_1899_num_43_1_71335

This gives a useful endpoint: the mission was publicly closed after Lévi had already resumed the 1898–99 institutional teaching cycle.

## 10. Paper-level formulation after the digital scans

A defensible formulation is now:

> **Lovejoy arrived in Paris not simply during Sylvain Lévi's tenure, but during Lévi's institutional return from a year-long Asian mission whose Académie funding explicitly targeted Buddhist research in the sub-Himalayan region. During that absence Alfred Foucher had replaced Lévi at the Collège de France; a corrected 1898–99 course poster cancels the Foucher replacement state, and a November faculty meeting no longer processes the pair as a substitution. Lévi is documented back in France by 9 October, eight days before the earliest Paris letter later cited for Lovejoy's account of his studies. Yet the regular EPHE teaching Lévi resumed that year consisted of the Bhagavad-Gītā and Indian literature, not a Pāli-Buddhism course. Lovejoy's reported disappointment therefore belongs to a demonstrable moment of institutional and curricular reconfiguration following Lévi's Buddhist field mission.**

This formulation remains below the threshold of claiming that the mission **caused** the missing course or that Lovejoy knew its administrative history.
