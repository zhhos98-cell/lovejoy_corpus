# I. Siṃsapā: Oldenberg 1882 pp.204–205 is the immediate carrier

## 1. Notebook wording

004 PDF p.8 / MS p.25 currently reads:

> `Buddh. indiff. to theoretical kn.`
>
> `[Source illegible], p. 20f. — Gotama & leaves of Simsapa-wood.`
>
> `There are more leaves on F. tree than in my hand, so do I know many more truths than those F. I have revealed to you. Why have I revealed some & not others? Bec. F. ones I have not revealed cannot be of profit to you, since they will not help you the more to turn away fr. earthly things, to suppress desire, to purify the intelligence, etc.`

The diagnostic string is not the canonical image of leaves by itself. It is the **ordered English causal vocabulary**:

`profit`
→ `turning from the earthly`
→ `subjection/suppression of desire`
→ `peace / knowledge / illumination`
→ `Nirvana`.

## 2. Oldenberg's exact sequence

Oldenberg/Hoey 1882 opens Part II, `The Doctrines of Buddhism`, with the Siṃsapā passage at pp.204–205. Its sequence is:

- the Exalted One at Kosambi in the Sinsapa grove;
- few leaves in his hand versus the many leaves in the grove;
- what he has learned but not told is much greater than what he has told;
- why he has withheld it: it `brings you no profit`;
- it does not lead to `turning from the earthly`;
- nor to `subjection of all desire`;
- nor to cessation of the transitory, peace, knowledge, illumination, Nirvana.

This is the notebook's wording architecture almost point for point.

The source relation can therefore be upgraded from:

`CANONICAL LOCUS KNOWN / 19C CARRIER HOLD`

to:

`SAṂYUTTA NIKĀYA SIṂSAPĀ LOCUS`
→ `OLDENBERG/HOEY 1882 pp.204–205`
→ `LOVEJOY WORKING ABRIDGMENT / MODERNIZATION`.

# II. Sabbāsava: Rhys Davids SBE XI pp.298–300 is the dominant verbal carrier

## 1. Notebook sequence

004 PDF pp.6–7 / MS pp.21–23 extracts:

- things `which ought not to be considered`;
- `Have I existed during the ages of the past, or not?`;
- what was I / how was I / what did I become;
- future equivalents;
- present self-questioning: `Do I really exist, or do I not? What am I? How am I? Whence did this being come? Whither will it go?`;
- one of six false/heretical notions springs up;
- a permanent/lasting/eternal self not subject to change;
- a `jungle` / `wilderness` of error/heresy.

## 2. Rhys Davids 1881 sequence

T. W. Rhys Davids, *Buddhist Suttas*, SBE XI, prints the *Sabbāsava Sutta* at pp.293ff.; the decisive passage is pp.298–300.

The published translation has, in the same order:

- `things which ought not to be considered`;
- `Unwisely doth he consider thus`;
- `Have I existed during the ages that are past, or have I not?`;
- repeated past/future questions;
- `Do I after all exist, or am I not? How am I? This is a being; whence now did it come, and whither will it go?`;
- one or other of six notions;
- the soul/self as `permanent, lasting, eternal` and never changing;
- the `walking in delusion, the jungle of delusion, the wilderness of delusion` etc.

The overlap includes unusual syntax and ordering, not merely common doctrinal content.

Thus the earlier broad label `contemporary translation stream` can be replaced by:

`MAJJHIMA NIKĀYA 2 / SABBĀSAVA`
→ `RHYS DAVIDS, SBE XI (1881), pp.298–300 TRANSLATION FAMILY`
→ `LOVEJOY EXTRACT / PARTIAL MODERNIZATION`.

## 3. Why physical-copy attribution remains one notch below Siṃsapā

The notebook's local wording appears to use `heresy` where Rhys Davids prints `delusion`. That difference can arise from:

- Lovejoy normalizing `diṭṭhi` terminology while excerpting;
- consultation of another source that reproduces Rhys Davids's English but changes the label;
- cross-contamination from Warren's 1896 vocabulary, which repeatedly uses `heresy / heretical jungle / heretical wilderness` for closely related Buddhist classification language.

Therefore the correct evidentiary grade is:

- **translation-family / proposition sequence: secure**;
- **Rhys Davids SBE XI as the immediate physical book in hand: strong, not yet absolute**.

A closer secondary excerpt published before 1898 could still intervene. It would have to reproduce the Rhys-Davids syntax across the whole past/future/present sequence, not merely cite the same sutta.
