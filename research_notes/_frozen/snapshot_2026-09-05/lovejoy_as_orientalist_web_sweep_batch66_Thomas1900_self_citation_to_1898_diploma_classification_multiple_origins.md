# Batch 66 — N. W. Thomas 1900 self-cites the 1898 Marillier-era diploma research: classification under multiple possible origins and unequal evidentiary value

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: recover methodological information about Thomas’s inaccessible-at-proposition-level 1898 RHR diploma publication through his own explicit 1900 retrospective self-citation.

## Core result

The full 1898 Thomas article in *Revue de l’histoire des religions* 38, pp.295–347 remains difficult to expose through searchable web text. But Thomas’s 1900 paper `Animal Superstitions and Totemism` in *Folk-Lore* 11, pp.227–267 directly cites that earlier Welsh study and tells us something unusually specific about what he had already been doing there.

On printed p.220 of the 1900 article, Thomas says that in his earlier `discussion of Welsh superstitions` — footnoted directly to **RHR XXXVIII, no.3** — he had tried to justify including the ominous character of animals among classificatory `categories` **despite the apparent multiplicity of possible origins**.

He then states that his present analysis is fairly exhaustive but that the classified sections are of **unequal value as evidence** for former European totemism. He privileges distributional comparison: very local variation must be taken seriously, yet agreement across distant regions can rule out purely local explanations.

This is not a substitute for reading the 1898 article itself. But it is direct authorial retrospective evidence that the Marillier-era diploma research already involved at least three operations highly relevant to Lovejoy 005:

1. **classification despite multiple possible origins**;
2. **separating descriptive category from historical genesis**;
3. **ranking different classes of evidence by their value for a historical inference**.

The strongest student-control claim can therefore be moved backward from Thomas 1905/1911 toward the actual Marillier-era research product.

---

## 1. Primary source and exact self-citation

N. W. Thomas, `Animal Superstitions and Totemism`, *Folk-Lore* 11.3 (1900), 227–267.

Wikisource facsimile/transcription: printed p.220 appears at scan page `/242`.

The key sentence says, in substance, that Thomas had already tried in a discussion of Welsh superstitions to show that ominous animal character could legitimately be included among classificatory `categories`, **in spite of the apparent multiplicity of possible origins**.

The footnote identifies the earlier publication:

> `Revue de l'Histoire des Religions, xxxviii., No. 3.`

That is Thomas’s 1898 article:

`La survivance du culte totémique des animaux et les rites agraires dans le Pays de Galles`, RHR 38 (1898), 295–347.

The 1896–97 EPHE annual report independently states that Thomas received the title `élève diplômé` through a thesis on the survival of animal cult in Wales. Therefore the 1900 footnote directly ties the later methodological reflection back to a research product generated on Marillier’s diploma track.

---

## 2. `Categories` do not imply one origin

The phrase `apparent multiplicity of possible origins` is methodologically important.

Thomas is willing to place heterogeneous animal superstitions inside a comparative classification without treating the category as proof of a single historical source.

This produces a clean distinction:

> **classificatory inclusion ≠ genealogical unity.**

That is exceptionally close to the evidentiary problem reconstructed from Lovejoy 005:

- `cannibalism` groups outwardly similar acts with different motives/mechanisms;
- `sacrifice` can include funerary, expiatory, magical, alimentary, fecundative and sacramental operations;
- `blood` can operate through kinship, life-transfer, protection, sympathetic relation, sacrifice or intrinsic efficacy;
- ceremonial form can survive after original motive has disappeared.

Thomas is not a source for Lovejoy. The importance is that a Marillier-trained comparator can explicitly separate a usable comparative category from a claim of common origin.

---

## 3. Thomas 1900 explicitly ranks categories by evidentiary value

After presenting his classificatory scheme, Thomas says it is fairly exhaustive and largely based on superstitions actually found among totems.

But he immediately adds that, **as evidence for the former existence of totemism in Europe**, the different sections are of unequal value.

This is more than taxonomy. It is a division of evidentiary burden:

- a feature may belong in the descriptive/comparative scheme;
- yet the same feature may be weak evidence for the historical proposition `Europe once had totemism`.

This closely parallels the strongest 004 and 005 operations:

- 004: formal/lexical recurrence may be real but weak evidence for semantic identity or full-system historical dependence;
- 005: outward ritual similarity may be real but weak evidence for common cause/origin or identical religious mechanism.

Thomas therefore supplies an independent former-student control for the rule:

> **the same datum can carry different weights for different historical questions.**

---

## 4. Distribution is used to test origin claims rather than merely map examples

Thomas says local distribution is more important than simply piling up cumulative examples concerning a few animals.

He emphasizes sharp neighboring contrasts: one locality may respect or avoid an animal while nearby people are indifferent or hostile to it. No origin theory can ignore this local structure.

Yet he also observes agreements in distant regions that can exclude a purely local origin.

The resulting procedure is:

`classify` → `map local variation` → `compare distant agreement` → `test competing origin hypotheses`.

This resembles the source/provenance logic seen in Lovejoy 005’s future-life material, where local development, missionary contamination, diffusion and independent development are separated before a moral/evolutionary history is proposed.

Again, this is a control for research grammar, not proof of transmission.

---

## 5. The 1900 classificatory scheme itself crosses the same problem domains as 005

Thomas’s scheme includes:

- descent from totem animal;
- taboo / sacrosanct animal;
- animals kept/fed for magical or superstitious purposes;
- magical powers derived from animals;
- augury and magic;
- annual ceremonies;
- sacrifice;
- communion with the sacred animal.

These are not identical to 005’s architecture, but the overlap is notable because Thomas’s classificatory research is separating categories that late-nineteenth-century synthetic theories often folded together.

The key comparison is not topic identity; it is the structure of the analytical work:

> **visible association → class assignment → evidentiary weighting → historical-origin test.**

This is another independent indicator that Marillier’s environment generated research products organized around classification under uncertain genealogy rather than around the mechanical application of one master theory.

---

## 6. What this adds beyond Thomas 1905/1911

Earlier project work used Thomas’s 1905 `Religious Ideas of the Arunta` and 1911 Britannica articles as later controls because they explicitly distinguish source contamination, diffusion, outward form, inward meaning and origin.

The obvious weakness was chronology: those publications postdate Lovejoy 005 by several years.

The 1900 self-citation narrows that problem substantially.

Thomas himself attributes at least one key operation — classificatory inclusion despite multiple possible origins — to the **1898 Welsh paper**, whose underlying thesis was submitted on Marillier’s EPHE diploma trajectory in 1897.

Thus the current student-control chronology becomes:

> **1897 diploma thesis / 1898 RHR publication: classification while keeping multiple origins open**
> → **1900: explicit retrospective statement + unequal evidentiary weighting + distributional origin tests**
> → **1905/1911: mature source-contamination and form/meaning/origin formulations.**

This makes Thomas considerably more valuable as a near-contemporary control for Lovejoy 005.

---

## 7. Relation to Marillier

The EPHE 1896–97 report directly records Thomas as receiving the diploma for his Welsh animal-cult thesis. Modern biographical work places him in Marillier’s `primitive religion` teaching environment.

Batch 63 already established that Marillier’s own 1888 teaching foregrounded:

- comparative value of testimony types;
- document classes;
- source-specific rules of criticism;
- reasoned bibliography.

Batch 62 established that Mauss later described the Marillier inheritance as `critique des faits`, bibliography and collective seminar document analysis.

Thomas now supplies a worked product/control in the middle:

> **source-critical teaching → student classificatory research → explicit separation of category and origin.**

This materially improves the claim that the Marillier environment transmitted a research grammar rather than one stable doctrine.

---

## 8. Relation to Lovejoy 005

Do not write:

> Thomas and Lovejoy used the same method because Marillier taught it to both.

The evidence is not that direct.

Safer formulation:

> **A near-contemporary diploma researcher from Marillier’s conference can be observed separating comparative classification from historical origin and ranking classes of evidence by their value for a genealogical proposition. Lovejoy, independently documented as an active auditor in the same conference system in 1898–99, performs homologous evidentiary separations in notebook 005 when he refuses to let outward ritual form settle motive, mechanism or origin.**

This is a strong generative-environment argument without requiring identical assignments or direct student-to-student transmission.

---

## 9. Consequence for the `school of redivision` formulation

The evidence for a fixed doctrinal `Marillier school` remains poor and should not be manufactured.

But the evidence for a reproducible habit of **redivision** is getting better:

- Marillier: compare testimony types / document genres; criticize sources; reject unilateral theoretical reduction in published work;
- Thomas: comparative categories can contain multiple origins; sections have unequal evidentiary value for historical inference;
- Söderblom: explicitly reorganizes Marillier material under `different divisions`;
- Mauss: inherits critique/classification while changing explanatory ontology;
- Lovejoy: same outward form does not establish same causal/religious mechanism or origin.

The recurring product is not a taxonomy but a way of breaking the evidentiary shortcuts built into taxonomies.

---

## 10. Retrieval consequence

The full Thomas 1898 article remains worth retrieving, but the target question is now much sharper.

Do not search merely for general words such as `totémisme`.

Prioritize passages around:

- why a class/category may contain phenomena of multiple origins;
- `origine`, `origines`, `multiple`, `divers`;
- evidentiary value / proof / indices / survivance;
- local versus widespread distribution;
- whether rites agraires and animal cults are genealogically linked or only associated;
- classificatory wording that the 1900 paper is explicitly recalling.

If those pages appear, we can test whether the 1900 retrospective accurately summarizes an already-explicit 1898 method or retrospectively sharpens it.

## Evidence cautions

1. The 1900 statement is a self-description of earlier work, not a verbatim quotation from the 1898 article.
2. It is legitimate evidence that Thomas remembered the 1898 work as classification under multiple possible origins; it cannot replace the 1898 primary for exact wording.
3. Thomas’s diploma status is directly documented; Lovejoy’s status is different (`active auditor`). Do not collapse them.
4. No direct Thomas→Lovejoy reading/contact claim is established.
5. Similar analytical operations can arise from shared disciplinary problems, not only common teaching.
6. `School of redivision` remains our analytical shorthand, not a period label.
