## p.3 — later reward ideas and the analyst's natural/supernatural distinction

Direct image securely gives the date `Nov. 10.`. The rest of the short heading remains difficult and is held.

The opening argument is clearer than the first-pass paraphrase: ideas of reward arise and `gradually get transferred to, or supplemented in, another world whose existence is already believed in.` The afterworld is therefore not introduced by the moral idea itself; an already-held future-life conception is being supplemented by reward language.

A second lexical correction is secure near the bottom of the page. The manuscript reads:

`a distinction bet. nat. & supernatural qualities of F. savage man.`

followed by:

`Such a distinction does not exist for F. savage mind.`

The former clean reading `real & supernatural` is withdrawn.

Safe consequence:

`ANALYTIC NATURAL/SUPERNATURAL DISTINCTION != ACTOR'S OWN CLASSIFICATION`.

This should not be expanded into a claim that Lovejoy rejects the distinction generally; it is local to the conceptual reconstruction on this page.

## p.4 — physical strength and control over nature

The former first-pass opening `before [philos.?] strength` is a paleographic error. Direct image gives:

`Tre is no diff. bet. F. superior phys. strength of one man & F. superior control over nature of another.`

Lovejoy then treats both endowments as capable of being carried over into the life after death, just as a prince can remain a prince. The comparison is being used to show continuity of status/power without converting that continuity into moral recompense.

Safe consequence:

`AFTERLIFE CONTINUITY OF STATUS OR POWER != MORAL REWARD`.

The later sacrifice/devotion paragraph remains partly difficult and is not silently normalized.

## p.5 — direct vengeance, including the souls of maltreated dogs

A previously omitted phrase is now directly legible. Lovejoy writes that the idea is one of:

`simple & direct vengeance, wh. one expects even fr. F. souls of dogs &c. A. one has maltreated.`

He then moves to punishment by gods. The exact syntax is compressed, but the local point is that divine punishment can initially function as vengeance for offences against the gods themselves rather than as an abstract moral tribunal.

The facing right leaf is also substantially clearer:

`Generally as a double of F. world, it is not perhaps a copy of F. terrestrial world of today but of yesterday; the morality supposed to be reigning there is commonly a little traditional & obsolete.`

Safe consequence:

`POST-MORTEM PUNISHMENT != AUTOMATICALLY ABSTRACT MORAL JUDGMENT`.

The page allows direct vengeance and inherited terrestrial order to generate apparently punitive afterlife structures.

## p.6 — substantive heading correction: purification, not “primitive”

This is the largest lexical delta in the tranche. The first-pass heading:

`Primitive bef. entering F. life.`

is wrong. The original clearly reads:

`Purification bef. entering other life.`

The following lines also resolve more fully:

`Cases where F. common people do not survive at all; F. souls lacking suff. force, perish, or are devoured by F. gods.`

and:

`F. fut. destiny depends not exclusively upon condition in life, but also upon F. manner of death`

with special regions for suicides, the drowned, and women dead in childbirth.

The facing leaf directly preserves the distinction:

`survivance, rather than immort.`

and says immortality is not a primitive idea; some traditions contain a second death or successive deaths through which the soul dwindles toward annihilation.

Safe consequence:

`SURVIVANCE != IMMORTALITY`.

Post-mortem existence is not one uniform state in this working schema: souls may fail to survive, be routed differently by manner of death, require purification, or pass through successive deaths.

## Combined result

The pp.3–6 sequence is stronger after direct-image control, but in a specific direction. Lovejoy is not simply arranging a ladder from non-moral to moral belief. He is decomposing several determinants that can coexist or be superimposed:

- continuity of worldly status;
- physical strength and magical control;
- ritual/religious efficacy;
- direct vengeance;
- manner of death;
- purification;
- later reward/punishment language.

A compact source-level formulation is:

`ALREADY-EXISTING FUTURE-LIFE BELIEF + HETEROGENEOUS DETERMINANTS + LATER MORAL SUPPLEMENTATION`.

This supports the broader repository observation that Lovejoy repeatedly distinguishes mechanisms hidden under superficially similar future-life outcomes. It does **not** by itself establish a mature later unit-idea method or a linear genealogy into his later historiography.
