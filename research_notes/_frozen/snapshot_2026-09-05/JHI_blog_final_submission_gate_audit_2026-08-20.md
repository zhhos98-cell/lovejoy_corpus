## 1. Skinner 1969: exact page lock

Primary bibliographic object:

Quentin Skinner, `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas`, *History and Theory* 8.1 (1969), pp. 3–53, JSTOR stable 2504188, DOI 10.2307/2504188.

The searchable JSTOR-derived transcript preserves printed page breaks.

### A. Direct attack on persistent fixed ideas — printed p.35

The transcript places on printed p.35:

> `The notion that any fixed “idea” has persisted is spurious.`

and immediately:

> Skinner's concern is not merely that such histories may sometimes go wrong, but that they `can never go right`.

Footnote 144 on the same page identifies Lovejoy, *The Great Chain of Being*, p.15ff., as setting out unit-ideas as objects of study.

**Draft consequence:** if the opening/closing uses the direct conceptual conflict, cite Skinner 1969, p.35.

### B. `forms of words` / `use` — printed p.37

Printed p.37 contains Skinner's explicit `à la Lovejoy` criticism of concentrating on `the forms of words involved`, followed by the claim that one must examine the situations/functions in which words can be used. The famous formula appears here:

> `we should study not the meanings of the words, but their use.`

The same page then specifies the relevant level as a sentence used by a particular agent on a particular occasion with a particular intention.

**Draft consequence:** cite Skinner 1969, p.37 for the `use` ending. This page is also what makes the draft's distinction between Lovejoy's semantic `usage` and Skinner's pragmatic/illocutionary `use` secure.

### C. Safe formulation of the disagreement

Keep:

> the later disagreement concerns the warrant by which a conceptual object can be identified as the same idea across changed linguistic/historical contexts.

Avoid:

- `Skinner accused Lovejoy simply of ignoring context`;
- `Skinner showed Lovejoy treated words as fixed essences`;
- `Lovejoy anticipated Skinner's use-theory`.

The primary texts do not support these simplifications.

This retains the transregional control without making the Blog depend on an un-page-locked quotation.
