# JHI Blog — precise web sweep on methodological levels in the Sāṃkhya/Buddhism controversy

Date: 2026-08-19
Status: synced research note

## Bottom line

A precise public-web sweep supports the existence of a **layered proof problem**, but not a single codified nineteenth-century hierarchy shared by all participants.

The best reconstruction is a stack of **inferential burdens**. Different scholars disagree about how far one may move upward through the stack and what kind of evidence licenses each move.

Analytical ladder:

1. **Witness / admissibility** — what text, recension, date, or documentary carrier is usable?
2. **Resemblance / classification** — what similarity has actually been established?
3. **Diagnosticity / specificity** — is the resemblance generic, or specific enough to have historical force?
4. **Scale / unit of inference** — does the evidence establish a word, doctrine, element, arrangement, or an entire system?
5. **Function / configuration** — what role does the term or doctrine perform in its local system?
6. **Genealogy / dependence** — does resemblance imply borrowing, common ancestry, survival, or composition?
7. **Direction / chronology** — if dependence exists, who borrowed from whom, and when?

This ladder is **our analytical reconstruction**, not period terminology.

## Primary-web controls

### Max Müller: generic commonality is below the threshold for genealogy

In _Chips from a German Workshop_, Müller explicitly says that the common ideas shared across Indian philosophy cannot establish Sāṃkhya → Buddhism dependence. What would matter are `definite similarities`; only such similarities could help determine whether Buddha borrowed from Kapila or Kapila from Buddha and thereby settle chronology.

This gives a clear distinction:

`generic similarity` ≠ `diagnostic similarity` ≠ `direction of dependence`.

Public text:
- https://www.gutenberg.org/files/24686/24686-h/24686-h.htm

### Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay: similarity, identity, and direction are separate propositions

Bankim's 1871 essay explicitly states that `Similarity is not identity`, and then, at the end, separates the demonstrated existence of similarity from the further question of whether Buddhism borrowed from Kapila or Kapila from Buddhism.

The structure is explicit:

`similarity` → does not require `identity` → still does not determine `direction`.

Public text:
- https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Buddhism_and_the_Sankhya_Philosophy

Caution: the Wikisource transcription is taken from the later collected edition rather than proofread directly against the 1871 scan, so publication-grade quotation should still be checked against the original _Calcutta Review_.

### Rajendralal Mitra: materials are not yet a system; lack of disproof is not proof

Mitra's preface to _The Yoga Aphorisms of Patañjali_ distinguishes pre-existing doctrines/materials from the later construction of a complete system (`The bricks existed, and Kapila was the architect`). He separately attacks Weber's phonetic genealogy and says that although he cannot prove there was no relation, there is `no proof worth the name` for the conjecture.

Two methodological separations result:

`antecedent material/doctrine` ≠ `complete system`;

`cannot disprove connection` ≠ `positive proof of connection`.

Public text:
- https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/yoga-sutras-with-bhojas-rajamartanda/d/doc1216993.html

Google Books bibliographic control:
- https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Yoga_Aphorisms_of_Patanjali.html?id=h_kIAAAAQAAJ

### Garbe: diagnostic detail can carry genealogy, but direction is a further step

Garbe's 1892 introduction starts from Müller and Oldenberg's complaint that the traditional Sāṃkhya/Buddhism dependence had not been sufficiently proved. He rejects generic parallels, insists on extant documentary ground, and argues that historical connection may betray itself most strongly in details and involuntarily preserved formal peculiarities. He then treats direction (`Buddha from Kapila` or the reverse) as an additional question.

This is the cleanest explicit proof protocol found in the sweep:

`resemblance` → `diagnostic specificity` → `historical connection` → `direction`.

Public full-text search result / scan mirror:
- https://www.scribd.com/document/711892238/Sen-Sankhya-Sutra-Vritti-Transltn-Richardgarbe

Google Books bibliographic control:
- https://books.google.com/books/about/Aniruddha_s_commentary_and_the_original.html?hl=en&id=yE2y0QF98E8C&output=html_text

Because Scribd is only a scan mirror, quotation should ultimately be checked against the public-domain scan / Google Books image state.

### Rhys Davids: chronology and textual survival impose a ceiling on system-level claims

Rhys Davids accepts that Sāṃkhya must predate its surviving texts, but stresses the chronological gap. His conclusion is notably scale-sensitive: one may infer earlier isolated thinkers with views from which Sāṃkhya later developed without thereby proving that Kapila authored the whole system or that the later system existed in full before Buddhism.

Public text:
- https://buddhadust.net/dhammatalk/tw-rhys-davids/buddhism-history-and-literature.htm

This maps directly onto the `doctrine/element` versus `whole system` distinction later visible in Lovejoy 004.

### Lovejoy 1898: usage, function, provenance, and confidence are explicitly partitioned

Lovejoy's published article independently validates the layered model.

He rejects Senart's claim by checking what the cited passages actually prove; he distinguishes `upādāna` from `upādānakkhandha` by textual usage; he says borrowed terms and perhaps borrowed ideas can nevertheless have an original `arrangement and application`; and in the `upādisesa` section he explicitly says etymology is underdetermined, so meaning must be settled by use. He labels his own evidence `relevant but not necessarily conclusive` and closes by saying the matter cannot be regarded as finally settled.

This supplies several separations in Lovejoy's own published voice:

`citation/witness` → `semantic use` → `causal/systemic function` → `provenance`;

and

`strong prevailing judgment` can coexist with `unsettled genealogy/text history`.

Public full text:
- https://dlbs.liberal.ntu.edu.tw/FULLTEXT/JR-ENG/love.htm

## Senart / Jacobi / Oldenberg as positions within the stack

The current repository's recovered primary texts remain the strongest source for their exact argument structure, while the public web independently confirms the tight chronology and bibliographic coupling of the controversy.

- Senart 1896 turns duplication/heterogeneity inside the nidāna formula into a compositional history: structural irregularity can be evidence of later amalgamation.
- Jacobi 1896/1898 presses stronger Sāṃkhya-Yoga genealogy and systematic analogy.
- Oldenberg admits real resemblance but requires stronger textual/source-critical warrant before direct borrowing or direction follows; his 1890 baseline already separates `Familienähnlichkeit` from demonstrated filiation.

## Modern historiographical control

The analytical model is not merely imposed on the controversy after the fact.

### Devin Griffiths — comparative method

Griffiths's _History of Humanities_ article argues that nineteenth-century comparativism repeatedly contained a tension between **genetic/historical** and **functional/contextual** explanations and that the modern comparative method developed through differentiation of distinct axes of similarity and difference. This strongly supports reading the Sāṃkhya/Buddhism debate as a dispute over what common patterns are entitled to mean historically.

- https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/693325

### Arie Molendijk — Max Müller

Molendijk's chapter on Müller's methods treats comparison as central to the nineteenth-century study of culture and emphasizes that Müller tied comparison to a broadly historical approach and to `historical facts`.

- https://academic.oup.com/book/12655/chapter/162612342

### Michael Dodson — contested authorization and Indian participation

Dodson's study of Vedic translation argues that the grounds of scholarly authorization inside nineteenth-century Orientalism were themselves contested and that Indian Sanskrit scholars actively engaged and criticized European philological practice. This supports the transregional correction: Indian scholars were participants in disputes over knowledgeable practice, not merely suppliers of textual data.

- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/modern-intellectual-history/article/abs/contesting-translations-orientalism-and-the-interpretation-of-the-vedas/A4591280526D98CFCC51C98B9C5365C1

### Broader history of Indology

Pascale Rabault-Feuerhahn's _Archives of Origins_ situates nineteenth-century Indology in relation to classical philology, Oriental languages and theology, and emphasizes Sanskrit's constitutive role in comparative grammar and questions of language/people genealogy.

- https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctvbqs8mf

## Consequence for the JHI Blog

The strongest blog claim is **not** that each participant embodied a sealed methodology. It is that the controversy distributed a series of proof burdens differently.

A precise sentence:

> **What divided the late-nineteenth-century Sāṃkhya/Buddhism controversy was not simply which genealogy was correct, but how far a scholar was entitled to move from resemblance to diagnostic similarity, from an element to a system, from local function to provenance, and from historical connection to the direction of borrowing.**

Lovejoy matters because 004 places him inside this argument before `history of ideas` became a named methodological programme.

For the blog, compress the seven-level analytical ladder to three reader-facing moves:

1. **What resembles what?**
2. **What exactly does the resemblance establish — a term, doctrine, element, or system?**
3. **When, if ever, may that resemblance become a genealogy?**

The full seven-level stack belongs in the research apparatus / later article.

## Main caution

Do not write `the controversy had a methodological hierarchy` as if this were a period-native schema.

Use:

- `layered proof burdens`;
- `levels of historical inference`;
- `different evidentiary thresholds`;
- `a controversy over what comparison was entitled to prove`.

Avoid:

- `all participants shared the same seven-step method`;
- `Lovejoy invented the hierarchy`;
- `Europe supplied method, India supplied evidence`.

## Current conclusion

**Web verdict: strongly supports the methodological-level framing.**

The most important upgrade is that the levels can now be anchored in explicit nineteenth-century statements by Müller, Bankim, Mitra, Garbe, Rhys Davids and Lovejoy, while modern history-of-humanities scholarship independently identifies the genetic-versus-functional tension and procedural differentiation as structural features of nineteenth-century comparativism.
