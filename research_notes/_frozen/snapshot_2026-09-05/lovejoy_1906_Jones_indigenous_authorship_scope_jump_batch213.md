# Batch 213 — William Jones 1905 as Indigenous scholarly warrant: author-controlled scope, receiver-controlled scale, and the politics of reattribution

Date: 2026-08-26
Status: synced / primary-source + institutional-biographical control
Scope: continue Batches 210–212 without reopening the already-settled Jones→Lovejoy `mechanicality` problem from Batch92. The new question is not whether Jones supplied Lovejoy with high-resolution evidence, but **who Jones was, what scope Jones himself assigned to his claims, and what happens to that scope when Lovejoy incorporates the article into the 1906 `primitive philosophy` synthesis**.

## Core result

The North-American warrant behind Lovejoy's 1906 universalizing comparison cannot be described adequately as `white ethnographers report anonymous indigenous beliefs → Lovejoy abstracts them`.

One of Lovejoy's most important high-resolution sources, William Jones, was himself a Native/Meskwaki scholar. Institutional records identify Jones as born on a Sauk and Fox reservation, educated at Harvard and Columbia, and as an anthropological/linguistic fieldworker on Algonquian languages. The American Philosophical Society preserves his field papers; later institutional and scholarly descriptions identify his Meskwaki/Fox background and upbringing in a Meskwaki-speaking family environment.

Jones's 1905 `The Algonkin Manitou` is also unusually explicit about **scope control**. He says that for the purposes of the article `Algonkin` will refer only to the Sauk, Fox, and Kickapoo, **not to other units of the same language family**. His concluding expansion beyond those three groups is only probabilistic: he says it `seems probable` that the same pattern holds among other central Algonkins such as Ojibwa, Ottawa, and Menominee.

Lovejoy's 1906 use of Jones therefore has a sharper political-epistemic structure than the generic phrase `native voice is transformed by Western theory` suggests:

> **an Indigenous scholar produces a deliberately bounded, locally evidenced conceptual-linguistic analysis; Lovejoy accepts that analysis as exceptionally diagnostic evidence, but controls the subsequent scale expansion from Sauk/Fox/Kickapoo to a trans-North-American packet and then to a cross-regional `primitive philosophy`.**

The key issue is therefore **scale sovereignty / scale control**, not simple presence or absence of Indigenous testimony.

A compact formula:

`Indigenous scholar-owned local scope`
→ `receiver accepts high-resolution warrant`
→ `receiver expands comparison set`
→ `receiver reattributes abstraction to collective primitive actors`.

This is a new layer beyond Batch92. Batch92 showed that Lovejoy strengthened Jones's interpretive verdict from irregular/affective interpretation to regular/intelligible causal law. Batch213 shows that he also **strengthened the social and cultural extension of the proposition**.

## 1. Jones is not merely a field-source node

The American Philosophical Society's William Jones Papers identify Jones as born in 1871 on a Sauk and Fox reservation, educated at Indian schools, Hampton, Andover, and Harvard (B.A. 1900), then Columbia (M.A. 1901; Ph.D. 1904), with field work on Algonquian languages in affiliation with the American Museum of Natural History.

A 2021 APS/CNAIR account by Meskwaki researcher Tierrany Keahna explicitly treats Jones as a Fox/Meskwaki anthropologist. Other institutional and historical records similarly identify his Native/Meskwaki ancestry and early upbringing in a Meskwaki family environment.

This matters for source coding.

Jones should not be coded simply as:

`ETHNOGRAPHER / EXTERNAL OBSERVER`.

A better role is:

`INDIGENOUS SCHOLAR / LINGUIST / ETHNOGRAPHER / ACTOR-NEAR ANALYST`.

That status does not make every proposition in Jones automatically `emic`, nor does it abolish mediation. Jones writes in the professional anthropology/folklore field, uses analytic vocabulary, and generalizes from evidence. But it materially changes the documentary chain by which Lovejoy obtains his strongest North-American evidence.

## 2. Jones's 1905 article imposes an explicit local scope ceiling

The opening of `The Algonkin Manitou` states its object with unusual precision.

Jones says his purpose is to give the meaning of `manitou` **as understood by three Algonkin peoples: Sauk, Fox, and Kickapoo**. Because their dialects, social form, and religious practices are sufficiently related, he will refer to them collectively as `Algonkin` for convenience.

He then adds a direct limitation:

> `the term shall apply to them only, and not to other units of the same family.`

This is a publication-level scope rule, not our retrospective caution.

The article's conclusion remains bounded. After summarizing the `cosmic, mysterious property` as potentially impersonal, efficacious, affectively apprehended and lacking a regular interpretive rule, Jones says only that it `seems probable` that something similar holds among other central Algonkins such as Ojibwa, Ottawa and Menominee.

Thus Jones's own inferential ladder is:

`Sauk + Fox + Kickapoo direct/near-direct evidence`
→ **assertive local synthesis**
→ `other central Algonkins`
→ **probabilistic extension only**.

William Jones, `The Algonkin Manitou`, *Journal of American Folk-Lore* 18.70 (1905), 183–190.

## 3. Lovejoy recruits Jones exactly where old missionary evidence reaches its ceiling

Batches77–78 already established the citation architecture in Lovejoy 1906.

Lovejoy first says seventeenth-century missionary evidence can prove concrete applications of `oki/manitou` to unusually powerful persons and things but cannot by itself prove an abstract, pervasive property or force.

He then introduces Jones's 1905 study at precisely that threshold. Jones supplies:

- grammatical gender as evidence for property/abstraction;
- impersonal substantive use;
- omnipresence/diffusion;
- transfer mechanisms;
- high-resolution linguistic and experiential material.

Thus Jones is not just another example inside Lovejoy's list. He is **proof-threshold evidence**.

The chain is:

`low-resolution missionary attestation`
→ `proof ceiling`
→ `Jones high-resolution Indigenous scholarly analysis`
→ `stronger proposition licensed`.

This makes the politics of the next step more consequential, because Lovejoy's scale expansion rests partly on evidence whose author had explicitly imposed a narrower scope.

## 4. Two receiver strengthenings must now be separated

Batch92 identified the first strengthening.

### A. Predicate / causal-strength strengthening

Jones:

- impersonal / pervasive / efficacious property;
- interpretation not governed by a regular rule;
- feeling rather than knowledge remains central.

Lovejoy:

- regular and intelligible laws;
- controllable/studiable causal energy;
- `Primitive Energetics`.

Code:

`SOURCE VERDICT`
→ `RECEIVER CAUSAL STRENGTHENING`.

Batch213 adds a second, independent strengthening.

### B. Scope strengthening

Jones:

- article-level assertive scope = Sauk/Fox/Kickapoo;
- extension to other central Algonkins = only `seems probable`.

Lovejoy:

- Jones becomes part of a North-American power packet;
- richer North-American analysis is used as analogical leverage for less finely resolved Melanesian/Polynesian/Bantu terms;
- cross-regional convergence is then attributed to `primitive philosophy`, `savage thought`, and in programmatic passages `most savages`.

Code:

`SOURCE SCOPE`
→ `RECEIVER SCALE STRENGTHENING`.

These two transformations should not be collapsed. Lovejoy alters both **what is claimed about the mechanism** and **how far the claim travels**.

## 5. This changes the project's source-politics model

A coarse model would be:

`indigenous informant`
→ `white ethnographer`
→ `Lovejoy`
→ `primitive philosophy`.

Jones makes that inadequate.

At least one high-value North-American path is:

`Meskwaki/Fox lifeworld + field interlocutors`
→ `William Jones as Indigenous professional scholar/linguist`
→ `bounded published analysis`
→ `Lovejoy selects diagnostic components`
→ `Lovejoy controls transregional abstraction and collective reattribution`.

The relevant asymmetry therefore lies less in who can speak at all than in **who controls the operation that changes evidentiary scale**.

This is analytically preferable to a generic language of `voice` because it gives us a documentary operation to test:

1. Who sets the source's explicit scope?
2. Who changes the comparison set?
3. At what step does possibility become probability, probability become type, and type become a predicate of a collective population?
4. Does the source author retain control over the final category's extension?

For Jones → Lovejoy, the answer to (4) is clearly **no**.

## 6. `Primitive` politics appears at the scale-jump, not simply in the local description

This result fits Batches208–212.

Notebook 005 frequently decomposes mixed rituals and beliefs into local mechanisms and historical layers. That decomposition can be cautious and anti-unilateral.

The political epistemic problem emerges at a later operation:

`local mechanism discrimination`
→ `portable mechanism`
→ `cross-cultural equivalence`
→ `collective actor attribution`.

Jones gives us a rare control because the source author **marks the boundary before Lovejoy crosses it**.

Therefore the strongest article-level formulation is now:

> **Lovejoy's `primitive philosophy` did not arise simply by ignoring indigenous conceptual evidence. In the strongest North-American portion of the 1906 proof, he relied on a Native scholar whose linguistic analysis materially improved the evidentiary resolution of the argument. The asymmetry appears one step later: William Jones explicitly restricted his `Algonkin` conclusions to Sauk, Fox and Kickapoo and offered extension to neighboring groups only as a probability; Lovejoy incorporated that bounded analysis into a transregional abstraction eventually predicated of `primitive philosophy` and `most savages`. The issue was therefore not exclusion of indigenous knowledge so much as unequal control over the scale at which that knowledge was made to speak.**

This should be preferred over a flattening claim that Lovejoy simply silenced or ignored Native authors.

## 8. Harvard overlap with Jones: institutional contemporaneity only

Jones's biographical record places him at Harvard beginning in 1896 and completing the A.B. in 1900. Lovejoy's Harvard formation occupies the overlapping later 1890s before his 1898–99 Paris period.

Thus there is an institutional overlap window in the late 1890s.

This is potentially interesting because Lovejoy's later 1906 proof gives Jones an unusually important evidentiary role.

But there is presently **no correspondence, course roster, club record, diary, or explicit retrospective statement** proving acquaintance, discussion, or direct Harvard contact.

Code:

`HARVARD INSTITUTIONAL CONTEMPORANEITY = CONFIRMED`

`PERSONAL/INTELLECTUAL CONTACT = UNPROVEN`.

Do not infer transmission through co-presence.

## 9. Relation to the India/Vedānta negative control

Batch211 showed that Lovejoy could perform decomposition and horizontal comparison without reassigning the result to a collective civilizational mind.

In the 1907 Vedānta review:

`Vedānta`
→ split Śaṅkara / Rāmānuja
→ compare arguments/problems with Green / contemporary idealism
→ preserve system-level difference.

In 1906:

`local ethnographic/linguistic differences`
→ extract recurrent causal mechanism
→ cross-region comparison
→ reattribute to `primitive philosophy` / `savage thought`.

Jones sharpens the contrast because the local source itself already supplies an explicit scale restriction. Lovejoy's universalization is therefore not simply forced upon him by a vague ethnographic witness; it is a **receiver-side decision to outrun a source-authored scope ceiling**.

## 10. Project vocabulary added by this batch

### `scope sovereignty`
Who controls the extension of a proposition from its evidentiary population to a larger comparative population.

Use cautiously; this is our analytical term, not actor vocabulary.

### `source-scope ceiling`
The maximum population/domain for which the source author directly claims warrant.

### `receiver scale strengthening`
A receiving author expands the extension of a proposition beyond the source's own asserted scope.

### `collective reattribution`
An analyst-generated comparative abstraction is attributed back to a larger actor collectivity (`primitive philosophy`, `savage thought`, etc.).

These operations are separable from lexical borrowing, causal strengthening, and source reliability.

## 11. Evidence cautions

1. Jones's Indigenous/Meskwaki scholarly position should not be romanticized into an unmediated communal voice. He is simultaneously a professional anthropologist, linguist, Harvard/Columbia-trained scholar, fieldworker, and historical actor shaped by coercive U.S. educational institutions.
2. `Indigenous scholar` does not make every analytic category in Jones emic.
3. Jones's opening scope statement is direct primary and unusually strong evidence for a source-authored comparison ceiling.
4. Jones's final extension to other central Algonkins is explicitly probabilistic, not a universal Algonquian claim.
5. Lovejoy's use of Jones as proof-threshold evidence and later cross-regional asymmetry were already directly controlled in Batches77–78.
6. The `regular law` transformation was already established in Batch92; Batch213's novelty is authorship + scope.
7. Hewitt's biography and 1902 publication are secure; direct Lovejoy citation remains HOLD.
8. Harvard overlap establishes co-presence only.
