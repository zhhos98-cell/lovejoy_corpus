# Lovejoy 1906–1907 — two modes of transverse reaggregation: `primitive philosophy` versus technical philosophy (Batch 211)

Date: 2026-08-26  
Status: synced  
Scope: bounded pressure test around notebooks 004/005 using Lovejoy 1906 `Primitive Philosophy` and the directly recovered 1907 review of Deussen/Woods/Runkle on Vedānta.  
Chronological rule: ordinary forward tracing stops at **1910**; later material enters only for explicit Buddhism / India / Sanskrit nodes.

## Core result

Batches 208–210 reconstructed a two-movement architecture around notebook 005 and the 1906 article:

1. **internal decomposition / stratification** — heterogeneous present objects are split into mechanisms, origins and historical layers;
2. **transverse reaggregation** — similarly classified relations are compared across otherwise heterogeneous traditions.

Batch 210 showed that 1906 then reattributes one such cross-cultural abstraction to a collective subject as `primitive philosophy` / `savage thought`.

The directly recovered 1907 Vedānta review provides an unusually sharp negative control. Lovejoy again decomposes a broad inherited category and then compares across traditions, but he does **not** reaggregate the results into one collective `Indian mind` or one representative Indian philosophy. Instead he insists on internal system differentiation and permits cross-tradition equivalence only at the level of **philosophical problems and arguments**.

The contrast is:

> **1906:** local heterogeneous practices → recurrent causal mechanism → generalized `primitive philosophy`  
> **1907:** broad `Vedānta` label → internally distinct systems → cross-tradition comparison by problem/reasoning without erasing system difference.

This suggests two different epistemic addresses for non-Western material inside the same early Lovejoy corpus:

- ethnographic `primitive` material is liable to **collective cognitive reaggregation**;
- Indian textual philosophy is treated as **internally differentiated technical philosophy** whose arguments can participate horizontally in philosophy itself.

The difference is not simply geographical or civilizational. It is a difference in **how comparison is allowed to close**.

---

## 1. 1906: decomposition closes in a collective philosophy

The 1906 article begins by dismantling broad observer categories:

- `god` and `spirit` may mistranslate words of efficacy;
- `religion` versus `magic` may conceal the same causal mechanism;
- one report can be valid for an observed custom yet invalid for theological interpretation;
- diverse rites need not share one historical origin.

But after this decomposition Lovejoy correlates North-American, Oceanic and Bantu materials into one causal-semantic profile. A recurrent mechanism becomes:

- a category more fundamental than personality;
- `Primitive Energetics`;
- the fundamental concept of `primitive philosophy`;
- an operative principle in `savage thought`;
- a likely dominant preoccupation of `most savages`.

The endpoint therefore **reattributes the analyst’s comparative abstraction to a generalized collective actor**.

Historical difference survives at the level of institutional genealogy, but philosophical/cognitive difference is reduced by the reconstructed common category.

---

## 2. 1907: Lovejoy dismantles a different representational monopoly

The Deussen review starts from a seemingly narrower bibliographic object, but the scale quickly expands.

Lovejoy accepts:

- Deussen’s authority;
- Woods/Runkle’s philological competence;
- the usefulness of the English Śaṅkara manual.

He nevertheless rejects the inference that this competence or availability justifies making Śaṅkara/Advaita the representative form of:

- Vedānta;
- Indian pantheism;
- the Upaniṣads;
- the Vedānta Sūtras.

He explicitly describes Śaṅkara as having acquired an `undue monopoly of attention`.

The operation is structurally familiar:

`strong witness / visible form / established authority`  
`≠ representative identity of the larger field`.

But unlike 1906, the correction proceeds by **restoring internal alternatives**, especially Rāmānuja.

---

## 3. The 1907 object is a differentiated field of systems, not one Indian worldview

The review treats Rāmānuja not as a secondary variant of the same doctrine but as a philosophically consequential rival.

Lovejoy’s own polemical preferences should be historicized, especially his harsh characterization of Śaṅkara. The structural point is more important:

> `Vedānta` does not name one homogeneous philosophical content.

The historical field contains systems that differ in:

- world/absolute relation;
- treatment of differentiation;
- consciousness;
- subject/object relation;
- self-consciousness;
- argumentative route.

A broad civilizational or school label is therefore analytically underdetermining.

This is closely compatible with notebook 004’s refusal to infer a whole Sāṃkhya system from a generic doctrinal resemblance and with its treatment of borrowed components as compatible with a distinctive configuration.

---

## 4. Yet Lovejoy still reaggregates — at the level of philosophical problems

The 1907 review is not anti-comparative.

Lovejoy explicitly wants Indian systems placed in relation to modern and contemporary Western philosophy. He compares or proposes comparison among:

- Śaṅkara;
- Rāmānuja;
- T. H. Green;
- contemporary British/American idealism;
- probably Royce in one OCR-uncertain locus.

But the comparative unit is not `Indian mentality` versus `Western mentality`.

It is:

> **a philosophical problem, argument, or structural relation.**

The same problem can be instantiated by historically independent systems without requiring:

- direct influence;
- common origin;
- civilizational identity;
- one representative national/racial philosophy.

Thus 1907 also performs transverse reaggregation, but the grouping remains **problem-relative**.

---

## 5. `Reasons > conclusions`: comparison is routed through argumentative production

The sharpest methodological sentence in the review says that analysis of the **reasons** leading philosophers to conclusions is more illuminating and profitable than even an accurate exposition of the conclusions themselves.

This changes the comparative object.

A proposition is not portable simply because two systems state something similar. One must reconstruct:

- what problem is being answered;
- what reasoning produces the conclusion;
- what relation the conclusion has to the rest of the system.

The cross-tradition comparison is therefore constrained by **argumentative function**.

This is the textual-philosophical analogue of 005’s mechanism test:

- 005: same rite/form ≠ same causal mechanism;
- 1907: same conclusion/label ≠ same philosophical reasoning or system.

But the final epistemic address differs sharply from 1906 primitive ethnology.

## 6. Two modes of reaggregation

| Operation | 1906 `Primitive Philosophy` | 1907 Vedānta review |
|---|---|---|
| starting whole | primitive religion / ethnographic reports / magic-religion labels | Vedānta / Indian pantheism represented by Śaṅkara |
| decomposition | report → custom / word / translation / causal mechanism | broad school → distinct systems / arguments / mediations |
| retained difference | institutional genealogy, local ritual form | system identity, doctrinal architecture, argumentative route |
| transverse comparison | recurring impersonal/transferable efficacy across independent races | recurring philosophical problems across Indian and Western systems |
| final comparative unit | causal category / belief family | problem / reasoning / structural relation |
| endpoint | generalized `primitive philosophy` / `savage thought` | differentiated technical philosophies in comparative dialogue |
| collective actor reaggregation | strong | weak / blocked |

This contrast is the main result of Batch 211.

## 7. Consequence for the status of India/Buddhism in the project

The early corpus should not be organized through a simple binary:

`West = philosophy`  
`non-West = primitive ethnography`.

Lovejoy’s own archive resists that map.

Indian material appears at least in two very different epistemic registers:

### 004 / 1898 / 1907

- technical texts;
- philological witnesses;
- competing philosophical systems;
- argument and conceptual configuration;
- legitimate contribution to philosophy itself.

### 005 / 1906 comparative ethnology

- ethnographic cases;
- rites/customs;
- lexical evidence from native languages;
- comparative causal mechanism;
- generalized `primitive philosophy` reconstructed across populations.

Both are comparative, both can be Orientalist in institutional/historical senses, and both involve non-Western material. But their **units of comparison and modes of reaggregation differ**.

This is a useful control against treating `Orientalism` or `primitive` as one homogeneous epistemic regime.

## 8. Connection to the claim that Oriental studies is a model rather than simply an opponent

The 1907 review strengthens an interpretation already latent in notebook 004.

Lovejoy expects scholarship on India to provide:

- textual criticism;
- internal differentiation of systems;
- historical-critical survey;
- philosophical problem reconstruction;
- analysis of reasons rather than merely doctrines;
- cross-tradition comparison that can produce philosophy.

This is a very different role from the passive supply of exotic examples.

Indian philosophy acts as a **technical comparative field** in which broad labels are decomposed and arguments are made portable at the problem level.

The safe claim is not that Indian philosophy invented Lovejoy’s method. It is:

> **Lovejoy’s early methodological formation repeatedly occurs while he is working inside an Indian-philosophical field that he treats as technically and philosophically commensurable with European philosophy.**

The 1906 primitive-ethnology route and the 1907 Vedānta route should therefore be kept analytically distinct.

## 9. Political consequence: not all transverse comparison has the same political effect

Batch 210 showed how mechanism comparison can generate collective cognitive reaggregation:

`same mechanism across heterogeneous peoples`  
→ `primitive philosophy`.

The 1907 review shows another possibility:

`same philosophical problem across heterogeneous systems`  
→ `comparability without collective cognitive identity`.

This difference matters politically.

The mere act of cross-cultural abstraction is not enough to explain hierarchy or flattening. We must ask **what the abstraction is reattributed to**.

- If it returns as the fundamental philosophy of `most savages`, heterogeneous populations become cognitively re-totalized.
- If it remains a problem-level comparandum, Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Green and other systems can remain distinct while participating in the same analytical space.

Thus the political/evidentiary unit should be:

> **comparison + endpoint of reattribution.**

This is more precise than treating all comparativism as either inherently flattening or inherently pluralizing.

## 10. Revised larger model

The early Lovejoy corpus now supports at least three linked operations:

### A. decomposition

Break broad categories, systems, rites or reports into relations/components.

### B. transverse comparison

Group components across objects according to a selected relation:

- causal mechanism;
- philosophical problem;
- logical function;
- semantic usage.

### C. reattribution

Ask where the analyst places the result:

- back into a collective actor (`primitive philosophy`);
- into a distinct system’s configuration;
- into an analyst-owned comparative class;
- into a cross-tradition problem space.

The third step is where political and epistemic consequences diverge most sharply.

This may be a stronger general schema for the project than `decomposition/recomposition` alone.

- that India enjoyed equal institutional or cultural status in every part of Lovejoy’s work;
- that cross-problem comparison is politically neutral.

The batch identifies a difference in documented comparative closure, not an explicit Lovejoy meta-theory.

## 12. Strongest current paper formulation

> **Lovejoy’s early comparative practice did not always close at the same level. In the 1906 `Primitive Philosophy` essay he decomposed ethnographic reports and ritual forms until an impersonal causal mechanism could be isolated, correlated that mechanism across historically independent populations, and then reattributed the abstraction as a fundamental concept of `primitive philosophy`. A year later, reviewing Deussen’s Śaṅkara manual, he attacked the opposite kind of representational monopoly: Vedānta could not be collapsed into one Advaita form. He called instead for the internal differentiation of Indian systems, analysis of the reasons producing their conclusions, and comparison with Western idealisms at the level of philosophical problems. Both operations move from decomposition to transverse comparison. Their endpoints differ. One constructs a generalized collective philosophy; the other preserves differentiated systems inside a shared problem-space.**

Compact analytical rule:

> **The politics of comparison lies not only in what is compared, but in where the comparison is made to terminate.**

This is project vocabulary, not Lovejoy’s phrase.
