## 2. Kingsley securely owns the gateway / smallpox blood packet

Mary Kingsley's *Travels in West Africa* (1897) directly reports that:

- village entrances carry guards/charms against spirit visitors;
- during smallpox or war, gateways are sprinkled with sacrificial blood;
- goats and sheep are kept for such sacrifices;
- villagers may eat the meat, while the blood has the distinct sacrificial/spirit function because `the blood is the life`.

Notebook 005 elsewhere has exact Kingsley anchors in this local reading run, including the p.89/p.90 page cluster.

Thus the gateway / epidemic / sacrificial-blood ethnographic packet is **Kingsley-owned**, not a Lovejoy-generated case.

## 3. Marillier 1898 already supplies the crucial spirit-versus-direct-force decomposition

Batch 143 recovered Marillier's 1898 *Revue de l'histoire des religions* discussion of foundation/building rites, especially printed p.417.

Marillier explicitly distinguishes at least three possible mechanisms for blood/remains placed in or around a structure:

1. **attach a guardian/protective spirit to the monument**;
2. **blood acts as a charm, its magical force operating directly as a vehicle of force/life**;
3. **propitiatory sacrifice** intended to disarm divine hostility.

The first two mechanisms are structurally almost identical to the p.90 distinction previously treated as possibly Lovejoy-local:

`SPIRIT FIXED / BOUND TO PLACE`

versus

`DIRECT PROTECTIVE BLOOD EFFICACY`.

Marillier further argues elsewhere in the same 1898 source field that blood rites should not automatically be read as covenant/union; some operate by immediate magical efficacy.

Therefore the p.90 binary cannot carry an originality claim for Lovejoy.
