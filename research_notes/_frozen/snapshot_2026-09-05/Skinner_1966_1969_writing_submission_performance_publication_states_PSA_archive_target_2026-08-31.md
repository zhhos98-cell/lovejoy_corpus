# Skinner 1966–1969: writing, submission, conference performance, and publication are distinct source states

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT SKINNER INTERVIEW + DIRECT 1968/69 BIBLIOGRAPHIC CONTROL / PSA ARCHIVAL ROUTE IDENTIFIED / EXACT SUBMISSION HISTORY STILL OPEN**

## Core correction

The previous `pre-canonical argument ecology` note correctly distinguished the 1968 Political Studies Association paper from the 1969 published article, but the word `draft` can still imply too late a genesis.

Skinner's own later interview supplies a decisive correction. Reflecting on `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas`, he says:

- the article was published in 1969;
- he **wrote it between 1966 and 1967**;
- he had considerable difficulty getting it published.

Therefore the physical/textual sequence should currently be reconstructed as:

`WRITING / MANUSCRIPT FORMATION, 1966–67`
-> `SUBMISSION / REJECTION OR DELAY HISTORY, DETAILS OPEN`
-> `PSA CONFERENCE VERSION / PERFORMANCE, 4 APRIL 1968`
-> `REVISED HISTORY AND THEORY PUBLICATION, 1969`
-> later `1970–75 methodological extensions`
-> `1988 anthology/reply canonization`
-> `2002 heavily revised republication`
-> `2012 / 2021 / 2026 anniversary rehistoricization`.

Thus:

`1968 CONFERENCE VERSION`
!= `GENESIS DRAFT`.

It is better called:

### `CONFERENCE PERFORMANCE STATE`

or

### `CONFERENCE VERSION`.

## I. Actor-level self-description

In the interview `Ideas, History and Social Sciences: An Interview with Quentin Skinner`, Skinner retrospectively describes his early methodological polemic and states that he wrote the essay in 1966–67 but struggled to get it published.

He also says he would not now write in the same way; he regards parts of the essay as philosophically ill-judged and abusive and later cut it heavily for republication.

This simultaneously establishes:

- an earlier manuscript chronology than the canonical 1969 date suggests;
- publication friction;
- later authorial re-evaluation;
- substantial version change.

The canonical object `Skinner 1969` is therefore a temporarily stabilized textual state, not the beginning or end of the methodological position.

---

## II. Dunn 1968 does not require circulation of the PSA version

John Dunn's `The Identity of the History of Ideas`, published in the April 1968 issue of *Philosophy*, states in its final note that the paper arose from several years of discussion with Peter Laslett and especially Skinner. Dunn directs readers to Skinner's published 1966 `The Limits of Historical Explanations` as partly analogous and partly contrasting.

This is sufficient evidence for a sustained pre-1968 method conversation.

Therefore the presence of a `Skinnerian` explanatory formulation in Dunn's article should **not** be used to infer that Dunn read the 4 April 1968 PSA conference text.

The more plausible current evidence architecture is:

`SKINNER 1966 + YEARS OF DISCUSSION`
<-> `DUNN'S DEVELOPING METHOD`

while

`UNPUBLISHED 1966–67 SKINNER MANUSCRIPT CIRCULATION`
remains possible but unproven.

---

## III. Austin enters through a reciprocal relation, not a one-author invention story

Dunn's 1968 article directly cites Austin's *How to Do Things with Words* twice.

Later Skinner explicitly credited Dunn with seeing the relevance of Austin's speech-act theory to the interpretation of texts in the history of political thought.

The currently reconstructed 1968 Skinner conference version did not cite Austin, while the 1969 published version did.

This makes a genuine relation available:

`DUNN -> AUSTIN RELEVANCE -> SKINNER LATER ACKNOWLEDGMENT`.

But the exact revision route remains open.

Do not write:

`Dunn caused Skinner to add Austin in 1969`

unless correspondence or version-level evidence directly proves the causal link.

The correct current code is:

`INTELLECTUAL CONTRIBUTION / ACKNOWLEDGED RELEVANCE = DIRECT`

`CLAUSE-LEVEL REVISION CAUSATION = HOLD`.

---

## IV. Leslie 1970: conference performance had an independent reception life

Margaret Leslie explicitly says `In Defence of Anachronism` arose from reflection on Skinner's 1968 PSA conference paper, later published in revised form as `Meaning and Understanding`.

This matters even more after the 1966–67 writing-date correction.

The 1968 conference paper was not merely a step on the way to publication. It became a **publicly effective version in its own right**:

`MANUSCRIPT ALREADY EXISTS`
-> `CONFERENCE PERFORMANCE`
-> `AUDIENCE / READER RECEPTION`
-> `LESLIE CRITICAL REFLECTION`
-> `SKINNER COMMENTS ON LESLIE DRAFT`
-> `LESLIE 1970 PUBLICATION`.

Therefore:

`PUBLICATION STATE`
!= `PUBLIC EFFECT STATE`.

A conference version can generate a reception branch that differs from the later article's reception.

## VI. Audience-reaction evidence ceiling

Adrian Blau's 2026 open-access introduction says the April 1968 paper faced a potentially hostile audience and that at least one audience member walked out.

A 2009 journalistic profile says a larger number `stalked out` and later returned apologetically.

These are not equivalent evidence.

Current hierarchy:

- `AT LEAST ONE WALKOUT` — usable as Blau's recent reconstruction, pending his source note;
- `A NUMBER WALKED OUT / LATER APOLOGIZED` — retrospective anecdotal account, lower grade;
- contemporaneous conference report/minutes — not yet recovered.

Do not dramatize the scene beyond the best-controlled wording.

## VII. New source-state ontology

This episode now supports a useful source ontology for methodological texts:

### S1 — working/writing state

Manuscript produced privately, perhaps revised before circulation.

### S2 — circulated/submitted state

Sent to editors/colleagues; may produce referee/editorial feedback.

### S3 — performed state

Conference/lecture version acquires audience and independent reception.

### S4 — published state

Journal/book version becomes formally citable.

### S5 — revised/reprinted state

Author alters the text and/or paratext.

### S6 — canonized state

Anthology, syllabus, anniversary volume or disciplinary retrospective stabilizes one version as `the` method text.

These states may overlap and do not form a simple one-way causal chain.

For Skinner:

`S1 1966–67`
-> `S3 1968`
-> `S4 1969`
-> `S5 2002`
while
`S6` grows through 1988, 2012, 2026.

The exact S2 history remains an archival target.

---

## VIII. Larger methodological consequence for the Lovejoy/JHI project

The project already distinguishes:

`PROGRAMME != PRACTICE`

and

`UNIT TYPE != RELATION TYPE`.

Add:

`TEXT IDENTITY != VERSION IDENTITY`.

A methodological text is itself a historical object with multiple versions, performances and receptions.

This applies symmetrically:

- Lovejoy's notebook / article / later methodological retrospective;
- Wiener conference typescript / JHI article;
- Skinner manuscript / conference performance / article / republication / anthology.

Therefore the history of methods should reconstruct **source states and carrier changes**, not cite a famous publication year as if a method suddenly came into existence at print release.
