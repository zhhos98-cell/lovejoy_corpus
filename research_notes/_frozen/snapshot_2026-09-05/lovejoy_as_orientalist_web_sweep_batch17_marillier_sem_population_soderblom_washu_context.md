## Research move

This batch shifts the 005 contextual reconstruction from a teacher–Lovejoy dyad to a **seminar population and production ecology**. The official EPHE report identifies Lovejoy’s exact institutional status among Marillier’s 1898–99 participants; Nathan Söderblom supplies a same-year, Marillier-supervised research product; Söderblom’s simultaneous Avestan work under Antoine Meillet shows how comparative-religion problems could be joined to specialist philology; and Washington University evidence gives a concrete institutional setting for Lovejoy’s later public/teaching activity and notebook reuse.

The result strengthens the context of notebook `MS38_004_001_061_005` without turning contextual proximity into claims about the exact origin of individual notebook sentences.

## 1. Lovejoy’s exact status in Marillier’s 1898–99 seminar

The official EPHE religious-sciences report for 1898–99 gives Marillier’s programme and participant categories with unusual precision.

### Monday conference: survivance

Marillier’s stated task was to establish that **moral elements do not enter at the origin of the eschatology of “non-civilized” peoples**. He defended the thesis of his earlier *La survivance de l’âme et l’idée de justice chez les peuples non civilisés*: the other life is initially conceived as a **continuation**, rather than compensation, reparation or punishment, of earthly life. New comparative evidence came especially from the Americas.

This is an exceptionally close institutional counterpart to 005 pp.1–30, where Lovejoy repeatedly separates naturalistic continuity, ritual/magical qualification, social status and strictly moral desert.

### Tuesday conference: sacrifice / anthropophagy

The second conference continued research on human sacrifice and ritual anthropophagy, especially Bantu southern Africa, and compared the reasons for human victims across expiatory, funerary, anthropophagic and magical sacrifice. It also compared Guinea, North-American Indigenous peoples and non-Aryan populations of India, and treated animal blood sacrifice, agrarian rites and fecundative sacrifice.

Again, this is not merely a topic resemblance: it is essentially the problem-field through which 005 pp.46–119 proceeds.

### Participant categories

The report records **20 registered participants** and distinguishes:

- titular students regularly attending: **Söderblom (diplômé), Travers, Schaefer**;
- auditors who **“ont pris une part active aux travaux”**: **Lovejoy, W. S. Andrews, Lorette**.

This wording upgrades the contextual claim about Lovejoy. He was not merely named on a registration list or present in the room; the institution explicitly classified him as an auditor who **took an active part in the work**.

Evidence limit: the phrase does not by itself prove that Lovejoy wrote a particular seminar essay, presented a paper, or received a specific comment from Marillier. N. W. Thomas’s 1896 eyewitness account establishes that such student essays/presentations/commentary were part of Marillier’s seminar practice, but individual assignment of that workflow to Lovejoy still requires a manuscript or administrative witness.

## 2. Söderblom is now the strongest same-year control

The EPHE diploma tables identify Nathan Söderblom’s 1898 diploma work as:

> *Traces dans le Mazdéisme d’une ancienne conception sur la survivance des morts*.

Persée’s Marillier authority record explicitly catalogues it as written by Söderblom **under the direction of Léon Marillier**. It was expanded/published as:

> Nathan Söderblom, “Les Fravashis: Étude sur les traces dans le mazdéisme d’une ancienne conception sur la survivance des morts,” *Revue de l’histoire des religions* 39 (1899), 229–260, 373–418.

This is a powerful control because it is a **research output from the same Marillier orbit, in the same year, on the same survivance problem** that structures the opening of 005. It gives us a way to distinguish three layers:

1. Marillier’s programme / comparative problem;
2. a regular diploma student’s specialist research product (Söderblom);
3. an active auditor’s working notebook and later abstractions (Lovejoy).

The comparison should therefore ask not only whether Söderblom and Lovejoy share examples, but how each turns the common seminar problem into an argument.

## 3. Söderblom also supplies a philological-control case

Söderblom was not working only inside Marillier’s comparative-religion conference. The EPHE historical-philological reports show him simultaneously working in **Zend/Avestan philology under Antoine Meillet**.

For the 1897–98 report, Söderblom and other auditors explained chapters of the *Vendidad* and Yashts. Meillet emphasized that the Avesta is composed of juxtaposed fragments, often transmitted in corrupt form, producing textual irregularity and obscurity. In the following report Söderblom again worked under Meillet on extended portions of the *Yasna*, especially several gāthās.

This matters methodologically. Söderblom’s Marillier-supervised survivance project sat at the junction of:

- specialist text/language work;
- source criticism and historical stratification;
- a comparative-religion question about the survival of the dead.

That is a useful external control on Lovejoy’s own early formation. The EPHE environment did not force a choice between philology and comparative classification; in at least one documented participant, the two were practiced simultaneously and fed into one research project.

## 4. Marillier’s 1899–1900 programme confirms a continuing research sequence

The next EPHE annual report, after Lovejoy’s Paris year, shows Marillier continuing almost exactly the same research programme. He treated:

- agrarian human sacrifices among non-Aryan peoples of India, especially Khonds of Orissa;
- the magical/fecundating power of blood;
- expiatory sacrifice and expulsion of evils;
- critical examination of Frazer’s *Golden Bough*;
- origin of death;
- nature of death and disease;
- the effect of beliefs about death/disease on eschatology;
- the **amoral** character of that eschatology;
- Christian interpolations in American eschatological myths.

This is useful as a **programme-continuity control**, not as evidence that Lovejoy attended the 1899–1900 conferences. It shows that the clusters visible across 005 were components of a multi-year Marillier research programme rather than an ad hoc list of examples encountered by Lovejoy alone.

The chronological warning remains essential: because 005 is longitudinal and includes a securely dated 20 Dec. 1905 reuse, thematic similarity to Marillier’s later reports cannot date an individual notebook page by itself.

## 5. Uppsala becomes a concrete manuscript-control target

No web-level item has yet been found explicitly labelled “Marillier lecture notes” or “Paris 1898 seminar notes.” The archive nevertheless remains the best external manuscript control because Söderblom was a documented regular participant, diploma student under Marillier, and author of a same-year survivance study.

## 6. Washington University Association: a later institutional channel around Lovejoy

The 1905 Washington University *Hatchet* gives a concrete view of the Association’s publishing infrastructure. Its page on university publications lists:

> **The Bulletin of the Washington University Association**
>
> Editorial Committee: **Arthur O. Lovejoy, Managing Editor**; Otto Heller; Ambrose P. Winston; W. R. Vickroy (for the Alumni Association).

The Association itself was an organized lecture/public-culture body; the 1903 *Hatchet* lists officers and a dedicated lecture committee. Its *Bulletin* began in 1903, and surviving library holdings include vols. 3–4 for 1905–06.

This adds an important piece to the 1901 Buddhism-syllabus problem. Daniel J. Wilson’s bibliography records *Syllabus: The Philosophy of Buddhism*, Washington University Association, 1901, with four lectures:

1. “The Place of Buddhism among the Philosophies of India”;
2.–3. “Two Essential Formulas of Buddhism”;
4. “Buddhist Ethics and its Goal.”

Because the Association *Bulletin* begins only in 1903, the 1901 item was very likely a **separate syllabus/pamphlet or Association lecture handout**, rather than an issue of the later Bulletin. The retrieval strategy should therefore search Washington University Association ephemera, lecture-series pamphlets, and Lovejoy papers, not only bound Bulletin volumes.

By 1905 Lovejoy’s role as **managing editor** of the Bulletin also shows that the Association was not peripheral to him. It was a concrete channel through which he helped organize/publicize university intellectual work.

## 8. Unresolved seminar peers: stop name-guessing and use registration files

The exact identities of **Travers, Schaefer, W. S. Andrews and Lorette** remain unresolved. Broad web searches have not safely disambiguated them. In particular, `W. S. Andrews` should not be equated with William Symes Andrews on name resemblance alone.

The correct next source is the EPHE student/attendance bureaucracy. Archives nationales / EPHE Vth Section student-administration files and the digitized registration/attendance registers can potentially supply full names, addresses, nationalities and cross-enrolments. Once those identities are fixed, their personal papers can be searched for a second or third independent Marillier notebook.
