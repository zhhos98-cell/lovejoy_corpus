# Irving King 1910 → 1911 — primitive-priority requalification under review pressure

Date: 2026-08-26
Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / CONFIDENCE-TIGHTENING CONTROL, NOT A FORMAL RETRACTION**

## Core result

A direct before/after comparison of Irving King's *The Development of Religion* (1910) and `Some Problems in the Science of Religion` (1911) shows a real tightening of the evidentiary status assigned to the historical-priority claim around impersonal/quasi-mechanical power.

The shift must be described carefully.

It is **not**:

`1910 dogmatic primitive-stage claim`
→ `1911 complete reversal`.

The 1910 book already contains strong caveats about reconstructing prehistoric life and repeatedly marks some origin claims as conjectural. The 1911 article, however, makes the priority ceiling substantially more explicit and restrictive in a text written because reviews had disclosed misunderstandings.

Best classification:

> **primitive-priority requalification / confidence tightening / explicit separation of comparative attestation from historical anteriority.**

Do not classify as an author-acknowledged self-correction on the model of Lovejoy's Kant 1909→1910 sequence.

## I. 1910 — the chapter advertises a genuinely primitive hypothesis

The table of contents for chapter VI, `The Mysterious Power`, presents a programmatic sequence:

- `A quasi-mechanical, impersonal potency`;
- wide prevalence among natural races;
- probable relation to primitive worship;
- `A truly primitive 'concept'`;
- conditions which may have produced it;
- `More naive than animism`;
- expression of an elementary `take-care` attitude;
- not itself a religious concept, though important in religious development.

The index likewise directs the reader under the power-conception to `truly primitive, 163`.

This is stronger than mere regional description. The book invites a genetic/prioritarian reading.

## II. 1910 body — the claim is strong but already qualified

### Printed p.156

After reviewing the transregional evidence, King says the notion of a `power` or potency is widespread among `ethnic races`, usually scarcely personal and rather impersonal/quasi-mechanical. He calls the need to isolate this idea essential to understanding what current discussions classify under magic and religion.

### Printed p.157

King asks what conditions **could conceivably** have produced `manitou`, `orenda`, `mana`. He acknowledges the obvious objection that an impersonal power appears too abstract to be a `truly primitive mode of thought`, then answers through the child analogy and a pre-personal `take care / watch out` attitude.

Thus the argument is genetically ambitious but linguistically modal.

### Printed p.163

King sums up what he calls the `primitive character, wide prevalence, and possible origin` of the quasi-mechanical impersonal force. He says it is not in itself a religious concept but raw material that can enter religion and magic.

The propositions are already partly decomposed:

- widespread impersonal power field = strong;
- `primitive character` = asserted with substantial confidence;
- exact origin = possible / reconstructive;
- inherently religious status = rejected.

### Printed pp.176–177 — internal ceiling

The later `Magic and Religion` chapter is crucial. King writes that impersonal force `seems possible` as a more primitive way of conceiving natural forces than spirits, but immediately adds an explicit footnote and methodological qualification:

- religions/practices of contemporary natural races cannot be taken as `truly primitive`;
- every current system has a long preceding history, mostly unknown;
- specific prehistoric histories cannot be reconstructed;
- psychology of present social change may suggest **characteristics** of truly primitive life and can be used only `within limits`.

He therefore already distinguishes:

`present comparative material`
→ `methodological clues / natural-history reconstruction`

from

`literal survival of an unreconstructed prehistoric stage`.

This prevents an exaggerated 1910→1911 reversal narrative.

## III. 1911 — historical primitiveness is explicitly lowered to speculation

King's 1911 HTR article opens by saying that reviews of his recent book had revealed misunderstandings about both the general point of view and specific meanings, and the article therefore restates fundamental problems.

In the impersonal-power discussion he again separates the wide distribution/character of the power field from the stronger historical question of whether the quasi-mechanical impersonal view is **actually primitive** or itself the product of sophistication.

At that stronger level, King explicitly says that one can only speculate.

Thus the 1911 hierarchy is sharper:

`comparative attestation / impersonal-power pattern`
= positive evidentiary object

`historical priority / actually primitive status`
= speculative / not demonstrated.

## IV. What changed

The best before/after comparison is one of **epistemic address**, not substantive abandonment.

### 1910

King is willing to use phrases such as:

- `A truly primitive 'concept'` (chapter synopsis);
- `primitive character`;
- `more primitive way`;
- `may have been a truly primitive way`;

while also adding substantial reconstruction caveats.

### 1911

The stronger priority proposition is isolated and assigned a much more explicit status:

> historical primitiveness = speculation.

The probable historical dynamic is therefore:

`strong genetic programme + embedded caveats`
→ `published review controversy / reported misunderstandings`
→ `more explicit proposition-level confidence partition`.

Do not assign this tightening to Leuba alone. King's 1911 article cites both George A. Coe and Leuba in connection with different criticisms and says reviews plural disclosed misunderstandings. The exact review responsible for the primitive-priority restatement is not identified.

## V. Relation to Lovejoy 1906

King's 1910 chapter directly uses Lovejoy 1906 for:

- comparative category formulation;
- correction of Fletcher's `wakonda = will` interpretation;
- evidence résumé.

King then gives the category his own genetic/social-psychological role.

The 1910→1911 requalification is therefore especially useful as a receiver-side control:

> **A Lovejoy-linked comparative object could be retained while the receiving author explicitly lowered the evidentiary status of its historical-priority address.**

This does not establish that Lovejoy's method caused King's proof partition.

## VI. JHI-facing consequence

The American epilogue can now say more precisely:

> **The comparative object did not simply travel intact. In King it was accepted strongly enough to organize a chapter and to support a genetic hypothesis, yet the controversy around that book forced a sharper public distinction between the wide attestation of impersonal power and the much harder claim that such a conception was historically prior.**

That is a publication-level analogue to the project's governing rule:

> **A stronger proposition does not automatically inherit the evidence that established a weaker proposition.**

But preserve the asymmetry:

- Lovejoy 1909→1910 Kant = explicit actor-acknowledged correction;
- King 1910→1911 = review-context requalification / confidence tightening, not self-confessed error.
