## 4. p.30 — inserted survivance sheets: non-Hova wording, corporeal double, second death

The left inserted sheet directly gives the dead as:

`often a grotesque & horrible creature, a sort of animated skeleton`

and explicitly refers to terminology used by one of the `non-Hova` tribes. The former canonical phrase `Hora/Hova [?] tribes` is withdrawn.

The same sheet describes a high / forest-clad place associated with Hova country and the dead; the place-name and several geographic words remain too uncertain for diplomatic freezing.

The facing inserted sheet is headed:

`F. occupations of dead or Anthropomorphism.`

Its argument is secure in substance: a more subtle conception treats the soul as a corporeal double of the body which separates from the body at death; it can subsequently undergo a `second death` or annihilation. The future life is therefore limited rather than necessarily immortal.

This independent Marillier material strongly matches the earlier Lovejoy page-level distinction:

`SURVIVANCE != IMMORTALITY`.

The value here is not that Marillier supplies the origin of Lovejoy's distinction, but that the notebook physically contains a source packet Lovejoy was using to elaborate the same problem through Madagascar/Hova material.
