# *History and Theory* 1960–1969: pre-existing metahistorical carrier architecture before Skinner 1969

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT OFFICIAL TOC / JSTOR ISSUE CONTROL / CARRIER-FORM ARCHITECTURE LOCKED / EDITORIAL INTENTION BEYOND PUBLIC FORM HELD**

## Core result

Quentin Skinner's 1969 `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas` did not enter an otherwise ordinary historical journal that lacked an existing venue for methodological or metahistorical argument.

By the time Skinner's essay appeared, *History and Theory* had already spent most of the 1960s distributing historical-method and philosophy-of-history work across several distinct carrier forms:

- ordinary full-length articles;
- review essays treated as substantive essays rather than bibliographic notices;
- communications;
- explicit symposia inside ordinary issues;
- numbered thematic `Beihefte` / supplements devoted to a single methodological or historiographical problem.

The institutional point is therefore:

`SKINNER 1969 METHOD TEXT`
entered
`PRE-EXISTING METAHISTORY INFRASTRUCTURE`.

This does **not** reduce the originality, polemical force or later influence of Skinner's argument.

It changes the venue-level historical claim:

`ARGUMENTATIVE RUPTURE`
!=
`CREATION OF METHOD JURISDICTION OR CARRIER FORM`.

---

## I. The journal begins with differentiated carrier roles

The official *History and Theory* volume pages show that the journal was already separating forms of scholarly intervention in its first volumes.

### Volume 1, 1960–61

Ordinary articles coexist with a labeled `REVIEW ESSAYS` section. These reviews are long-form intellectual interventions, not simply short book notices.

### Volume 2, 1962–63

The architecture becomes even more explicit:

- `ARTICLES`;
- `REVIEW ESSAYS`;
- at least one labeled `COMMUNICATION` — F. A. Hayek, `The Uses of 'Gresham's Law' as an Illustration in Historical Theory`.

The same volume carries articles on historical explanation, chronology, narrative sentences, historiographical controversy and relative time.

Therefore, before the mid-1960s:

`HISTORICAL THEORY / METHOD`
was already the journal's ordinary object,
while
`ARTICLE / REVIEW ESSAY / COMMUNICATION`
were already differentiated intervention roles.

---

## II. 1963: explicit symposium engineering

The official Volume 3 contents identify Number 1 as:

`Symposium: Uses of Theory in the Study of History`.

Its internal architecture includes:

- Heinz Lubasz, `Introduction`;
- Samuel H. Beer, `Causal Explanation and Imaginative Re-Enactment`;
- Charles Tilly, `The Analysis of a Counter-Revolution`;
- Michael Walzer, `Puritanism as a Revolutionary Ideology`;
- William Nisbet Chambers, `Party Development and Party Action: The American Origins`;
- followed by substantive review essays including J. G. A. Pocock on Hexter and Jacob M. Price on E. H. Carr.

This is direct evidence that by 1963 *History and Theory* could deliberately aggregate a methodological problem as a **symposium carrier**, combining abstract explanation problems and historically executed cases.

Thus:

`METHOD DEBATE AS EXPLICIT SYMPOSIUM OBJECT`
pre-dates Skinner's 1969 article by six years.

Do not infer that every symposium contributor shared one method.

`SYMPOSIUM AGGREGATION`
!= `METHOD CONSENSUS`.

---

## III. Beihefte create a second method carrier beside ordinary issues

The journal's numbered `Beihefte` are especially important for carrier history.

They allow a methodological/historiographical problem to be made into a concentrated thematic publication object outside the normal mixed issue architecture.

### Beiheft 5, 1964–65: `The Historiography of the History of Philosophy`

The official journal page and JSTOR contents include:

- John Passmore, `The Idea of a History of Philosophy`;
- Maurice Mandelbaum, `The History of Ideas, Intellectual History, and the History of Philosophy`;
- W. H. Walsh, `Hegel on the History of Philosophy`;
- Eugene Kamenka, `Marxism and the History of Philosophy`.

Mandelbaum's essay is particularly important for the Lovejoy/Skinner project because it treats `history of ideas`, `intellectual history`, and `history of philosophy` as objects of explicit methodological differentiation several years before Skinner's 1969 intervention.

This means:

`HISTORY-OF-IDEAS FIELD DEFINITION / CRITIQUE`
was already being carried by
`THEMATIC METAHISTORICAL SUPPLEMENT INFRASTRUCTURE`.

Skinner's later critique therefore entered an ecology in which Lovejoyian history of ideas had already been classified, differentiated and criticized by other actors.

---

## IV. 1966 ordinary issues continue to carry high-level metahistorical intervention

Volume 5 (1966) includes Hayden White's `The Burden of History` as an ordinary article, alongside long review essays on intellectual and historiographical works.

This further blocks a simple division in which:

`ORDINARY ARTICLE = EMPIRICAL HISTORY`
while
`SUPPLEMENT / REVIEW = METHOD`.

In *History and Theory*, methodological intervention could occupy several carrier roles.

Carrier form and intellectual role are related but not identical.

`METHOD CONTENT`
!= `ONE FIXED PUBLICATION FORM`.

---

## V. Skinner 1969 is a normal issue article inside this already differentiated system

Direct official/JSTOR issue control for *History and Theory* 8.1 (1969) gives:

1. Quentin Skinner, `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas`, pp.3–53;
2. A. R. Louch, `History as Narrative`, pp.54–70;
3. David P. Jordan, `Gibbon's "Age of Constantine" and the Fall of Rome`, pp.71–96.

Skinner's essay is therefore a long lead **ordinary article**, not itself labeled as a special symposium, review essay or thematic supplement.

That matters historically.

The argument can be radical or rupture-producing while its publication form remains one already available inside the journal's normal method infrastructure.

Compact distinction:

`RADICAL ARGUMENT`
!= `NOVEL CARRIER FORM`.

And:

`LEAD ARTICLE POSITION`
!= `CORPORATE JOURNAL ENDORSEMENT`.

---

## VI. The same 1969 volume also contains a thematic supplement on quantitative history

The official journal page for Beiheft 9 identifies the theme:

`Studies in Quantitative History and the Logic of the Social Sciences`.

The issue includes Jacob M. Price's survey of quantitative work and other contributions on quantitative/social-scientific historical method.

Thus within the same annual volume that carried Skinner's critique of prevailing approaches to the history of ideas, the journal also sustained a separate concentrated carrier for quantitative history and social-science logic.

This is strong execution evidence for methodological aggregation without one-school capture:

`SKINNER CONTEXTUAL / INTENTIONALIST CRITIQUE`
coexists with
`QUANTITATIVE / SOCIAL-SCIENCE METHODOLOGY`
inside one venue/year.

Therefore:

`ONE HIGHLY VISIBLE METHOD ARTICLE`
!= `VENUE METHOD ORTHODOXY`.

## VII. Relation to Mandelbaum -> Skinner

The repo already directly controls a crucial textual edge:

- Skinner 1969 cites Mandelbaum 1965 for field-definition control;
- Skinner acknowledges Maurice Mandelbaum for critical comments on earlier drafts;
- Mandelbaum's 1965 essay appeared in *History and Theory* Beiheft 5 as part of a thematic issue on the historiography of philosophy.

The carrier relation can now be stated more precisely:

`MANDELBAUM 1965 THEMATIC METHOD SUPPLEMENT`
-> visible/cited methodological resource for
`SKINNER 1969 ORDINARY LEAD ARTICLE`.

But preserve:

`EARLIER VENUE PRESENCE + DRAFT COMMENT`
!= `EDITORIAL CAUSE OF SKINNER PUBLICATION`
!= `PROOF THAT H&T CORPORATELY ENGINEERED A RUPTURE`.

The journal supplied a durable method ecology in which these arguments could circulate and be published in different forms.

## VIII. Four-venue implication

Adding *History and Theory* changes the current APSR–JHI–*Political Theory* comparison.

### APSR

A broad disciplinary flagship with political theory distributed across relatively sparse research-article space, formal-theory work, presidential/association interventions, and durable review infrastructure.

### JHI

A field journal in which history-of-ideas method, proof burden, transmission, regional intellectual history and explicit methodological critique can enter as ordinary full-length research articles and reviews.

### History and Theory

A dedicated metahistorical venue that, well before 1969, differentiates method work across ordinary articles, review essays, communications, explicit symposia and thematic Beihefte.

### Political Theory

A new specialist political-philosophy venue that explicitly declares multi-role / school-nonexclusive scope in 1973 and then aggregates controversy through articles, comments, symposia and metatheory classification.

The strongest comparative proposition is therefore:

> **The late-1960s/1970s methodological transformation was carried by a differentiated ecology of publication forms, not by one rupture text moving from nowhere into a newly created field.**

This does not erase substantive methodological conflict.

It changes the infrastructure history of how conflict became recurrent and portable.

## IX. Canonization implication

Later method canons can compress this ecology by extracting Skinner 1969 as a portable foundational text.

That portability is historically real, but it can hide the publication environment in which the essay first appeared:

- earlier H&T methodological articles;
- review-essay infrastructure;
- the 1963 theory symposium;
- Mandelbaum's 1965 Beiheft contribution;
- parallel quantitative/social-science method work in 1969;
- direct and indirect traffic with JHI and political-theory networks.

Therefore:

`FOUNDATIONAL TEXT IN LATER CANON`
!= `SOLE ORIGIN OF CONTEMPORARY METHOD JURISDICTION`.

And:

`TEXTUAL CANONIZATION`
can obscure
`CARRIER ARCHITECTURE`.
