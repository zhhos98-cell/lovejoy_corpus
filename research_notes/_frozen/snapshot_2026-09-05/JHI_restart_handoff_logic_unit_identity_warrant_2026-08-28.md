# 2. Formal logic is real but underdetermines the hardest historical problem

## 1907–1910 formal work

Direct controls show a concrete formal architecture:

`construct/audit class or relation space`
→ `map propositions onto admissible cases`
→ `derive only licensed inferences`
→ `suspend judgment where multiple cases remain`.

This is why some later historical practices can legitimately be called **logician-like** at the structural level.

But the historical problem is more difficult.

Formal logic normally takes its terms/classes as already given. It can tell us what relation obtains between S and P once S and P are fixed. It does **not** tell the historian why heterogeneous historical tokens from different authors/periods should first be put into the same S.

Thus:

> **formal logic is strong on relation/inference control but underdetermines historical aggregation/equivalence.**

This is a central correction.

# 7. Mature `unit-ideas` are themselves heterogeneous logical types

The three core principles of *The Great Chain of Being* are not all the same kind of formal object.

- **plenitude**: modal/existential completeness or realization principle;
- **continuity**: no-gap / neighboring-transition constraint over a series;
- **gradation**: ordering/ranking relation by degrees of perfection, privation, capacity, etc.

Yet Lovejoy treats them all as component/unit-ideas and studies their combinations.

Therefore:

> **unit-idea does not denote a single ontological or formal type such as proposition, concept, or logical atom. It denotes a historiographical functional role.**

# 8. Invariant thickness: how much can change while Lovejoy still says `same`?

This has become one of the most useful analyst variables.

## Thick invariant

- relatively detailed proposition;
- stable corollary/inferential profile;
- less tolerance for semantic/inferential change.

## Thin invariant

- minimal modal, functional or structural core;
- broad tolerance for changing rationale, formulation, context and application.

The thinner the invariant, the easier recurrence is to establish, but the heavier the burden to justify treating discarded differences as non-constitutive.

### Mature stress test: principle of plenitude

Lovejoy's actual *Great Chain* practice treats plenitude as something like:

`genuine possibility / potentiality`
→ pressure toward realization / fullness.

The metaphysical rationale can vary substantially across Plato/Neoplatonism/Spinoza/Leibniz.

This means mature practice is closer to:

> **thin modal core + family of characteristic consequences + variable rationale**

than to an unchanged proposition migrating intact.

This creates a substantive vulnerability: differences in modal strength, compossibility, possible worlds, temporal mode, function and actor use may be more constitutive than Lovejoy permits.

# 10. Persistence and recurrence are different identity-warrant routes

Do not treat `same idea` as having one route of proof.

## Route P — historical persistence / lineage

`earlier state`
→ identifiable transmission/inheritance/continued potency
→ transformation/recombination
→ later state.

Large transformation may be tolerated if historical persistence and enough structural continuity are present.

## Route R — recurrence without lineage

Historically independent cases may still be grouped as the same analytical type if sufficient conceptual/functional/mechanistic invariance is demonstrated.

1906 is a key example: independence among North American, Oceanic and African evidence weakens a common-borrowing hypothesis but **strengthens independent recurrence/type robustness**.

This yields a crucial metarule:

> **the evidentiary valence of a fact depends on the relation being tested.**

The same `independence` is:

- negative/braking evidence for common transmission;
- positive evidence for independent recurrence of a type.

# 16. 1941 is the strongest explicit epistemic partition

Lovejoy distinguishes three relations:

1. logical;
2. psychological;
3. historical, especially genetic.

Logical and psychological relations are initially analytical/hypothetical constructions in the historian's own mind. They must then be returned to historical data to test whether they were actually operative.

Thus:

`analyst-recognized logical relation`
≠ `actor-recognized implication`
≠ `historically operative relation`
≠ `genetic connection`.

This is one of the strongest mature controls for the entire project.

Additionally, 1941 directly shows positive non-intellectual causal mechanisms:

- prior affective taste predisposes uptake of a justificatory aesthetic philosophy;
- ambitions/passions can produce ideological rationalizations;
- those rationalizations can then mediate political deeds/mass action;
- prior intellectual conditioning shapes receptivity.

Therefore older hypotheses that Lovejoy's actual practice excludes affective/social causal engines were withdrawn.

# 19. 1944 Spitzer: mature method explicitly downgraded to empirical working hypotheses

Lovejoy's reply to Spitzer is an important self-correction/control.

1. distinguishable ideas can recur in different authors/periods;

# 20. The strongest current interpretation of `logician-like`

Do not say merely that Lovejoy `liked analysis`.

The concrete structural homology is:

> **construct/discriminate a state or class space → type the relevant relation → restrict what inferences are licensed → suspend or re-grade claims when several possibilities remain.**

In historical inquiry, however, an extra prior gate exists:

> **what historical tokens belong in the same class/unit in the first place?**

That prior aggregation problem is where formal logic is least sufficient and where unit-idea historiography is most contestable.

This is the cleanest current answer to the original `logicalian method vs actually discussing logic` distinction.

# 21. The current deepest metarule

A good compact formulation is now:

> **Evidentiary force is relation-indexed.**

Evidence is not globally strong/weak or positive/negative. Its valence depends on the exact proposition/relation being tested.

Examples:

- independence weakens common transmission but strengthens independent recurrence;
- reading/contact supports an edge but not source exclusivity or causal weight;
- lexical sameness supports neither doctrinal identity nor genealogy without further work;
- local passage certainty does not entail corpus-wide lexical doctrine;
- logical implication does not entail historical operation;
- same conclusion does not entail same motive;
- non-textual practice can evidence a transformation without proving practice is the ultimate causal engine.

This metarule has a strong structural homology with Lovejoy's formal logical work, but the genealogy of the habit remains open.

# 22. Current best formulation of the unit-idea problem

Avoid the crude question:

`Did Lovejoy ignore context by treating ideas as timeless atoms?`

A more exact formulation is:

> **At what scale does Lovejoy isolate an invariant, how thick is that invariant, what changes are treated as accidental rather than constitutive, and what warrants allow the historian to identify that invariant across contexts?**

The problem then divides into:

1. target-state accuracy;
2. object-type choice;
3. invariant selection;
4. invariant thickness;
5. persistence vs recurrence route;
6. relation typing;
7. mediation/evidence;
8. historical function/configuration;
9. actor ownership vs analyst ownership.

The most Skinner-facing pressure point is not whether context changes. Lovejoy knows it does.

It is:

> **which contextual changes the historian may strip away while preserving a claim of sameness, and what authorizes that stripping.**
