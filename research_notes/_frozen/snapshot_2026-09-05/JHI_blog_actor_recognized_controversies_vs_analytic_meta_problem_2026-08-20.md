# JHI Blog — actor-recognized controversies vs analytic meta-problem

Date: 2026-08-20
Status: pre-submission conceptual correction

## Core correction

Do **not** describe all of Garbe, Rhys Davids, Warren, Senart, Jacobi, Oldenberg and Lovejoy as participants in one undifferentiated methodological controversy over `what comparison could prove`.

The primary sources instead support **two overlapping actor-recognized controversies / problem fields**, plus a higher-order methodological pattern that is our reconstruction.

## A. Actor-recognized controversy 1: interpretation of the twelve nidānas / paṭicca-samuppāda

This is explicit in Senart 1896.

At the opening of `À propos de la théorie bouddhique des douze nidānas`, Senart says the theory has exercised Western interpreters and that recent exegetes have approached it from different points of view. He explicitly names:

- Warren, who sought to understand it by giving the logical link among the members a more flexible / indeterminate scope;
- Waddell, who looked to iconography;
- Jacobi, who most recently sought to interpret it comparatively through Sāṃkhya.

Thus Senart himself retrospectively places Warren and Jacobi inside the **same exegetical problem-field**, while emphasizing that their approaches differ.

Lovejoy 1898 then explicitly constructs the meaning of `upādāna` as a debated technical problem. He says its meaning `has been much discussed`, places Warren's identity reading alongside other readings, and then calls Senart's interpretation a third / recent view. Lovejoy therefore knowingly treats Warren and Senart as alternative contributors to the same technical dispute over upādāna and the causation formula.

Important asymmetry: Warren 1893 could not have regarded himself as responding to Senart 1896. The actor-recognition is retrospective (Senart includes Warren; Lovejoy includes Warren and Senart), not fully reciprocal.

## B. Actor-recognized controversy 2: historical relation of Buddhism to Sāṃkhya-Yoga

This is even more explicit.

Jacobi 1898 opens by saying that in 1896 he had tried to prove that Buddhist philosophy arose from Sāṃkhya-Yoga and that his argument had provoked the `Widerspruch` (opposition/objection) of Oldenberg and Senart. He says he will examine those objections and then develop his own interpretation of the nidāna chain.

Oldenberg's third-edition excurs likewise explicitly frames a `Frage nach dem Verhältniss des Buddhismus zum Sānkhyasystem` and names Garbe, Jacobi, Senart and Dahlmann among the recent scholars arguing for dependence. He states that their work has made him soften his earlier skepticism but that he cannot accept several of their stronger claims. Oldenberg then directly compares Jacobi's sequences and invokes Senart's objections.

Therefore `Garbe / Jacobi / Senart / Oldenberg / Dahlmann` are not merely a retrospectively assembled bibliography. The participants themselves describe a live question and respond to one another.

Rhys Davids is adjacent to this controversy and directly used in Lovejoy 004 as a chronological/textual control, but should not automatically be cast as a polemical participant in the same exchange without a similarly explicit self-positioning source.

## C. The overlap between the two controversies is historical, not invented by us

The two controversies overlap because the nidāna chain itself became evidence in the Sāṃkhya/Buddhism relation problem.

Jacobi's 1898 title literally joins the two objects: `Über das Verhältnis der buddhistischen Philosophie zu Sāṃkhya-Yoga und die Bedeutung der Nidānas`.

His opening says he will answer Oldenberg/Senart's objections to his Sāṃkhya-origin thesis and then explain the individual nidānas. His later argument uses the nidāna sequence as a principal sign of Sāṃkhya influence.

Oldenberg likewise treats the nidāna comparisons as evidence inside the larger dependence question. Senart's 1896 essay treats Warren and Jacobi as different recent approaches to the same obscure nidāna theory and then uses the formula's compositional history to discuss Sāṃkhya-derived materials.

Thus there is a **real actor-level junction** between:

1. `What do the nidānas mean / how is the chain structured?`
2. `What does the chain show about Buddhism's relation to Sāṃkhya?`

Notebook 004 sits precisely at that junction.

## D. What remains OUR analytic meta-problem

The actors do **not** appear to describe the entire exchange as a shared controversy over:

- `levels of historical inference`;
- `relation-type discrimination`;
- `historical warrant`;
- `what comparison could legitimately prove`;
- `provenance versus configuration`.

Those are our second-order reconstructions.

They are supported because the actor-recognized disputes repeatedly require distinctions among identity, causal relation, resemblance, borrowing, system, textual meaning, composition and historical dependence. But we must formulate this as:

> **The piece argues that these overlapping substantive controversies repeatedly turned on a further problem: what different kinds of evidence were entitled to establish.**

Not:

> **They were all participating in one methodological controversy over what comparison could prove.**

## E. Consequence for pitch

The strongest pitch should lead with actor-recognized controversy, not our methodological abstraction.

Preferred architecture:

1. Senart himself grouped Warren and Jacobi among recent interpreters of the twelve nidānas.
2. Jacobi explicitly described Oldenberg and Senart as objectors to his Sāṃkhya/Buddhism thesis.
3. Lovejoy 1898 placed Warren and Senart side by side as competing interpretations of upādāna.
4. **Then** state our intervention: reading these exchanges through Lovejoy's notebook reveals how questions of meaning, composition and historical dependence repeatedly became questions about the strength and type of evidence.

Possible pitch sentence:

> `The notebook places Lovejoy at the intersection of two explicitly connected late-nineteenth-century debates: how the Buddhist chain of twelve nidānas should be understood, and what that chain could show about Buddhism's relation to Sāṃkhya. Senart himself grouped Warren and Jacobi among recent interpreters of the nidānas; Jacobi later described Oldenberg and Senart as objectors to his Sāṃkhya thesis; Lovejoy's 1898 article then set Warren and Senart side by side as competing readings of upādāna. I use this documented exchange to ask a second-order question: how did scholars decide what a resemblance, a textual identification, or a compositional argument was actually entitled to establish?`

This wording keeps the actor-level controversy and our analytic claim distinct.

## Bottom line

### Secure actor-level claim

**Yes:** there was an explicitly recognized controversy/network, but it is best understood as **two overlapping substantive debates**, not one homogeneous methodological debate.

### Secure Lovejoy claim

**Yes:** Lovejoy knowingly enters the technical upādāna/nidāna debate by placing Warren and Senart among competing interpretations. His notebook also engages the broader Sāṃkhya/Buddhism relation problem.

### Our analytic claim

**Our reconstruction:** the overlap between these debates can be read as exposing a recurring problem of historical inference — what kind of relation a given piece of evidence establishes and how far a claim may be upgraded.
