## 2. p.104 — Grant Allen 1897 materially changes the ownership ceiling

p.104 is W3 for two key statements:

- `the manner of the H.S. is in turn modified into sacr. of dom. an., dressed as a man & so on`;
- domestic-animal sacrifice `comes not fr. wild an. sac. directly, but by modification of H.S.`.

Round 26 treated the second statement as a strong Lovejoy-local path exclusion/selection because human->animal substitution was field-known but the exact `not wild->domestic directly; instead via human sacrifice` route had not been located.

That ceiling is now too high.

### A. Grant Allen explicitly supplies the domestic-animal <- human-victim route

Grant Allen, *The Evolution of the Idea of God* (1897), repeatedly argues that sacred domestic animals were accepted as substitutes for earlier human/divine-human victims.

Particularly close propositions include:

- the habit of eating sheep, goats and oxen arose `mainly out of the substitution of such a divine animal-victim for the divine human-victim of earlier usage`;
- domestic animals were `early accepted as substitutes for the human victim`;
- the sacred domestic beast functions as a regular substitute for a man-god.

This is not merely the generic proposition `animal can replace human`. It identifies **domestic sacrificial animals specifically** as deriving from human-victim substitution.

### B. Allen explicitly separates wild-animal killing from sacred domestic-animal killing

Allen also states that among early peoples the killing/eating of wild animals is not morally or ritually problematic in the same way, whereas killing domestic herds is restricted and sacrificially marked.

Thus the contrast required by p.104 is already present:

`WILD ANIMALS -> ORDINARY HUNT/KILL FIELD`

versus

`DOMESTIC SACRIFICIAL ANIMALS -> SACRED SUBSTITUTE FOR HUMAN VICTIM`.

The notebook's `not fr. wild an. sac. directly` therefore closely matches an upstream historical distinction rather than inventing it.

### C. Allen/Tylor also supply the anthropomorphic treatment of the animal substitute

Allen gives intermediate cases in which the animal substitute is deliberately humanized:

- a goat `dressed up as a girl` offered to Artemis Munychia;
- the Tenedos calf replacing a newborn child is shod in buskins while the mother cow is treated as a woman in childbirth.

Allen himself draws the inference that these are attempts to substitute a sacred animal for a genuine human victim.

Tylor, *Primitive Culture*, vol. II, p.404, supplies the classic Tenedos example: newborn calf substituted for newborn child, shod with buskins, mother cow tended as if a human mother.

This is exceptionally close to the notebook wording `dom. an., dressed as a man & so on`.

### D. Revised p.104 ownership

The previously surviving A2 object largely disappears.

The source field now supplies all three crucial pieces:
