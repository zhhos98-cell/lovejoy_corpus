# Howison / Berkeley — formal logic training ecology before Lovejoy

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **DIRECT CONTEMPORARY CURRICULAR CONTROL / TEACHER-FIELD CONTROL / LOVEJOY ENROLLMENT NOT YET PROVED**

## Core result

The newly opened genealogy of Lovejoy's `logical analysis` should not begin only at Harvard/Royce or with Lovejoy's 1907 formal-logical publications. His undergraduate Berkeley environment already contained formal logic as an explicit part of philosophical propedeutic under George Holmes Howison.

A contemporaneous 1890–91 survey of American university philosophy curricula in *The Monist* reproduces the University of California programme. Its first course is:

> `PROPÆDEUTIC TO PHILOSOPHY. Empirical psychology, including formal logic, deductive and inductive.`

The same programme states that Courses 1, 2, and 3 are **permanent and repeated from year to year in substantially the same form**. Lovejoy entered Berkeley in 1891 and remained through graduation in 1895. This makes formal logic part of the directly documented curricular ecology of his undergraduate philosophical formation.

However, the present evidence does **not** prove Lovejoy's enrollment in that exact course. The correct relation is:

`formal logic built into Berkeley philosophical propedeutic immediately before and during Lovejoy's undergraduate period`

+ `Lovejoy documented as coming under Howison's philosophical influence`

≠ `Lovejoy demonstrably took Course 1`.

The enrollment edge remains open.

## I. 1890–91 University of California philosophical scheme

The *Monist* survey `Philosophy in American Colleges and Universities` reports the University of California curriculum in unusually useful detail.

The permanent introductory sequence is:

1. `Propædeutic to Philosophy`: empirical psychology, including formal logic, deductive and inductive;
2. `Introduction to Philosophy`: outline history of European philosophy;
3. `Elementary Ethics, including Civil Polity`: history and criticism of ethical and political theories, including conflicts such as perfectionism/hedonism and freedom/necessity.

The report then states explicitly that Courses 1–3 are permanent and recur year to year in substantially the same form. The changing advanced courses for 1890–91 include `Philosophy from Kant to Hegel`; the graduate course treats Kant's *Dialektik* and *Methodenlehre* and Hegel's *Lesser Logic*.

This matters because the Berkeley curriculum did not separate `logic` from the philosophical route later associated with Howison's idealism. Formal logic, empirical psychology, history of philosophy, Kant/Hegel, ethics and civil polity occupied one connected training environment.

Primary control:
- `The Monist`, vol. 1 (1890–91), `Philosophy in American Colleges and Universities`, University of California section; public-domain transcription at Project Gutenberg, ebook 56634.

## II. Howison was himself a logic teacher, not only an idealist metaphysician

The biographical notice attached to Howison's 1904 International Congress paper `Philosophy: Its Fundamental Conceptions and Its Methods` records earlier appointments as:

- Professor of Logic and the Philosophy of Science, Massachusetts Institute of Technology, 1871–79;
- Lecturer on Logic and Speculative Philosophy, University of Michigan, 1883–84;
- later Mills Professor at the University of California.

This is useful attributional control. The teacher under whom Wilson says Lovejoy's central interest shifted from religion to philosophy had substantial prior professional identification with logic and philosophy of science.

Again, professional biography does not establish pedagogical uptake by Lovejoy. It does remove the temptation to treat Howison as relevant only to idealist metaphysics while looking elsewhere for every logical operator in Lovejoy.

Primary/public control:
- International Congress of Arts and Science, 1904, Howison biographical notice and paper `Philosophy: Its Fundamental Conceptions and Its Methods`; public-domain Project Gutenberg edition, ebook 38267.

## III. Independent Berkeley historical control

A later institutional history of the Berkeley psychology department describes Howison's first psychology course in fall 1888, `Propædeutic to Philosophy`, as consisting of empirical psychology and formal logic, both inductive and deductive.

This independently matches the 1890–91 contemporaneous curriculum description. Use the *Monist* programme as the stronger contemporaneous curricular witness; use the Berkeley history as a corroborating institutional reconstruction.

Control:
- `History of the University of California Psychology Department at Berkeley`, section `1888–1898: The Beginning`, UC Berkeley digital collections.

## IV. Revised formation model

The live formation sequence now has at least two directly evidenced logic ecologies before Lovejoy's own formal-logic publications:

### Berkeley / Howison

`formal logic, deductive + inductive`
+ empirical psychology
+ history of philosophy
+ Kant/Hegel
+ ethics/civil polity

inside one philosophical propedeutic environment.

### Harvard / Royce–James period

Royce's papers preserve logic teaching, proposition/syllogism exercises, a 1896 Philosophy 12 examination, logic collections from 1893–98, and later increasingly formal work. Lovejoy studied at Harvard in this period, but exact enrollment in a Royce logic course/seminary remains unresolved.

The stronger historical question is therefore no longer `Where did Lovejoy first encounter logic?` in the abstract. Formal logic was institutionally available already at Berkeley. The specific transmission problem becomes:

> **Which parts of the Berkeley and Harvard logical repertoires did Lovejoy actually take up, and which later forms of analysis were acquired or reformulated through professional controversy after 1900?**

This control must not be used to derive notebook 004 or 005 from Howison's logic teaching.

What it changes is the prior probability of the interpretation. When 004 distinguishes temporal antecedence from logical subdivision, or separates semantic, causal, classificatory and genealogical questions, we can no longer frame philosophy/logic only as a later retrospective analogue. Lovejoy entered his Orientalist and comparative-religion work after years inside philosophical institutions in which logic was an explicit curricular object.

The evidence ceiling remains:

`documented philosophical/logic ecology`

but

`curricular availability + teacher relation`

The project's earlier Harvard/Paris reconstruction distributed Lovejoy's formation among Sanskrit/Pāli philology, comparative religion, psychology/religion and source-specialist jurisdictions. The logical-analysis branch now adds another distributed jurisdiction rather than replacing those.

Current best model:

`Berkeley philosophical propedeutic incl. formal logic`
+ `Harvard philosophy / Royce–James + active logic ecology`

## Evidence ceiling

### Secure

- the 1890–91 University of California philosophy scheme explicitly included formal logic, deductive and inductive, inside `Propædeutic to Philosophy`;
- the same contemporary report says the first three courses were permanent and repeated year to year in substantially the same form;
- Lovejoy attended Berkeley from 1891 to 1895 and was philosophically influenced by Howison (biographical control already canonical in the repository);
- Howison had previously held professional posts explicitly in logic/philosophy of science and logic/speculative philosophy;
- a later Berkeley institutional history independently describes the 1888 propedeutic in the same empirical-psychology + formal-logic terms.

### Open

- Lovejoy's exact Berkeley course enrollments;
- whether he took `Propædeutic to Philosophy` specifically;
- exact textbooks/exercises encountered by Lovejoy in logic;
- whether any Berkeley student notebooks, examinations or Howison class records preserve Lovejoy's work;
- proposition-level transmission from Berkeley logic into 004/005.

### Do not claim

- Lovejoy demonstrably studied formal logic under Howison without enrollment evidence;
- Howison caused Lovejoy's later history-of-ideas method;
- Berkeley formal logic explains the Buddhist notebooks by itself;
- the 1890–91 curriculum is sufficient evidence for Lovejoy's exact 1891–95 personal schedule;
- Howison's idealism and logical teaching constituted one stable method later inherited intact by Lovejoy.
