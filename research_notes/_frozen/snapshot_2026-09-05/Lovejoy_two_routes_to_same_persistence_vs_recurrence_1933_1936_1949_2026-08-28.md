# Lovejoy — two routes to `same`: persistence through transformation vs recurrence without lineage

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **GATE-A MAJOR REFRAME / DIRECT + CONTROLLED SECONDARY PRIMARY TRANSCRIPTION**

## Core result

The branch should no longer treat every Lovejoyian `same idea` judgment as one kind of identity claim.

At least two fundamentally different historical routes can license sameness:

1. **persistence through a continuous historical lineage**, where substantial conceptual transformation can be tolerated because later states are historically generated from earlier ones;
2. **recurrence without demonstrated lineage**, where the historian must rely more heavily on conceptual/content invariance because no transmission chain supplies diachronic continuity.

Lovejoy's mature 1949 wording explicitly pairs `persistence or recurrence` of components. The distinction becomes much more intelligible when read against the 1933 Monboddo/Rousseau case and the 1936 temporalization of the Chain.

Project shorthand:

`PERSISTENCE ROUTE` ≠ `RECURRENCE ROUTE`.

This is likely one of the keys to why Lovejoy sometimes tolerates very large changes while still saying `the same idea`, yet in other cases demands close structural identity.

---

## I. Persistence route — historical continuity can carry identity through conceptual change

The temporalization of the Great Chain is the strongest mature case.

The static Chain changes into a temporal process:

- complete inventory → unfolding program;
- simultaneous realization → realization distributed across cosmic history;
- descending emanation → ascending development;
- permanent fullness → fullness as future goal;
- in Schelling, even God can enter Becoming.

Lovejoy nevertheless says the inversion of the Scale does not alter its `essential character` and identifies surviving elements such as generativeness, diversity-production and maximal fullness.

The warrant here is not purely synchronic resemblance.

It includes a visible **historical transformation path**:

`earlier configuration`
→ reinterpretation / temporalization
→ transformed configuration.

The chain of historical development can therefore help sustain identity even when conceptual form changes sharply.

---

## II. Recurrence route — content can be same while lineage remains open

`Monboddo and Rousseau` supplies the opposite structure.

Lovejoy sees a striking cluster of similar/same thoughts in two writers but explicitly leaves open whether the relation is:

- spontaneous/independent recurrence;
- direct influence;
- common ancient sources;
- common contemporary evidentiary environment.

Here:

`content identity / strong similarity`
≠ `historical persistence / lineage`.

Because the genetic connection is uncertain, sameness must be carried by some sufficiently stable conceptual/propositional structure rather than by a documented chain of descent.

Thus recurrence is a **type identity** problem, not a continuity-of-object problem.

---

## III. 1949 actor-level control: `persistence or recurrence`

In `Historiography and Evaluation: A Disclaimer` (JHI 10.1, 1949, 141–142), Lovejoy says that a later writer/group/sect selects components of an earlier complex, combines them with materials from other sources, and produces divergent ramification through historical processes.

His key formulation, controlled through Mouton's exact transcription of p.142, is that:

- the historian can discern the **persistence or recurrence** of one or another component;
- the original complex as a whole seldom persists as initially constituted.

This wording should be treated as significant, not rhetorical variation.

### `Persistence`

Likely relation:

`token/state t1`
→ historically connected transformation
→ `token/state t2`.

Identity may be diachronic and tolerant of internal change.

### `Recurrence`

Likely relation:

`token x at t1`
and
`token y at t2`

instantiate sufficiently similar conceptual content, possibly without any transmission edge.

Identity is type-level and does not require continuous descent.

Lovejoy does not formalize the distinction this way, but his case law strongly supports it.

---

## IV. Different routes imply different warrant burdens

### Persistence route needs

- historical carrier/lineage;
- evidence of transformation/reinterpretation;
- a reason for treating changed states as phases of one historical career;
- some retained core or continuity of problem/function/structure.

It can tolerate **thicker conceptual change** because genealogy itself contributes to continuity.

### Recurrence route needs

- sufficiently diagnostic conceptual identity/similarity;
- control of lexical ambiguity;
- scale specification;
- protection against accidental/generic resemblance;
- no assumption of influence merely from recurrence.

It may require a **thicker synchronic invariant** because genealogy is absent.

This yields a strong new hypothesis:

> **conceptual invariance and historical continuity can partially substitute for one another in Lovejoy's practical identity judgments.**

Not completely: neither route removes the need for target identification.

---

## V. A third possibility: transformed recurrence

Some cases may combine both routes imperfectly.

A historically transmitted idea may be so radically transformed that its conceptual invariant becomes very thin.

Conversely, an independently recurring idea may later enter an actual transmission chain.

Thus future coding should not force binary classification when the history contains multiple episodes.

Recommended fields:

- `identity_route = persistence | recurrence | mixed | uncertain`;
- `lineage_confidence`;
- `invariant_type`;
- `invariant_thickness`;
- `transformation_degree`;
- `historical_function_change`.

---

## VI. Relation to the genealogy vector

The genealogy vector developed elsewhere tracks:

- target identification;
- recipient prior state;
- edge existence;
- alternatives;
- causal delta/weight;
- propagation;
- confidence.

The present distinction sits **before** or beside that vector.

A recurrence claim does not need a positive direct edge.

A persistence claim usually presupposes some continuing historical path, though the path can involve many carriers and transformations.

Therefore:

`same idea?`
should not automatically trigger
`who influenced whom?`.

First ask whether Lovejoy is asserting:

- type recurrence;
- diachronic persistence;
- or both.

---

## VII. Why this matters for Skinner

A strong contextualist critique hits the two routes differently.

### Against recurrence

The issue is whether abstract conceptual content can be sufficiently identical across different speech acts/contexts to count as one historical unit.

### Against persistence

The issue is whether continuous historical descent is enough to license one identity despite changes in meaning, use, inferential role and practical function.

Thus `same idea across time` hides two distinct controversies:

1. **type identity across separate contexts**;
2. **diachronic identity through transformation**.

Skinnerian objections need not have equal force against both.

---

## VIII. Consequence for the temporalization case

Lovejoy's famous judgment that the inverted/temporalized Scale retains its `essential character` becomes less mysterious if treated as a persistence claim.

He is not simply comparing two isolated conceptual structures and declaring them identical.

He is narrating a historical process in which the older complex is:

- reinterpreted;
- inverted;
- temporalized;
- redeployed.

Historical derivation provides part of the identity warrant.

This does not settle whether Lovejoy thins the invariant too aggressively, but it changes the criticism:

> **the question is how much transformation a historically continuous idea can survive, not whether every occurrence must be qualitatively identical.**

---

## IX. Consequence for `unit-idea` ontology

`Unit-idea` cannot mean simply:

> an unchanging conceptual atom numerically identical wherever it appears.

Lovejoy's practice instead permits at least:

- persistent components that change through a historical career;
- recurrent components that appear independently;
- compounds whose components persist/return while the whole changes;
- components whose relations, functions and accompaniments vary.

The real methodological burden is therefore an **identity-accounting system**, not atomism.

## X. Immediate test queue

1. Recode major Great Chain cases as `persistence` vs `recurrence` rather than bare `same`.
2. Use 1915 `recur again and again` carefully: `recur` may already point primarily toward type recurrence, not continuous lineage.
3. Check whether 1936 uses `persist`, `recur`, `survive`, `revive`, `revival`, `transformation` with distinguishable meanings.
4. Test the 1902 otherworldliness sequence: continuous tradition, independent recurrence, or mixed?
5. Test 1906 primitive energetics: deliberately cross-cultural recurrence without genealogy — likely a pure recurrence/type case.
6. Compare criteria thickness: does persistence systematically tolerate thinner invariants than recurrence?

## Current shorthand

> **Lovejoy has at least two routes to `same`: persistence through historical transformation and recurrence of sufficiently invariant content without demonstrated lineage. The first can borrow identity warrant from historical continuity; the second must lean more heavily on conceptual diagnosticity. `Same idea` should never again be coded without specifying which route is doing the work.**
