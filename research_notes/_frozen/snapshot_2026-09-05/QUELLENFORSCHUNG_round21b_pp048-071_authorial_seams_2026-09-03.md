## 1. pp.48–49 / ms pp.135–137 — Senart source block, then an explicit response boundary

P.48 remains inside the Senart problem packet: `upādāna` as a reduction to `upādānaskandhas`, recurrence of the skandhas, and the resulting duplication/compositeness problem. The recurrence audit should not be treated as independently Lovejoy-generated merely because he expands it in notebook form.

Senart 1896 pp.284–285 directly supplies the core propositions:

- `upādāna` as reduction for `upādāna skandhas` / `pañca upādānaskandhas`;
- the skandha series therefore occurring twice;
- the formula as a composite series;
- ready-made scholastic categories fused in an `ordre plus ou moins accidentel`.

P.49 first translates/digests this conclusion and then marks a clean authorial seam:

`On Senart's account of the matter there is simply this to be said:`

From there Lovejoy grants several premises — inherited terms, heterogeneous materials, non-strict temporality, repetitions — but rejects Senart's stronger inference:

`it is entirely false` that the formula is therefore illogical/unintelligible or that its terms were merely put together in an order `more or less accidental`.

The following image-secure claim remains a strong local Lovejoy synthesis:

`There is a discoverable logical system wh. governs the arrangement, and the repetitions are intentional ...`

Calibration:

- p.48 Senart duplication/compositeness problem: S3 / T3 / A0–A1;
- p.49 pre-boundary Senart digest: S3 / T3 / A0;
- `On Senart's account...` onward: A2;
- `entirely false` / `discoverable logical system`: W3, strong A2.

This is one of the strongest surviving examples where stricter Quellenforschung **strengthens** rather than weakens the authorial locus by stripping away the surrounding source-owned language.
