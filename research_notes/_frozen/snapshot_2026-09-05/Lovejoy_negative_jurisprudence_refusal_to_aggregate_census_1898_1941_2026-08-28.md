# Lovejoy — negative jurisprudence / refusal-to-aggregate census, 1898–1941

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **RESTART-SAFE CHECKPOINT / NEGATIVE CASE CENSUS / M1–M6 AGGREGATION-MODE CONTROL**

## Purpose

The identity branch has accumulated many positive recognition cases (`same`, `recurs`, `disguised`, `fundamental identity`). To avoid reconstructing Lovejoy as structurally biased toward sameness, this note isolates cases in which he **refuses aggregation, splits a category, downgrades a scale, breaks identity, or suspends a stronger relation claim**.

The main result is that Lovejoy's negative judgments are not one operation. They fall into at least four distinct refusal types:

1. **SPLIT** — one label/class is decomposed into nonidentical items;
2. **DOWNGRADE** — a stronger scale/category is rejected while a weaker grouping survives;
3. **BREAK** — a constitutive invariant is lost, so identity itself fails;
4. **SUSPEND** — content identity or similarity may stand, but genealogy/causation/historical operation is not promoted.

A fifth refusal type remains underdocumented:

5. **REPRESENTATIVENESS DEMOTION** — explicit abandonment/downgrading of a population-level type because the counter-distribution is too large.

No secure direct Lovejoy case of type 5 has yet emerged from the present census. That bounded negative is itself important for the 1906 `Primitive Energetics` problem.

# I. 1898 notebook 004 — classificatory SPLIT despite semantic proximity

Aggregation mode: **M1 strict classificatory identity**.

Lovejoy checks a proposed identity among Buddhist technical categories against the canonical classificatory apparatus and refuses it when the system itself keeps the terms/items distinct.

Judgment form:

`adjacency / semantic or causal proximity`
↛ `same classificatory unit`.

Refusal type: **SPLIT**.

Negative threshold: an explicit classificatory distinction at the relevant system scale.

This is the earliest clear case showing that Lovejoy can prefer nonidentity even where a looser thematic analyst could plausibly group the items.

---

# II. 1906 Kant — historical rubric DOWNGRADE after constituent audit

Primary: `Kant's Antithesis of Dogmatism and Criticism`, *Mind* 15.58 (1906), 191–214.

Aggregation mode: inherited historical binary / macro-rubric, closest to **M4 configurational/historical classification** rather than a unit equivalence.

Lovejoy calls the received `dogmatism / criticism` antithesis seriously misleading when used as a historical contrast between Kant and his predecessors. The umbrella rubric silently bundles several different propositions about predecessor method, Kantian novelty, criteria of truth, and historical rupture.

He reopens those propositions locally and concludes that the distinction, **insofar as it is intended to correspond to the historical contrast**, falls to the ground.

Refusal type: **DOWNGRADE / scope cancellation**.

Important nuance:

- technical or local distinctions can survive;
- the whole-period historical discontinuity does not.

Thus:

`local difference or actor rubric`
↛ `macro historical rupture`.

This is a direct same-year counterweight to 1906 `Primitive Philosophy`: Lovejoy can dismantle an over-aggregated historical category as well as construct one.

---

# III. 1908 `Thirteen Pragmatisms` — shared name SPLIT into logically independent contentions

Primary: `The Thirteen Pragmatisms`, JPP&SM 5 (1908), parts I–II.

Aggregation mode: shared historical label incorrectly functioning like **M1** identity/system unity.

Lovejoy identifies a baker's dozen of contentions under `pragmatism` and insists that they are logically independent in the relevant sense. Acceptance or refutation of one does not transfer automatically to the rest.

Refusal type: **SPLIT + evidentiary insulation**.

Negative rule:

`same historical name`
↛ `same proposition`
↛ `logical entailment among propositions`
↛ `shared argumentative fate`.

This is not a denial that a pragmatist movement can exist historically. It is a denial that movement membership performs the work of propositional identity or logical unity.

---

# IV. 1908 Smith controversy — SUSPEND genealogy when load-bearing local relations fail

Primary: `The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus`, *The Monist* 18.4 (1908), 597–609.

Aggregation/relation mode: **M6 genealogy/persistence**, with Gate B historical-descent claims.

Lovejoy grants several broad contextual probabilities (syncretic tendencies, mixed Jewish/Greek/Persian environments) while refusing the stronger pre-Christian genealogy where chronology, source interpretation, sectarian identity, or semantic priority is unsecured.

Examples preserved in the canonical dossier include:

- linguistic possibility ≠ same sect ≠ historical continuity;
- lexical range ≠ historical priority;
- broad syncretic probability ≠ a specific descent relation;
- potentially diagnostic evidence with conjectural chronology remains suspended.

Refusal type: **SUSPEND**.

This is not primarily a failure of conceptual identity. It is a refusal to promote weaker evidence into a stronger historical relation.

---

# V. 1909 `Pragmatism and Realism` — arbitrary essentialization refused, configuration retained

Primary: `Pragmatism and Realism` (1909), controlled in the 1908–09 consolidated dossier.

Aggregation mode: movement/complex, combining **M3/M4** features.

Lovejoy denies that a named doctrine is one `logical brick` with a single stable essence. Selecting one motive as the essential pragmatist idea can manufacture affinities/incompatibilities at will.

But he does not dissolve the historical complex. He requires more complete enumeration of motives, compatibility testing, and warranted recombination; novelty may lie in the `new compound in intellectual chemistry`.

Refusal type: **DOWNGRADE one-essence identity → retain configuration-level unity**.

This is a particularly useful negative case because it shows that decomposition does not entail anti-holism. The rejected proposition is the **wrong kind of unity**, not unity as such.

---

# VI. 1923 Rousseau — lexical sameness SPLIT into different doctrinal targets

Primary: `The Supposed Primitivism of Rousseau's "Discourse on Inequality"`, *Modern Philology* 21.2 (1923), 165–186.

Aggregation mode: lexical grouping attempting to do **M1 doctrinal identity** work.

Lovejoy distinguishes at least chronological, juristic, and cultural senses of `state of nature` and stresses that the senses need not have the same denotation.

Refusal type: **SPLIT**.

Negative rule:

`same recurrent phrase`
↛ `same doctrine / same denotation`.

A second refusal follows at actor-position scale:

`doctrine/stage occurs in Rousseau's narrative`
↛ `Rousseau idealizes or endorses it`.

Thus lexical identity and target presence/valuation must both be controlled before the macro label `primitivism` is applied.

---

# VII. 1924 Romanticisms — essence claim rejected, overlap family retained

Primary: `On the Discrimination of Romanticisms`, *PMLA* 39.2 (1924), 229–253.

Aggregation mode: **M3 overlap family** versus a stronger M1-like macro essence claim.

Lovejoy denies that the common label `Romanticism` proves one historical entity identical in essentials across German, English, French, etc. episodes. Pairwise overlap can vary: one pair can share one element, another pair a different one.

Refusal type: **DOWNGRADE**.

Rejected:

`one name`
→ `one essence / one identical macro-complex`.

Retained:

`plural Romanticisms linked by variable overlaps and historical associations`.

This is the cleanest control showing why absence of a universal invariant is not by itself failure of a family category.

---

# VIII. 1933 Monboddo / Rousseau — content comparison allowed, genealogy SUSPENDED

Primary: `Monboddo and Rousseau`, *Modern Philology* 30.3 (1933), 275–296; current exact wording status mediated/secondary-controlled pending original image.

Aggregation mode: content identity/comparison + **M6 genealogy**.

Lovejoy identifies a substantial cluster of similar/connected theses and then explicitly leaves the causal explanation open among:

- independent/spontaneous occurrence;
- direct influence;
- common ancient sources;
- common contemporary evidentiary environment.

Refusal type: **SUSPEND genetic upgrade**.

Rule:

`same / strongly similar thought-content`
↛ `direct influence`
↛ `exclusive source`
↛ `causal direction`.

This is crucial negative jurisprudence because it demonstrates that `same idea` is not automatically genealogical in Lovejoy's own practice.

# IX. 1936 plenitude / Whitehead — identity BREAK by reversal of the operator

Primary: *The Great Chain of Being*, Lecture XI; prior direct/controlled audit in the repository.

Aggregation mode: **M1 principle identity + M6 long-range persistence/recurrence**.

Lovejoy treats Whitehead's move from `Infinite Fecundity` toward a `principle of limitation` as contrast/reversal rather than another disguised plenitude instance.

Refusal type: **BREAK**.

Negative threshold:

`pressure toward maximal realization/fullness`
→ `primordial selection/limitation`

reverses the constitutive relation.

This is one of the strongest mature cases where resemblance, historical adjacency, or meta-level thematic relevance does not preserve object-level identity.

---

# X. 1936 continuity — identity BREAK at irreducible new quale

Primary: *Great Chain*, especially Lecture XI, p.332.

Aggregation mode: **M1 continuity principle + M6 persistence**.

Lovejoy's retrospective logic of qualitative continuity gives an explicit negative threshold: if a member introduces a genuinely new quality or kind rather than another degree of something common to the series, continuity is breached.

Refusal type: **BREAK**.

Rule:

`difference = degree of common q`
→ continuity can survive;

`difference = irreducible new quale`
→ continuity fails at that edge.

This is unusually close to an actor-level identity/failure criterion.

---

# XI. 1936 unilinear gradation — identity BREAK when rank becomes irreducibly cross-cutting

Primary: *Great Chain*, Lecture II, Aristotle analysis.

Aggregation mode: **M1 ordering principle**.

Lovejoy notes that Aristotelian organismic traits do not correlate into one natural line: x can be superior to y in one respect and inferior in another. Only when one determinate attribute is privileged can a single linear series be formed.

Refusal type: **BREAK / non-instantiation of unilinearity**.

Rule:

`one privileged ascending variable`
→ unilinear gradation candidate;

`multiple irreducibly cross-cutting rankings with no privileged rule`
→ no single line.

Important: local forms of gradation can still exist. What fails is the stronger **unilinear** operator.

---

# XII. 1941 Romanticism — one systematic unity explicitly rejected

Primary: `The Meaning of Romanticism for the Historian of Ideas`, JHI 2.3 (1941), p.261.

Aggregation mode: attempted M1/M4 macro-unity versus **M3 period/movement grouping**.

Lovejoy says the new ideas of the 1780s–1790s were in large part heterogeneous, logically independent, and sometimes antithetic. It is historically misleading to suppose one `Romantic` idea, one fundamental idea with the rest as implicates, or one systematic unity.

Refusal type: **DOWNGRADE / SPLIT**.

Yet he retains:

- a Romantic period;
- several Romantic movements;
- histories of individual ideas and their alliances.

Thus the negative judgment is scale-specific:

`no Romanticism as one idea/system`
≠ `no meaningful Romantic historical grouping`.

---

# XIII. 1941 logical relation → historical operation — SUSPEND until evidence returns

Primary: same paper, pp.264–266.

Aggregation mode: unit identity already provisionally fixed; problem shifts to Gate B.

Lovejoy says logical and psychological relations are first constructed analytically by the historian as tentative hypotheses, then tested against historical data to determine whether they actually operated.

Refusal type: **SUSPEND promotion**.

Rule:

`analyst can reconstruct implication/opposition/presupposition`
↛ `actor recognized it`
↛ `relation historically operated`
↛ `genetic relation`.

This is perhaps the clearest mature generalization of negative jurisprudence: analytical possibility does not inherit historical actuality.

# XV. The missing negative case: explicit population-level representativeness demotion

The current corpus has many examples where Lovejoy:

- splits labels;
- refuses identity;
- rejects macro historical rubrics;
- suspends genealogy;
- distinguishes principle identity from empirical satisfaction.

What has **not yet** been recovered in direct, secure form is a comparably explicit Lovejoy case of:

> `I proposed type X as fundamental/dominant/representative of population P, but counter-distribution is large enough that X must be demoted or P split.`

This is exactly the negative control most relevant to 1906 `Primitive Energetics`.

Current status should therefore be coded:

`M5 REPRESENTATIVENESS-DEMOTION CONTROL = OPEN / BOUNDED NEGATIVE IN CURRENT CENSUS`.

Do not infer from this bounded negative that Lovejoy never performed such a correction. It specifies the next search target.

---

# XVI. New conceptual result: Lovejoy's negative method is scale-preserving, not merely skeptical

A striking pattern across the census is that Lovejoy often does **not** move from `too broad` to `nothing`.

He more often performs:

`strong macro claim`
→ `split constituent propositions/relations`
→ `reject or suspend the overstrong identity/relation`
→ `retain a weaker family, period, configuration, local relation, or contextual probability`.

This is a form of **scale-preserving downgrade**.

Examples:

- Kant: historical rupture falls, technical/local distinctions remain;
- pragmatism: one logical brick falls, historical compound remains;
- Romanticism: one essence falls, period/movements remain;
- Smith: direct genealogy fails, broad syncretic probability can remain;
- Monboddo/Rousseau: content similarity remains, genealogy suspended.

This is methodologically more informative than labeling Lovejoy globally `cautious` or `atomistic`.

---

# XVII. Consequence for aggregation-mode topology

The refusal census supports the M1–M6 distinction and adds a practical rule:

> **A negative judgment must be typed by what exactly is being refused: membership, scale, invariant, representativeness, or relation promotion.**

The project should therefore never code a case merely `Lovejoy rejects X` without specifying:

- `SPLIT` — identity membership denied;
- `DOWNGRADE` — stronger scale rejected, weaker object retained;
- `BREAK` — invariant lost/reversed;
- `SUSPEND` — stronger relation not promoted;
- `REP-DEMOTE` — population representativeness downgraded (currently open as a direct Lovejoy control).

This is the negative counterpart to relation-indexed evidentiary force.

## Compact conclusion

> **Lovejoy has a substantial negative jurisprudence. He repeatedly splits shared labels, downgrades overstrong historical scales while preserving weaker groupings, breaks identity when a constitutive operator reverses, and suspends genealogy or historical operation when local warrants fail. The conspicuous missing control is population-level representativeness demotion. That absence, currently only a bounded negative, makes the 1906 move from regional mechanisms to a `fundamental concept of primitive philosophy` a particularly valuable stress test.**
