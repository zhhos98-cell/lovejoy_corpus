## 1. Lovejoy explicitly dismantles eighteenth-century homogeneous political Man

The essay's historical architecture is much stronger than Wilson's summary alone suggested.

Lovejoy contrasts two ruling category systems:

### Eighteenth-century `Nature`

- one simple, universally valid religion / morality / economics / political form;
- `natural rights` of `man as man`;
- a constitutional scheme imagined as equally appropriate to all peoples at all times;
- history treated largely as deviation from an invariant rational norm.

### Nineteenth-century `Evolution`

- what is good under the `laws of social evolution` becomes **variable, contingent, relative to time, place and circumstances**;
- historical beliefs, ideals and institutions become sequentially intelligible rather than mere error;
- reform becomes complex, many-sided, gradual and provisional;
- different stages of human progress can require different institutions.

Lovejoy explicitly cites Lessing/Herder as earlier sources of stage-sequence thinking and quotes J. S. Mill for the proposition that political institutions are relative to a prior theory of human progress.

This is direct actor-level evidence for the project's post-Enlightenment stratification problem, not merely a reconstruction imposed from later Lovejoy.

---

## 2. The strongest new sentence: generic Man is split on two axes

After social evolution, Lovejoy introduces `race and nationality` as the second nineteenth-century category.

He says the Enlightenment political theorists knew `man only as Man` and assumed essential human uniformity. Against that, the nineteenth century learned to treat peoples as possessing historically consequential national/racial difference.

Lovejoy then gives the decisive formulation:

> the conjunction of **stages of social evolution** and **racial or national genius** destroys the unity of eighteenth-century generic Man; that Man is split in two directions, **longitudinally and cross-wise**.

The two axes are therefore explicit in Lovejoy's own vocabulary:

- **longitudinal:** successive stages of social/cultural evolution;
- **cross-wise:** contemporaneous racial/national differentiation.

This is exceptionally close to the project's structural model of temporal + population/geographic stratification.

---

## 3. The two axes do not carry identical modal force

Lovejoy does **not** treat stage and race as interchangeable variables.

He says:

- institutions appropriate to one stage of culture are **quite certain** to be inappropriate to an earlier stage;
- institutions appropriate to one racial/national genius **may be** inappropriate to another, and he explicitly inserts: `I do not, in this case, say "must be"`.

He also warns that the category of race has become `badly overworked`, that racial self-consciousness can become morbid, and that systematic cultivation of national peculiarities can damage intellectual and moral sanity.

Therefore:

> **developmental staging is the harder hierarchy; racial/national differentiation is a real political constraint in Lovejoy's argument, but it is more qualified and less deterministic.**

Do not flatten the two.

---

## 4. `Race` semantics: mixed historical-national category with racialized political consequences

The direct text does not support reducing `race` to a purely hereditary biological variable.

Its semantic field includes:

- `race and nationality`;
- `racial or national genius`;
- distinctive art, literature and institutions;
- national character;
- history, anthropology and political experience;
- `racial character`, intellectual possibilities, past history, prejudices and traditions as jointly relevant to institutional fit.

This is best coded as a **mixed racial-national-historical population category** typical of the period, not as a clean modern biology/culture distinction.

At the same time, its political consequences are genuinely racialized. The conclusion explicitly applies the framework to colonial policy and to Black enfranchisement/disfranchisement. Lovejoy says the argument may incline one to regret the `hasty` way the previous generation enfranchised Black Americans and to sympathize with attempts to disfranchise illiterate Black voters, while declining to give a final explicit judgment on the specific cases.

Thus the correct firewall is:

- do not call the essay a worked-out biological race theory;
- do not sanitize it into neutral contextualism either.

---

## 5. Political relativization does NOT eliminate a higher evaluative criterion

The old natural-rights basis is rejected, but Lovejoy does not embrace value relativism.

He argues:

- rights come from society rather than `Nature`;
- rights are only as inalienable as compatible with the general and permanent interests of society;
- governments are to be judged not by origin in abstract rights but by **concrete ends**;
- modern political reasoning increasingly evaluates institutions by total practical consequences and general welfare.

He lists goods including:

- civil liberty;
- order and security;
- culture;
- domestic/civic virtue;
- commercial integrity;
- continuity of national development;
- noble pleasures and the capacity to enjoy them;
- a `better breed of citizens` from generation to generation;
- maintenance/elevation of the `standard of civilization`.

The phrase `better breed of citizens` should not by itself be converted into an eugenics claim without additional evidence; in this passage it sits inside a broad civic/civilizational list.

His final criterion is therefore approximately:

> **general evaluative ends + historically differentiated institutional suitability.**

That resolves the 1903 `natural rights vs rational end` fork strongly in favor of the second branch.

## 6. Final classification of the 1906 political position

The earlier four possibilities can now be adjudicated.

### Strongly present

1. **institutional-fit relativism** — yes;
2. **developmental-capacity hierarchy** — yes, especially through stage-of-culture sequence and `intellectual possibilities`;
3. **racial/national differentiation** — yes, as an independent cross-wise axis;
4. **general teleological/civilizational criterion above institutional forms** — yes.

### Not established in a strong form

- a purely hereditary biological theory of political capacity;
- an assertion that institutions *must* differ by race;
- a nihilistic or fully relativist politics with no common evaluative end.

Best compact description:

> **Lovejoy replaces one homogeneous political Man with a two-axis historical population grid — longitudinal stages of culture and cross-wise racial/national differentiation — while retaining a supra-institutional consequentialist/civilizational criterion for judging forms of government.**

## 7. Same-year 1906 scalar hinge is now direct-primary, not hypothetical

Chronology:

- **19 Feb 1906:** `Democracy in the Twentieth Century` delivered;
- **April 1906:** Bulletin publication and Lovejoy's *Mind* paper `Kant's Antithesis of Dogmatism and Criticism` (vol.15.58, 191–214);
- **1 July 1906:** `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`, *The Monist* 16.3, 357–382.

The three direct primaries show three scalar operations within months:

### Democracy

`generic universal Man`
→ split **longitudinally** by stage + **cross-wise** by race/nationality
→ institutions judged relative to population/history/stage
→ still evaluated by higher practical/civilizational ends.

### Kant

accepted whole-period `dogmatism/criticism` rubric
→ disaggregation into local doctrinal / argumentative / continuity claims
→ inherited macro-classification rejected when local warrants fail.

### Primitive Philosophy

heterogeneous local ethnographic and lexical evidence
→ recurrent impersonal/quasi-mechanical mechanism
→ cross-regional equivalence
→ reaggregation as `primitive philosophy`, `savage thought`, broad populations.

Thus the former paired hypothesis is now confirmed and widened:

> **In 1906 Lovejoy was not moving in one direction from totality to decomposition or from context to abstraction. He was actively controlling scale in multiple directions: dismantling unsupported wholes, differentiating formerly universal political subjects, and rebuilding cross-cultural wholes when he judged local evidence sufficient.**

The research problem is therefore **warrant for scale movement**, not a linear developmental trajectory.

## 8. New research lead generated by the direct text

One final narrow question remains potentially high-value:

> **What classificatory grammar is Lovejoy carrying across the political and comparative-religion papers?**

The Democracy essay explicitly uses:

- `stages of social evolution` / `stage of culture`;
- `race and nationality`;
- history + anthropology;
- relative institutional applicability.

The July *Monist* article begins by arguing against Brinton, Guyau, Tylor, Spencer and Grant Allen, yet still attributes a reconstructed category to `primitive philosophy`, `savage thought`, `most savages`, broad `races`, and a relevant `stage of culture`.

This raises a specific source-history problem:

> Lovejoy may reject Tylor/Frazer-type **substantive master explanations** while retaining or reworking the field's **classificatory grammar of stage / race / primitive philosophy**.

This is not yet a demonstrated Tylor→Lovejoy transmission claim. It is the only newly generated question that appears capable of materially sharpening the historiographical account.

A final round, if run, should therefore be **targeted only at this 1906 classificatory-grammar transfer**, not another broad Lovejoy web sweep.

## 9. Consequence for JHI article

The political material is now too strong to carry at length in a 2400-word article, but one sentence can do major work:

> In February 1906 Lovejoy himself described nineteenth-century thought as having split the Enlightenment's generic `Man` in two directions — longitudinally by stages of social evolution and cross-wise by racial/national difference; five months later his `Primitive Philosophy` paper recombined evidence from historically distinct populations into a new cross-cultural cognitive category.

Use cautiously because the second clause is our analytical juxtaposition, not Lovejoy's declaration that the two papers form one project.

## Evidence discipline

Do not claim:

- that `Democracy` caused or supplied the method of `Primitive Philosophy`;
- that `race` means only biological heredity;
- that Lovejoy rejects racial hierarchy altogether;
- that political forms are evaluated without a common normative/teleological criterion;
- that the phrase `better breed of citizens` alone proves eugenic doctrine;
- that Lovejoy's February and July papers explicitly cite each other.
