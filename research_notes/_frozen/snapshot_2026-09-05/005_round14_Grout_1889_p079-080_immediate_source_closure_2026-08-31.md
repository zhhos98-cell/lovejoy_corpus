## 1. Exact sequence behind notebook p.79

Notebook p.79 summarizes a Zulu prayer translated by Lewis Grout. The local setup is:

`illness`
→ `neglected ancestors/shades`
→ `demand for a particular cow`
→ `offering in return for relief/cure`.

Grout 1889 supplies this sequence directly. In his account of consulting the *inyanga*, the paternal shades complain that the sick man no longer acknowledges them and has failed to make an animal offering. The diviner then states that if an animal is slaughtered the man will recover and that the shades require a **particular cow**.

The sick man and household subsequently reason that the demanded cow should be given and that they will then see whether the sickness leaves. The prayer repeats the same logic: since the shades have demanded their cow, let the sickness depart and let the sick man recover.

This closes the ethnographic carrier for the notebook's transaction/test structure.

## 2. Exact sequence behind notebook p.80

Notebook p.80 continues the prayer and emphasizes:

- promised cure / empirical outcome as verification;
- a transfer or removal of evil;
- retention/use of sacrificial matter;
- food for the shades and communal eating;
- Lovejoy's classification of the case as mixed rather than mystical-unitive.

Grout's second version is especially close. The father demands the sick man's favorite cow. The sick man again says, in effect, let them have the offering and then he will see whether he recovers. During the slaughter, when the cow bellows, the speaker asks that the cow **bring out the evil**. The sick man then pours the gall upon himself and asks that evil end. The beef is left overnight in a hut so that the shades may feast; the remainder is then cooked and eaten by the neighbors.

Thus the p.80 combination of cure-test, evil removal, gall, shade-feeding and communal feast is directly recoverable from Grout 1889.

## 4. What happens to Callaway

Henry Callaway, *The Religious System of the Amazulu* (1870), remains a real and important parallel source in Lovejoy's bibliography. Callaway independently contains a very close structure:

- illness attributed to the *Itongo*;
- a specified bullock/cow;
- recovery expected after slaughter;
- gall poured on the sufferer;
- prayers to ancestors;
- consumption of the flesh;
- recovery/non-recovery used to evaluate the causal diagnosis.

So the earlier identification of Callaway as a strong lower-witness control was sound.

But the Round-14 distinction is now sharper:

`CALLAWAY = PARALLEL ZULU DOCUMENTARY FIELD + DIRECTLY LISTED BIBLIOGRAPHIC SOURCE`

while

`GROUT 1889 = BEST IMMEDIATE CARRIER FOR THE SPECIFIC p.79–80 NARRATIVE/PRAYER CLUSTER`.

There is no need to route individual p.79–80 clauses through Callaway merely because Callaway contains analogous Zulu practice. Conversely, this result does not prove that Lovejoy did not read/use Callaway elsewhere in the same African dossier.

## 5. Why the closure matters methodologically

The p.79–80 passage is a clean source-transformation microhistory. The source already provides a rich ritual complex. Lovejoy does not discover the ethnographic conjunction; he changes its analytical status.

The operation is:

`THICK ETHNOGRAPHIC EVENT`
→ split into causal and functional predicates
→ `transaction / cure-test / expiation / feeding / ancestor relation`
→ test whether those predicates warrant `mystical union`
→ **NO AUTOMATIC PROMOTION**.

This is the same evidentiary jurisprudence visible elsewhere in 005:

`same outward rite ≠ same mechanism ≠ same historical origin ≠ same social function`.

The Grout closure therefore strengthens the notebook argument precisely by reducing Lovejoy's ownership claim. The novelty lies less in possessing unusual facts than in changing what a source's facts are permitted to establish.
