# Lovejoy 1914 review of Leuba — direct primary feedback closure

Date: 2026-08-26
Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY / CLOSES FORMER EXACT-FEEDBACK HOLD**

Primary object directly controlled:

Arthur O. Lovejoy, review of James H. Leuba, *A Psychological Study of Religion: Its Origin, Function and Future*, *International Journal of Ethics* 24.2 (January 1914), 216–220. DOI 10.1086/intejethi.24.2.2376508.

## Core revision

The previous evidentiary ceiling

`Lovejoy 1914 reviews Leuba = direct bibliographic; exact proposition corrected = HOLD`

is superseded.

Lovejoy explicitly uses the review to correct Leuba's presentation of Lovejoy 1906. The content of that correction is now direct-primary.

## 1. Lovejoy's first proof partition: non-animistic ≠ demonstrated pre-animistic

Before correcting Leuba, Lovejoy summarizes the current anthropological tendency as recognizing a **non-animistic** phase of early belief which was **probably, but not demonstrably, also pre-animistic**.

This is unusually valuable wording for the project's warrant architecture.

It separates:

`non-animistic character / comparative content`

from

`historical priority before animism`.

The latter remains probable rather than demonstrated.

Thus Lovejoy 1914 directly publishes the distinction:

> **comparative/category status can be stronger than historical-priority warrant.**

This is not reconstructed solely from our analytic model; it is actor-level epistemic grading.

## 2. What Lovejoy says Leuba misrepresented

Lovejoy says Leuba quotes a few sentences from his criticism of Marett and then expresses dissent. A reader would naturally infer that Leuba's view and Lovejoy's were opposed.

Lovejoy corrects this impression directly:

- the theory Leuba accepts about the **origin and content of the primitive `idea of impersonal power`** is essentially the theory Lovejoy says he advanced in 1906;
- Leuba is substantively in agreement with Lovejoy's criticism of Marett;
- Leuba's `dynamism` corresponds to what Lovejoy had called `primitive energetics` or `manitouism`;
- Lovejoy says Leuba makes substantial use of both the hypothesis and the principal evidence while conveying too purely critical an attitude toward Lovejoy's formulation.

This closes the feedback relation:

`Lovejoy 1906`
→ `Leuba 1912 reads/criticizes Lovejoy`
→ `Lovejoy 1914 says the apparent disagreement is overstated and re-specifies the actual overlap`.

## 3. The disagreement is narrowed rather than erased

Lovejoy does not claim total identity.

He says the two differ chiefly over:

- the most convenient **name** for the phenomenon;
- one **minor question**, which he thinks largely arises from terminological confusion.

He also argues that Leuba makes **less use of the hypothesis than the facts justify**. For Lovejoy, mana/manitou-like ideas interpenetrate other elements of early belief and have important consequences for religious development that Leuba underuses.

Thus reception is again proposition-specific:

- broad impersonal-power hypothesis: Lovejoy says **substantial agreement**;
- nomenclature: difference;
- one minor interpretive issue: difference;
- explanatory reach/developmental use: Lovejoy thinks Leuba **underextends** the hypothesis.

## 4. Lovejoy's own priority language requires an attribution firewall

Lovejoy calls his 1906 paper the `original formulation` of the hypothesis and of its principal evidence.

This is an important **actor priority claim**, but it must not be converted uncritically into the project's historical priority verdict.

Current repository controls show:

- impersonal-power / mana / preanimistic problem fields were already distributed across Marett, Codrington, Fletcher and other anthropology/religion literature;
- quasi-mechanical / force / energy vocabularies were not uniquely Lovejoyan fingerprints;
- Lovejoy's stronger documentary delta lies in his particular synthesis, lexical/grammatical source criticism, proof architecture, cross-regional reaggregation and category formulation.

Therefore use:

> **Lovejoy in 1914 claimed priority for his formulation.**

Do not write without qualification:

> **Lovejoy originated the impersonal-power hypothesis.**

## 5. The feedback loop changes the reception model

The early reception chain is now directly content-controlled at both ends:

`Lovejoy 1906`
→ `King 1910 directly incorporates category/evidence and reallocates religious status`
→ `Leuba 1910 reviews King; King 1911 identifies at least one methodological criticism on reply side`
→ `King 1911 retains widespread impersonal-power evidence while treating primitiveness/priority as speculative`
→ `Leuba 1912 directly retains the evidence field but contests Lovejoy's strong mechanical characterization and uses developmental psychology for genesis`
→ `Lovejoy 1914 directly re-enters the controversy and argues that Leuba's apparent opposition to his 1906 position is mostly illusory`.

Reception is therefore demonstrably a **feedback system**, not a one-way citation tree.

## 6. A second methodological criticism in the 1914 review

Lovejoy criticizes Leuba's broader procedure on two grounds.

First, he says Leuba begins a psychological interpretation of religious history but stops at the supposed primitive stage; Lovejoy wants the historico-psychological analysis continued through later phases of religious evolution.

Second, Lovejoy attacks ambiguity in Leuba's definition of `religion`. He distinguishes at least three possible jobs for a definition:

1. stipulating what the author chooses to call religion;
2. identifying common elements across diverse historical uses of the term;
3. analytically describing complex observable phenomena across phases of individual and racial development.

Lovejoy prefers the third task and argues that Leuba sometimes reasons circularly by excluding features simply because they fall outside his prior definition or prevailing usage.

This is significant for the larger project because it shows Lovejoy again separating **lexical/stipulative definition**, **historical usage**, and **analytical classification**. It should not automatically be equated with notebook operators, but it is a strong published later analogue to relation-specific classification.

## 7. JHI-facing consequence

The short JHI epilogue can now use the American reception chain with much greater precision:

> **The 1906 abstraction was itself rapidly decomposed in American debate. King retained the power field while lowering claims about its historical primitiveness; Leuba retained much of the hypothesis but challenged parts of its characterization and changed the evidentiary channel for questions of genesis; Lovejoy then re-entered the exchange in 1914 to argue that Leuba had made their substantive disagreement appear larger than it was.**

The point is not that all parties shared one method. It is that a comparative category circulated as separable propositions — existence, characterization, priority, religious status, nomenclature and developmental reach — each of which could be accepted, narrowed, rejected or re-warranted independently.

This is the cleanest public analogue yet recovered for the notebook-level rule:

> **a stronger proposition does not automatically inherit the warrant of a weaker one.**
