# *Political Theory* 1975 editorial census: actor taxonomy as execution evidence

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT PRIMARY PDF / FULL 3-PAGE EDITORIAL CONTROL / CLASSIFICATION AMBIGUITY LOCKED**

## Core result

The May 1975 *Political Theory* editorial census is stronger evidence than a retrospective description of the journal's pluralism because it records the editors' own day-to-day classification problem.

The key finding is not merely that the journal published both historical and contemporary work. The editors explicitly say that their categories are unstable at the article level:

- an essay may be read as `historical or modern`;
- it may also be `methodological or conceptual`;
- classification therefore proceeds with `cautious tentativeness`.

This gives direct execution-level warrant for the repo's anti-exclusive coding rule:

`ONE ARTICLE != ONE NECESSARY METHOD ROLE`.

It also sharpens the stronger proposition:

`EDITORIAL CATEGORY != NATURAL ONTOLOGY OF ARTICLE`.

The journal's own editors were already confronting the same aggregation problem that retrospective method histories can accidentally erase.

---

## I. Direct submission / acceptance profile

The editorial reports:

- approximately **185 manuscripts** received between March 1974 and January 1975;
- **18 accepted** from that profiled period;
- approximately **30 additional pieces** accepted/published from the earlier period since the journal's 1973 inception;
- an acceptance rate of roughly **1 in 10** for the profiled ten-month period.

The editors explicitly warn that the profiled submission set and the accepted/published set are not symmetrical and should be treated as suggestive rather than conclusive.

This matters methodologically: the document is an actor-produced census with its own declared denominator and classification limitations, not a clean statistical sample.

---

## II. `Historical` versus `modern` is explicitly provisional

Of the 185 submitted pieces, the editors classify approximately:

- **100** as `historical`, because they treat individual theorists from Plato to the present;
- **85** as residual `modern`, including methodology, critical political theory and conceptual analysis.

The article then reports **48** accepted/published items, of which **24** are said to be historical and **20** modern.

### Arithmetic control

The printed figures `24 + 20` total **44**, not 48.

Do not silently normalize this discrepancy. Possible explanations include an editorial/printing omission or four items not assigned in the summarized binary, but the document as printed does not resolve it.

Therefore preserve:

`PRINTED TOTAL = 48`

and

`PRINTED BINARY SUBTOTAL = 44`.

No downstream percentage should be calculated as if this discrepancy had been resolved.

---

## III. The journal itself says some `historical` cases are not quite historical

The editorial's most revealing internal correction appears in its treatment of twentieth-century theorists.

It records **20 submissions** in the twentieth-century `historical` category, then immediately says that **none of these are quite historical at all**.

John Rawls alone accounts for ten submissions and three published articles in this category.

This is direct actor-level proof that the journal's object-based classification (`essay on an individual theorist`) does not map cleanly onto method-role (`historical`).

Compact distinction:

`OBJECT = NAMED THEORIST`
!=
`ROLE = HISTORICAL INQUIRY`.

That distinction is directly relevant to the repo's `UNIT × RELATION` architecture. A proposition can use an author/theorist unit without thereby becoming a historical claim, and a historical article can carry methodological or conceptual roles at the same time.

---

## IV. `Philosophy of intellectual history` appears as an editorial intake category

Among the 85 submissions classed as `modern`, the editors identify **23** as concerning some aspect of:

- methodology;
- philosophy of intellectual history;
- philosophy of social science.

They gloss these as **metatheory rather than theory**: work about how political theory is done rather than simply doing it.

They report having published **five** articles on metatheory, explicitly excluding essays on Strauss, Marx, Althusser and other theorists that also carry metatheoretical concerns.

This is a high-value bridge for the present project.

By 1975, inside the new *Political Theory* venue, `philosophy of intellectual history` was not merely the retrospective label of an older field. It was an active editorially recognized component of contemporary metatheoretical submissions.

Thus:

`HISTORY-OF-IDEAS / INTELLECTUAL-HISTORY METHOD PROBLEM`
was being processed as
`LIVE POLITICAL-THEORY METATHEORY`.

This strongly complements Haddock 1974, which placed Mandelbaum, Passmore, Dunn, Pocock, Skinner and Parekh/Berki in one methodological problem-space.

---

## V. Topic counts reinforce overlap rather than field replacement

The same editorial census records a very mixed intake:

- ancient, medieval, early-modern, American and twentieth-century named-theorist studies;
- democratic theory and public policy;
- methodology / philosophy of intellectual history / philosophy of social science;
- political psychology and political sociology;
- literary analysis in a political register;
- obligation, alienation, anarchism and civil disobedience;
- miscellaneous contemporary political problems.

This is not evidence that all methods were equally valued or equally accepted.

It is evidence that the journal's actual intake and publication work required repeated classification across **objects, periods and methodological roles that overlapped**.

Therefore the safest institutional model is:

`METHOD AGGREGATION + EDITORIAL SORTING`

rather than:

`ONE SCHOOL CAPTURES VENUE`.

---

## VI. Relation to the 1973 founding program

Recent archival disciplinary history reconstructs *Political Theory* as emerging in 1970 from a Columbia CSPT chapter meeting intended to `build bridges` and quotes the founding editors' 1973 aspiration to stimulate thought, controversy, clarification, understanding and new perspectives in political philosophy.

The 1975 census is useful because it shows what that broad program looked like in execution two years later.

The bridge claim should therefore be split into two evidentiary levels:

1. `FOUNDING PROGRAM` — broad, controversy-producing, perspective-generating venue;
2. `EDITORIAL EXECUTION` — high-volume intake whose own categories overlap and require tentative classification.

Do not infer that every published article shared one editorial doctrine.

`FOUNDING PROGRAM`
!= `ARTICLE POSITION`
!= `EDITORIAL TAXONOMY`.

`OBJECT CLASSIFICATION`
!= `METHOD ROLE`
!= `EDITORIAL CATEGORY`.

### New historical claim

By 1975, `philosophy of intellectual history` was explicitly present inside *Political Theory*'s editorial metatheory category. This is direct evidence against a simple post-Skinner chronology in which history-of-ideas methodological questions had already become an obsolete predecessor discourse.

### Hold

Do not infer from the census alone:

- equal editorial preference across methods;
- a deliberate anti-Skinner editorial policy;
- causal influence from CSPT founding debates to any individual acceptance decision;
- a complete census of the field outside this journal.
