# Batch 130 — Paris 1898: Lévi's state mission dossier, a 9 October return anchor, and instruction outside the formal programme

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: continue the Paris line while dynamic Salamandre files are being retrieved separately. This pass targets two remaining uncertainties: (1) the administrative/documentary route for Sylvain Lévi's 1897–98 mission and return; (2) whether instruction outside the formal EPHE programme is merely a biographical possibility or a documented feature of Lévi's teaching practice.

## Core result

Three independent documentary layers now line up around Lovejoy's October 1898 Paris window.

First, the Archives nationales state-administrative dossier for Lévi's Asian mission is identifiable as **F/17/2984 B**. Scholarly work citing the dossier identifies Lévi's 1896 mission request and a **5 February 1898 letter from Kathmandu** to Foreign Minister Gabriel Hanotaux. This is not a vague collection lead but an exact state mission file containing document-level references.

Second, a separate Archives nationales scholar-manuscript group, **AB/XIX/3360–3366**, contains Lévi's own research manuscripts; specifically **AB/XIX/3361** is described as holding manuscripts of his report on the 1897–98 India/Japan mission and manuscripts for *Le Népal*. This is a distinct provenance and should not be collapsed into the state dossier, the later-looted private correspondence, or the Collège de France scientific archive.

Third, and most importantly for chronology, the 1898–99 EPHE annuaire gives a new hard return anchor: **Sylvain Lévi was physically present in France at Vimy on 9 October 1898**, representing the École pratique together with Finot at the inauguration of the Abel Bergaigne monument. That is only eight days before the earliest Wilson-cited Lovejoy Paris letter of 17 October 1898. The mission-to-Paris transition is therefore now much narrower than `returned by September 1898`.

The other major result is methodological. Later primary EPHE reports explicitly document Lévi conducting scholarly instruction **outside his regular conferences**, including continuing Bhagavad-Gītā work with Stickney, regular work by Maspero and Oger at Lévi's home, and supplementary sessions comparing Chinese versions of the Questions of Menander with the Pāli *Milinda-pañha*. Therefore `formal course catalogue` and `total instructional contact with Lévi` are demonstrably non-equivalent categories. This does not prove Lovejoy's 1898 private readings, but it makes Wilson's letter-based account institutionally normal rather than exceptional.

## 1. New state archive route: F/17/2984 B

Roland Lardinois's history of French Indianism cites the Archives nationales mission file as:

`AN, F17/2984 (2)`

for Sylvain Lévi's request to the Minister of Public Instruction for a mission to India and the Far East, probably April 1896. A separate archival guide identifies the same mission unit as:

`F17 2984 B — Mission Sylvain Lévi en Inde et au Japon`.

Sources:

- https://books.openedition.org/editionscnrs/60554
- archival guide: *Guides to the Sources for the History of the Nations*, France archives section.

The request is historiographically useful because Lévi frames the mission as a deliberate movement away from ten years of cabinet work toward contact with `l'Inde réelle et vivante` and with indigenous learned practice.

This file should now be treated as the **state-administrative mission dossier**, potentially containing authorization, funding, itinerary, official correspondence and reporting steps.

### Digital status

No public digital image for F/17/2984 B has yet been confirmed in the current web layer. The exact cote is strong; scan status remains unresolved.

## 2. A precisely dated mission letter inside F/17/2984

Lardinois cites another document from the same file:

- Sylvain Lévi to Foreign Minister Gabriel Hanotaux;
- Kathmandu;
- **5 February 1898**.

The quoted passage says Lévi had gone to Benares, studied the method of the pandits, and sought direct contact with the religious life of India.

Source:

https://books.openedition.org/editionscnrs/60564

This gives a state-file documentary anchor from the middle of the mission and proves that F/17/2984 is more than a pre-departure authorization file. It contains correspondence generated during the mission itself.

Therefore its retrieval value for the return problem is high: the same dossier may contain later dispatches, travel-stage reports or closure correspondence that could constrain the Japan–Russia–France sequence more tightly.

## 3. Separate authorial manuscript route: AB/XIX/3361

An archival guide describes:

`AB XIX 3360–3366 — Papiers de Sylvain Lévi (1863–1935)`.

Within it:

- `3360` — manuscript of *Le Théâtre indien*, 1890;
- **`3361` — manuscripts of his report on the India/Japan mission in 1897 and 1898, and manuscripts of *Le Népal*, 1898–1905**;
- `3362–3363` — manuscripts of works, 1907–1912;
- `3364–3365` — miscellaneous manuscripts, notes and studies;
- `3366` — printed articles and corrected proofs.

Archival-guide control:

*Guides to the Sources for the History of the Nations*, archive entry for Archives nationales, AB XIX 3360–3366.

The significance is provenance. We now have at least four non-identical Lévi archival bodies relevant to the Paris problem:

1. `F/17/2984 B` — **state mission administration**;
2. `AB/XIX/3361` — **Lévi's authorial mission-report manuscripts**;
3. Collège de France / Bibliothèque d'études indiennes — **scientific archive / manuscripts and collections**;
4. the wartime-looted private papers later recovered from Russia — **private archive**, known to contain little professional correspondence in the portion inspected by scholars.

A negative search in one cannot be transferred to the others.

### Digital status

No digital scan of AB/XIX/3361 has yet been confirmed. This is currently an exact archival component lead, not a digital object.

## 4. New hard return anchor: Lévi is in France on 9 October 1898

The IVᵉ Section's 1900 annuaire records the inauguration of the Abel Bergaigne monument at **Vimy on 9 October 1898**. It explicitly says the École pratique was represented by Michel Bréal, Gabriel Monod, Émile Châtelain, **S. Lévi**, **L. Finot**, and former students of Bergaigne.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1900_num_1_1_2270

This is the strongest public return-date evidence recovered so far.

Current chronology:

- 5 Feb 1898 — Lévi writes from Kathmandu;
- later 1898 — mission continues through Japan and return via Russia;
- **9 Oct 1898 — Lévi is physically present at Vimy, France, in an official EPHE scholarly delegation**;
- 17 Oct 1898 — earliest Lovejoy Paris letter used by Wilson;
- 3 Nov 1898 — IVᵉ Section conferences officially begin;
- 1898–99 — Lévi's realized regular EPHE teaching is Bhagavad-Gītā + Indian literature.

### Evidence limit

Vimy is not Paris. The 9 October source proves Lévi had returned to France and was again operating inside the EPHE scholarly network by that date. It does not prove his exact Paris arrival day or that Lovejoy had met him by 17 October.

Nevertheless, the temporal gap between `Lévi back in the French institutional network` and the first Wilson-cited Lovejoy letter is now only eight days.

## 5. The formal 1898–99 Sanskrit report remains clear: no regular Pāli course

The official 1898–99 `Langue sanscrite` report says Lévi gave two regular weekly conferences:

- Bhagavad-Gītā explanation;
- a continuous survey of Indian literature.

Stickney is singled out as a constant and very active participant in the Bhagavad-Gītā work.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1900_num_1_1_2251

Thus the regular realized teaching layer still supports the same conclusion:

> **the Pāli-Buddhist study Lovejoy reportedly wanted was not among Lévi's recovered regular 1898–99 offerings.**

This is independent corroboration of Wilson's letter-based narrative, not proof of the private reading itself.

---

## 6. Direct primary proof that Lévi taught beyond the formal conference schedule

The following-year Sanskrit report is especially important. After describing two regular conferences, it explicitly says:

`En dehors de ces deux conférences, M. Lévi a poursuivi, avec M. Stickney, la lecture de la Bhagavad-Gîtâ...`

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1901_num_1_1_2303

This sentence supplies exactly the documentary category needed for the Lovejoy problem:

`regular conference programme`

≠

`all textual instruction undertaken by Lévi with individual students`.

We should therefore stop treating the absence of a formal Pāli title as if it exhausted the possible forms of instruction.

---

## 7. Home instruction is explicitly documented in a later Lévi annual report

The 1907–08 Sanskrit report says Henri Maspero and Oger, both former students, came **regularly on Tuesdays from 5 to 7 to Lévi's home** to prepare two Sanskrit-Chinese lexica under his direction.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1908_num_1_1_2695

This is a cleaner modality control than a general biographical statement about Lévi's salon. It is an official EPHE annual report recording:

`named students + regular weekly time + Lévi's home + supervised textual research`.

It must not be back-projected as evidence that Lovejoy's 1898 reading occurred at the same address or on the same model. But it demonstrates that home-based scholarly supervision was an actual documented component of Lévi's teaching practice.

---

## 8. Pāli also appears explicitly in supplementary sessions

The 1908–09 report records a series of supplementary sessions beginning in February in which Lévi and Dufresne compared two Chinese versions of the `Questions de Ménandre` with the Pāli *Milinda-pañha*.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1909_num_1_1_8916

Again the point is not to use a 1908–09 event as evidence for Lovejoy. It is to establish the institutional modality:

- regular course titles do not exhaust actual work;
- supplementary sessions could be language-specific;
- Pāli could enter through comparative Buddhist textual work rather than as a standalone course heading.

This makes Mauss's retrospective memory of Pāli/Buddhist translation with Lévi and Finot and Wilson's account of Lovejoy's private sacred-text reading mutually intelligible within a broader documented teaching practice.

---

## 9. Formal Pāli instruction becomes explicit in the later Vᵉ Section programme

The 1905–06 Vᵉ Section programme advertises under Louis Finot:

- `Explication de textes du canon pâli`;
- `La littérature bouddhique`.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1904_num_18_14_19656

This later control matters because it proves that `Pāli canon` and `Buddhist literature` were perfectly normal explicit course categories in the same Lévi–Finot network when offered formally.

It does **not** prove that a formally advertised course with those words existed in 1896–97 or was the exact course Lovejoy expected in 1898. Batch128's earlier Mauss testimony therefore remains a **teaching precedent**, not yet a formal-programme precedent.

## 10. The IVᵉ Section 1898–99 participant list is now located, but it does not authorize a negative claim

The IVᵉ Section's published `Liste des élèves et des auditeurs réguliers pendant l'année scolaire 1898-1899` is now exactly identified as pp.116–129 of the Annuaire 1900:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1900_num_1_1_2272

Its own preamble states:

- the school register records **399 registrations** for the two semesters;
- the printed list mentions only auditors recognized by directors in their reports.

Current indexed text searching has not surfaced `Lovejoy` in this IVᵉ Section list.

This is **not** evidence that Lovejoy did not attend Lévi. The printed list declares itself selective, and the web-searchable transcription may not expose every name reliably. By contrast, Lovejoy is positively named in the Vᵉ Section Marillier report as an active auditor.

Correct status:

`IVᵉ published-list indexed hit not found`

not

`Lovejoy absent from IVᵉ Section`.

## 11. Revised reconstruction of the eight-day October window

The safest current microchronology is now:

**Before 9 October**  
Lévi completes the Asian mission and returns to France by an exact date not yet recovered.

**9 October 1898**  
Lévi is documented at Vimy in the official EPHE delegation for Bergaigne.

**17 October 1898**  
Lovejoy writes the earliest of the three Paris letters Wilson cites for the Lévi/Vernes/Réville reconstruction.

**1 and 5 December 1898**  
Further Wilson-cited Lovejoy letters.

**1898–99 realized teaching**  
Lévi: Bhagavad-Gītā + Indian literature; no regular Pāli course recovered.

The remaining personal-transmission question has therefore become extremely narrow:

> **What exactly does Lovejoy say on 17 October about his arrival, intended studies, contact with Lévi, and the unavailable Pāli course?**

This makes the JHU MS-0873 transcription of the 17 October letter even more valuable than before. It is now the one document most likely to connect two independently reconstructed chronologies that already come within eight days of each other.
