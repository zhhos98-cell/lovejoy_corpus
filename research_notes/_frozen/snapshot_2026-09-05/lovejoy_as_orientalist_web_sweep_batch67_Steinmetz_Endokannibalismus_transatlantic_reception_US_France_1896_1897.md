# Batch 67 — `Endokannibalismus` across France and the United States, 1896–97: same bibliographic object, different disciplinary evaluations

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: use the exact-title match in Lovejoy 005 as a transatlantic reception problem rather than treating Steinmetz only as a French/Marillier source node.

## Core result

Rudolf S. Steinmetz’s 1896 `Endokannibalismus` was not confined to the Marillier/Mauss Paris controversy. The same 60-page Vienna offprint was reviewed almost immediately in at least three different disciplinary venues relevant to the emerging comparative human sciences:

- **France:** Léon Marillier, *Revue de l’histoire des religions* 34 (1896), pp.113–115 — content still pending direct page capture;
- **United States / sociology:** W. I. Thomas, *American Journal of Sociology* 2 (1897), 610–611 — full text recovered;
- **United States / philosophy-science review ecology:** *The Monist* 6.4 (July 1896), p.636 — bibliographic review entry recovered, full review text currently not exposed.

Marcel Mauss simultaneously used and criticized `Endokannibalismus` in his 1896 RHR intervention on Steinmetz.

This matters because Lovejoy’s 005 does not merely intersect a Paris-local reading list. The exact title he writes into his sacrifice bibliography belonged to a **transatlantic methodological object** whose comparative method, source reliability and universalizing claims were being evaluated in French religious studies and American sociology before Lovejoy’s Paris year.

The transmission route to Lovejoy remains unknown. The value of the comparison is field-level: it shows the same research object circulating across the American/French infrastructures we are reconstructing.

## 1. W. I. Thomas 1897: `Endokannibalismus` praised as a model of ethnological method

Primary source:

William Isaac Thomas, review of Rudolf S. Steinmetz, `Endokannibalismus`, *American Journal of Sociology* 2 (1897), 610–611.

The review describes Steinmetz’s work as a comparative account accompanied by a tabular exhibit of:

- tribes practicing endocannibalism;
- motives assigned to the practice;
- reliability of the information in each case.

It lists multiple motives and `negative conditions` rather than presenting one immediate ritual explanation.

Thomas’s most revealing judgment is methodological. He says the article is valuable even more from the **methodological** than the folk-psychological standpoint because ethnological material is hard to manage owing to the unreliability of sources and the vastness of the evidence. He contrasts Steinmetz favorably with comparative writers who display mainly facts supporting their own opinions and praises him for presenting practically all the facts involved.

Thomas ends by calling the article an unsurpassed model for ethnological research, while allowing that Steinmetz’s conclusions may not all be valid.

This gives us an American reception in which the work’s key virtue is **evidentiary completeness and explicit source-reliability control**.

---

## 2. Yet W. I. Thomas also reproduces Steinmetz’s universalizing conclusion

The methodological praise should not be mistaken for the same classificatory caution later visible in Lovejoy 005.

Thomas summarizes Steinmetz’s conclusion that some form of cannibalism characterized lower stages of human development as universally as animism, ancestor worship and blood vengeance, and that ordinary hunger preceded superstitious/ritual motives.

Thus one can have:

- broad evidence collection;
- source-reliability tabulation;
- contrary/negative conditions;

and still move toward a very strong universal developmental conclusion.

This is important for the Lovejoy comparison. Lovejoy’s later resistance to one-origin or one-mechanism claims should not be narrated as the simple invention of `using counterexamples` or `checking sources`. Steinmetz and his American reviewer already valued extensive counterfact-sensitive evidence.

The sharper question is what **historical/classificatory license** that evidence is allowed to carry.

---

## 3. Mauss 1896: admiration for evidentiary scope, criticism of categories and interpretations

Mauss’s contemporary critique gives a strikingly different evaluation of the same research practice.

He acknowledges Steinmetz’s requirement that contrary facts be mentioned and explained and recognizes the service rendered by renewed review of ethnographic facts.

But he attacks:

- explanations that are too general and vague;
- statistical rubrics that are too broad to prove much;
- excessive confidence in indigenous explanations or ethnographer interpretations;
- failure to distinguish analytically among customs and cults that the theory itself treats as different.

Mauss explicitly says the same problem occurs in `Endokannibalismus`.

The issue is therefore not simply whether Steinmetz gathered enough examples. It is whether the **rubrics under which the examples are grouped** preserve the distinctions required for explanation.

This is exceptionally close to the central 005 problem.

## 5. The *Monist* 1896 notice makes the American circulation still broader

*The Monist* 6.4 (July 1896), p.636 contains a book-review entry for Steinmetz’s `Endokannibalismus`, giving Vienna, Anthropologische Gesellschaft, 1896, 60 pages.

The currently exposed Oxford Academic record does not provide enough full text to determine the review’s substantive judgment.

Do not use it for a methodological claim.

Its value is circulation evidence: the same specialist anthropological offprint appeared almost immediately in an American journal at the philosophy/science boundary — the same journal in which Lovejoy would publish `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` a decade later.

This is not a route-of-reading claim. It is a disciplinary-media control.

## 6. The object now links the U.S.–France frame more tightly

Batch 65 showed that Jastrow’s contemporary disciplinary model divided the new history of religions among source philologists, Indologists, anthropologists and other specialists, with Paris as the desired coordinating structure.

`Endokannibalismus` supplies a concrete object moving through those adjacent disciplines:

> Vienna anthropological publication
> → Paris RHR / Marillier review
> → Mauss methodological controversy
> → Chicago/American sociology review
> → American *Monist* notice
> → Lovejoy 005 exact-title bibliography.

This is a better way to write `transatlantic field` than merely listing institutions. The field becomes visible because **the same small bibliographic object is being evaluated and repurposed across them**.

## 7. Lovejoy 005: exact title, but mechanism pluralization

The notebook evidence remains independently secure: 005 PDF p.62 / MS p.125 reads `Endokannibalismus — Steinmetz.`

The later cannibalism sequence then refuses to let the formal act of eating human flesh determine one mechanism. Lovejoy separates:

- hunger/taste;
- revenge;
- affection/funerary relation;
- selected-body-part quality transfer;
- magical or sympathetic efficacy;
- propitiation;
- provision/communication with the dead;
- sacrifice;
- sacramental communion.

The contrast with the American W. I. Thomas review is analytically useful. Thomas admires Steinmetz for exhaustive evidence and source reliability yet still reports a universal developmental conclusion. Lovejoy’s notebook is more interested in preventing one comparative label from doing too much causal/genealogical work.

This is a difference in **evidentiary license**, not simply a difference between careful and careless research.

## 8. A useful three-position matrix

### Steinmetz / W. I. Thomas reception

- broad comparative data;
- explicit reliability concern;
- negative conditions / contrary evidence;
- strong universal primitive inference remains possible.

### Mauss

- acknowledges broad data work;
- attacks over-wide rubrics and vague explanation;
- demands stronger source/interpretive criticism and categorical discrimination;
- can nevertheless reunify some practices under communion/social mechanisms.

### Lovejoy 005

- source criticism and negative-case procedure;
- repeatedly strips outward form of automatic diagnostic authority;
- pluralizes causal bearers/mechanisms;
- later abstracts one cross-cutting family of impersonal/transferable efficacy without making it the origin of every rite.

Marillier’s exact position between these remains open until pp.113–115 are recovered.

---

## 9. Consequence for the paper’s novelty

Do not say:

> Lovejoy innovated by taking ethnographic source reliability seriously.

That is demonstrably false as a priority claim.

Do not say:

> Lovejoy’s use of negative cases distinguishes him from Steinmetz.

Steinmetz explicitly foregrounded contrary facts, and W. I. Thomas praised his data handling.

Better:

> **Lovejoy’s distinctive notebook operation lies in restricting the inferential work that broad comparative categories are allowed to perform. Even when evidence is abundant and formally comparable, the observed class does not by itself settle motive, causal bearer, religious mechanism or historical origin.**

This formulation aligns 005 with the 004 evidentiary-burden argument and survives the Steinmetz control.
