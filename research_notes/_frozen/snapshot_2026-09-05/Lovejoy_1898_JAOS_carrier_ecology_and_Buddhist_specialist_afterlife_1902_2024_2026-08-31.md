# Lovejoy 1898 — JAOS carrier ecology and Buddhist specialist afterlife, 1902–2024

## Core result

The 1898 Buddhist paper had a **specialist afterlife independent of Lovejoy's later canonization as historian of ideas**. This materially sharpens the blog's existing formulation that the early formation was `known but differently classified`.

The stronger relation is not:

`BUDDHIST WORK FORGOTTEN -> RECOVERED BY ARCHIVE`.

It is:

`1898 PAPER RETAINED AS A BUDDHIST TECHNICAL CONTRIBUTION`
+
`LOVEJOY LATER CANONIZED AROUND OTHER OBJECTS / ROLES`
->
`SAME EARLY WORK SURVIVES UNDER DIFFERENT SCHOLARLY CLASSIFICATIONS`.

This is a direct historical instance of the repo's broader distinction:

`PRESENCE != ANALYTICAL CENTRALITY != CARRIER ROLE`.

The claim should remain scale-controlled. Continued citation proves specialist survival and argumentative availability, not continuous prominence or major influence across Buddhist studies as a whole.

## I. Immediate carrier classification: Pali Text Society, 1902–03

The *Journal of the Pāli Text Society* 1902–03 contains a section explicitly headed **`BUDDHIST BIBLIOGRAPHY`**. On p.44 it lists:

- Sylvain Lévi, `Le Bouddhisme et les Grecs`;
- **Arthur Oncken Lovejoy, `The Buddhist technical terms upādāna and upādisesa`, JAOS, July 1898, 126–136**;
- Monier-Williams on Buddhism;
- Hermann Oldenberg, `Buddhistische Studien`;
- Louis de La Vallée Poussin on the authority of Buddhist Āgamas;
- other Buddhist/Indian studies.

Direct Pali Text Society PDF:

https://palitextsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JPTS_1902-3_V.pdf

Alternative mirror:

https://noolaham.net/project/822/82160/82160.pdf

Historical significance:

> Within a few years of publication, Lovejoy's paper was not merely a biographical oddity by a future philosopher. It had already been classified inside a specialist Buddhist bibliography alongside major Indological/Buddhist scholars.

This is a **carrier-role fact**, not an influence claim.

## II. The 1898–99 JAOS publication ecology is itself mixed-resolution

Lovejoy's paper appeared in *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 19 (1898), 126–136. The following volume, JAOS 20 (1899), makes the surrounding carrier ecology unusually visible.

Direct JSTOR issue table of contents:

https://www.jstor.org/stable/i225070

The same volume contains, among other work:

- Charles R. Lanman, `Sanskrit Diction as Affected by the Interests of Herdsman, Priest, and Gambler`, 12–17;
- Edward Washburn Hopkins, `Lexicographical Notes from the Mahābhārata`, 18–30;
- Crawford H. Toy, `Taboo and Morality`, 151–156;
- Washburn Hopkins, `Economics of Primitive Religion`, 303–308;
- Charles Carroll Everett, `The Psychology of the Vedanta and Sankhya Philosophies`, 309–316;
- Morris Jastrow, Jr., `The Historical Study of Religions in Universities and Colleges`, 317–325;
- Crawford H. Toy, `Relation between Magic and Religion`, 327–331;
- Charles R. Lanman, `Henry Clarke Warren: An Obituary Notice`, 332–337.

Direct JSTOR controls include:

- Toy, `Taboo and Morality`: https://www.jstor.org/stable/592321
- Hopkins, `Economics of Primitive Religion`: https://www.jstor.org/stable/592336

A contemporary *Journal of American Folk-Lore* bibliographical notice explicitly grouped four JAOS 1899 pieces under the `Section for the historical study of religions`: Hopkins on primitive religion, Everett on Vedānta/Sāṃkhya psychology, Jastrow on university study of religions, and Toy on magic/religion.

Wikisource transcription:

https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Journal_of_American_Folk-Lore/Volume_13/Issue_48/Bibliographical_Notes

### Calibration

This ecology matters because it blocks a misleading retrospective unit:

`LOVEJOY 1898 = PHILOSOPHER MAKES AN ISOLATED EXCURSION INTO BUDDHISM`.

A safer historical description is:

> Lovejoy's specialist paper occupied a carrier in which philological source work, comparative religion, broad `primitive religion` generalization, Indian philosophical comparison, and disciplinary self-definition were adjacent publication roles.

This does **not** imply that JAOS possessed one unified method, nor that adjacency proves direct influence among contributors.

It does show that the Harvard curricular juxtaposition documented elsewhere in the repo had a contemporary publication analogue beyond the classroom.

## IV. Later Buddhist scholarship continues to treat the paper as an argumentative source

### Rupert Gethin — five khandhas

Rupert Gethin's `The Five Khandhas: Their Treatment in the Nikāyas and Early Abhidhamma` includes Lovejoy's 1898 paper in its scholarly reference apparatus alongside Conze, Lamotte and Warder.

Public PDF witness:

https://s47fcc2c7bb8760f5.jimcontent.com/download/version/1553316339/module/14020393624/name/Gethin%2C%20Rupert%3B%20The%20Five%20Khandhas%20--%20Their%20Treatment%20in%20the%20Nik%C4%81yas%20and%20Early%20Abhidhamma.pdf

This establishes bibliographic survival inside modern early-Buddhist analysis.

### Soonil Hwang, 2006 — Nirvāṇa

Soonil Hwang, *Metaphor and Literalism in Buddhism: The Doctrinal History of Nirvana* (2006), does more than list Lovejoy. It reconstructs the Oldenberg–Lovejoy–Masefield line on `upādi`, quotes Lovejoy's claim concerning the oldest/most numerous texts, identifies the textual evidence used by Lovejoy, and then criticizes the interpretation.

Public PDF controls:

https://smallpdfs.buddhistuniversity.net/hwang-soonil_2006_metaphor-and-literalism.pdf

and a Routledge-hosted web PDF route recovered in the current sweep.

This is the strongest later afterlife state found so far:

`LOVEJOY 1898`
-> `NAMED INTERPRETIVE LINEAGE`
-> `ARGUMENT RECONSTRUCTED`
-> `ARGUMENT CRITICIZED`.

Thus the paper remained **contestable specialist scholarship**, not merely a historical curiosity.

## V. 2024: the paper still appears in a live doctrinal dispute genealogy

A recent monograph, *The Theory of the Four Stages of Liberation in Pāli Literature* (2024), explicitly places Oldenberg (1882), Lovejoy (1898), Masefield (1979), Huzita (1988) and Hwang (2006) in the interpretive history of `upādi`, summarizes Lovejoy's position and cites pp.126–136.

Public text witness recovered in the sweep:

https://dokumen.pub/the-theory-of-the-four-stages-of-liberation-in-pli-literature-1nbsped-9783031558726-9783031558733.html

The publication is Springer-era contemporary scholarship; use the public witness as discovery/control and cite the formal edition if this enters publication-facing apparatus.

> **As late as 2024, Lovejoy 1898 remained legible to a specialist author as one historical position in a still-discussed Pāli doctrinal problem.**

## VII. Dual afterlife model

The existing blog currently distinguishes surviving biographical knowledge from the less visible scholarly work inside the biographical fact. The web sweep permits a more precise model.

### Specialist Buddhist route

`JAOS 1898 specialist article`
-> `JPTS 1902–03 Buddhist Bibliography`
-> `Thomas 1933 technical citation`
-> `Gethin modern reference`
-> `Hwang 2006 reconstruction/critique`
-> `2024 doctrinal genealogy`
-> `current Buddhist digital-library record`.

### Lovejoy / history-of-ideas route

Existing repo evidence independently shows later Lovejoy classification increasingly organized around:

- philosopher;
- history-of-ideas programme;
- `unit-ideas`;
- *The Great Chain of Being*;
- JHI founding/institutional history;
- later contextualist critique and reassessment.

These routes need not be forced into one reception history.

The stronger historical proposition is:

> **Lovejoy's Buddhist work did not simply disappear and later return. It survived in at least two differently organized scholarly memories: as a specialist technical contribution inside Buddhist studies and as an early episode in a career increasingly canonized through other philosophical and historiographical objects.**

This gives empirical content to:

> `The formation was known but differently classified.`
