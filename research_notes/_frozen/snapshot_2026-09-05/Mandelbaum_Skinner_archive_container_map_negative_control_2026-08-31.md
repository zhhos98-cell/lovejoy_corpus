# Mandelbaum ↔ Skinner 1966–69 archive-container map and negative control

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT DRAFT-FEEDBACK EDGE SECURE / NAMED-PERSON ARCHIVE SEARCH MOSTLY NEGATIVE / PROVENANCE ROUTE NARROWED**

## Core result

Skinner 1969 directly proves that Maurice Mandelbaum sent critical comments on earlier drafts of `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas`. The open problem is no longer whether the manuscript crossed that social/intellectual boundary, but where the surviving evidence of the exchange is housed and whether any specific revision can be assigned to Mandelbaum.

A targeted public archive sweep now rules out several obvious containers as likely main repositories for that 1966–69 exchange.

The resulting rule is:

`DIRECT ACKNOWLEDGMENT OF DRAFT COMMENTS`
!=
`SURVIVING COMMENTS LOCATED IN THE COMMENTER'S NAMED PAPERS`.

This is a useful negative archival result because it prevents repeated searches of small or chronologically irrelevant collections.

---

## I. Maurice Mandelbaum named collections: publicly visible holdings are too small / wrong in scope

### Dartmouth — Maurice Mandelbaum papers, MS-777

Direct finding-aid control:

- dates: 1954–1983;
- extent: only **0.25 linear feet / one box**;
- contents: typescript drafts of three essays, incomplete draft of *History, Man and Reason*, and correspondence with Philip D. and Patricia Curtin.

The public scope does not list general professional correspondence, Skinner, *History and Theory*, or 1960s manuscript-referee files.

This does not prove Skinner material is absent from every sheet in the box, but it makes MS-777 a low-priority target for the present question.

### Johns Hopkins — Maurice Mandelbaum dissertation, MS-0408

Direct finding-aid control:

- collection consists of a typescript of Mandelbaum's 1936 Yale dissertation, `Historical relativism in recent philosophy of history`;
- no later professional correspondence is described.

Therefore this collection is not a plausible container for 1966–69 Skinner draft comments.

### Johns Hopkins — scattered Mandelbaum records

JHU public search surfaces scattered records involving Mandelbaum, such as Phi Beta Kappa chapter files and isolated correspondence (e.g. Nora Barlow), but no named general Mandelbaum professional papers covering the period have surfaced in the current public catalog search.

Thus:

`MANDELBAUM WAS AT JHU`
!=
`JHU PUBLIC CATALOG CURRENTLY EXPOSES HIS 1960s PROFESSIONAL CORRESPONDENCE`.

## II. Skinner correspondence containers found publicly are too late

The St Andrews James Henderson Burns papers contain a `Quentin Skinner correspondence` series, but its public dates are **1976–2007**.

The first substantial file runs March 1976–January 1979; later files extend through 2007.

These files are useful for later Skinner publication/workshop practices but cannot contain the original 1966–69 Mandelbaum exchange unless there are retrospective copies, for which there is currently no evidence.

Therefore do not repeatedly search this series for the 1969 article's initial manuscript formation.

---

## III. Richard Vann papers at Wesleyan begin too late

Wesleyan Special Collections holds Richard T. Vann papers, collection 2006-060.

Direct finding-aid control:

- dates: **1972–1986**;
- extent: 0.25 linear feet;
- contents include correspondence, AAUP files, Center for Humanities speakers, and papers about Louis Mink.

Vann was executive editor of *History and Theory* in the Skinner publication period, but the surviving named collection as publicly described begins after 1969.

Thus:

`VANN = EXECUTIVE EDITOR IN 1969`
+
`VANN PAPERS EXIST`

does not currently yield
`1966–69 SKINNER SUBMISSION FILE`.

## IV. What this leaves: institutional/editorial records and source-side correspondence

The negative controls make four provenance routes higher priority.

### A. *History and Theory* institutional/editorial records

The journal was and remains based at Wesleyan. 1969 front matter identifies the editorial structure around the article:

- George H. Nadel — editor;
- Richard T. Vann — executive editor;
- Louis O. Mink — associate editor / correspondence-and-contributions operation in the period;
- Maurice Mandelbaum — editorial committee.

The named-person papers found publicly do not supply the 1966–69 routing file. The likely archive target is therefore an institutional/journal record group, unprocessed office files, accessions not easily indexed by Google, or files still held by the journal/Wesleyan rather than an individual's named papers.

This should be asked as a precise archival query rather than another general web search:

> Do Wesleyan Special Collections or the *History and Theory* editorial office retain editorial correspondence, submission registers, referee reports, or manuscript files for vol. 8 (1969), especially Quentin Skinner's `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas`, submitted c.1966–69?

### B. Skinner source-side personal correspondence

No comprehensive public Skinner papers catalog covering 1966–69 surfaced in this pass. Later interlocutor collections can preserve Skinner correspondence, but the known Burns series starts in 1976.

The strongest clue is Adrian Blau's 2026 statement that his reconstruction draws on **previously unexamined correspondence** as well as a previously unexplored 1968 version of the essay.

Therefore Blau's chapter notes/acknowledgements are now the most efficient provenance map for the source-side correspondence.

### C. Correspondents' own papers

Skinner's final 1969 acknowledgment names readers of earlier drafts:

- F. H. Hahn;
- M. Mandelbaum;
- J. G. A. Pocock;
- J. W. Burrow;
- M. H. Black;
- J. A. Thompson;

and separately expresses particular debt to John Dunn.

If Skinner-side papers remain unavailable, correspondence may survive in one or more of these recipients' papers. Search priority should be determined by collection richness and chronological fit, not fame.

### D. Rejection / acceptance correspondence

Skinner later recalled that the essay was written in 1966–67 and was difficult to publish. A secondary transcription reports several journal rejections before *History and Theory* accepted it, but that stronger formulation remains below direct-primary level.

Target documents:

- cover letters;
- rejection letters;
- referee reports;
- revision requests;
- acceptance letter;
- correspondence around the April 1968 PSA version.

These would allow the distinction:

`S2a COLLEGIAL DRAFT CIRCULATION`
vs
`S2b EDITORIAL/REFEREE CIRCULATION`.

S2a is now direct. S2b remains OPEN.

## V. Evidence hierarchy for the Mandelbaum edge

### DIRECT

Skinner 1969 final acknowledgment: Mandelbaum sent critical comments on earlier drafts and pointed out mistakes.

### DIRECT BUT NON-SPECIFIC

Mandelbaum's multiple direct citations in Skinner 1969, including footnote 1 for field-definition control and Mandelbaum 1967 for historical narrative.

### INSTITUTIONAL DIRECT

Mandelbaum was on the *History and Theory* editorial committee in the publication period.

### OPEN

- exact draft read;
- date of Mandelbaum's comments;
- surviving letter/marginalia;
- clause-level changes caused by his comments;
- whether Mandelbaum also had a formal editorial/referee role in the manuscript's acceptance.

Critical firewall:

`DRAFT READER`
!=
`REFEREE`
!=
`EDITORIAL DECISION-MAKER`
!=
`CAUSE OF A PARTICULAR REVISION`.
