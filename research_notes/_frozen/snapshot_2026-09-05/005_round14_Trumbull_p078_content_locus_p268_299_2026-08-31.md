## 2. Trumbull p.299 — the theoretical `dead ↔ living giver` relation

In the appendix to *The Blood Covenant*, Trumbull discusses Herbert Spencer's proposed relation between primitive blood offerings to the dead and blood-covenant union.

The relevant sequence is unusually close to Lovejoy's p.78 analytical wording:

- a living person offers blood over the newly dead;
- the offering establishes or signifies a **sacred bond**;
- the blood relation can be construed as a form of **union** between giver and ghost;
- that union can imply submission, friendliness, or alliance toward the spiritual being.

This is the strongest currently identified source locus behind Lovejoy's phrase that an animal/blood rite at a tomb may be interpreted as `establishing a relation between the dead and the sacrificer`.

The likely source path is:

`HERBERT SPENCER, PRINCIPLES OF SOCIOLOGY II §364`
→ `TRUMBULL, BLOOD COVENANT, APPENDIX p.299`
→ `LOVEJOY p.78 ANALOGY / TEST`
→ `LOVEJOY WITHHOLDS AUTOMATIC COMMUNION CLASSIFICATION`.

### Attribution consequence

The broad relational analogy is not Lovejoy's invention. It is explicitly available in the Trumbull–Spencer theoretical field.

Lovejoy's observable operation is narrower:

`RELATION / UNION ANALOGY AVAILABLE`
→ ask whether the concrete rite actually warrants that interpretation
→ retain substitution, alimentary, propitiatory, magical and union mechanisms as separable.

## 3. Trumbull p.268 f. — grave + animal sacrifice morphology
