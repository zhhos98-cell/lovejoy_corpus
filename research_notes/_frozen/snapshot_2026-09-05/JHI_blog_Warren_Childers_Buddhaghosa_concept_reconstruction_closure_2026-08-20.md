Date: 2026-08-20
Status: active focused synthesis
Scope: deepen the notebook-led JHI Blog architecture by testing the Warren / Childers / Buddhaghosa layer of MS38_004_001_061_004. This note does not reopen broad source hunting. ## Core result

This run materially sharpens the case. Notebook 004 does not merely move from words to doctrines in a loose way. It repeatedly **separates different relations that can obtain among the same pieces of technical vocabulary**:

- lexical / dictionary meaning;
- classificatory inclusion;
- causal position;
- temporal succession;
- logical inclusion;
- historical provenance;
- system-level function.

The strongest new formulation is therefore:

> **The notebook treats technical vocabulary as historical evidence only after lexical meaning has been checked against textual use, causal position, logical relation, temporal relation, and the scale of the conceptual structure being reconstructed.**

This is not proto-Skinnerian contextualism. It is better described as **concept reconstruction through technical philology**: a philological practice in which the same term can occupy different logical, causal and historical relations, and no one relation is allowed automatically to decide the others.

The most important new micro-collation is the **Henry Clarke Warren 1893 → notebook 004 → Lovejoy 1898** line.

## 1. Warren 1893 is a stronger methodological source than the Blog architecture has so far exploited

Henry Clarke Warren's paper, `On the so-called Chain of Causation of the Buddhists`, was presented in the Proceedings of the American Oriental Society, April 6–8, 1893, and later printed in JAOS XVI, pp. xxvii–xxx.

A full public transcription is currently available at:

`https://buddhadust.net/dhammatalk/warren/warren-on-p-s.htm`

Bibliographic/digital controls:

- Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek object for the April 1893 Proceedings: `https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/GAGMI476SC4E55MP35TXSUOTFZAJPOBW`
- URN: `urn:nbn:de:bvb:12-bsb11543969-0`
- Google Books / Proceedings 1874–1893: `https://books.google.com/books/about/Proceedings_American_Oriental_Society_18.html?id=b0aHBipiwyUC`

Warren's methodological proposition is explicit. He objects to the Western title `Chain of Causation` because it imports overly exact Occidental categories. He insists that `paccaya` should not be flattened into one uniform relation called `cause`: Buddhaghosa's Visuddhimagga distinguishes twenty-four kinds of paccaya. Warren therefore proposes to examine each link separately and asks what kind of dependence is involved in each case. He also says that Time should have only a secondary role, and he deliberately refuses to assign fixed portions of the formula to successive lives.

Most important for Lovejoy, Warren makes one local judgement while doing this relation-by-relation reconstruction:

> `The relation, therefore, of Existence and Desire or Attachment is that of effect to cause, and that of Attachment to Desire is identity.`

Thus the same Warren paper contains both:

1. a **methodological rule**: do not assume that every adjacent link has the same kind of causal/temporal relation;
2. a **specific semantic judgement**: `upādāna` / attachment and `taṇhā` / desire stand in a relation of identity.

This becomes exceptionally useful because Lovejoy accepts the first kind of problem while rejecting the second judgement.

### Source-control caveat

The web transcription is not a substitute for an image-level transcription of printed p. xxvii–xxx. Publication-grade quotation should still be checked against the BSB/JAOS page images. But Lovejoy 1898 itself gives Warren's exact `identity` sentence and cites JAOS XVI, p. xxvii, so Lovejoy's direct access to this Warren paper is not conjectural.

## 2. Warren → 004: Lovejoy explicitly rejects the `identity` judgement from the text

Notebook 004 PDF p.22 / manuscript p.75 reads:

> `Warren, “Buddh. in Transl.,” regards relation of attachment to desire as A. of “identity.” But not so if we are to follow F. present text.`

Lovejoy then immediately compares the **four upādānas** with the **six sorts of taṇhā/desire**, using Buddhaghosa / Vibhaṅga material.

This is an unusually clean authorial hinge:

`Warren: attachment : desire = identity`

→ `Lovejoy: “But not so if we are to follow the present text”`

→ classify four attachments against six desires

→ infer that adjacency and lexical proximity do not establish conceptual identity.

The important operation is not merely disagreement with Warren. Lovejoy subjects Warren's proposition to a **classification test**. A relation Warren calls identity fails when the canonical subdivisions of the two terms are compared.

### Bibliographic correction from notebook to print

The notebook shorthand attributes the proposition to `Warren, “Buddh. in Transl.”`.

Lovejoy's 1898 article is more exact:

- footnote 1 cites Warren, *Buddhism in Translations*, p.189, for Buddhaghosa's ordinary `attachment` meaning;
- footnote 2 cites **JAOS XVI, p. xxvii** for the sentence `the relation of desire to attachment is that of identity.`

This should not be overinterpreted as a chronology of drafting, but it is valuable Quellenforschung evidence that the notebook's abbreviated source attribution and the published bibliographic control are not identical. The printed article identifies the earlier Warren paper as the precise source of the `identity` proposition.

## 3. 004 → 1898: the rejected identity becomes a functional distinction

Lovejoy's 1898 article does not merely say that `taṇhā` and `upādāna` are different words. It reconstructs the difference in **function**.

The published position is:

- `taṇhā` = particular cravings / desires;
- `upādāna` = the more chronic condition of will produced by those cravings, the habitual identification of will and interests with the skandhas.

Thus the notebook's local move:

`four attachments ≠ six desires`

is stabilized in print as:

`particular desire ≠ chronic volitional attachment`.

This is a true Warren → 004 → 1898 closure at proposition level. It is especially valuable for the Blog because it can be explained without long doctrinal exposition: one source says two adjacent concepts are identical; the notebook writes `But not so if we are to follow the present text`; the article turns that objection into two differentiated causal/psychological roles.

## 4. Warren's broader methodological warning and Lovejoy's later temporal architecture

The same Warren 1893 paper argues that the twelve links should not all be interpreted through one homogeneous sense of `cause` and that Time should have only a secondary role. Warren deliberately refuses to allocate fixed blocks of the series to successive existences.

Lovejoy's 1898 paper, while reconstructing the chain differently, retains the methodological caution:

> the formula does **not** express a `strict temporal sequence`.

He then nevertheless gives a broad 1–2 / 3–9 / 10–12 past-present-future distribution.

Existing Batch48 has already shown that this temporal architecture is a recomposition of several available resources rather than a Lovejoy invention:

- Oldenberg line: `bhava` on the renewal / continued-existence side;
- Hardy: upādāna vs karma as different causes of renewed existence;
- Warren: different relations among links, time not uniformly controlling;
- Lovejoy: one assembled temporal-causal configuration.

The new point is that **Warren's methodological principle and Warren's mistaken `identity` judgement are in the same directly read paper**. Lovejoy can therefore retain the demand to discriminate kinds of relation while changing Warren's answer for one pair of links.

This is stronger than a generic claim of `influence`:

> **Lovejoy selectively appropriates an inferential discipline from the very source whose local semantic conclusion he rejects.**

Do not claim direct verbal dependence of Lovejoy's `not strict temporal sequence` sentence on Warren until the original p. xxvii–xxx is image-controlled. The conceptual contact is secure; the sentence-level transmission remains unproved.

## 5. Buddhaghosa is not simply an authority: he becomes evidence for separating relations

The Warren micro-collation also clarifies the function of Buddhaghosa in 004 and 1898.

Buddhaghosa supplies:

- the conventional attachment/clinging gloss for `upādāna`;
- classifications of desire and attachment;
- the larger Visuddhimagga apparatus in which different kinds of `paccaya` can be distinguished.

Lovejoy does not treat any one gloss as sufficient to settle identity. In 004 he uses Buddhaghosa's subdivisions to **disaggregate** Warren's identity claim. In 1898 he can accept Buddhaghosa's ordinary attachment meaning while still arguing that attachment has a distinct causal/psychological function from desire.

Thus:

> **same lexical gloss does not settle same causal function.**

This is exactly the kind of operator the Blog needs: a dictionary/commentarial equivalence is one proposition; system-level identity is another.

## 6. Childers supplies a second axis: dictionary/classificatory material versus logical and temporal position

Notebook 004 copies Childers extensively on the five khandhas. Childers's dictionary entry gives:

- the five khandhas as `elements or attributes of being`;
- their component subdivisions;
- their destruction and regeneration through kamma;
- khandha as aggregate/collection.

The notebook then moves beyond simple copying. On PDF p.42 / manuscript p.123 it writes:

> `Viññāṇa is temporally (?) an antecedent of nāmarūpa, and logically a subdivision of it.`

This is analytically important because the **same concept occupies two different relations depending on the axis of analysis**:

- in the dependent-origination sequence, viññāṇa precedes nāmarūpa;
- in a logical/classificatory decomposition of the person, consciousness belongs within the mental side of nāmarūpa / the khandha structure.

The current Childers controls establish the upstream classifications, but no exact Childers wording has been located that supplies Lovejoy's `temporally ... logically` distinction. Until such a source is found, this should be treated as a strong authorial synthesis, not awarded an absolute priority claim.

A nearby 004 passage, PDF p.50 / manuscript p.139, makes the same issue explicit at a more general level. Against the simple argument from duplicated khandhas, the corrected transcription reads in substance:

> `For me, the difficulty is not first of all duplication but the relation among terms that precede one another temporally as well as logically.`

The reading is lower confidence than p.42, so the Blog should quote p.42 if a verbatim manuscript sentence is needed. But together the two passages show that **temporal relation and logical inclusion were consciously differentiated inside the notebook**.

## 7. This materially strengthens the `concept reconstruction through technical philology` reading

The combined Warren / Buddhaghosa / Childers evidence makes the notebook's operation more precise than `close reading` or `contextualism`.

004 repeatedly treats the following as separable claims:

1. what a technical term is glossed as;
2. how it is subdivided in canonical/commentarial classification;
3. where it stands in a causal chain;
4. where it stands in a temporal sequence;
5. what larger logical/classificatory set contains it;
6. where the term/doctrine historically came from;
7. what explanatory function it performs in the present configuration.

This is why a safe analytical description is:

> **concept reconstruction through technical philology**.

A stronger but still defensible sentence:

> **For the young Lovejoy, a word's derivation, its dictionary gloss, its place in a causal series, its logical inclusion in a classificatory scheme, and the historical provenance of the doctrine in which it appeared were separate propositions requiring separate evidence.**

This should not be presented as a named period method or as the invention of later intellectual history. It is an operation visible in 004.

## 9. Consequence for the Blog's Quellenforschung selection

This deep run suggests a more economical Blog evidentiary sequence than the current five-scholar matrix.

### Recommended close-up A — comparison / scale

Keep a very short Garbe–Rhys Davids / `system existed as a whole` scene. Its job is to establish that the notebook begins inside a live genealogy problem and that Lovejoy already controls the scale of the historical object.

### Recommended close-up B — the main microgenesis

Make **Warren → 004 → 1898** the most legible source-transformation flash:

- Warren: attachment : desire = identity;
- notebook: `But not so if we are to follow the present text`;
- compare four attachments with six desires / Buddhaghosa;
- print: particular cravings vs chronic condition of will.

This is cleaner for a general JHI Blog reader than beginning with the full Senart controversy because the evidentiary movement is immediately visible.

### Recommended controversy expansion — Senart

Then use Senart briefly to raise the stakes from one pair of terms to the historical interpretation of the whole formula:

- composite provenance / repetition;
- Lovejoy accepts compositeness but rejects the move to systemic unintelligibility;
- borrowed terms/ideas ≠ borrowed arrangement/application.

Senart remains the strongest complete polemical three-node closure and can still be the named `actual controversy`, but he need not carry all the explanatory burden.

### Recommended coda — upādisesa / Childers–Oldenberg

Use one sentence or short paragraph:

- rival etymologies remain possible;
- meaning settled by usage rather than etymology;
- evidence `relevant but not necessarily conclusive`;
- issue not finally settled.

This closes with epistemic calibration rather than another genealogy.

## 10. Revised first-order payoff

The first-order story can now be even simpler:

> **A young Arthur Lovejoy opened a notebook on Sāṃkhya and Buddhism and began adjudicating disagreements among the textual authorities he was reading. One authority called two adjacent categories identical; Lovejoy wrote `But not so if we are to follow the present text.` Another source supplied a causal distinction that he turned into a fact/manner question. Elsewhere he separated a concept's temporal position from its logical inclusion. The 1898 article turned these notebook operations into a published reconstruction of dependent origination.**

Only after this should the Blog abstract the methodological stakes:

> **The issue was not simply what Buddhist technical terms meant. It was what kind of relation — lexical, logical, causal, temporal, or historical — a resemblance or sequence was entitled to establish.**

This gives the Blog a genuinely first-order archival spine while allowing the history-of-ideas methodological payoff to emerge from the sources.
