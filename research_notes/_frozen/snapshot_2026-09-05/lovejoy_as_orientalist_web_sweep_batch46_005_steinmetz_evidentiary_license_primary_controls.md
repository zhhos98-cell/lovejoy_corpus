# Batch 46 — Lovejoy 005 only: Steinmetz’s evidentiary license and the limit Lovejoy imposes on comparative generalization

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope lock: **MS38_004_001_061_005 only**.

## Research move

The Steinmetz line can now be sharpened beyond the earlier contrast between `many motives` and `one origin`.

The more exact historical question is:

> **What sort of evidence did Steinmetz think licensed a universal developmental conclusion, and how does Lovejoy 005 restrict that license?**

Three near-primary / contemporary controls now converge:

1. W. I. Thomas’s 1897 review says *Endokannibalismus* systematically tabulated peoples, motives, and the reliability of information in each case.
2. Thomas nevertheless summarizes Steinmetz’s causal conclusion in strongly universal terms: primitive humans were necessarily omnivorous, the inhibitions against eating human flesh had not yet developed, and superstition could only become a motive after hunger had first established the practice.
3. A. H. Keane later quotes Steinmetz’s own concluding pp.59–60 in German: `dass eine hohe Wahrscheinlichkeit dafür spricht den Endocannibalismus ... als ständige Sitte der Urmenschen, sowie der niedrigen Wilden anzunehmen`.

The evidence therefore supports a methodological distinction sharper than `Steinmetz monist / Lovejoy pluralist`:

> **Steinmetz treats a sufficiently broad, source-graded comparative corpus as warrant for reconstructing a regular primitive condition. Lovejoy 005 repeatedly requires an additional mechanism-and-history test before permitting a developmental generalization.**

## 1. Steinmetz’s apparatus was already source-critical

W. I. Thomas, reviewing the 1896 study in *American Journal of Sociology* 2 (1897), pp.610–611, describes a table containing:

- tribes practising endocannibalism;
- motives assigned to the practice;
- reliability of the information in each case.

This matters because the contrast with Lovejoy should not caricature Steinmetz as someone who merely grouped outwardly similar rites. His evidence-management procedure already recognized:

- heterogeneous motives;
- unequal evidentiary quality;
- contrary cases and uncertain reports.

Thomas explicitly praises Steinmetz for exposing large bodies of difficult ethnological evidence instead of selecting only supporting facts.

## 2. But the evidentiary apparatus licenses a universal developmental reconstruction

Thomas then summarizes Steinmetz’s causal model.

The main negative conditions for cannibalism include absence of:

- aesthetic horror of the corpse;
- fear of retaliation by the disturbed spirit;
- imaginative sympathy with the corpse;
- concern that eating dishonours/defiles the person eaten.

Thomas reports Steinmetz’s further claim that primitive man must have been omnivorous and could not afford to reject suitable food. The deterrents characteristic of later/civilized life were originally absent.

Most important, Thomas reports the temporal-causal priority claim:

> superstition could not have produced cannibalism unless periodic hunger had already given rise to the practice.

The structure is therefore not simply `one cause` but a particular **license from distribution to prehistory**:

> broad comparative distribution + recurrent motive/condition pattern + source reliability grading
> → inference to a regular primitive state
> → later motives such as animistic/superstitious explanations are secondary historical accretions.

## 3. Steinmetz’s pp.59–60 conclusion is independently recoverable through a direct quotation

A. H. Keane, *Man Past and Present*, quotes Steinmetz’s concluding pages directly and gives the source as pp.59–60:

> `dass eine hohe Wahrscheinlichkeit dafür spricht den Endocannibalismus ... als ständige Sitte der Urmenschen, sowie der niedrigen Wilden anzunehmen`

In paraphrase: Steinmetz regarded the evidence as yielding a high probability that endocannibalism was a regular custom of primitive humanity and of the lowest-ranked contemporary peoples.

This is not yet a facsimile of Steinmetz pp.59–60, but it is stronger than a modern summary because it preserves Steinmetz’s German wording and exact page locus in a near-contemporary secondary witness.

## 4. An additional source confirms that the 1896 work was a 60-page original/offprint

The contemporary *Monist* bibliographic notice gives:

- Rudolf S. Steinmetz, *Endokannibalismus*;
- Vienna: Anthropologische Gesellschaft;
- 1896;
- 60 pages.

The American Journal of Sociology notice identifies it as reprinted from *Mittheilungen der anthropologischen Gesellschaft in Wien*, vol. XXVI.

Later bibliographies also record the article as MAGW XXVI (1896), pp.1–60. A 1928 collected reprint appears in Steinmetz, *Gesammelte kleinere Schriften zur Ethnologie und Soziologie*, vol. I, beginning around p.132 (modern bibliographic reports vary on the terminal page, 260/271; do not use the reprint pagination without direct inspection).

## 5. Lovejoy 005 uses a narrower evidentiary license

The notebook’s contrast is clearest when the early source-critical pages and later anthropophagy pages are read together.

### Early future-life block

005 asks whether an apparent moral distinction in afterlife fate might instead result from:

- manner of death;
- ritual/taboo violation;
- clan relation;
- social exclusion;
- missionary contamination;
- later moralization.

A widespread or repeated distinction does not automatically establish one original moral cause.

### Sacrifice/cannibalism block

005 later separates:

- famine;
- revenge;
- affection/kin relation;
- funerary provision;
- acquisition of qualities;
- sympathetic efficacy;
- propitiation;
- sacramental union.

Ceremoniality, repetition, publicity, or distribution do not identify the mechanism by themselves.

### Selected-organ transfer

At PDF pp.108 and 119, a highly recurrent comparative form — eating flesh or a selected body part — is classified by the property thought to be transmitted. The mechanism can be sympathetic/transferable efficacy rather than communion.

Thus Lovejoy’s working rule is effectively:

> distribution/form/motive report
> → source criticism
> → mechanism discrimination
> → historical-layer test
> → only then developmental inference.

## 6. The difference is therefore about evidentiary warrant, not evolution versus anti-evolution

This formulation avoids a misleading opposition.

Lovejoy does make developmental arguments. 005 repeatedly reconstructs:

- later reinterpretations of older funerary practices;
- syncretic fusion of originally distinct sacrifices;
- moralization of older non-moral eschatologies;
- changes in the social/economic conditions under which human sacrifice declines.

The difference is that **formal recurrence or wide distribution is not sufficient warrant for a universal primitive origin**. The notebook requires the mechanism generating the form to be identified and permits several mechanisms to converge upon the same visible rite.

This is the stronger methodological contrast with Steinmetz.

## 7. A useful later control: Deniker separates behavioural class from efficacy mechanism

Joseph Deniker’s *The Races of Man* (1900) provides a non-genealogical control. In discussing cannibalism, he first treats the belief that one acquires the qualities of a person by consuming the whole body, heart, eyes, liver, blood, etc., and separately discusses Steinmetz’s comparative category of endocannibalism.

This cannot be used to claim Lovejoy read Deniker. Its value is taxonomic: around 1900 it was already possible to separate:

- **behavioural/morphological class:** endocannibalism;
- **causal mechanism:** acquisition/transfer of qualities.

005 pushes this separation harder by refusing to let the transfer mechanism automatically count as communion.

## 8. What remains blocked

Two Steinmetz primary loci remain the highest-value retrieval targets:

1. **p.52, §26**, cited by Mauss in his discussion of endocannibalism / clan solidarity;
2. **pp.59–60**, the conclusion quoted by Keane.

A direct scan of these pages would upgrade the Steinmetz column from contemporary/near-primary synopsis to proposition-level primary collation.

The Marillier review, RHR 34 (1896), pp.113–115, remains equally important because it can show whether Marillier accepted Steinmetz’s evidentiary license, challenged the universal reconstruction, or redistributed the relevant cases differently.

## 9. Strongest safe formulation

> **Steinmetz’s *Endokannibalismus* was methodologically sophisticated at the level of evidence: contemporary reviewers emphasize its comparative table of cases, motives, and source reliability. Yet the work used that corpus to license a strong reconstruction of primitive humanity, culminating in the claim that endocannibalism was probably a regular custom of early humans and the lowest-ranked contemporary peoples. Lovejoy’s 005 notebook retains the dense comparative and source-critical apparatus while narrowing what such evidence can establish: recurrent forms must first be decomposed by causal mechanism and historical layer, and several mechanisms may produce the same outward rite.**
