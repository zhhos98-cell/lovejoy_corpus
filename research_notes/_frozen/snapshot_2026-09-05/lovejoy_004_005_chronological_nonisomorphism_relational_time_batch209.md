# Lovejoy 004 → 005 — chronological non-isomorphism and relation-specific historical time (Batch 209)

Date: 2026-08-26  
Status: synced  
Scope: notebooks `MS38_004_001_061_004` and `MS38_004_001_061_005`, with 1898 Lovejoy print and Söderblom 1901 as bounded controls.  
Chronological rule: ordinary forward tracing stops at **1910**; later material enters only for explicit Buddhism / India / Sanskrit nodes.

## Core result

Batches 54, 73 and 156 already established that notebooks 004 and 005 share a recurring discipline of relation-type discrimination, proof-level separation and diagnostic reclassification. Batch 209 does **not** repeat that claim. It isolates a different consequence:

> **the relations Lovejoy separates do not share one common historical clock.**

Notebook 004 repeatedly allows the same term, doctrine or system to occupy different positions on different axes:

- something can be temporally antecedent yet logically included in what follows;
- present components can have different historical origins while belonging to one current doctrinal configuration;
- historically `derived` elements can nevertheless perform central present functions;
- a formula can be composite and non-homogeneous in temporal sequence while remaining technically intelligible;
- borrowed terms or even ideas can receive a new arrangement and application that defines the present system.

Notebook 005 transfers an analogous temporal problem into comparative religion. A single present religious configuration can contain older non-moral machinery, later ethical interpretation, imported categories, surviving naturalistic relations and ritual forms of different genealogies. `Primitive` can therefore name an **earlier relation inside a later object**, not merely the globally earlier position of an entire people.

The strongest formulation is:

> **004 and 005 exhibit chronological non-isomorphism: historical provenance, temporal sequence, logical inclusion, present function and classificatory status need not line up. Once these coordinates are allowed to diverge, a doctrine, religion or rite can no longer be assigned one homogeneous historical age. Historical time moves inside the object.**

`chronological non-isomorphism`, `relation-specific historical time`, and `historical time moves inside the object` are project analytical terms, not Lovejoy’s vocabulary.

---

## 1. What is new here after the existing 004 relation inventory

The 004 relation inventory already demonstrated systematic distinctions among:

- lexical identity;
- logical inclusion;
- causal dependence;
- temporal succession;
- textual attestation;
- historical derivation;
- system-level function.

Its main rule was:

> one established relation does not automatically settle another.

Batch 209 asks what happens **to chronology** once that rule is taken seriously.

The answer is stronger than `Lovejoy distinguishes different relations`. If relation-types are not reducible to each other, neither are their temporal positions. The same component can be:

- earlier historically;
- later in textual organization;
- included logically inside another category;
- causally prior in one sequence;
- synchronically central to the present system.

Thus there is no single scalar coordinate called `the age of the idea/system` that can absorb all the relations.

This is the temporal consequence that had not yet been isolated explicitly in the repository.

---

## 2. 004 PDF p.42 / MS p.123: one term occupies two incompatible-looking coordinates

The cleanest manuscript statement is the `viññāṇa / nāmarūpa` note.

Lovejoy’s corrected notebook reads:

> `Viññāṇa is temporally (?) antecedent to nāmarūpa and logically a subdivision of it.`

The question mark after `temporally` matters and should remain in publication quotation.

This is more than a distinction between temporal and logical relation. It demonstrates **non-isomorphism** directly:

- temporal axis: `viññāṇa` precedes `nāmarūpa`;
- logical/classificatory axis: `viññāṇa` belongs inside `nāmarūpa`.

The same item therefore cannot be represented adequately by one linear position.

A tabular chronology would be forced to choose `before` or `inside`. Lovejoy’s working page preserves both by assigning them to different relations.

This is a particularly important bridge to the larger project because it shows, before Paris, a concrete case in which **“in advance” has more than one meaning**. Temporal antecedence and logical subordination are simultaneously true propositions about the same conceptual object.

---

## 3. 004 PDF p.45: non-exclusive classifications prevent one-to-one temporal staging

The saṅkhāra page explicitly records:

> `52 divisions not mutually exclusive.`

The primary problem is classificatory rather than historical. Yet its temporal consequence is important.

If classifications overlap, then a later historical reconstruction cannot safely treat each category as a mutually exclusive developmental stage. One item can belong to more than one analytical class at once, and recurrence of a term or quality does not prove identity of role.

This is not evidence that Lovejoy consciously theorized non-linear chronology. It is evidence that his working object already resists a one-item/one-position grid.

The graphic and tabular apparatus of 004 consequently permits simultaneous memberships whose historical relations must be reconstructed separately.

---

## 4. 004 pp.49–52: composite historical provenance does not determine present configuration

The Senart block is the strongest system-level case.

Lovejoy concedes several historical facts:

- the nidāna formula is composite;
- its terms are not all original to Buddhism;
- objective and subjective elements are mingled;
- the sequence is not homogeneous as strict temporal order;
- individual notions can have Upaniṣadic or Sāṃkhya-Yoga antecedents.

Yet he rejects the further conclusion that the current arrangement is accidental, unintelligible or merely a heap of survivals.

This creates a clean separation between two times:

### Component time

When and where did each notion arise? Which elements are inherited, borrowed or secondary?

### Configuration time

What relation do those components have **after** they have been arranged inside Buddhism’s technical system?

The present configuration can therefore be younger than some of its components while not being reducible to the chronology of those components.

That is chronological non-isomorphism at system scale:

> **age of component ≠ age/identity/function of configuration.**

---

## 5. 004 closure: `original` and `derived` elements coexist inside one functioning system

The notebook closes its historical-structural analysis with an explicit partition:

### Original elements

- flux / Three Characteristics;
- Dependent Origination.

### Derived elements

- theory of sense perception;
- psychology;
- khandhas.

Whatever exact historical confidence should be assigned to each allocation, the form of the reconstruction is unmistakable. Buddhism is not assigned a single historical age. Its present intellectual equipment is **stratified internally by provenance**.

This becomes still more important when compared with the 1898 article. The article retains Buddhist psychology and khandha material as central to interpreting the system even though the notebook labels them historically `derived`.

Therefore:

> **historically derived ≠ synchronically peripheral.**

A later/imported element can become central in the current configuration.

This blocks another common shortcut:

`historical priority`  
`≠ explanatory priority in the present system`.

---

## 6. Lovejoy 1898 makes the provenance/configuration split explicit in published prose

The 1898 JAOS article provides the secure authorial endpoint.

Against Senart, Lovejoy grants that the terms, and perhaps even the ideas, of dependent origination are largely borrowed. Yet he insists that their **arrangement and application** are original and characteristic.

This sentence is decisive because it establishes in published Lovejoy prose that:

- component provenance can be external/earlier;
- present systematic identity can be internally constituted/later;
- historical borrowing does not determine present meaning or configuration.

The article therefore converts the notebook’s layered provenance into an explicit proposition:

> **the historical age/source of an ingredient and the historical identity of the configuration in which it is used are different questions.**

Again, this is not yet mature history-of-ideas vocabulary. It is a philological/systemic solution to a live Buddhist problem.

---

## 7. 004 also contains distinct internal Buddhist temporalities

The later Nirvāṇa notes add another form of non-synchronism.

Lovejoy distinguishes tendencies inside Buddhism itself:

- one philosophical tendency sharpens the original doctrine of impermanence;
- another, described as popularizing and later, tones down Nirvāṇa and tends toward mystical / Vedāntic reinterpretation.

The exact historical reconstruction is contestable and should not be treated as established Buddhist history. Its evidentiary importance lies in the form of Lovejoy’s model:

> **one tradition can contain different historical tendencies moving in different doctrinal directions.**

The tradition is therefore internally diachronic rather than a single contemporaneous doctrinal block.

This point is structurally homologous to 005’s later moral overlays, imported strata and fused cult complexes.

## 8. 005 transforms philological stratification into religious/ritual stratification

Batch 208 showed that 005 contains two temporal grammars: ranked civilizational development and relation-specific stratification.

The second grammar now looks especially clear when placed beside 004.

### 005 eschatology

- manner-of-death / ritual / social allocation can predate moral judgment;
- older journey, bridge, trial and hell-like machinery can later be ethically reinterpreted;
- borrowed moralized eschatology can coexist with animistic-naturalistic and non-moral ideas.

### 005 pantheon / cult

- natural/local divinities, ancestors, dead chiefs and tribal gods can have different historical origins;
- later identification can fuse them into one current pantheon;
- `more primitive naturalism` can survive inside a later ancestor-dominated configuration.

### 005 sacrifice

- a present ritual complex can contain funerary, alimentary, magical, propitiatory, fecundative or sacramental elements with different histories;
- outward unity does not imply one historical origin;
- independent chronology can veto a neat developmental sequence, as on p.103.

Thus the object changes from a doctrinal formula to ritual/religious complexes, but the temporal problem is recognizably similar:

> **the present whole contains components whose historical clocks do not coincide.**

---

## 9. `Greek primitive / N.T. intermediate` becomes more intelligible after 004

The 005 teaching note

> `Greek primitive / N.T. intermediate`

should not be read merely as a stray broad evolutionary label.

004 already supplied a mode of historical analysis in which a canonical tradition can contain components of different provenance and historical status. The 1905 teaching vocabulary now makes that internal temporalization explicit at a wider comparative scale: `primitive` and `intermediate` can be predicated of layers within Greek / Christian textual history.

The strongest safe inference is:

> **The category `primitive` is portable because the historical unit has already fragmented below the level of whole civilizations or whole traditions.**

One can ask for the `primitive` layer of Greek eschatology or of a causal representation without claiming that the entirety of Greek culture occupies the same primitive position.

This helps explain why `primitive` can expand its analytical reach even when a simple people-by-people chronology proves inadequate.

## 10. Söderblom 1901 shows that this non-synchronized temporal grammar was live in the same Paris field

Direct primary control from Batch 207 matters here as context, not transmission proof.

Söderblom explicitly says:

- his classificatory scheme is not an a priori system;
- it contains several distinct and **parallel evolutions**;
- these evolutions can **coexist within the same religion**;
- retribution does not appear at one identical `degree of civilization` among all peoples;
- Israel prevents an evolution conceived as always equal or `pour ainsi dire mécanique`.

This supplies a contemporary vocabulary for precisely the temporal structure now visible in Lovejoy 005.

The safe historical statement is:

> **The Marillier milieu made evolutionary language compatible with asynchronous, internally coexisting developments.**

Do not claim Söderblom taught this rule to Lovejoy or that Lovejoy read the 1901 book before every relevant 005 entry.

## 11. The 004 → 005 relation is not simply `philology becomes anthropology`

The stronger sequence is:

### 004

One philosophical system contains:

- historically prior/borrowed elements;
- present systematic relations;
- temporal and logical orders that diverge;
- non-exclusive classifications;
- original and derived strata.

### 005

One religious/ritual complex contains:

- older and later interpretations;
- imported and local elements;
- different causal mechanisms;
- superimposed cults;
- `primitive`, intermediate and later relations that need not synchronize at the level of whole peoples.

The continuity is therefore not one doctrine or one taxonomy. It is an increasingly portable capacity to represent **historical heterochrony inside a present configuration**.

`heterochrony` is useful analytically but should be handled cautiously because it is modern project vocabulary. `Chronological non-isomorphism` is more exact for the documentary claim: the different analytical axes simply do not map one-to-one onto one chronology.

## 12. Larger implication for the `primitive` project

This result provides a concrete mechanism for the larger thesis about post-unified chronology.

A single world-historical table assumes that historical objects can be assigned positions as wholes. But 004 and 005 repeatedly generate objects that cannot be dated that way:

- the same concept is before another temporally and inside it logically;
- a later system contains older borrowed concepts;
- derived components become central to present function;
- one religion contains parallel developments;
- one rite contains layers with different genealogies;
- an ancient/classical tradition contains a `primitive` stratum.

Once this happens, chronology is reorganized by **internal stratification**.

The historical question changes from:

> `Where does this people / religion / system stand on one timeline?`

into:

> `Which relation inside this present configuration is earlier, later, inherited, overlaid, logically prior, causally operative, or historically secondary?`

This is one plausible technical function of `primitive` in the emerging human sciences:

> **it provides an address for an earlier relation that remains present inside a historically later object.**

That formulation should be presented as a project-level reconstruction, not as a sentence Lovejoy himself wrote.

---

## 13. Political consequence

The move from whole-object chronology to internal stratification does not dissolve hierarchy. It changes where hierarchy can operate.

Whole peoples can still be ranked through `higher stage`, `more developed peoples`, or conventional evolutionist language. But once the analytical object is internally stratified, the category can also be applied **below the level of the people**:

- a rite can have a primitive mechanism;
- a canonical tradition can have a primitive layer;
- a moral system can preserve a primitive non-moral structure;
- a modern/later religion can contain relations assigned to different historical strata.

This potentially makes `primitive` more mobile and more invasive. It no longer requires the historian to classify an entire society as temporally behind; it can identify an earlier layer anywhere inside a present cultural formation.

The political history should therefore track two distinct operations:

1. **external staging** of peoples/civilizations;
2. **internal temporalization** of practices, concepts and traditions.

005 provides evidence for both.

That distinction may be more useful than treating `primitive` simply as an ideological contradiction between universal humanity and hierarchy.

## 15. Strongest current paper formulation

> **The bridge between Lovejoy’s Buddhist and comparative-religion notebooks is not merely that both distinguish different kinds of relation. Those relations also carry different times. In 004, consciousness can be temporally antecedent to name-and-form while logically included within it; the dependent-origination formula can be historically composite without losing present systematic intelligibility; and historically derived psychology can remain central to the functioning Buddhist configuration. Lovejoy’s 1898 article makes the principle explicit by allowing borrowed terms, perhaps even borrowed ideas, to acquire an original and characteristic arrangement and application. In 005 the same temporal non-isomorphism appears at the scale of rites and beliefs: older journey structures acquire later moral uses, imported eschatologies coexist with naturalistic survivals, distinct cults become superimposed, and a present sacrifice can contain elements with different histories. The result is a chronology that no longer assigns one age to one object. Historical time is distributed across relations within the object itself.**

Project-level compact formulation:

> **004 separates kinds of relation; 005 makes their different historical clocks visible. `Primitive` becomes one way of addressing those asynchronous strata.**

The second sentence is analytical compression, not actor vocabulary.
