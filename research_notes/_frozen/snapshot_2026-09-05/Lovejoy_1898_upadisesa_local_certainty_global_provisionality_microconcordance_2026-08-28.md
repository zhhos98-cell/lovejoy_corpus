# Lovejoy 1898 — upādisesa microconcordance: local certainty, global provisionality

## Core result

The notebook-to-print transformation in the `upādisesa` argument is more precise than either of two simple models:

- `private uncertainty → public certainty`;
- `private certainty → public hedging`.

The direct concordance instead shows **nested confidence by evidentiary scale**.

Lovejoy remains highly confident about the semantic force of particular passages, while refusing to let those locally strong readings become a universally settled lexical/doctrinal conclusion when contrary textual evidence remains.

Best shorthand:

> **local witness certainty ≠ corpus-level closure**.

Or analytically:

`C_local passage = high`

while

`C_general meaning across corpus = provisional`.

This is analyst notation, not Lovejoy's.

## I. Manuscript MS p.173 / PDF p.66 — strong local semantic judgment

Notebook 004 gives a direct worked judgment on `anupādisesa`.

The high-confidence corrected transcription reads in substance:

> `No one familiar with Buddhist modes of thought could suppose that anupādisesa here meant the extinction of the skandhas, i.e. physical death.`

Lovejoy then argues that for a being freed from desire physical life/death is indifferent and that making death itself the *summum bonum* would be profoundly un-Buddhist.

The notebook conclusion is explicitly strong:

> `Clearly, the word anupādisesa here means the moral condition of being freed from attachment.`

It then generalizes over the three *Sutta-Nipāta* passages under examination:

> in these three passages `upādisesa` **clearly** does not refer to persistence of the five skandhas but to an ethical condition; positively it characterizes the anāgāmin with residual impurity, negatively the Arahat, before or after physical death.

### Epistemic status

At the level of these passages:

- semantic relation: high confidence;
- physical-residue interpretation: rejected;
- ethical-residue interpretation: asserted.

There is no manuscript question mark attached to this local conclusion.

This is not a trajectory from confidence to caution at the local-witness scale.

## III. Print p.134 — a higher-scale evidentiary firewall is added

Before presenting the local passages, Lovejoy explicitly distinguishes etymological possibility from semantic decision.

Printed p.134:

- both derivations `seem to be etymologically possible`;
- therefore the meaning of `upādi` must be settled by **use**, not by etymological argument alone;
- Lovejoy can contribute only a few points `relevant but not necessarily conclusive`.

This introduces a higher-order distinction:

`etymological admissibility`
≠ `semantic determination`;

and:

`strong local evidence`
≠ `sufficient corpus-wide evidence`.

The phrase `relevant but not necessarily conclusive` does not weaken every individual interpretation in advance. It limits the inferential scope of the selected witnesses as evidence for the general problem.

## IV. Print p.136 — contrary witnesses block global closure

At the end of the article Lovejoy explicitly reopens the aggregate evidentiary question.

He says, in substance:

- the oldest and probably most numerous texts point toward the Oldenberg-type interpretation;
- other passages can be cited for the more usual view;
- therefore `the matter cannot be regarded as finally settled`.

The structure is:

`several locally decisive/strong readings`
+
`preponderance of early/numerous evidence`
+
`surviving contrary witnesses`
→
`no final corpus-wide closure`.

This is not inconsistency. It is an evidentiary scale distinction.

## V. Confidence is nested by scale

The microconcordance supports at least four levels.

### Level 1 — passage-level semantic judgment

Question:

> What does `anupādisesa` mean **here**?

Lovejoy confidence: **high** (`Clearly` / `Plainly`).

### Level 2 — small local cluster

Question:

> What does the term do in these three *Sutta-Nipāta* passages?

Lovejoy confidence: **high**; ethical residue is consistently identified.

### Level 3 — broader distributional/preponderance claim

Question:

> Which interpretation is supported by the oldest / most numerous texts?

Lovejoy confidence: **qualified positive** (`probably`, preponderance rather than universality).

### Level 4 — universal/general lexical conclusion

Question:

> Has the meaning of the term been finally settled across all relevant evidence?

Lovejoy confidence: **withheld** because contrary passages survive.

Thus:

`C_passage`
≠ `C_cluster`
≠ `C_preponderance`
≠ `C_universal closure`.

## VI. The actual private→print transformation

The best model is now not simply `question marks become hedges`.

Three simultaneous operations occur.

### 1. Local certainty survives

A strong manuscript judgment can remain strong or nearly verbatim in print.

### 2. Scope is made explicit

The publication tells the reader what the local witnesses are entitled to prove and what they are not entitled to prove.

### 3. Counterevidence is promoted to the metalevel

Contrary passages do not necessarily overturn the local readings. They block the **upgrade** from strong local interpretation to final general settlement.

This is a direct instance of inferential jurisdiction:

> **certainty at a lower evidentiary scale does not automatically migrate upward.**

## VIII. Consequence for `scale → relation → warrant`

The current governing model becomes more exact:

> **choose the scale → type the relation → assess the warrant at that scale → control any attempted upgrade to a wider scale or stronger relation.**

The final step matters.

The recurring Lovejoyian problem is often not whether proposition P is supported. It is whether P can legitimately be **promoted**:

- passage → corpus;
- resemblance → identity;
- identity → genealogy;
- contact → dependence;
- component → whole complex;
