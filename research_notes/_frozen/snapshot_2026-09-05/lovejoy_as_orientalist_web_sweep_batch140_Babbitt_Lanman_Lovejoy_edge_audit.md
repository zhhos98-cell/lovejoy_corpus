# Batch 140 — Babbitt–Lanman–Lovejoy edge audit: the Harvard transmission topology is now concrete, but the 1890s interpersonal edge is still absent

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: continue Batch139 by testing whether Babbitt's Harvard return can be connected more tightly to Charles Rockwell Lanman's Indianist teaching and whether any direct Babbitt–Lovejoy relation is digitally visible before 1900.

## Core result

The Babbitt→Lanman edge can now be strengthened considerably. A.H. Dakin's biography of Paul Elmer More states that More and Irving Babbitt first became close in **autumn 1892**, when the two of them formed the **entire enrollment of one of Professor Lanman's classes**. The Rutgers Database of Classical Scholars independently describes them as fellow graduate students of **Sanskrit, Pāli, and Indian philosophy** at Harvard. More then served as **assistant in Sanskrit to Lanman in 1894–95**.

A contemporary Harvard Crimson notice supplies an institutional control for the latter point: in announcing 1895–96 courses, it says that Lanman would again have charge of the Sanskrit courses that had been conducted the previous year by More.

This sharpens the Harvard topology:

`Babbitt → Paris EPHE / Lévi / Pāli-Sanskrit`

→ `Babbitt returns Harvard`

→ `Babbitt + More in Lanman class, 1892`

→ `More assists/teaches Sanskrit under Lanman, 1894–95`

→ `Lanman resumes Sanskrit courses, 1895–96`

→ `Lovejoy studies Sanskrit and Buddhist sacred texts under Lanman, 1896–97`.

What remains missing is the one edge that would turn this topology into a transmission narrative:

`Babbitt → Lovejoy`.

No pre-1900 letter, enrollment record, advising record, or contemporary notice linking the two has surfaced in the current digital sweep. Their later intellectual relation is real and reciprocal — Lovejoy reviewed Babbitt in 1920, Babbitt answered him directly in 1922, and Lovejoy replied — but this later controversy cannot be projected backwards into the 1890s.

---

## 1. Babbitt and More were actually in a Lanman class together in 1892

A.H. Dakin's biography of Paul Elmer More reconstructs the beginning of the Babbitt–More friendship in **autumn 1892** and states that they constituted the whole of one of Professor Lanman's classes.

Digitized scholarly witness:
https://dokumen.pub/paul-elmer-more-9781400875009.html

The biography draws heavily on More's papers and recollections. It is not a current Harvard enrollment sheet, so the exact course number/title remains to be closed from the 1892–93 Harvard course announcement or enrollment records.

Nevertheless this materially upgrades Batch139. We no longer have only the proposition that Babbitt returned from Paris and studied 'ancient Eastern languages' at Harvard. He can be placed in a specific Lanman classroom setting immediately after his Paris study.

---

## 2. The Lanman class belonged to a shared Sanskrit/Pāli/Indian-philosophy formation

The Rutgers Database of Classical Scholars entry for Paul Elmer More says that he met Babbitt as a **fellow student of Sanskrit, Pali, and Indian philosophy in graduate school at Harvard**.

Institutional scholarly database:
https://dbcs.rutgers.edu/all-scholars/more-paul-elmer

The same entry records More as:

`asst. in Sanskrit to Charles Lanman, 1894-5`.

This supplies a second independent biographical control on the Lanman-centered cohort. The exact division among Sanskrit, Pāli and Indian-philosophy courses still needs a primary Harvard catalogue/enrollment page before assigning a specific course title to autumn 1892.

---

## 3. Contemporary Harvard evidence confirms More's Sanskrit teaching role

The Harvard Crimson's June 1895 report on the coming Radcliffe curriculum says:

- in Indo-Iranian languages, Professor Lanman would have charge of the Sanskrit courses;
- those courses had been conducted in the previous year by **Mr. More**.

Contemporary source:
https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1895/6/8/radcliffe-elective-pamphlet-the-radcliffe-college/

This contemporary notice independently confirms the More→Lanman teaching succession and fixes it immediately before Lovejoy entered Harvard graduate study.

The sequence is therefore not merely a chain assembled from later biographies. At least part of the handoff is visible in Harvard's own contemporary public record.

---

## 4. The topology around Lovejoy is now unusually tight

By 1895–97 Harvard contained simultaneously or in immediate succession:

- **Irving Babbitt**, recently returned from EPHE Pāli/Buddhist study and scholarly reconstruction identifies Lévi as his Paris teacher;
- **Paul Elmer More**, Babbitt's fellow Lanman/Indianist student, who had just served as Sanskrit assistant/instructor;
- **Charles Rockwell Lanman**, Harvard Sanskrit professor and editor of the Harvard Oriental Series;
- **Henry Clarke Warren's 1896 *Buddhism in Translations***, which the Lovejoy notebook directly uses;
- **Arthur O. Lovejoy**, whose Harvard course list, according to Wilson, documents Sanskrit and Buddhist sacred-text study under Lanman.

Thus the historical problem is no longer whether Harvard possessed a route into Lévi's Paris Indianism. It plainly did.

The remaining question is **which route Lovejoy actually used**.

---

## 5. The later Babbitt–Lovejoy relation is direct but chronologically useless for proving 1890s transmission

Lovejoy later reviewed Babbitt's *Rousseau and Romanticism* in 1920. Two years later Babbitt opened `Schiller and Romanticism` by explicitly addressing `Professor Lovejoy's notice` and arguing against Lovejoy's interpretation. Lovejoy published a reply in the same 1922 debate.

Primary/publication control for Babbitt's response:
- Irving Babbitt, `Schiller and Romanticism`, *Modern Language Notes* 37.5 (May 1922), 257–268.
- JSTOR volume/item context: https://www.jstor.org/stable/i346714

Searchable witness reproducing the opening:
https://www.scribd.com/document/852504536/shiller-romanticismo

Lovejoy's later `On the Discrimination of Romanticisms` (1924) explicitly cites Babbitt's `Schiller and Romanticism`:
https://www.jstor.org/stable/457184

This demonstrates that the two scholars eventually had a direct and substantive intellectual exchange.

But the chronology is separated by roughly a quarter century. It cannot support:

`they must therefore have known each other at Harvard in 1895–97`.

The correct evidentiary use is only:

`later relation secure / early relation unresolved`.

## 6. The strongest digital next step is now the official Harvard course series

Harvard's own ListView exposes a continuous digitized series:

**Harvard University, Faculty of Arts and Sciences. Courses of instruction, 1879–2009**

Official digital root:
https://listview.lib.harvard.edu/lists/drs-467484628

Persistent archival root:
`HUL.ARCH:40128815`.

The list explicitly contains child objects for:

- 1892–93;
- 1895–96;
- 1896–97;
- 1897–98.

This is important because it means two unresolved problems can probably be closed digitally from the same official series:

### Babbitt/More/Lanman
Recover the exact 1892–93 Indo-Iranian/Sanskrit course(s) in which Babbitt and More could have formed the entire class; compare the course description against Dakin/DBCS.

### Lovejoy
Recover the exact 1896–97 course descriptions corresponding to Wilson's Harvard Catalogue references and Lovejoy's documented Lanman/Toy/Everett study.

The current automated fetch of the ListView root is rate-limited at child-object level, so we have not yet resolved the 1896–97 child URL/page images in this runtime. This is a digital-access ceiling, not an archive-access problem.

## 7. Current judgment on Babbitt as transmitter

Evidence ladder:

**Secure institutional topology**
- Babbitt studied Pāli/Buddhist traditions at EPHE;
- scholarly institutional history identifies Lévi as his teacher;
- Babbitt returned to Harvard;
- Babbitt and More were in a Lanman class in 1892 according to Dakin;
- More served in Lanman's Sanskrit teaching apparatus;
- Babbitt was teaching at Harvard during Lovejoy's graduate years;
- Lovejoy studied Sanskrit/Buddhist texts under Lanman.

**Still unproved transmission event**
- Babbitt recommended Paris to Lovejoy;
- Babbitt told Lovejoy about Lévi's Pāli teaching;
- Lovejoy took a course with Babbitt;
- Lanman explicitly referred Lovejoy to Lévi through Babbitt's precedent.

Therefore the paper may use Babbitt to demonstrate that **knowledge of Lévi's Pāli pedagogy had a concrete returned Harvard carrier**, but not to name him as Lovejoy's recommender.
