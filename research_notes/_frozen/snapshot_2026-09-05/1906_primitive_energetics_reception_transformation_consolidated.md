## Core result

The early reception of `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy` cannot be represented as a single theory moving intact from Lovejoy to later authors.

At minimum, the 1906 paper contains four separable components:

1. **substantive category** — non-personal / communicable / productive efficacy;
2. **causal thesis** — regular, intelligible, quasi-mechanical manipulation, hence `Primitive Energetics`;
3. **evidentiary dossier** — manitou / wakonda / orenda / mana and related lexical, grammatical and customary materials;
4. **proof architecture** — independent existence-control, proposition-relative source competence, escalating warrants, and cross-evidential corroboration.

Reception repeatedly separates these components and assigns them new jobs.

The larger sequence is:

> **argument disaggregation → authority/source-function partition → warrant substitution → controversy-mediated recirculation → authorial feedback → citation shadow → verdict recomputation → epistemic-address shift → quotation/source transformation.**

The governing reception rule is:

> **citation, evidentiary use, theoretical assent, and proposition-level verdict are different relations.**

A text can remain a recommended evidence summary after its strongest causal thesis is rejected. A later synthesis can inherit access to the dossier while dropping Lovejoy's name. A recipient can accept the empirical pattern while narrowing the claim about native conceptual consciousness. A shared canonical ethnographic source can be excerpted or reweighted so that the same source supports different causal arrows.

The stable object is therefore not `Lovejoy's theory` as one indivisible unit. It is a movable packet of **sources, propositions, warrants, and verdicts**.

## I. Immediate reception: disaggregation rather than whole-theory adoption

### Leuba 1912 — method-aware reception

James H. Leuba's *A Psychological Study of Religion* directly cites Lovejoy's 1906 *Monist* article.

Leuba keeps the broad problem of a non-personal power but separates it from Lovejoy's stronger causal characterization.

He treats the following as distinct propositions:

- non-personal Wakonda / Manitou / Mana-type power exists as a serious ethnographic category;
- it works by `quite regular and intelligible laws`;
- it deserves a quasi-mechanical description;
- Lovejoy's paper is a useful survey of the evidence.

Leuba accepts the first and fourth while contesting the second and third. His objection is evidentiary: the means used to bring the power into operation do not exhibit a definite and stable quantitative relation to effects. He therefore prefers a magical rather than strongly mechanical characterization.

This yields the first decisive reception control:

> **category existence can survive rejection of causal strength.**

And:

> **evidentiary competence does not entail theoretical assent.**

### King 1910 — adjacent synthesis, primary edge still bounded

Irving King's *The Development of Religion* places the impersonal-power problem inside a developmental psychology of religion. Leuba 1912 explicitly directs readers to King's chapter VI, `The Mysterious Power`, beside Lovejoy as an alternative good summary of the evidence.

Nicolas Meylan supplies a precise secondary locator that King p.163 directly refers to Lovejoy and concludes that impersonal force is not in itself a religious concept. The exact p.163 facsimile/citation was not closed in the staged research.

Therefore retain only:

- King and Lovejoy are directly made functionally adjacent by Leuba 1912;
- King's exact direct-Lovejoy proposition remains **secondary-controlled / primary-text hold** in the older research state;
- do not reconstruct a King→Lovejoy or Lovejoy→King verdict from secondary wording alone.

### James/Kallen 1911 — packet assimilation

The posthumous *Some Problems of Philosophy* cites Lovejoy with Jastrow, Jevons, Frazer and Marett in a compact primitive-magic / mana authority packet.

The local use preserves Lovejoy as one bibliographic warrant for a broad textbook-level statement while losing the fine-grained `vera causa` and source-function architecture.

Because Horace M. Kallen largely prepared the unfinished book for press, the footnote should be attributed to the **1911 James/Kallen text**, not confidently to William James personally without manuscript/typescript collation.

### Marett / Jevons 1909 — parallel proof problem, no direct Lovejoy edge recovered

The British pre-animism debate independently asks whether a non-personal-power conception or present analogies suffice to prove a historical pre-animistic stage.

No Lovejoy citation was recovered in the checked Marett/Jevons materials.

Use this as:

> **parallel proof-threshold ecology / bounded negative reception control**, not evidence of non-reading or deliberate exclusion.

## II. Leuba: reception becomes reproof and warrant substitution

Leuba does not stop at accepting or rejecting Lovejoy's answer.

When the proposition changes from:

`does a non-personal power category exist?`

to:

`what is its psychological origin, and does it precede animism?`

he changes evidence.

He explicitly declines to reproduce the full linguistic/historical dossier and turns to developmental psychology, especially the child, to supplement anthropological data.

The new structure is:

`Lovejoy / King ethnographic-linguistic dossier`
→ sufficient for existence of a problem/category
→ insufficient for psychological genesis
→ `child causal cognition` becomes an independent genetic control.

This is reception by **reproof**:

> **the proposition-space is retained while the warrant is replaced.**

The project formulation is:

> **Lovejoy re-proved custom through language; Leuba re-proved anthropological origin through developmental psychology.**

This is a structural comparison, not evidence that Leuba consciously borrowed a methodological rule from Lovejoy.

## III. Source-function partition inside Leuba

Leuba's 1912 book shows that a recipient can decompose not only an argument but an authority.

### King

- King's general `valuational attitude` theory of religion = **THEORETICAL TARGET**; Leuba thinks graded values fail to discriminate religion qualitatively from the rest of life.
- King's chapter VI = **EVIDENCE AGGREGATOR**; Leuba recommends it for impersonal-power data.

### Lovejoy

- regular/intelligible-law / mechanical characterization = **THEORETICAL + WARRANT TARGET**; weakened/rejected.
- 1906 article as comparative dossier = **EVIDENCE SUMMARY**; retained/recommended.

So the useful reception unit is:

> **source × proposition × evidentiary job.**

A citation can remain active after the proposition that originally gave the source theoretical force has been rejected.

Compact rule:

> **citation-level survival can conceal proposition-level defeat.**

## IV. King–Leuba controversy and recirculation

The reception network is not a simple Lovejoy→Leuba dyad.

King's own 1911 *Harvard Theological Review* article explicitly says reviews of *The Development of Religion* revealed misunderstandings and identifies J. H. Leuba's *Psychological Bulletin* review at vol. VII, p.301, alongside George A. Coe.

This establishes a secure primary chain:

`King 1910 book`
→ `Leuba 1910 review`
→ `King 1911 public restatement/reply`.

The exact Leuba 1910 review text was not recovered in the staged work. Therefore do not claim that Lovejoy or the impersonal-power chapter was already the object of that 1910 dispute.

By 1912, however, Leuba explicitly juxtaposes King ch. VI and Lovejoy as evidence summaries while separately auditing Lovejoy's causal claim.

The reception mechanism is best described as:

> **controversy-mediated recirculation.**

Books, reviews, replies and later syntheses alter which parts of an argument packet remain salient.

## V. Lovejoy 1914: reception becomes feedback

A direct bibliographic primary establishes that Lovejoy reviewed Leuba's *A Psychological Study of Religion* in *International Journal of Ethics* 24.2 (Jan. 1914), pp.216–220.

Daniel J. Wilson's annotated bibliography says Lovejoy used part of the review to correct a misconception of his own 1906 position.

The exact 1914 review text was not directly collated in the staged research, so preserve the evidence grades:

- review existence, authorship, date, pagination = **DIRECT PRIMARY**;
- Lovejoy corrects Leuba on his 1906 position = **SECONDARY-CONTROLLED, HIGH** through Wilson;
- exact proposition corrected / exact wording = **NOT DIRECTLY CLOSED**.

Do not infer whether Lovejoy defended mechanical regularity, religion/magic classification, terminology, or some other point merely from Leuba's criticism.

The secure historiographical result is already substantial:

> **reception became bidirectional: Lovejoy publicly re-entered the downstream interpretation of his own 1906 argument.**

The graph is therefore a feedback system, not a one-way citation tree.

## VI. Jung 1917: Lovejoy as a revision-layer historical warrant

Direct edition-state collation distinguishes two authorized English states of Jung's essay.

### 1916 `New Paths in Psychology`

The first English collection contains no recovered:

- `Lovejoy`;
- `primitive energetics`;
- `mana`;
- later primordial-energy proof sequence.

### revised second-edition state prepared in 1917

The expanded `Psychology of the Unconscious Processes` introduces a new argument:

`modern scientific energy/conservation`
→ hypothesized as a primordial psychic image
→ historical persistence must be proved
→ cross-cultural `dynamistic religions` become historical evidence
→ Lovejoy is named and `primitive energetics` enters at precisely that proof-bearing point.

The footnote gives *The Monist* XVI, p.363.

Thus:

> **Lovejoy is a revision-layer source in Jung.**

The reception function changes radically:

`Lovejoy 1906: comparative causal category produced from ethnographic evidence`
→
`Jung 1917: that category itself becomes historical evidence for a psychic genealogy`.

The best compact description is:

> **proof syntax persists; source granularity collapses.**

Jung retains a demand for historical warrant for a strong proposition but takes Lovejoy's synthesized power-category as a package rather than reproducing Lovejoy's fine source hierarchy.

The precise first German appearance remains outside the closed evidence; use the directly collated 1916→1917 English-state insertion rather than claiming absolute first citation in 1917.

## VII. Jung 1928: appropriation becomes epistemic re-audit

`On Psychical Energy` / `Über die Energetik der Seele` returns below the level of the synthesized category to the ethnographic packet.

Jung now uses Lovejoy in at least three distinct functions:

1. source-routing apparatus back to McGee and the North-American wakonda packet;
2. substantive support for efficacy, power, productivity and regular/manipulable energetic characterization;
3. an interpretation to be corrected at the level of native conceptual status.

Jung accepts much of Lovejoy's causal/semantic diagnosis but objects that an abstract `concept of primitive philosophy` is the modern analyst's abstraction rather than necessarily the form in which the indigenous actor possesses the datum.

Thus:

`empirical power-pattern` → ACCEPT;

`regular/manipulable efficacy` → ACCEPT / REUSE;

`analytical comparative abstraction` → ACCEPT / REUSE;

`same-form native abstract concept` → NARROW / REJECT.

This produces one of the strongest external stress tests of Lovejoy's own warrant architecture:

> **native use can warrant an empirical relation without automatically warranting attribution of the analyst's abstraction to native consciousness.**

The reception trajectory is:

> **appropriation → evidentiary unpacking → epistemic re-audit.**

Exact 1928 facsimile wording remained a publication-level page-image hold; proposition-level continuity was controlled through edition history and republication of the original version.

## VIII. Malinowski 1915: citation shadow and downstream recombination

Malinowski's Polish 1915 synthesis is a crucial non-direct control.

Searchable text contains no recovered:

- `Lovejoy`;
- `Primitive Energetics`;
- `Monist XVI`.

Yet the impersonal-power dossier contains mana, arungquilta, churinga, manitu and orenda, and the note explicitly sends readers to:

- King ch. VI;
- Leuba ch. IV, pp.70–84;
- alongside Marett, Hubert–Mauss, Preuss, Durkheim and primary ethnographic authorities.

Leuba's cited chapter is precisely the location where Lovejoy is named, audited and retained as an evidence summary.

The secure result is therefore not hidden Lovejoy influence. It is:

> **Lovejoy becomes one step removable from the citation surface while remaining available through named intermediary syntheses.**

Project term:

> **citation shadow.**

A source can disappear as a name before the dossier route through which it remains accessible disappears.

This still does not identify the immediate source of any particular Malinowski sentence.

## IX. Jones → Lovejoy → Leuba → Malinowski: relay without verdict inheritance

The North-American manitou packet allows one proposition to be tracked through several authors.

### Jones 1905

Direct field/linguistic evidence supports:

- impersonal/cosmic property;
- diffusion;
- transferability;
- efficacy.

But Jones explicitly says interpretation is not governed by a regular rule and is based more on feeling than knowledge.

### Lovejoy 1906

Uses Jones as high-resolution grammatical/native evidence but strengthens the comparative conclusion into `quite regular and intelligible laws` and `Primitive Energetics`.

### Leuba 1912

Keeps the category and dossier but rolls back the mechanical strengthening for lack of stable means–effects relation.

### Malinowski 1915

Rejects sharply abstract native concepts, emphasizing vague/felt force, yet analytically describes manitu as essentially a mechanical factor.

Thus:

> **relay does not imply verdict inheritance.**

And:

> **components travel; verdicts are recomputed.**

Malinowski is not simply `Lovejoy without the name`; he recombines components in a configuration that matches different upstream authors on different dimensions.

The proper reception object is multidimensional:

`source × component × verdict × evidentiary job × citation visibility`.

## X. `Mechanical` is distributed field vocabulary, not a Lovejoy fingerprint

Hubert–Mauss supplies a decisive pre-Lovejoy control.

Their 1902–03 magic theory compares magical force to mechanical force at the level of explanatory function while explicitly preserving a distinction: mana is spiritual/magical and does not simply act mechanically in the ordinary physical sense.

This proves that `mechanical force`, `energy`, impersonal efficacy and related physics analogies were already distributed across comparative-religion discourse.

The logical strengths form a spectrum:

| writer | mechanical relation | evidentiary / logical strength |
|---|---|---|
| Hubert–Mauss 1902–03 | magical force comparable to mechanical force; distinction retained | FUNCTIONAL ANALOGY |
| Jones 1905 | efficacy/transfer but no regular interpretive rule | EFFICACY WITHOUT REGULAR MECHANICS |
| Lovejoy 1906 | quasi-mechanical, regular/intelligible laws, study/control | CAUSAL-LAW ASSIMILATION |
| King 1910 | mechanical-force comparison indicated by secondary locator | PRIMARY WORDING NOT CLOSED |
| Leuba 1912 | rejects strong mechanical label absent stable means/effects relation | EMPIRICAL THRESHOLD / ROLLBACK |
| Saintyves 1914 | mana/dynamism linked very strongly to scientific-force genealogy | PAGE-SPECIFIC SECONDARY CONTROL, DIRECT TEXT NOT CLOSED |
| Malinowski 1915 | vague/felt native force, analyst-level `mechanical factor` | ANALYST-LEVEL MECHANICAL DESCRIPTION + ANTI-ABSTRACTION |

Therefore:

> **lexical recurrence is weaker evidence than recurrence of an inferential configuration.**

A stronger Lovejoy-specific reception packet would require several elements to coincide, such as explicit name/Monist locator, `Primitive Energetics`, regular/intelligible-law proposition, Marett correction, efficacy/productivity semantic center, and Lovejoy's particular proof architecture.

## XI. Epistemic address: the category survives by changing owner

Jung 1928 and Malinowski 1915 converge at one structural level even though only Jung directly cites Lovejoy.

Both can retain an analyst-level comparative abstraction while denying that the native actor possesses the same sharply abstract conceptual object.

### Jung

- empirical efficacy/power pattern = retained;
- comparative analytical abstraction = retained;
- same-form native abstract concept = rejected/narrowed.

### Malinowski

- impersonal/mysterious-power field = retained;
- analyst-level mechanical description = possible;
- native cognition = vague, affective, non-abstract.

The shared operation is:

> **the category survives by changing epistemic address.**

Or:

> **etic retention / emic de-abstraction.**

These are project terms and should not be treated as neutral descriptions of indigenous cognition.

The important historical point is that the abstraction migrates from object-language toward analyst metalanguage.

The reception data therefore need an additional dimension:

`recipient × source × proposition × evidentiary job × verdict × epistemic address × citation visibility`.

## XII. Durkheim 1912: same source field, inverse proof vector

Durkheim is not a demonstrated Lovejoy recipient in the 1912 *Formes élémentaires*. No direct Lovejoy or `Primitive Energetics` citation was recovered.

But Durkheim and Lovejoy inhabit an overlapping mana/wakan/orenda documentary field and assign it different proof functions.

### Durkheim's Australian problem

Durkheim analytically extracts an anonymous and impersonal force from Australian totemic organization while explicitly stating that Australian actors do not represent the force in that abstract form; it is represented sensibly through totemic animals/plants/objects.

He then turns to North-American and Melanesian cases, where analogous powers appear in a more generalized and abstract form, and uses them to warrant the analytical attribution back to Australia.

The proof vector is:

`Australian structure`
→ analyst infers impersonal force
→ local emic abstraction incomplete
→ North America / Melanesia provide higher-abstraction analogues
→ cross-cultural comparison retro-validates the Australian extraction.

Relative to Lovejoy:

### Lovejoy

`native lexical / grammatical controls`
→ strengthen analyst abstraction locally
→ abstraction returned to customs.

### Durkheim

`local structural inference`
→ external comparative analogues
→ back-validation into a locally less abstract field.

Project formulation:

> **same dossier, inverse proof vector.**

Durkheim also uses independent scholarly convergence as another warrant channel, making his own category a triangulated reconstruction rather than a simple lexical inference.

This is a field-level comparison, not Lovejoy→Durkheim transmission.

## XIII. Codrington as a same-source laboratory: Lovejoy versus Durkheim

Codrington's mana passage is internally divided.

It says, in combination, that mana is:

- a non-physical power/influence;
- mobile and transferable;
- possessed and imparted by spirits;
- something that essentially belongs to personal beings to originate;
- obtainable/directable in religious practice.

The surrounding chapter reinforces the hybrid causal architecture:

`impersonal efficacy`
+
`personal/spiritual origination or direction`
+
`material mediation / transfer`.

### Lovejoy 1906

Uses Codrington as a major authority for efficacy and transferability while leaving adverse personal-origin evidence visible in the source packet and reweighting the semantic center toward efficacy/productivity rather than `supernormal` status.

This is:

> **proposition-relative reweighting of a mixed witness.**

### Durkheim 1912

Quotes the same definition at a proof-bearing point for the anonymous/diffuse force. His printed quotation visibly marks an ellipsis. Inside the omitted section lies Codrington's clause that spirits possess/impart mana and personal beings essentially originate it.

The quotation then resumes with the proposition that Melanesian religion consists in obtaining mana.

Later Durkheim's theory moves in almost the reverse causal direction:

`prior anonymous impersonal force`
→ individualized into
`spirits / demons / gods`.

Therefore the documentary difference is not simply two readings of mana.

It is:

### Lovejoy

`mixed witness`
→ extract/reweight propositions
→ adverse clause remains visible.

### Durkheim

`mixed witness`
→ marked selective quotation
→ adverse directional clause omitted from local excerpt
→ remaining packet warrants a different causal genealogy.

Use the precise formulation:

> **Lovejoy preserves and reweights adverse evidence; Durkheim's marked ellipsis removes a directional contradiction from the proof-bearing excerpt.**

Do **not** call this secret falsification. The ellipsis is typographically visible. Textual effect does not prove subjective intent.

This adds a new layer to source-function analysis:

`source text`
→ `quoted/excerpted proposition set`
→ `evidentiary function`
→ `claim licensed`.

Project term:

> **quotation architecture.**

## XV. What should and should not be inferred

### Secure/useful

- Lovejoy 1906 was directly received and propositionally audited by Leuba.
- Leuba simultaneously used Lovejoy as evidence summary and rejected a stronger causal thesis.
- Jung's Lovejoy citation belongs to the enlarged 1917 revision layer in the directly collated English states.
- Jung later accepts parts of Lovejoy while narrowing the epistemic license assigned to them.
- Lovejoy reviewed Leuba in 1914; Wilson identifies a self-corrective component, but exact wording remains uncollated.
- Malinowski directly cites King and Leuba in the impersonal-power dossier while Lovejoy's name is absent in the searchable text.
- mechanical vocabulary existed independently of Lovejoy and cannot identify reception by itself.
- Durkheim supplies a powerful shared-field/proof-vector control without a recovered direct Lovejoy edge.
- Codrington's mixed mana passage is transformed differently by Lovejoy and Durkheim.

### Withhold

- Do not claim Lovejoy caused Leuba's developmental child method.
- Do not claim King directly adopted Lovejoy until the exact primary page/citation is controlled.
- Do not assign the James/Kallen footnote personally to William James without manuscript evidence.
- Do not infer Lovejoy→Malinowski from mechanical wording or Leuba mediation.
- Do not infer Lovejoy→Durkheim from shared sources or mechanical vocabulary.
- Do not claim Jung first encountered Lovejoy in 1917 beyond the directly collated edition-state boundary.
- Do not specify the exact 1914 Lovejoy→Leuba correction without direct pp.216–220.
- Do not treat `etic/emic` project shorthand as timeless native/analyst ontology.
- Do not treat marked Durkheim ellipsis as covert falsification or infer intention from quotation structure alone.

## XVI. Canonical historiographical formulation

A compact synthesis is:

> **The early afterlife of `primitive energetics` was a history of divisible argumentative resources rather than whole-theory transmission. Leuba could retain Lovejoy as an evidence source after rejecting his strongest causal characterization; Jung could move the category first into a proof-bearing role in analytical psychology and later reopen it as a propositionally graded source; Malinowski could enter the same dossier through King and Leuba after Lovejoy's name had disappeared; Durkheim could work from overlapping canonical ethnographic materials under a different proof vector and quotation architecture. What circulated most continuously was access to a problem and an evidence packet. The verdicts, warrants, epistemic address, and even the causal arrows were repeatedly recomputed.**

Short project formulas:

> **components travel; verdicts are recomputed.**

> **citation-level survival can conceal proposition-level defeat.**

> **a category can survive by changing evidentiary job or epistemic owner.**

These are analytical formulations, not actor vocabulary.
