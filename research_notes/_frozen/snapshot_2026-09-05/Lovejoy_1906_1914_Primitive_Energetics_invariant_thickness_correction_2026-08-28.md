## Core correction

Previous project shorthand often described Lovejoy's 1906 `Primitive Energetics` mechanism as:

`impersonal`
+
`communicable/localizable efficacy`
+
`quasi-mechanical / regular / intelligible causal operation`.

The 1912 Leuba objection + 1914 Lovejoy reply shows that this is too **thick** if used as the Gate-A identity criterion for the theory.

Leuba explicitly rejects Lovejoy's language of `quite regular and intelligible laws`, arguing that mana/manitou/wakonda behavior contains much unpredictability and that the ritual means do not exhibit a definite stable quantitative relation to effects. Leuba therefore calls the power **magical**, not mechanical.

Lovejoy in 1914 nevertheless says:

- Leuba's theory of the origin and content of the primitive `idea of impersonal power` is **essentially** the theory Lovejoy advanced in 1906;
- Leuba's `dynamism` is **precisely** the conception Lovejoy proposed to call `primitive energetics` / `manitouism`;
- their disagreements concern chiefly nomenclature and **one minor question** arising largely from a confusion about terms.

The most natural direct-primary identification of that `minor question` is Leuba's explicit p.75 disagreement over `regular / intelligible / mechanical` versus `magical` force.

Therefore:

> **Lovejoy's own 1914 same-theory judgment tolerates a disagreement over whether the impersonal power should be called mechanical and how regular/predictable its operation is.**

This requires a correction to the current Gate-A invariant thickness.

---

# I. Leuba 1912 p.75 — exact point of dissent

Leuba reproduces Lovejoy's 1906 criticism of Marett:

- the fundamental notion is not the unpredictable/abnormal/portentous;
- it is a force conceived as operating by `quite regular and intelligible laws`;
- it can be studied and controlled;
- Lovejoy therefore names the complex `Primitive Energetics`.

Leuba then says he questions the appropriateness of the phrase `quite regular and intelligible laws`.

His reasons are specific:

1. much in the behavior of Wakonda/Manitou/Mana is unpredictable;
2. the means used to invoke the power do not show a definite and stable quantitative relation between means and effects;
3. therefore the invoked power is, in his terminology, `not a mechanical Power, but a magical force`.

Immediately after this disagreement, however, Leuba agrees on the cross-cultural object:

- Manitou, Wakonda, Orenda, Mana designate a **non-personal Power or Potency**;
- the notion is found beyond the named American/Melanesian cases;
- he continues to treat Lovejoy as one of the principal summaries of the evidence.

Thus Leuba himself preserves:

`same broad impersonal-power phenomenon`
+
`different characterization of its causal regularity/mechanicality`.

---

# II. Lovejoy 1914 p.219 — explicit same-theory judgment across that dissent

Lovejoy complains that Leuba's exposition makes their positions appear opposed.

He then says the theory Leuba accepts concerning the **origin and content** of the primitive idea of impersonal power is essentially Lovejoy's own 1906 theory.

He further equates:

`Leuba: dynamism`
≈
`Lovejoy: primitive energetics / manitouism`.

He reduces the remaining disagreement to:

- the most convenient name;
- one minor question chiefly caused by confusion over terms.

Because Leuba's p.75 `regular/mechanical` objection is the explicit local disagreement he has just staged against Lovejoy, the safe inference is:

> **whatever Lovejoy continued to think about the best description of regularity/mechanicality, he did not regard that disagreement as identity-destroying.**

Do not overstate this as `Lovejoy abandoned mechanicality`. The evidence supports a thinner conclusion:

`mechanical/regular predicate`
= **non-constitutive or at least non-identity-essential by 1914**.

# III. Revised invariant for `Primitive Energetics`

## Previous overly thick candidate

`impersonal power`
+
`localized/transmissible efficacy`
+
`quasi-mechanical regular lawfulness`
+
`manipulability/control`.

This may still describe a **rich 1906 formulation**, but it should not be treated as the minimum identity criterion across 1906→1914.

## Revised thin Gate-A invariant

The 1914 self-identification more securely warrants:

> **a primitive/early conception of impersonal power or potency, distinct from a personal-agent ontology, widely expressed in native causal/power terms and capable of interpenetrating other elements of belief/practice.**

Potential additional features such as:

- transfer/localization;
- ritual manipulation;
- regularity;
- quasi-mechanical operation;
- historical pre-animistic priority

must now be coded separately by strength and role rather than automatically bundled into the identity core.

# IV. Identity core vs inferential profile

The case gives a particularly useful Gate-A distinction:

## Thin identity core

`impersonal power/potency`
+
anti-reduction to personal divine/spiritual agency.

## Rich 1906 inferential/mechanism profile

Possible additional claims include:

- wide diffusion;
- localization/transfer;
- controllability/manipulation;
- patterned causal operation;
- relation to magic/religion;
- historical priority or pre-animistic status.

The 1914 evidence shows that at least one rich-profile feature, **mechanical/regular characterization**, can vary while Lovejoy preserves `essentially same theory` identity.

Therefore:

`same unit/theory`
↛ `same full inferential profile`.

This is directly analogous to mature thin-invariant cases but is now demonstrated in Lovejoy's own early comparative-religion self-comparison.

# V. Chronological status is likewise not identity-essential

Lovejoy 1914 describes the field's recognized phase as:

- non-animistic;
- `probably, but not demonstrably`, also pre-animistic.

This is another separation:

`content/type identity: impersonal-power conception`
≠
`historical priority claim: pre-animistic stage`.

The latter can remain probabilistic while the former is strongly affirmed.

So the 1906/1914 packet must not bundle:

`what the conception is`
with
`when it originated relative to animism`.

This is another concrete case of relation-indexed evidentiary force.

# VI. Consequence for 1906 `logic` coding

The 1906 article's `hidden logic` / coherent causal order should now be coded with a thinner constitutive claim.

Safe:

- `I/K`: Lovejoy attributes a connected causal intelligibility to primitive thought/practice;
- impersonal-power mechanism is the candidate object;
- native lexical/grammatical/practical evidence supports that reconstruction.

Needs qualification:

- do not make `quite regular and intelligible laws` the invariant by which every token is admitted to the category;
- do not equate `primitive energetics` with full scientific/mechanical law in a strict quantitative sense;
- Leuba's 1912 challenge plus Lovejoy's 1914 identity judgment explicitly shows that strict quantitative mechanicality is not necessary for same-theory recognition.

This makes `I/K` even safer than any drift toward `I/F`.

# VII. New Gate-A lesson: invariants can be thinner than the founding formulation

This is an especially valuable jurisprudential case because the invariant is not inferred only by comparing distant texts. Lovejoy himself tells us that a later reformulation remains `essentially` / `precisely` the same conception despite a contested property.

Thus:

`founding formulation contains features {a,b,c,d}`
+
`later accepted same-theory case rejects/changes d`
→ `d is not safely constitutive of identity`.

This gives a portable procedure for other Lovejoy units:

> **Use Lovejoy's own positive same-identity judgments to subtract nonessential features from the candidate invariant.**

Call this the **self-identity subtraction test**.

It complements the negative-threshold test:

- negative threshold asks what change breaks identity;
- self-identity subtraction asks what changed feature demonstrably does **not** break identity.

Together they provide upper and lower bounds on invariant thickness.

# VIII. Consequence for `threshold mismatch` in 1906

This correction makes the 1906 problem more exact, not less.

If the minimal identity core is thin:

`impersonal power/potency`

then cross-regional aggregation becomes easier. More cases can legitimately enter the type even if their causal regularity, ritual mechanisms, and ontological details differ.

But the representativeness burden rises correspondingly:

> **the thinner the core, the easier recurrence is to establish and the harder it becomes to justify promotion from recurrence to one `fundamental concept of primitive philosophy`.**

This is a direct instance of the existing project rule:

`thin invariant`
→ `high recurrence tolerance`
→ `higher explanatory/representativeness burden`.

The 1914 self-defense therefore strengthens the distinction between:

- **Gate-A type identity**: perhaps relatively easy under the thin impersonal-power core;
- **R macro representativeness/fundamentality**: still independently demanding.
