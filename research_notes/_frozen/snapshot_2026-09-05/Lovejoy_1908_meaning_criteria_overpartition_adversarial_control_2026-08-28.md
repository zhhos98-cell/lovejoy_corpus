# Lovejoy 1908 meaning criteria — overpartition as adversarial control

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **PRIMARY STRUCTURE SECURE / EXACT CRITERIA MEDIATED-COLLATION / STRONG RECEPTION COUNTER-CONTROL / THESIS-BEARING**

## Why this matters

The active branch has treated Lovejoy's repeated separation of relation-types and criteria as a possible longitudinal strength: he often prevents one evidential or semantic relation from silently licensing a stronger conclusion.

The 1908 James controversy supplies an essential adversarial correction.

Lovejoy's analytical separation itself is historically secure. But later James scholarship repeatedly argues that **Lovejoy over-separates what James's theory connects**. This creates a recurrent risk pattern that is also visible in the 1930 relativity controversy, where McGilvary argues that Lovejoy attributes too strong an operationist collapse to Einstein.

The portable Lovejoy operation may therefore have a characteristic failure mode:

> **jurisdiction partition can become overpartition: analytically distinct relations are treated as independent even where the historical target's theory makes their connection constitutive.**

This is directly relevant to the eventual unit-idea/Skinner problem.

## 2. The overall decomposition rule is primary-secure

Lovejoy's governing claim is that the thirteen items grouped under `pragmatism` are not merely verbally distinguishable but **logically independent**:

- one may accept one while rejecting all the others without inconsistency;
- refuting one leaves the philosophical standing of the others untouched.

Wilson's annotated bibliography confirms Lovejoy's own topical classification:

- **three theories of meaning**;
- one epistemologically functionless theory about the nature of truth;
- eight theories of knowledge;
- one ontological theory.

Thus `meaning` is not a marginal subcase. The first three pragmatisms are already a criteria-differentiation exercise.

## 3. The three meaning theories Lovejoy separates

Reliable later transcriptions of Lovejoy's taxonomy give the three meaning theses as follows in substance:

### Meaning theory 1 — predictive import of the judgment

The meaning of a judgment consists wholly in the **future consequences predicted by it**, whether or not anyone believes it.

Operator:

`truth/content of proposition P`
→ predicted future experiential consequences.

### Meaning theory 2 — consequences of believing the judgment

The meaning of a judgment consists in the **future consequences of believing it**.

Operator:

`subject accepts proposition P`
→ consequences in the believer's subsequent experience/life.

### Meaning theory 3 — relation to conscious purpose

The meaning of an idea or judgment consists at least partly in apprehending the **relation of some object to a conscious purpose**.

Operator:

`object / proposition`
→ relation-to-purpose
→ meaningfulness/content.

Lovejoy's central analytical move is to treat these as different criteria, not stylistic paraphrases of one criterion.

## 4. The first/second criterion split — proposition consequence versus belief consequence

The historically most consequential separation is between criteria 1 and 2.

Lovejoy reads James as moving between:

`what experiential consequences follow if P is true`

and

`what experiential consequences follow from believing P`.

The two relations are formally different:

- the first concerns the predictive import/content of a proposition;
- the second concerns effects of an agent's doxastic state.

Lovejoy then uses the Absolute/religious-metaphysical case to argue that their extensions diverge. A proposition may have no specific future experience predicted by its truth while belief in it still consoles, motivates or otherwise changes the believer's life.

Thus:

`no predictive consequence of P`

may coexist with

`practical/affective consequence of believing P`.

If the two are separate criteria of meaning, a proposition can be meaningless under one and meaningful under the other.

This is a classic Lovejoy **relation-typing** move.

## 5. The reception counter: later James scholars say the split is too sharp

### Ellen Kappy Suckiel

Suckiel, `Pragmatic and Cognitive Meaning in William James`, *Southern Journal of Philosophy* 16 (1978), 675–686, directly rejects the Lovejoy framework.

Her core claim, as summarized in bibliographic abstracts and later scholarship, is that James does **not** have two independent/separable criteria of meaningfulness. Rather:

- predictive import and consequences in the life of the believer belong to one connected account;
- practical consequences arise because the believer anticipates the proposition's consequences;
- Lovejoy's `predictive import` versus `consequences of belief` bifurcation therefore overstates separability.

Wilson's bibliography codes her criticism exactly this way: Lovejoy's fundamental mistake lies in holding predictive import and consequences of belief separable in James's theory.

### Later reconstruction

More recent work on James's pragmatic method likewise notes that explicit formulations of James's maxim concern the practical effects of a sentence's truth rather than merely the effects of believing it, and treats the subjectivistic `consequences of believing` reading as a Lovejoyian reconstruction that later readers inherited.

The reception issue is therefore not merely whether Lovejoy counted the pragmatisms correctly. It concerns **whether his analytical partition corresponds to James's own functional architecture**.

## 6. This creates a recurrent Lovejoy-critical pattern with 1930

### 1908 James

Lovejoy:

`predictive import of P`
≠
`consequences of believing P`.

Counter-control:

James scholarship argues that these are functionally connected inside one theory and that Lovejoy's independent-criteria reading overpartitions them.

### 1930 Einstein / operationism

Lovejoy:

`meaning of X`
≠
`operation / empirical criterion used to test X`.

McGilvary 1931 counter-control:

Einstein need not define concepts **by** the empirical operations used to test them; Lovejoy therefore attacks a stronger collapse than Einstein actually requires.

### Common risk

In both cases Lovejoy may proceed by:

1. isolating two relation-types;
2. showing they are not logically identical;
3. treating the target as if it required identity or independent criteria;
4. attacking the resulting contradiction/collapse;
5. while the target may instead posit a mediated, functional or asymmetric connection between the relations.

This is not enough to say Lovejoy `always misreads holistic thinkers`. It is enough to establish an adversarial hypothesis:

> **Lovejoy's analytical strength at detecting non-identity can become a historical weakness when non-identity is promoted too quickly to independence.**

That formulation should be tested against additional disputes before becoming a general characterization.

## 7. Why this sharpens `typed relations + inferential jurisdiction`

The repo's strongest candidate should now be stated with an internal failure condition.

Positive form:

> identify the relation actually at stake and prevent evidence licensed for one relation from silently doing work in another.

Adversarial form:

> after distinguishing two relations, do not infer that the actor's theory treats them as independent; recover the bridge the actor claims between them.

This produces a better historical protocol for studying Lovejoy himself:

`relation A ≠ relation B`

is only step 1.

Then ask:

- does the target claim identity A=B?;
- implication A→B?;
- probabilistic/evidential connection?;
- functional integration?;
- pragmatic coupling?;
- historical association?;
- constitutive but non-identical relation?;

Lovejoy's criticism is strongest only after the target's **bridge type** is correctly reconstructed.

## 8. Direct consequence for the 1938 Carnap essay

Lovejoy's 1938 essay says Carnap interchanges:

- thinkability / thought-content;
- empirical verifiability / testability.

That distinction is direct-primary and analytically clear. But the 1908/1930 adversarial controls warn against stopping there.

The next Carnap-side question is therefore not simply:

> are Lovejoy's two criteria distinct?

They plainly are in Lovejoy's reconstruction.

The harder question is:

> **what exact bridge did Carnap himself claim between empirical confirmability/testability and cognitive significance in 1928 and 1936–37, and does Lovejoy represent that bridge as identity when Carnap treats it as a weaker relation?**

This is now a priority adversarial test.

It also explains why recovering the Baltimore typescript `RC 081-03-06` remains valuable even though the published `Testability and Meaning` is available: the event-specific wording can show exactly which bridge Lovejoy confronted in December 1935.

## 9. Direct consequence for the unit-idea/Skinner branch

The same distinction may matter for historical identity.

Lovejoy's mature practice can distinguish:

`idea/component A`
from
`its motives, functions, labels and combinations`.

But:

`A is non-identical with its context-relations`

does not establish

`A is historically independent of those relations`.

This is almost exactly the methodological risk exposed by the James reception control.

Potential bridge to Skinner:

> **the difficult question is not whether an idea can be analytically distinguished from context, but what kind of dependence relation still connects the analytically distinguishable component to the speech act, problem, motive, vocabulary and configuration in which it operates.**

Do not yet claim that the 1908 James error directly caused the mature unit-idea problem. The point is a structural adversarial control on our own reconstruction.

## 10. Field-level control from Carnap 1935

The newly recovered Carnap-to-Neurath letter of 2 June 1935 makes the test stricter.

Carnap explicitly endorses `deutliche Trennung der verschiedenen Teilbehauptungen in einer Gesamtauffassung` — clear separation of partial assertions inside an overall view — and uses logical independence to separate universal revocability from physicalism.

Therefore generic decomposition/independence testing is not Lovejoy-specific.

The historiographically useful contrast may instead be:

- both Lovejoy and Carnap separate propositions/criteria;
- both demand relation-specific clarity;
- disagreements concern the **bridge relations** they permit after separation;
- Lovejoy may have a recurrent tendency to construe a target's bridge as a stronger identity claim than the target accepts.

This is a substantially better-controlled model of the 1935 encounter than `analysis versus metaphysics`.
