# JHI vs *Political Theory*, 1973–1977: information-layer architecture beyond research articles

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **MATCHED-WINDOW CARRIER-ROLE COMPARISON / PT STANDING FIELD-MAP DIRECT / JHI BOOKS-RECEIVED + REVIEW/SURVEY PATH DIRECT / NO CLAIM OF TOTAL HISTORICAL ABSENCE**

## Core result

The matched JHI/PT regional-object census showed that research-output incidence alone is a weak guide to a venue's information horizon.

A second comparison now distinguishes **how each venue made material outside its ordinary research-article layer visible**.

For early *Political Theory*, the information layer is unusually explicit and infrastructural:

`STANDING CROSS-JOURNAL BIBLIOGRAPHY`
+
`NAMED COMPILER LABOR`
+
`CORE MONITORING LIST`
+
`READER-SUPPLIED SUPPLEMENTS`
+
`EDITORIAL RELEVANCE SELECTION`
+
`RECLASSIFICATION ACROSS SOURCE DISCIPLINES`.

For JHI in the matched 1973–77 window, the issue architecture looks different. Across the already audited twenty-issue window, the recurring visible information/reception forms are instead:

- `Books Received`;
- intermittent `Review-Article` units;
- occasional explicit literature/bibliographic surveys embedded as named scholarly items.

The current twenty-issue TOC audit does **not** show a recurring PT-style cross-journal `Political Theory Articles` bibliography series inside JHI for this window.

Therefore the stronger comparison is:

> **The two venues both possessed information/reception layers beyond ordinary articles, but they externalized field awareness through different carrier forms. PT built a standing cross-journal selection/reclassification apparatus; JHI more often routed peripheral or synthetic visibility through received-book lists, review-articles, and topic-specific bibliographic surveys.**

This is an observed matched-window architecture, not a claim about either journal's entire history.

## I. PT: standing bibliographic field-map

Companion dossier:

`research_notes/Political_Theory_1973_1979_bibliographic_field_mapping_compiler_succession_distributed_monitoring_2026-08-31.md`.

Direct controls already locked:

- Linda Marasco is named as compiler of the inaugural 1973 `Political Theory Articles` bibliography;

- by 1974 the journal describes surveillance extending across more than 100 major journals;
- Marasco remains visible in later bibliographic labor;

- Nederman 1979 states that his article bibliography draws from more than fifty English-language journals across political science, philosophy, history, classics, sociology and anthropology;
- selection is based on editorial estimation of relevance to current political-theory debate;
- items are reclassified under PT categories rather than left in source-venue containers.

Thus PT's bibliography is not merely passive back matter.

`BIBLIOGRAPHIC FIELD MAP`
=
`SOURCE CORPUS + SELECTION RULE + CLASSIFICATION LABOR`.

## II. JHI matched-window issue architecture

The 1973–77 JHI census already inspected/reconstructed all twenty issue architectures for denominator control.

Within that matched window, recurring TOC-visible non-article information/reception roles include `Books Received`; some issues also contain explicitly labeled `Review-Article` units. Topic-specific survey items appear inside the scholarly content architecture rather than as one standing cross-journal field bibliography.

Do not infer that JHI lacked other invisible office-level monitoring, editorial reading, correspondence, or subscription awareness. The claim is only about **published issue architecture**.

`NO STANDING TOC SERIES OBSERVED`
!= `NO EDITORIAL MONITORING EXISTED`.

`BOOK ARRIVAL / FIELD INTAKE / BIBLIOGRAPHIC VISIBILITY`.

`BOOKS RECEIVED VISIBILITY`

## IV. JHI review-articles can make regional/civilizational fields visible

The regional-object comparator already identified two high-value cases outside the strict article denominator.

### 1975 — Latin America

Jorge J. E. Gracia, `Importance of the History of Ideas in Latin America: Zea's Positivism in Mexico`, JHI 36.1 (1975), 177–184.

JSTOR directly classifies this as `Review-Article`.

This places Latin-American history-of-ideas method/reception into a dedicated interpretive review carrier even though the year has no narrow strict regional-title hit.

Role:

`REGIONAL FIELD / METHOD VISIBILITY THROUGH REVIEW-ARTICLE`.

### 1977 — Indian / Western philosophy

Jeffrey J. Lunstead, `Recent Books Which Relate Indian and Western Philosophy`, JHI 38.4 (1977), 719–725.

JSTOR directly identifies the reviewed books:

- Bimal K. Matilal, *Epistemology, Logic, and Grammar in Indian Philosophical Analysis*;
- J. N. Mohanty, *Phenomenology and Ontology*;
- Arthur C. Danto, *Mysticism and Morality: Oriental Thought and Moral Philosophy*.

Role:

`INDIAN/WESTERN COMPARATIVE FIELD VISIBILITY THROUGH REVIEW-ARTICLE`.

This is a real information/reception carrier, but not the same operation as PT's standing bibliography.

`REVIEW-ARTICLE SYNTHESIS`
!= `FIELD-WIDE ARTICLE SURVEILLANCE`.

## V. JHI topic-specific bibliographic surveys

The matched-window issue architecture also contains items whose primary carrier role is literature surveillance/synthesis.

Direct examples:

- `On Recent Copernicana`, JHI 34.3 (1973);
- `Machiavelli Studies since 1969`, JHI 37.2 (1976).

The latter was deliberately excluded from the strict 1976 denominator during the harmonized census because its carrier role is explicit field/literature survey rather than ordinary historical-argument article.

These items show that JHI can package bibliographic awareness as a **topic-bounded scholarly intervention**.

Model:

`TOPIC / AUTHOR FIELD`
-> `SURVEY / REVIEW SYNTHESIS`
-> `JHI READERSHIP`.

This differs from PT's model:

`MULTIDISCIPLINARY JOURNAL UNIVERSE`
-> `RELEVANCE SELECTION`
-> `GENERAL POLITICAL-THEORY FIELD MAP`.

## VI. Why the distinction matters for global/regional visibility

The matched census already established:

- JHI strict regional incidence: 5/233 ≈ 2.15%;
- PT: 2/122 ≈ 1.64%;
- raw difference small;
- role composition more informative.

The information-layer comparison adds another dimension.

A regional/civilizational object can become visible through:

- ordinary research article;
- note/discussion;
- review-article;
- topic bibliography/survey;
- books-received list;
- standing cross-journal bibliography;
- communication/rejoinder.

Therefore:

> **GLOBAL / REGIONAL VISIBILITY IS NOT A SINGLE ARTICLE-SHARE VARIABLE. It is a role-distributed property of a carrier ecology.**

PT may have a broader general information-surveillance apparatus than its own article-object distribution suggests.

JHI may admit internally addressed regional objects at article level while also using review-articles to synthesize Latin-American and Indian/Western philosophical fields.

Neither pattern is reducible to `global journal` versus `Western journal`.

## VII. New comparative carrier formula

For matched-window venue comparison, distinguish at least:

`OUTPUT HORIZON`
+
`REVIEW / RECEPTION HORIZON`
+
`BIBLIOGRAPHIC / INFORMATION HORIZON`.

PT 1973–77:

- output horizon: sparse non-Euro-American title objects, no internal-tradition hit under strict rule;
- review/communication horizon: China/Averroes etc. visible;
- bibliographic horizon: standing, distributed, cross-journal field surveillance.

JHI 1973–77:

- output horizon: sparse overall but several internal-tradition regional article roles;
- review horizon: Latin-American history-of-ideas and Indian/Western philosophy directly visible;
- information horizon: recurring Books Received + topic-specific bibliographic/review surveys rather than a recurring PT-style general article bibliography in the matched issue architecture.

Compact rule:

`SAME INFORMATION FUNCTION`
!= `SAME CARRIER FORM`.
