# International Society for the History of Ideas — conference chronology, 1960–1975

Date: 2026-08-31  
Status: **ACTIVE INSTITUTIONAL CHRONOLOGY / 1ST, 2ND, 4TH SECURE; 3RD TOPIC SECURE, DATE/PLACE PARTLY OPEN**

## Core result

The International Society for the History of Ideas was not a short-lived adjunct to JHI. It operated for at least fifteen years as an international conference/publication network closely linked to Philip P. Wiener and overlapping with JHI, while remaining institutionally distinct from `Journal of the History of Ideas, Inc.`.

Its surviving conference sequence suggests a specific organizational technology:

> **choose a large transhistorical problem-concept, assemble scholars from multiple countries/disciplines/regions, and circulate results through JHI or edited proceedings.**

The politics of the chosen concepts also intensify over time:

`INDIVIDUALISM`
→ `VIOLENCE / AGGRESSION`
→ `FREEDOM OF THOUGHT / EXPRESSION`.

This makes the Society an important carrier for the post-Lovejoy transformation of history of ideas into an international problem-centered conference field.

---

# I. Society formation / personnel

Wiener's biographical record states that in **1960 he became vice president of the International Society for the History of Ideas**.

Popper's papers contain a folder:

`International Society for the History of Ideas (Aaron Noland, Philip W. Wiener), 1959–1974`.

This places Society organization before the first meeting and identifies Wiener/Noland as core contacts.

J. Bronowski's papers preserve a Society file dated 19 February 1960–7 July 1965 containing:

- correspondence on proposed topics;
- conference activities;
- May 1965 meeting minutes;
- related material.

Thus the Society had continuing organizational life between major conferences.

---

# II. First meeting — Cambridge, 1960 — SECURE

Date:
31 August–6 September 1960.

Place:
Cambridge, United Kingdom.

Theme:
`Individualism since the Renaissance: Growth or Decline?`

Herbert Butterfield explicitly refers to this as the Society inaugurating its career.

Key methodological statement:

- individualism cannot be contained within a conventional book-to-book genealogy of ideas;
- ideas can arise from despair, frustration, battle, predicament and human experience;
- history of ideas must repeatedly `sink back` into general history;
- comparative religions/civilizations may be necessary to explain Western individualism.

Direct conference→JHI routes recovered through Paolo Rossi's archive include:

- Max Loehr, `The Question of Individualism in Chinese Art` → JHI 22.2 (1961), 147–158;
- James R. Hightower, `Individualism in Chinese Literature` → JHI 22.2, 159–168;
- Aaron Noland, `Individualism in Jean Jaurès' Socialist Thought` → JHI 22.1, 63–80;
- Philip P. Wiener, `Some Problems and Methods in the History of Ideas` → JHI 22.4, 531–548.

The Cambridge meeting therefore links methodological debate, Chinese comparative material, political thought and general history in one Society programme.

---

# III. Second conference — Mexico City, 1962 — SECURE AT CONFERENCE LEVEL

Paolo Rossi archival records identify the Society's **II Conference** in Mexico City, 1962.

A near-contemporary/first-person Uruguayan source reports that the Mexico conference was organized by the International Society for the History of Ideas with participation/support from:

- Leopoldo Zea's Committee on the History of Ideas in America;
- the National University of Mexico / UNAM;
- Rector Ignacio Chávez, who opened the sessions.

This is institutionally critical because the Society encounters an already existing Latin-American `historia de las ideas` movement rather than simply exporting a US programme.

Exact theme, participant list, paper titles and proceedings remain high-priority archival targets.

---

# IV. Third conference — violence/aggression — TOPIC/PROCEEDINGS SECURE; FULL EVENT METADATA OPEN

The book:

Philip P. Wiener and John Fisher, eds., *Violence and Aggression in the History of Ideas* (Rutgers University Press, 1974)

is explicitly identified in participant/source records as:

`Proceedings of the Third International Conference of the International Society for the History of Ideas`.

Mike Wallace's CV records:

`Political Paternalism and Violence`, International Society for the History of Ideas' **Seminar on Violence**, 1972.

This strongly suggests the third conference/proceedings grew from a 1972 Society violence meeting/seminar, but the exact conference date/location must still be recovered before promotion.

The proceedings include interdisciplinary work on political, philosophical, ethical, religious and social violence.

Institutional publication route:

`SOCIETY CONFERENCE / SEMINAR`
→ `WIENER + FISHER EDITED PROCEEDINGS`
→ `RUTGERS UNIVERSITY PRESS, 1974`.

Important open question: why Rutgers University Press becomes the proceedings publisher years before JHI itself moves to Rutgers under Kelley (1991). Do not infer organizational connection from publisher alone.

# V. Fourth conference — Venice, 1975 — SECURE AND ARCHIVALLY RICH

Theme:
`Freedom of Thought and Expression in the History of Ideas`.

Place:
Fondazione Giorgio Cini, Venice.

Dates:
28 September–2/3 October 1975 (sources differ in end-date wording by one day; direct programme should settle).

Rockefeller Foundation Annual Report 1975 records:

`International Society for the History of Ideas, Philadelphia`
— $10,000 for an international conference on `The History of Freedom of Thought and Expression`.

This confirms:

- a Philadelphia organizational address by 1975;
- major foundation support;
- the conference's formal international status.

Helmut Pappe papers preserve:

- full programme;
- organizational correspondence;
- paper summaries/abstracts;
- accommodation/publication arrangements.

Listed/attested participants and themes include:

- Philip P. Wiener — introductory remarks;
- Enrico Berti — ancient Greek dialectic and freedom of thought/speech;
- Tony Tanner — licensing/press;
- Jean Starobinski — French Enlightenment/free expression;
- Shlomo Avineri — freedom of writing/expression in Hegel and Marx;
- François Bondy — freedom in French postwar ideologies;
- Richard McKeon — canonic/prohibited books, orthodoxy/heresy, production/preservation/dissemination/interpretation/use of texts.

McKeon's paper was published in *Critical Inquiry* 2.4 (1976), explicitly identifying the Society conference provenance.

Thus Society conference publication was not restricted to JHI; it entered a broader network of intellectual/cultural journals.

# VI. Conference programme as a transformed Club technology

A structural comparison is now possible.

### Hopkins Club, 1920s–30s

Topic papers organized around recurring intellectual problems:
- primitivism;
- progress;
- religion;
- diffusion;
- disciplinary crossings.

### International Society, 1960s–70s

International week-long/large conferences organized around:
- individualism;
- violence/aggression;
- freedom of thought/expression.

The shared organizational form is:

`PROBLEM-CONCEPT`
→ `MULTIPLE PERIODS / DISCIPLINES / REGIONS`
→ `COMPARATIVE DISCUSSION`
→ `PUBLICATION`.

This is a plausible institutional scaling-up of problem-centered history-of-ideas work, but a direct genealogy from Club organizational practice to Society design remains to be demonstrated through Wiener/Lovejoy/Noland correspondence.

---

# VII. Increasing political address

The Society's later topics are strongly political:

- violence and aggression;
- political violence;
- freedom and censorship;
- Hegel/Marx;
- postwar ideology;
- orthodoxy/heresy.

This should be compared with:

- early Cold War political themes in JHI;
- Lovejoy's own liberal/anti-totalitarian public engagements;
- Wiener's editorial projects;
- 1970s political/intellectual-history debates.

The Society may offer a direct institutional site where history-of-ideas technique becomes a vehicle for contemporary political problematization.

Do not reduce conference scholarship to Cold War ideology without paper-level analysis.

---

# VIII. Society vs JHI Inc. — keep legally/institutionally distinct

Current evidence supports overlap through:

- Wiener;
- Noland;
- JHI publication of Cambridge papers;
- JHI editor/regional networks;
- shared intellectual problems.

But:

`INTERNATIONAL SOCIETY FOR THE HISTORY OF IDEAS`
!=
`JOURNAL OF THE HISTORY OF IDEAS, INC.`

unless future legal/governance records show formal affiliation.

The Society is described as Philadelphia-based in Rockefeller's 1975 report, while JHI Inc. had its own separate corporate history.

Investigate:

- Society charter/legal status;
- officers/presidents after 1960;
- financial accounts;
- membership;
- relation to JHI Board of Directors;
- use of JHI office/staff;
- whether JHI Inc. acted as fiscal sponsor.
