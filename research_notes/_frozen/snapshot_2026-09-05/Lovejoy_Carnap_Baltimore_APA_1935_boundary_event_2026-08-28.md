# Lovejoy–Carnap at the Baltimore APA meeting, December 1935 — boundary-event dossier

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **CONTEMPORANEOUS MEETING NOTICE + CARNAP SAME-DAY DIARY RECOVERED / QUINE RECOLLECTION CONTROL / EXACT INTRODUCER FUNCTION STILL SECONDARY / RESTART-SAFE**

## Why this matters

The Baltimore event is now directly anchored by contemporaneous evidence rather than resting chiefly on later reconstruction.

At the Thirty-Fifth Annual Meeting of the Eastern Division of the American Philosophical Association at Johns Hopkins, **29–31 December 1935**, Rudolf Carnap delivered the Baltimore version of `Testability and Meaning` on **31 December 1935**. Carnap's diary for that day records that Arthur O. Lovejoy then spoke at length about Carnap's larger manuscript after a **programme change**, objecting that the resulting conventionalism would prevent logical positivists from continuing to dismiss metaphysics as meaningless. Carnap records that he replied at length to Lovejoy and then to Demos, Weiss, Savery, and others.

This is stronger than the earlier state of the dossier. The Lovejoy–Carnap exchange is now a **same-day actor-documented substantive encounter**. Quine's later recollection remains useful as an independent actor-memory of the style and significance of Carnap's reply, but it is no longer the sole actor-level basis for saying that Lovejoy challenged Carnap and Carnap answered.

The event joins three histories later often separated:

- Lovejoy's critical-realist / formal-logical philosophical career;
- the arrival of logical positivism / scientific empiricism in the United States;
- the retrospective story of the American `analytic turn`.

The event remains a boundary encounter, not evidence that Lovejoy became a logical positivist or belonged to a later analytic school.

## 1. Primary chronology correction — 28 December was travel, not the Baltimore paper

The earlier dossier inherited a date ambiguity from secondary reconstruction. That ambiguity is now substantially resolved.

### Contemporaneous published meeting notice

*The Journal of Philosophy*, vol. 32, no. 25 (5 Dec. 1935), pp. 684–691, prints `Abstracts of Papers to be Read at the Thirty-Fifth Annual Meeting of the Eastern Division of the American Philosophical Association, The Johns Hopkins University, December 29, 30, and 31, 1935`.

Stable issue record: https://www.jstor.org/stable/i335097

This fixes the formal meeting dates as **29, 30, and 31 December 1935**.

### Carnap diary, 28–29 December

Carnap's surviving diary independently explains how the `28 December` date entered later narrative.

On **Saturday 28 December**, Carnap records a 7 a.m. departure, breakfast at Nelson Goodman's, then travel by car with Goodman, Quine, David Prall, and Henry S. Leonard. They drove through New York and stopped overnight at the West Chester Mansion House in Pennsylvania.

On **Sunday 29 December**, Carnap records a further two-hour drive to Baltimore, lodging at the Southern Hotel, and explicitly identifies the event as the Eastern Division meeting of the American Philosophical Association. He attended three afternoon papers and intervened in discussion.

Primary diary edition: Rudolf Carnap, *Tagebücher und Leselisten*, diary 39, 15–31 Dec. 1935, VALEP: https://valep.vc.univie.ac.at/files/Tagebuecher/39.html

Therefore:

`28 Dec. = departure / road journey / West Chester overnight`

→ `29 Dec. = arrival in Baltimore / meeting begins`

→ `30 Dec. = meeting, probability symposium, press interview, official banquet`

→ **`31 Dec. = Carnap paper + Lovejoy intervention + Carnap reply`**.

The old formulation `Baltimore APA, 28 Dec. 1935` must not be used for the talk itself.

## 2. Carnap same-day diary — direct evidence for Lovejoy's intervention and objection

Carnap's diary entry for **Tuesday 31 December 1935** is the central new evidence.

Sequence recorded by Carnap:

1. James Burnham gives a paper on causation in history.
2. Carnap gives his Baltimore paper, identified in the diary as `Testability and Meaning`; the edition also points to the surviving eleven-page typescript `Testability and Reduction. Vortrag Baltimore (Meeting Philosophical Association) 31.12.1935`, RC 081-03-06.
3. Carnap then records: **Lovejoy speaks at length, following a programme change, about Carnap's larger `Testability` manuscript.**
4. Carnap summarizes Lovejoy's criticism: if the position now makes everything conventional, then logical positivists can no longer declare metaphysics meaningless.
5. Carnap records that he then replied at length to Lovejoy and to interventions by Demos, Weiss, Savery, and others.
6. The discussion lasted until about 12:30; Carnap then left for Washington and Chicago.

The VALEP editorial note attached to this entry states that the APA `Eastern Division` record lists Lovejoy's contribution as **`An Examination of Logical Positivism`** and cites *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Association* 9 (1935), p. 173.

Earlier coding:

`publicly challenged Carnap; Carnap replied` = Quine later actor-recollection.

Revised coding:

`publicly challenged Carnap; Carnap replied` = **same-day Carnap diary, direct actor record**.

## 3. Exact substantive content now partly recoverable

The older dossier could establish that Lovejoy criticized Carnap but not what he said. Carnap's diary now supplies a compact actor-level characterization of the objection.

Carnap writes, in substance, that Lovejoy's point was:

> if everything has become conventional, logical positivists can no longer represent metaphysics as meaningless.

This should be treated as **Carnap's same-day précis of Lovejoy**, not as a verbatim transcript of Lovejoy's speech. It nevertheless gives a much more precise problem than the generic label `metaphysician attacks positivist`.

The target appears to concern the relation among:

- conventional choice / language framework;
- criteria of meaningfulness;
- the status of metaphysical propositions;
- whether plural or conventional linguistic frameworks undercut the right to exclude metaphysics as nonsensical.

That is unusually important because Carnap's 1936–37 `Testability and Meaning` itself loosens earlier strict verificationist formulations. The Baltimore dispute should therefore be reconstructed at the level of **what conventions and empirical criteria license**, not as a textbook collision between `metaphysics` and a single fixed `verification principle`.

## 4. Programme status — what is secure and what remains open

The VALEP edition explicitly identifies the meeting programme/proceedings locus as:

- `Eastern Division`, *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Association* 9 (1935), p. 173;
- Lovejoy contribution: `An Examination of Logical Positivism`.

Carnap's own diary adds `Programmänderung` immediately before describing Lovejoy's intervention.

This creates an important distinction:

- Lovejoy was **formally associated with a titled contribution on logical positivism** in the APA record;
- the precise relation between that listed contribution and the actual 31 Dec. sequence was altered by a **programme change**;
- Verhaegh's statement that Lovejoy **introduced Carnap** remains strong secondary reconstruction and still needs exact original-programme/function control before being promoted to direct-primary status.

Do not conflate:

`Lovejoy listed as speaker / programme changed`

with

`Lovejoy formally chaired or introduced Carnap`.

The substantive exchange is now primary-secure even while the ceremonial `introducer` function remains one evidentiary step lower.

## 5. Carnap diary on 30 December — local intellectual context before the clash

The previous day's diary is useful field control.

On **30 December**, Carnap attended the probability symposium with Morris Cohen, F. S. C. Northrop, and William Savery; discussed objections to solipsism; gave a Baltimore *Morning Sun* interview explaining logical positivism; and discussed its relation to American philosophy with an interlocutor identified in the edition as Irving. At the official banquet he discussed anti-metaphysics with Arthur E. Murphy and heard the presidential address.

The diary therefore places the Lovejoy exchange inside an already active two-day process of:

`American philosophy mapping`
+ `anti-metaphysics explanation`
+ `meaning / scientific philosophy controversy`
+ `public positioning of logical positivism`.

This further weakens any reading of 31 December as an isolated spontaneous quarrel.

## 6. Quine's later recollection — independent actor-memory and methodological afterlife

Quine later recalled the Baltimore episode in `Homage to Carnap`, collected in *The Ways of Paradox and Other Essays*, revised and enlarged ed. (Harvard University Press, 1976), p. 42.

His recollection is explicit that Lovejoy made a strongly critical intervention and that Carnap replied by separating alternative interpretations of what Lovejoy might mean and deriving different consequences from each: roughly, if Lovejoy means A, then p; if B, then q.

The evidentiary roles should now be separated:

- **Carnap diary, 31 Dec. 1935:** occurrence, date, order, programme change, Lovejoy's objection as Carnap understood it, Carnap's extensive reply;
- **APA proceedings/programme:** formal listed title and session infrastructure; exact page still to retrieve directly;
- **Quine later recollection:** independent actor-memory of Carnap's reply style and later significance;
- **Verhaegh:** synthetic reconstruction, including the `Lovejoy introduced Carnap` claim and wider arrival narrative.

Coding:

- `direct interlocutor` — **direct-primary secure**;
- `boundary critic` — **direct-primary secure**;
- `substantive objection concerning conventionalism / metaphysical meaning` — **same-day actor précis secure, not verbatim Lovejoy text**;
- `infrastructural contact` — secure;
- `Lovejoy formally introduced Carnap` — strong secondary, original-function check still open;
- `doctrinal influence` — unproved;
- `school membership` — not warranted.

## 7. Historiographical afterlife — why the scene can mislead

Later retellings turned Baltimore into a miniature origin scene for `analytic philosophy in America`. Quine's language about moving with Carnap through the `metaphysicians' camp` makes the generational/dramatic sorting easy: Carnap and Quine become the analytic newcomers while Lovejoy becomes an older metaphysical opponent.

The direct-primary Lovejoy sequence in this repository complicates that retrospective dichotomy. By 1935 Lovejoy had long practiced formal-logical criticism, logical disaggregation, proposition-sensitive warrant control, and the separation of semantic, evidentiary, and formal-logical jurisdictions.

The event therefore should not be represented as:

`old metaphysics`
→ `new analysis`.

A better working model is:

> **a collision between competing analytical architectures and competing jurisdiction maps: what counts as meaning, what is conventional, what empirical procedures warrant, and what philosophical propositions may be excluded.**

That formulation remains interpretive. It is now, however, anchored in direct evidence that Lovejoy's actual Baltimore objection concerned conventionalism and the exclusion of metaphysics.

## 8. Connection to Lovejoy's 1930 operationism / meaning dispute

Verhaegh places Lovejoy five years earlier inside the relativity / operationism / meaning controversy. The 1930 paper argues against identifying the empirical procedure by which a concept is tested or evidenced with the content the concept means.

The chronological sequence can now be made more exact:

`1930 Lovejoy: experimental criterion / verifying experience ≠ conceptual meaning`

→ `1931 McGilvary reply`

→ `American controversy over meaning, criteria and scientific philosophy`

→ `1935 Carnap: Testability paper at Baltimore`

→ **`31 Dec. 1935 Lovejoy: conventionalism threatens the positivist exclusion of metaphysics`**

→ `Carnap replies at length`.

This makes Baltimore an extension of a documented problem-space rather than an accidental ceremonial contact.

## 9. Connection to the active `jurisdiction partition` hypothesis

Current Lovejoy sequence:

- 1908: logically independent theses under one movement-name must be assessed separately;
- 1909: historical complexes require logical analysis plus historical comparison;
- 1911: formal implication, meaning/intelligibility, evidence/verification, and historical truth have distinct jurisdictions;
- 1914: a purported deductive system can fail standards of logical adequacy;
- 1930: an experimental criterion or operation need not be identical with conceptual meaning;
- 1935: Lovejoy challenges what conventionalism licenses logical positivism to say about metaphysics;
- 1935: Carnap replies, remembered by Quine as splitting alternative meanings and conditional consequences.

The striking recurrence is not that Lovejoy and Carnap shared a doctrine. It is that both operate by controlling **which distinction licenses which consequence**.

Current research hypothesis:

> **The Baltimore clash may be reconstrued as a dispute between rival forms of jurisdiction partition rather than as analysis versus non-analysis.**

Warrant level: structural-homology / analyst hypothesis. No genealogy or influence claim.
