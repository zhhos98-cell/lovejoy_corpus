## MD-004 — Alice C. Fletcher, `The Import of the Totem` — RESOLVED

The complete Smithsonian Annual Report article is present, pp.577–586.

The article begins by sharply limiting its own scale. Fletcher states that the study concerns the Omaha totem and explicitly says there will be no attempt to treat totems `in a world sense`. She grounds this refusal in many years of research `within a limited area`, arguing that sufficiently close studies across tribes and races do not yet exist to warrant a final universal summing-up.

Her methodological description is also unusually explicit. She presents the work as first-hand research in which the investigator must guard against projecting her own mental atmosphere onto Indian thought. Because totemic implications are embedded in ordinary life and not easily objectified by informants, explanations are reconstructed indirectly from contradictions, small acts and sayings, and analysis of songs, rituals and ceremonies. The article then proceeds with high-resolution Omaha linguistic and social-structural material.

### Consequence for the project

This strengthens the source-politics argument beyond the earlier generic collaboration control:

> **Fletcher's own published object imposes a local source-scope ceiling before Lovejoy receives it.**

The article itself warns against world-scale totalization. Therefore, when local Omaha evidence later becomes evidence for a cross-cultural `primitive philosophy`, the scale expansion is demonstrably on the receiver/comparativist side rather than licensed by Fletcher's stated evidentiary scope.

Formal sole authorship remains secure. The present return does not by itself prove hidden sentence-level coauthorship, nor does it justify attributing particular passages to Francis La Flesche or Dorsey without separate documentary evidence.

## MD-005 — Marillier, `La survivance de l’âme et l’idée de justice...` — RESOLVED

The direct Marillier text is present and replaces the earlier dependence on page-addressed relay evidence.

Three controls are now direct-primary:

### Contact / contamination control

Marillier explicitly asks whether apparently moralized Indigenous afterlife doctrines are genuinely indigenous or instead the result of prolonged relations with Christians and Muslims. In the North American discussion he likewise considers the `happy hunting-grounds` image as possibly a transformed Christian paradise, noting that Indigenous groups had long been in contact with whites and could have borrowed and superadded the new conception to older beliefs.

This is direct evidence that the workshop's comparative method included a contamination/contact filter before treating a feature as an indigenous developmental datum.

### Differentiated determinants of post-mortem destiny

Marillier states that, across the traditions reviewed, the fate of souls is often determined not by the moral quality of earthly conduct but by variables including chance, arbitrary divine action, social rank, manner of death, and unequal skill or strength in overcoming dangers.

### Rites and practices are not equivalent to moral desert

He further states that acts affecting post-mortem destiny are often ceremonies, rites or magical practices rather than acts judged morally good or bad. Funeral treatment can determine whether a soul reaches a favorable destination. Death-type likewise structures destinations in multiple examples.

### Developmental moralization

At the end of the article Marillier describes a historical transformation in which belief in gods and future life, originally explanatory/metaphysical in function, becomes progressively moralized as societies grow more complex: morality develops, and gods are `moralized` along with humans.

### Consequence for 005

The determinant field and the distinction between mechanism/rite/status/death-type and moral desert are now directly secured upstream in Marillier. Lovejoy's documentary delta remains local selection, reallocation, compression and repeated testing rather than invention of the determinant categories.

It also treats `upādāna` in detail and presents the formula as historically/compositionally layered, including arguments about independent pieces, later conjunctions and reinterpretation.

A lexical pass over the OCR return produced **no Lovejoy hit**. Because OCR absence is not absolute proof of bibliographic absence, use the bounded formulation:

> **No Lovejoy citation surfaced in the present full-object OCR control, while Warren, Oldenberg, Senart and Deussen are explicitly present in the same interpretive discussion.**

This makes Lovejoy's 1898 intervention look, at minimum, non-canonical in this 1909 specialist synthesis. It does not prove that Oltramare never read Lovejoy.

A useful positive comparison is that Oltramare continues to analyze the formula through compositional history, doctrinal recombination and reinterpretation rather than by treating its present logical order as transparent evidence of a single chronological genesis.

## MD-010 — Dahlmann 1897 reception packet — PARTIAL

The *Wiener Zeitschrift für die Kunde des Morgenlandes* review range is present. The JRAS 29.2 (1897), 407–410 control is still missing.

The Wiener review strongly endorses Dahlmann's reconstruction of an older Sāṃkhya/Sāṃkhya-Yoga phase as a precursor to Buddhism. It explicitly describes Buddhist use of inherited concepts that fit imperfectly after transfer into a system lacking their original presuppositions, and treats the older Sāṃkhya phase as supplying the context in which the apparent contradiction becomes intelligible.

This is useful **controversy ecology only**. It shows that late-1890s Indological reception already had a live vocabulary of older source systems, transferred concepts, altered fit and historical reconstruction. It is not evidence that Lovejoy read this review.
