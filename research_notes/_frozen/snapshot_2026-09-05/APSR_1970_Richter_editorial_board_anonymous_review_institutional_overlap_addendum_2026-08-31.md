# APSR 1970: Richter editorial-board overlap and anonymous manuscript evaluation

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT FRONT-MATTER CONTROL / RANNEY-ERA INSTITUTIONAL OVERLAP LOCKED / PROCESS ARCHITECTURE LOCKED / INDIVIDUAL DECISION CONTROL OPEN**

## Core result

The 1967 CSPT publication-access anxiety around Austin Ranney must not be reconstructed as a simple opposition between an external political-theory camp and a behaviorally controlled APSR apparatus.

Direct APSR front matter from Volume 64, No. 3 (September 1970) lists **Melvin Richter** on the journal's Editorial Board under managing editor Austin Ranney. The same front matter states that manuscripts submitted to the Review were sent out anonymously for editorial evaluation.

This creates two important controls:

`PUBLICATION ANXIETY / METHOD DIFFERENCE`
!= `EXTERNALITY TO EDITORIAL APPARATUS`;

and

`FORMAL BOARD MEMBERSHIP`
!= `CONTROL OF INDIVIDUAL MANUSCRIPT DECISIONS`.

It also narrows the next evidentiary gate. Once board overlap and anonymous external/editorial evaluation are visible, explaining differential publication outcomes requires manuscript-routing, referee, recommendation, and decision evidence rather than factional membership alone.

---

## I. Direct 1970 board composition

The September 1970 APSR front matter identifies the editorial organization under Austin Ranney and includes among the Editorial Board:

- Austin Ranney — Managing Editor;
- Fred I. Greenstein;
- Harvey C. Mansfield;
- Warren E. Miller;
- Walter F. Murphy;
- **Melvin Richter**;
- John E. Turner;
- Vernon Van Dyke;
- Myron Weiner.

The editorial office is listed at North Hall, University of Wisconsin.

The point is not that this board was methodologically homogeneous. Quite the opposite: the board itself contains actors associated with sharply different kinds of political inquiry.

For the current CSPT/APSR problem, Richter is the high-value edge. Recent archival scholarship reconstructs him in 1967 as worrying that Ranney's accession to the APSR editorship could worsen publication prospects for political theorists. By 1970 Richter was formally inside the Ranney-era APSR editorial apparatus.

Therefore the sequence is:

`1967 RICHTER PUBLICATION ANXIETY ABOUT RANNEY-ERA APSR`
->
`1970 RICHTER ON RANNEY-ERA APSR EDITORIAL BOARD`.

This does not establish that Richter changed his view, that publication anxiety disappeared, or that he personally controlled political-theory submissions.

---

## II. The manuscript process explicitly included anonymous editorial evaluation

The same front matter instructs authors to submit manuscripts in duplicate and states that manuscripts are sent out **anonymously for editorial evaluation**.

This matters because later explanations of political-theory underrepresentation cannot be assigned automatically to the named managing editor or board as a corporate preference.

At minimum, the decision architecture included a manuscript-evaluation layer in which submitted work was circulated without author identification.

The safe process model is:

`SUBMISSION`
-> `ANONYMOUS EDITORIAL EVALUATION`
-> `EDITORIAL DECISION`.

The front matter does not reveal:

- number of evaluators per manuscript;
- evaluator identities;
- whether board members or outside referees were used in each case;
- field-specific reviewer pools;
- recommendation rules;
- revision rounds;
- rejection reasons;
- field-specific submission or acceptance rates.

Therefore:

`ANONYMOUS REVIEW ARCHITECTURE`
!= `KNOWN FAIRNESS OF EVERY DECISION`;

but also

`LOW FIELD REPRESENTATION`
!= `DIRECT MANAGING-EDITOR EXCLUSION`.

---

## III. Why institutional overlap matters for `embedded differentiation`

Richter's simultaneous position is structurally important.

He could participate in building CSPT as alternative/supplementary political-theory infrastructure while also sitting within APSR's editorial apparatus.

That is precisely the kind of relation the current project calls **embedded differentiation**:

`BUILDING SPECIALIZED COMMUNICATION INFRASTRUCTURE`
+
`CONTINUED PARTICIPATION IN PARENT-DISCIPLINE INFRASTRUCTURE`.

It is stronger evidence than merely showing that the two institutions existed at the same time.

But do not inflate it into a claim that CSPT had institutional control over APSR, or that Richter represented CSPT officially on the APSR board.

`PERSONAL / PROFESSIONAL OVERLAP`
!= `FORMAL ORGANIZATIONAL LINKAGE`.

## IV. Ranney 1970 and Polsby 1971–72 must remain separate editorial states

This direct 1970 board evidence belongs to the **Ranney** editorial state.

The direct `Present and Absent` census published in June 1972 belongs to the subsequent **Nelson Polsby** editorial regime and audits Volume 65 (1971).

Do not write:

`RICHTER SAT ON THE BOARD THAT PRODUCED THE 3/46 POLITICAL-PHILOSOPHY CENSUS`.

The evidence does not license that continuity.

Instead preserve:

`RANNEY 1970 BOARD / PROCESS STATE`
!=
`POLSBY 1971–72 BOARD / CENSUS STATE`.

The chronological continuity to test is the persistence or change of venue-capacity patterns across editorial transitions, not the persistence of one board roster.

---

## V. Relation to Ranney's later oral-history account

Ranney later recalled that criticism of his preference for formal theory was fair and that classical-theory manuscripts often produced unusually divergent referee evaluations.

The 1970 front matter supplies contemporary process architecture that makes the latter recollection historically plausible as a kind of mechanism: manuscripts were in fact sent out anonymously for editorial evaluation.

But the two sources remain different evidence levels:

`1970 CONTEMPORARY FRONT MATTER = PROCESS ARCHITECTURE`

`1991 ORAL HISTORY = RETROSPECTIVE ACCOUNT OF HOW THAT PROCESS SOMETIMES WORKED`.

Neither supplies the missing individual referee reports.

Thus the next proof burden remains unchanged:

> **To explain why classical/political-philosophy work was weakly represented, recover submission/referee/decision evidence rather than infer causation from board membership or published counts.**

## VI. Evidence ceiling and archive target

### Direct locked

- Richter is listed on the APSR Editorial Board in September 1970 under managing editor Austin Ranney;
- Harvey Mansfield is also on that board;
- manuscripts are described as being sent out anonymously for editorial evaluation;
- editorial office location is North Hall, University of Wisconsin.

### Not established

- Richter's role in any specific theory manuscript;
- whether he reviewed Wolin, Kalleberg, Sartori, or any other named author;
- whether he advocated for increased theory publication inside APSR;
- whether his 1967 anxiety changed by 1970;
- identities or reports of individual referees;
- field-specific acceptance rates.

### Highest-value next gate

Recipient-side or central manuscript files containing:

- APSR submission letters;
- referee reports;
- editorial decision letters;
- revision requests;
- correspondence with Ranney or editorial staff;
- theory-specific reviewer assignments.

The Sheldon Wolin papers are an especially promising recipient-side target because `Political Theory as a Vocation` moved from a 1968 CSPT paper into APSR publication in 1969. A direct APSR correspondence file, if present, would allow reconstruction of one successful theory manuscript route through the Ranney apparatus.

Do not assert that such a file exists until an item-level locator is found.
