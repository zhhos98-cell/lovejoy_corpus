## 2. Brinton 1897: the sacrifice sketch

Primary public text:

Daniel G. Brinton, *Religions of Primitive Peoples*, American Lectures on the History of Religions, Second Series 1896–1897 (New York: Putnam, 1897), Lecture V, `Primitive Religious Expression: In the Rite`, especially pp.186–191.

Public primary:

- https://www.gutenberg.org/files/61220/61220-h/61220-h.htm

### Brinton's sequence

#### A. Origin in alimentary / honorific gift

Brinton opens sacrifice from food offered to the divine visitant. First-fruits, the first ear of corn, and the young kid are free-will thank-offerings or `honorific` meals offered to the deity.

#### B. Papuan ancestor offering as illustration

He illustrates the grateful offering with Papuan ancestral worship: decorated skulls of deceased relatives receive food and trinkets in a joyous festival.

#### C. Development into piacular / propitiatory sacrifice

As loss and affliction appear, gods are imagined angry or neglectful; sacrifice becomes conciliatory, piacular, atoning and expiatory.

#### D. Cost / suffering escalates efficacy

The greater the sacrifice or self-denial, the greater its efficacy. Brinton invokes Germanic escalation from animals to men to the chief, then self-mutilation, scarification, scourging and human victims.

#### E. Sacrifice can magically constrain gods

A sufficiently exalted sacrifice can compel divine powers and extort the desired result with or without their volition.

#### F. Atonement resolves into communion

Brinton then makes the decisive aggregation move: piacular atonement is `in reality` union or communion with the god, and this lies largely at the base of ritual worship.

#### G. Agricultural blood/food rites become communion evidence

His American/German sequence is:

- Nicaragua: human sacrifice at maize gathering + worshippers' blood dropped on corn + corn eaten as holy food;
- Peru: victim's blood mixed with meal and baked into cakes distributed as sacred food;
- German harvest: corn spirit represented as animal/child, slain and eaten;
- Aztec rite: divine representative slain, blood mixed with dough and eaten, producing participation in the divine nature.

The final explanatory predicate is therefore not merely `sacrifice` but **communion / union with deity**.

### Brinton's footnote stack for this run

The 1897 notes identify the following immediate authorities:

- Jacob Grimm, *Teutonic Mythology*;
- A. B. Meyer in *Globus*;
- W. Robertson Smith for `honorific` / `piacular` terminology;
- Holtzmann, *Deutsche Mythologie*;
- Oviedo, *Historia de las Indias* for Nicaragua;
- Balboa, *Histoire du Pérou* for Peru;
- Frazer, *The Golden Bough* II for the German harvest/corn-spirit case;
- Sahagún, *Historia de la Nueva España* for the Aztec rite.

This footnote stack is useful for testing whether Lovejoy simply followed Brinton's citations.

## 3. Lovejoy 005 does not preserve Brinton's aggregation rule

The overlap is strongest at the level of problem/case cluster. The classification changes sharply.

| Brinton 1897 | Lovejoy 005 | Result |
|---|---|---|
| First-fruits are an early thank-offering / sacrifice | First-fruit rites must first be tested against purification, taboo and communal renewal | visible offering ≠ sacrifice by default |
| Ancestor food offering illustrates honorific sacrifice | Funerary offerings can be provision, social continuity, loyalty, transaction, propitiation or other mechanisms | ancestor offering decomposed |
| Piacular sacrifice is a developmental second form | Propitiatory/expiatory sacrifice is common, but is not allowed to absorb funerary, magical or alimentary forms | type retained, hierarchy refused |
| Bodily pain/self-mutilation belongs to sacrifice by costly self-denial | Circumcision, beating, scarification and bloodletting do not by themselves establish sacrifice | injury ≠ sacrificial membership criterion |
| Magical efficacy can constrain the gods | Some rites operate directly on forces of nature without addressing or constraining personal gods | efficacy detached from personality |
| Blood + grain / harvest victim supports divine communion | Agricultural blood/seed rites can be fecundative transfer of life/power; plant-soul sacrifice can be quasi-mechanical/magical | agricultural rite recoded by causal mechanism |
| Atonement is in reality communion; union lies broadly at base of ritual worship | Alimentariy and expiatory/propitiatory sacrifice are common; sacramental union is comparatively exceptional | direct reversal of aggregation thickness |

The strongest internal controls are:

- MS p.129: direct action on natural forces rather than divine wills;
- MS p.131: similar rites need not share one original type; syncretism can later combine distinct motives;
- MS pp.185–195 / PDF pp.91–105: first-fruit and agricultural rites are repartitioned among purification, magical, propitiatory, alimentary, fecundative, mystical and sacramental mechanisms;
- PDF p.105: sacrifice of the plant-soul to itself is `quasi-mechanical and magical rather than sacramental`;
- PDF p.117: alimentary and expiatory/propitiatory forms common, sacramental union comparatively exceptional.

This is not merely a different answer to `what is sacrifice?` It is a different **identity jurisprudence** for ritual evidence.

## 5. Rival candidate: Brinton 1876, *The Religious Sentiment*

Brinton's *The Religious Sentiment: Its Source and Aim* (1876), chapter VI, contains an earlier theory of rites and sacrifice.

Public primary:

- https://www.gutenberg.org/files/30061/30061-h/30061-h.htm

Its sacrifice architecture is:

- rites divided broadly into propitiatory versus memorial/institutionary;
- sacrifice defined as a gift;
- value measured by the pain/self-denial incurred by the giver;
- German/Gaul human sacrifice and Cybele self-mutilation;
- specific performance, propitiation and vicarious sacrifice;
- sacrifice linked to sin and atonement.

This overlaps significantly with 005's discussion of human sacrifice, self-injury and propitiation. It remains an important **background Brinton control**.

But it is a weaker content match for the specific 005 sacrifice dossier than the 1897 Lecture V because it lacks the distinctive later cluster:

`first-fruits`
+
`Nicaragua / Peru blood-and-grain`
+
`harvest/corn spirit`
+
`Aztec sacramental meal`
+
`explicit communion as the broad ritual invariant`.

Thus current ranking is:

1. **Brinton 1897 Lecture V — strongest content-level candidate/comparator**;
2. Brinton 1876 chapter VI — important earlier theory/background, weaker match;
3. unknown Brinton periodical item — remains open because the manuscript transcription itself says `article [title uncertain]`.

The exact item cannot be declared closed until MS p.125 is re-inspected at image level or another bibliographic clue surfaces.

### 1906 primitive energetics

`heterogeneous rites + vocabularies + regional materials`

So the important continuity is not simply `Lovejoy became a splitter` or `Lovejoy became a lumper`.

It is:

> **classification is relation-dependent. A broad inherited category can be decomposed when its tokens fail a mechanism test; a new category can be constructed when a thinner causal predicate recurs across otherwise heterogeneous tokens.**

This helps explain why decomposition and macro-generalization coexist in the same early Lovejoy corpus.
