# Lovejoy 1898 — publication transformation as claim architecture: four trajectories

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **CANONICAL PRIVATE/PUBLIC TYPOLOGY / DIRECT NOTEBOOK–JAOS PAIR**

## Governing result

The transformation from notebook 004 to Lovejoy's 1898 JAOS article cannot be described by one directional formula such as:

- publication makes private notes more confident;
- publication makes private notes more cautious;
- question marks simply become modal hedges.

The direct concordance supports at least **four different trajectories**, depending on the scale, relation, available warrant, and publication burden of the proposition.

> **Publication transforms claim architecture, not merely tone.**

The four current trajectories are:

1. **exploratory question → public assertion**;
2. **strong local judgment → strong local judgment retained**;
3. **strong local judgment → higher-scale inferential cap**;
4. **high-scale exploratory hypothesis → deletion / softening / burden removal**.

These trajectories coexist inside the same notebook/article pair.

---

# I. Trajectory 1 — exploratory question → public assertion

## Notebook: `Upādāna, Relation to Karma`

Notebook 004, PDF p.34 / MS p.97, frames the distinction as an exploratory question, with Hardy as source prompt:

> `May ... be called [the] cause of [the] fact of continued existence, & karma [the] cause of [the] manner of continued existence?`

The exact first abbreviation before `cause of the fact` remains a medium-confidence transcription, but the fact/manner distinction and interrogative form are secure enough for internal analytical use.

### Private epistemic status

- candidate causal partition;
- relation under active test;
- proposition not yet written as a conclusion.

## Print: p.130

The published article converts this into a direct causal distinction.

Lovejoy says, in substance:

- karma, in the relevant special sense, causes the **particular condition** in which one is reborn;
- `upādāna` causes the **fact of rebirth in itself / rebirth as such**.

The private candidate distinction therefore becomes a published explanatory rule.

### Transformation

`Q: fact vs manner?`
→
`assertion: fact of rebirth vs particular condition of rebirth`.

This is genuine **epistemic sharpening** from notebook to print.

### Important nuance

The wording is not simply copied. The publication improves the conceptual specification:

`manner of continued existence`
→
`particular condition of rebirth`.

Thus public assertion follows not merely from increased confidence but from a more exact formulation of the relation.

# II. Trajectory 2 — strong local judgment → strong local judgment retained

## Notebook: MS p.173 / PDF p.66

In the `anupādisesa` passage, Lovejoy writes:

- `No one familiar with Buddhist modes of thought could suppose...` physical death is meant;
- `Clearly` the term here means the moral condition of freedom from attachment;
- across the three relevant *Sutta-Nipāta* passages, the ethical reading is also stated strongly.

## Print: p.135

The article carries the passage over almost verbatim:

- `No one familiar...`;
- death as *summum bonum* is the least Buddhistic interpretation;
- `Plainly` the word in this passage has the moral/ethical sense.

### Transformation

`Clearly`
→
`Plainly`.

The local semantic judgment is **not softened** by publication.

This blocks a generalized model in which scholarly print necessarily dilutes notebook certainty.

---

# III. Trajectory 3 — strong local judgment → higher-scale inferential cap

The `upādisesa` section simultaneously exhibits a different transformation at a wider scale.

## Local level

Specific passages receive high-confidence interpretation.

## Corpus-wide/general level

Printed p.134 says:

- both etymological derivations are possible;
- usage must therefore decide rather than etymology;
- the selected points are `relevant but not necessarily conclusive`.

Printed p.136 says:

- the oldest and probably most numerous texts favor one interpretation;
- contrary passages can nevertheless be cited;
- the matter `cannot be regarded as finally settled`.

### Transformation

This is not local de-confidence.

It is **scope control**:

`strong passage-level interpretation`
↛
`universal corpus-level closure`.

Publication makes the inferential firewall explicit.

### Confidence structure

- `C_this witness = high`;
- `C_local cluster = high`;
- `C_distributional preponderance = qualified positive`;
- `C_final universal meaning = withheld`.

Thus public epistemology is nested by scale.

# IV. Trajectory 4 — high-scale exploratory hypothesis → deletion / softening

The notebook contains substantially larger historical and metaphysical claims than the article assumes as its publication burden.

## A. Broad Sāṃkhya/Buddhism chronology and system priority

Notebook 004 explores:

- chronology of Sāṃkhya and Buddhism;
- whether recognizable doctrines establish the existence of the later Sāṃkhya system as a whole;
- more diagnostic criteria of system identity.

Most of this disappears from the 1898 article.

## B. Original versus derived elements

The notebook closes with an explicit historical anatomy:

- original elements;
- derived elements.

The published article does not print this broad stratification as a settled historical model.

Instead it retains a narrower proposition:

> terms, perhaps even ideas, may be borrowed while `arrangement and application` remain original and characteristic.

## C. Stronger Nirvāṇa metaphysics

The notebook can state a broad conclusion about absolute cessation of existence and says, revealingly, that this is enough for the purposes of the paper while the more philosophical issue need not be pursued.

The article narrows the published burden to the technical semantics of `upādisesa/anupādisesa` and acknowledges contrary passages.

### Transformation

`high-scale exploratory historical/metaphysical proposition`
→
`remove from burden / narrow / retain only locally controllable derivative claim`.

This is not ordinary hedging. The proposition itself changes status within the architecture of the publication.

# V. Four-trajectory matrix

| Trajectory | Notebook state | Print state | Example | Epistemic operation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 sharpen | question / candidate relation | explicit assertion | karma vs upādāna: fact/manner → fact/particular condition | proof completed / relation specified |
| T2 retain | strong local conclusion | strong local conclusion | `Clearly` → `Plainly` on anupādisesa | confidence preserved |
| T3 cap | strong local readings + broad problem | local strength retained, global closure withheld | upādisesa pp.134–136 | inferential scope restricted |
| T4 prune | broad/high-scale hypothesis | omitted, softened, or replaced by narrower claim | Sāṃkhya chronology; original/derived; Nirvāṇa metaphysics | publication burden reduced |

The same article can therefore be more assertive, equally assertive, more explicitly provisional, and silent relative to the notebook — depending on the proposition.

# VI. Publication operates on scale × relation × warrant

The four trajectories map directly onto the current governing model.

## Scale

What level is being claimed?

- passage;
- term;
- causal function;
- whole formula;
- whole system;
- genealogy;
- metaphysical conclusion.

## Relation

What relation is at stake?

- semantic identity;
- causal role;
- temporal relation;
- borrowing/provenance;
- configuration;
- historical priority;
- metaphysical implication.

## Warrant

What does the evidence support at that scale?

Publication then determines how far the claim can be publicly promoted.

The best general rule is now:

> **public assertion strength is proposition-specific and conditioned by both relation type and scale of inference.**

---

# VII. Publication as an inferential promotion system

A useful way to describe the notebook/article difference is to treat the notebook as a field of candidate propositions and the article as a system of **promotion decisions**.

Possible outcomes:

- candidate relation is promoted to assertion;
- strong local judgment is admitted unchanged;
- local evidence is admitted but denied higher-scale promotion;
- broad claim is excluded from the publication burden.

Analyst model:

`candidate claim`
→ `scale/relation typing`
→ `warrant test`
→ `promotion / retention / cap / exclusion`.

This is not Lovejoy's actor vocabulary.

# IX. Strong project consequence

The branch can now formulate Lovejoy's early proof practice more precisely:

> **The recurrent operation is not skepticism and not decomposition alone. It is controlled promotion of claims: evidence sufficient for one relation or scale is not automatically sufficient for a stronger or wider proposition.**

This is visible in 1898 before later explicit logical/historical methodological statements.

It should still be attributed modestly. The constituent proof practices were available in contemporary philology/Indology. The documentary Lovejoy-specific result is the way he selects, reassigns, and publicly calibrates them in this notebook/article pair.
