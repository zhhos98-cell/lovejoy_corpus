# Batch 62 — Mauss 1902 explicitly reconstructs the Marillier workshop method; 2025 historiography confirms the classification/criticism profile

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: continue the Paris/Marillier line after Batch 61, prioritizing newly surfaced historiography and primary evidence for seminar/research practice rather than another thematic concordance.

## Core result

This round materially upgrades the 005 documentary-workshop argument.

Until now, the strongest reconstruction of Marillier's seminar procedure depended on a conjunction of:

- EPHE annual reports;
- N. W. Thomas's description of pupil work, lecturer comment and discussion;
- Söderblom's explicit receipt/use of Marillier notes;
- Marillier dossier/household-paper evidence;
- Lovejoy's own notebook structure and exact-page source use.

We now have a much more explicit retrospective statement from **Marcel Mauss himself**, Marillier's former student and immediate successor at the EPHE.

In his opening lecture of **27 January 1902**, printed in *Revue de l'histoire des religions* 45, pp.36–55, Mauss says that in the **critique des faits** he only has to follow Marillier's example. He then specifies the inherited research procedure:

1. train students in bibliographic research, **`exhaustive autant que possible`**;
2. compensate collectively for the scattered and poorly known ethnographic source base through **`un labeur commun`**;
3. exercise critical faculties **in common**;
4. devote one conference to close analysis and examination of documents concerning a defined class of facts in specified societies;
5. make it explicitly a **`travail de séminaire que nous ferons ensemble`**;
6. read ethnographic documents collectively, in a way Mauss compares to a philological conference, generating the critical hypotheses needed to recover the underlying fact.

This is extremely close to the paper-based research workshop model reconstructed from notebook 005. The existence of that workflow no longer needs to be inferred mainly from later/adjacent witnesses: **Marillier's successor explicitly identifies it as the example inherited from Marillier.**

The second new control is Pascal Le Maléfan's 2025 Bérose essay, which summarizes Marillier's scientific profile through the same Maussian testimony: Marillier's work was above all one of **examination and classification**; he scrutinized traveller/ethnographer testimony, the observer's position, theories, arguments and interpretations, especially through reviews and courses. Le Maléfan simultaneously emphasizes the major discontinuity: Marillier remained committed to a psychological/evolutionist explanatory frame, whereas Mauss sought a sociological one.

This distinction is important for Lovejoy. It lets us separate two layers more securely:

> **Marillier's documentary-critical/classificatory practice** can be transmitted without implying acceptance of **Marillier's psychological/evolutionist theory**.

That is almost exactly what the Lovejoy 005 evidence needs.

## 1. Primary source: Mauss's 1902 opening lecture

Original publication metadata now controlled via JSTOR:

Marcel Mauss, `L'enseignement de l'histoire des religions des peuples non-civilisés à l'École des Hautes Études`, opening lecture of 27 January 1902, *Revue de l'histoire des religions* 45 (1902), 36–55.

JSTOR volume:  
`https://www.jstor.org/stable/i23657016`

Stable article:  
`https://www.jstor.org/stable/23661805`

The searchable reproduction of the text gives the crucial methodological sequence. Publication quotation should ultimately be checked against the original page image/XML, but the wording is stable across the 1969 Mauss reprint and later reproductions.

### A. Explicit attribution to Marillier

Mauss says, in substance and with a short directly recoverable phrase:

> `en ce qui concerne la critique des faits, je n'aurai qu'à suivre l'exemple que M. Marillier nous a donné.`

This is much stronger than a generic tribute. The inherited object is specifically **critique of evidence/facts**.

### B. Bibliography as collective infrastructure

Mauss immediately says students will be trained in bibliographic research **`exhaustive autant que possible`** because ethnographic facts are scattered and the source field remains badly known.

He describes the remedy as **`un labeur commun`** intended to build the bibliographic instrument that ethnography lacks.

This is directly relevant to 005's form:

- opening bibliography;
- recurring book/page citations;
- source clusters rather than continuous lecture prose;
- inserted/different paper units;
- parallel use of ethnographies across multiple classificatory questions.

It supports reading bibliography not as decorative reading list but as part of seminar research production.

### C. Critical faculties exercised in common

Mauss says the group will next exercise **`en commun, nos facultés critiques`**.

One conference will be devoted to close analysis/examination of documents referring to a defined class of facts in specified societies.

This matters because the 1898–99 EPHE report's thematic blocks can now be connected to a documented **procedure**, not just to subjects.

### D. Explicit `travail de séminaire`

Mauss calls this:

> `un travail de séminaire que nous ferons ensemble`.

He describes collective reading of ethnographic texts and compares it to a **philological conference**: the group should generate all necessary critical hypotheses to recover the real fact behind the report.

That is a nearly exact institutional description of the methodological move repeatedly visible in 005:

`reported ethnographic form → source/observer criticism → alternative hypotheses → reclassification by determinant/motive/mechanism`.

### E. Difference in explanation retained

Mauss then explicitly breaks with the anthropological/psychological methods Marillier had adopted. He wants religious facts explained by religious/social facts and stresses immediately determining causes rather than general psychological motives.

But he ends by conceding that Marillier himself often connected a rite/belief to collective-life facts; Mauss presents his own move as elevating procedures Marillier had already used into a necessary principle.

Therefore the 1902 text itself contains **continuity and rupture simultaneously**:

- continuity in documentary criticism, exhaustive bibliography, collective seminar analysis, classification and some contextual explanation;
- rupture in the preferred explanatory ontology: psychological/evolutionary versus sociological/collective.

This is exactly the distinction our Lovejoy paper needs to preserve.

## 2. Independent institutional control: EPHE report 1901–02

Persée preserves Mauss's first report after succeeding Marillier:

`https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1901_num_15_11_19570`

The report says Mauss **`s'est efforcé de maintenir les traditions que son maître, M. Marillier, avait implantées dans ce genre d'études`**.

It then records:

- `Étude critique de documents` concerning Melanesian magic;
- pupil participation in explanation/discussion;
- scientific seminar work;
- students being trained in the principal procedures of ethnographic bibliography.

This is institutional corroboration of the 1902 lecture's retrospective description.

By 1902–03 the EPHE report again describes the Tuesday conference as **analytical and critical explanation of ethnographic texts** and says the group intentionally drew no conclusion before first determining the value and accuracy of each report:

`https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1902_num_16_12_19593`

The continuity in seminar mechanics is therefore not merely rhetorical in an inaugural lecture; it is visible in the actual teaching reports immediately after Marillier.

## 3. New historiographic control: Pascal Le Maléfan 2025

Full bibliographic identity:

Pascal Le Maléfan, `Prédécesseur “maudit” de Marcel Mauss : Léon Marillier, psychologue des religions`, *Bérose — Encyclopédie internationale des histoires de l'anthropologie* (2025), article 3892.

Searchable full-text route currently exposed through ResearchGate:

`https://www.researchgate.net/publication/390921671_Predecesseur_maudit_de_Marcel_Mauss_Leon_Marillier_psychologue_des_religions`

The essay is explicitly presented as filling a gap in the history of the human sciences: Marillier is often cited but remains poorly known.

### A. What Le Maléfan already establishes

Le Maléfan foregrounds Marillier as a French representative/transmitter of English Tylorian religious anthropology and reconstructs his method as both historical and psychological.

More important for us, he quotes Mauss's debt:

> `M. Marillier nous apprit la critique des faits.`

He characterizes Marillier's work as **`une œuvre considérable d'examen et de classement`** and specifies three dimensions:

- examination of traveller/ethnographer reports;
- sustained criticism of the observer's position;
- examination of theories, argumentation and interpretations through reviews and teaching.

This independently validates the vocabulary we have been using for 005: **documentary criticism + classification**, rather than transmission of a finished theory.

### B. What Le Maléfan prevents us from saying

The same essay insists that Marillier's explanatory theory remained psychological and evolutionary. He sought general intellectual/emotional laws of the mind and retained a progressive evolutionary history of religious complexity.

Mauss's critique is therefore not cosmetic. The Durkheimian/sociological school rejects or transforms major parts of Marillier's explanatory framework.

Consequently the Lovejoy paper should not present `the Marillier method` as one seamless package transmitted to Mauss and Lovejoy.

Better decomposition:

**Marillier research practice**
- source criticism;
- bibliographic exhaustiveness;
- document collection;
- close examination;
- classification;
- comparison;
- collective seminar work.

**Marillier explanatory commitments**
- psychological laws;
- Tylorian/intellectualist anthropology;
- survivance;
- evolutionary hierarchy/progression;
- emotion/sentiment in religion.

Mauss can inherit much of the first while sharply revising the second.

Lovejoy can likewise participate in the first without the paper needing to claim wholesale adoption of the second.

## 4. Why this is a structural upgrade for 005

The existing one-notebook paper architecture already argued:

> documents → dossiers → seminar problems → competing classifications → student redivision → later abstraction.

Before this round, a skeptical reader could still ask whether `seminar workshop` was too much reconstruction from scattered traces.

That objection is now much weaker.

Mauss, speaking as Marillier's former student and successor, explicitly describes the inherited model as:

> **exhaustive bibliography → collective source work → common critical exercise → close document analysis → seminar research.**

Lovejoy is independently documented as an **active auditor** in Marillier's 1898–99 conferences, and 005 independently preserves exactly the material form one would expect from such work: bibliographies, page-specific extracts, dated/course-linked slips, clusters of cases, explicit reclassification and source criticism.

We still cannot say every notebook page was produced during a classroom meeting or that every book was assigned by Marillier. But the **genre-level production model** is now very strongly controlled.

## 6. Consequence for the Mauss–Lovejoy comparison

This round makes the relation cleaner rather than more direct.

Do **not** upgrade to `Mauss influenced Lovejoy`.

Instead distinguish:

### Shared inherited research grammar

Both can be situated in a Marillier environment/practice emphasizing:

- bibliographic collection;
- source criticism;
- examination/classification;
- collective/seminar analysis;
- skepticism toward taking reports at face value.

### Divergent explanatory systems

- **Mauss:** increasingly social/collective explanation; later generic sacrificial/social mechanisms.
- **Lovejoy 005:** plural motive/causal-bearer distinctions, later abstracted toward impersonal/transferable efficacy in 1906.
- **Marillier:** psychological/evolutionary explanatory framework, despite substantial documentary and classificatory criticism.

Thus the useful historical claim is not lineal influence but **differential appropriation of a shared critical-documentary research grammar**.

## 7. A stronger explanation of the phrase `classificatory labor`

Batch 59, after Meylan, proposed moving from a genealogy of published concepts to a microhistory of classificatory operations.

Le Maléfan/Mauss now gives that phrase an unusually period-grounded basis. `Classification` is not merely our retrospective analytical vocabulary: Mauss himself characterizes Marillier's achievement as examination/classification, and his successor seminar operationalizes that through exhaustive source work and critical document analysis.

This means the paper can make a more exact historiographic move:

> **Lovejoy's notebook is valuable not because it reveals a hidden Marillier theory but because it preserves one student's classificatory labor inside a research culture whose contemporaries explicitly described its master as a critic and classifier of facts.**

That is much safer than claiming a hidden genealogy of `unit-ideas`, and more documentary than a generic `French influence` story.

## 8. New source relationship worth noting: Marillier as RHR codirector

The current EPHE prosopographic notice states that Marillier became **codirector with Jean Réville of the *Revue de l'histoire des religions* from 1896** and co-secretary of the organizing commission for the 1900 International Congress of History of Religions.

Official EPHE prosopography:

`https://prosopo.ephe.psl.eu/l%C3%A9on-marillier`

This strengthens Batch 61's interpretation of RHR as part of Marillier's active documentary/review infrastructure: he was not simply a contributor whose totemism articles happened to share issues with reviews of Rhys Davids/Trumbull. He occupied an editorial role in the journal from 1896.

Still, editorial responsibility does **not** identify the anonymous reviewers of those individual notices and does not establish Lovejoy's reading route.
