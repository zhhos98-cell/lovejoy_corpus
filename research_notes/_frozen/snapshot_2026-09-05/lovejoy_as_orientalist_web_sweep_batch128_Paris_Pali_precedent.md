# Batch 128 — Paris Pāli precedent: Lovejoy's expected course now has a recent institutional genealogy

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: continue the Paris 1898–99 digital sweep while the user retrieves dynamic-viewer files. This pass asks a narrower question raised by Wilson's letter-based reconstruction: **why would Lovejoy have expected to take Pāli Buddhism with Sylvain Lévi in 1898–99?**

## Core result

The expectation no longer looks like an isolated biographical wish. A strong recent Paris teaching precedent is now visible.

Marcel Mauss's 1935 memorial for Sylvain Lévi recalls his own Indianist initiation after he entered the Lévi/Finot orbit in the mid-1890s. After describing the first stage of severe Sanskrit grammar, chrestomathy, exercises, versions and themes, Mauss says that **"the following year" Lévi together with Louis Finot initiated them into Pāli and Buddhist translation**, while Lévi separately guided them through Manu and Hindu theatre. Modern institutional history dates Mauss's first meeting with Lévi to 1895, making c.1896–97 the natural calendar reading of this retrospective sequence.

This is not yet the same thing as proving a formally advertised course entitled `Pāli Buddhism` in the 1896–97 programme. The annual-report/facsimile reconciliation is still open. But it does establish that **Pāli + Buddhist translation belonged to the recent teaching repertoire of the exact Lévi–Finot circle from which Lovejoy later sought instruction.**

That materially changes the interpretation of Wilson's statement. Lovejoy's reported plan to take Pāli Buddhism with Lévi can now be treated as institutionally plausible and historically specific, rather than as a vague label imposed by a later biographer.

---

## 1. First-stage control: the official 1895–96 Sanskrit report matches Mauss's retrospective structure

The IVe Section annual report for 1895–96 states that Louis Finot handled grammatical theory and slow analysis of elementary texts for beginners, while Sylvain Lévi trained the newcomers in rapid Sanskrit reading and used commentary to introduce fundamental notions of Indian history, religion and literature.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1896_num_1_1_2053

This is an unusually good institutional control on Mauss's later memory of being told by Lévi to submit first to Finot's severe elementary Sanskrit discipline while Lévi simultaneously trained the group through chrestomathy and Indianist commentary.

It means the retrospective passage is not floating free of the contemporary institutional record: its **first stage** maps closely onto the published annual report.

---

## 2. Mauss's second-stage recollection: Pāli and Buddhist translation

Mauss's memorial `Sylvain Lévi` (1935), reprinted in *Œuvres*, III (1969), pp.537–538, states that after the initial Sanskrit stage:

> the following year Lévi, together with Finot, initiated them into Pāli and Buddhist translation; Lévi alone guided them in Manu and Hindu theatre.

The passage is quoted and chronologically contextualized in Roland Lardinois's institutional history:

https://books.openedition.org/editionscnrs/60559

Lardinois places the first Mauss–Lévi meeting in 1895. On that chronology, `l'année suivante` naturally points to the following phase around 1896–97.

### Evidence discipline

The following claims are currently different in strength:

- **secure:** Mauss retrospectively testified that Lévi and Finot initiated his group into Pāli and Buddhist translation;
- **strong chronological inference:** this belongs to the phase after his 1895 entry into Lévi's orbit, likely c.1896–97;
- **not yet secure:** that the public EPHE programme formally advertised a course under the title `Pāli`, `Pāli Buddhism`, or `Buddhist translation` in that exact year.

The last point requires direct annual-report/facsimile reconciliation rather than inference from Mauss's memoir.

---

## 3. Mauss is independently fixed inside the same cross-sectional Indianist ecology in 1896–97

The Ve Section report for 1896–97 records that Alfred Foucher was away on mission and, on Sylvain Lévi's proposal, Louis Finot replaced him. Finot's conference concerned the metaphysical principles of Vedānta exegesis. The same report explicitly lists **M. Mauss** among the `élèves titulaires`.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1896_num_10_6_19458

This course itself was Vedānta, not Pāli. Its importance is structural: Mauss was circulating across the same IVe/Vᵉ Section Indianist teaching network in the likely Pāli-precedent period. Therefore his memoir is describing an institutional ecology in which Lévi, Finot, Foucher and their students crossed language, textual and history-of-religions instruction rather than occupying sealed course silos.

---

## 4. Personalized teaching is itself historically documented in this circle

Lardinois also quotes Mauss saying that Lévi's 1896–97 Brāhmaṇa/sacrifice course was personally directed to him and became the basis for Lévi's *La doctrine du sacrifice dans les Brâhmanas* and for the Hubert–Mauss sacrifice project.

This matters methodologically for the Lovejoy problem. It proves that we should not assume:

`published annual course title = totality of what Lévi taught an individual student`.

The Lévi circle could include regular conferences, supplementary work and individualized instruction. Thus even if the 1896–97 annual summary does not give an obvious `Pāli` heading, that would not by itself falsify Mauss's retrospective account. The same caution applies in 1898–99 when Wilson says Lovejoy occasionally read sacred texts privately with Lévi.

---

## 5. Finot and Foucher's own 1898 description explains why institutional labels are porous

A major contemporary source is Louis Finot and Alfred Foucher, `L'enseignement du sanscrit à l'école pratique des Hautes Études`, *Revue internationale de l'enseignement* 35 (Jan.–Jun. 1898), pp.488–492.

Primary digital text:

https://education.persee.fr/doc/revin_1775-6014_1898_num_35_1_3636

They explicitly explain that Sanskrit teaching operated in **both** the historical-philological and religious-sciences sections. Only the philological conference taught Sanskrit directly `for itself`, but the study of Indian religions could not dispense with the sacred language. They say that the two forms of teaching differed more in the orientation of the commentary — philological in one case, religious in the other — than in the texts selected, and that historically both forms had been entrusted to Sylvain Lévi.

This is decisive for interpreting Lovejoy's wording as reconstructed by Wilson. A late-nineteenth-century student saying he wanted `Pāli Buddhism with Lévi` need not be referring to a neatly bounded modern course catalogue category. The institutional practice explicitly braided language, textual explanation and religious history across sections.

## 6. The 1897–99 interruption makes Lovejoy's disappointment historically intelligible

The exact primary sequence is now:

### 1897–98

The IVe Section report says:

`M. S. Lévi, chargé d'une mission dans l'Inde, n'a pu diriger aucune conférence pendant l'année scolaire 1897-98.`

Finot gave three conferences weekly.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1899_num_1_1_2200

### 1898–99

The next report has Lévi back. His regular conferences were the Bhagavad-Gītā and a continuous survey of Indian literature; Stickney is named as a very active participant in the Bhagavad-Gītā work.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0001_1900_num_1_1_2251

The recovered Ve Section Indian-religions programme for Lovejoy's year likewise points to Foucher's Nyāya/Manu teaching and Stickney's Sāṃkhya/Garbe series, not to a formal Pāli-Buddhism offering.

Therefore the present model is:

`recent Lévi–Finot Pāli/Buddhist teaching precedent`

→ `1897–98 Lévi entirely absent on Asian mission`

→ `1898–99 Lévi returns, but his formal regular offering is reconfigured around Bhagavad-Gītā + Indian literature`

→ `Lovejoy reportedly arrives expecting Pāli-Buddhist study, finds the desired course unavailable, and instead occasionally reads sacred texts privately with Lévi`.

The first three steps are independently supported outside Wilson. The fourth still depends on Wilson's use of Lovejoy's 1898 letters until MS-0873 or another letter witness is recovered.

## 7. Immediate next-year control: Buddhism returns in another institutional configuration

The Ve Section report for 1899–1900 shows Foucher teaching the archaeology of Buddhism alongside continued Manu work, with an additional Nyāya philosophical-text reading added at Hubert's request.

Primary source:

https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1899_num_13_9_19527

This is not evidence for Lovejoy's attendance. It is a programme-rotation control. Buddhist subject matter moved in and out of the regular offerings and could be configured as archaeology, textual philology, Pāli, Sanskrit Buddhist literature or history of religions. The absence of the exact desired offering in 1898–99 therefore belongs to a rotating teaching ecology rather than to an institutional absence of Buddhist studies.

## 8. What this changes in the paper-level claim

Before this pass the safest formulation was:

> Lovejoy reportedly hoped to take Pāli Buddhism with Sylvain Lévi, but the exact institutional meaning of that plan was unresolved.

The evidence now permits a stronger formulation:

> **Lovejoy's reported expectation of Pāli-Buddhist study with Sylvain Lévi had a recent precedent in the Lévi–Finot teaching circle. Marcel Mauss later recalled that after his initial Sanskrit training Lévi and Finot initiated their group into Pāli and Buddhist translation. Contemporary EPHE evidence confirms the underlying Lévi–Finot training structure, while the 1898–99 official programme shows Lévi newly returned from mission but teaching Bhagavad-Gītā and Indian literature rather than the desired Pāli course. Lovejoy's disappointment therefore fits a documented change in the Paris Indianist teaching cycle, although the exact course he expected and the texts he read privately still await his letters.**

Short form:

> **The missing Pāli course had a teaching genealogy.**
