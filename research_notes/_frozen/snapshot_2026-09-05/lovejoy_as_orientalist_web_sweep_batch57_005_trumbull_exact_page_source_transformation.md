## 2. Trumbull pp.118–129 reconstruct the reading sequence behind Lovejoy pp.85–86

The primary text makes the manuscript sequence unusually intelligible.

### Trumbull p.118 — blood as life-bearing and curative

Trumbull discusses the use of human blood in the treatment of disease, including blood-baths associated with leprosy, and interprets such practices through the life-bearing power of blood. The conceptual formula is transferred blood → transferred life.

Lovejoy begins his explicit Trumbull extract at exactly this page and records the life-giving/curative problem.

### Trumbull pp.124–126 — rejuvenation and prophylactic covering

A few pages later Trumbull gathers cases in which blood is taken for renewed vitality, including the Louis XI story about the blood of children. He then moves to protection: warriors are washed in blood against wounds, with the blood functioning as a kind of outer covering of life.

Lovejoy’s p.85–86 notes reproduce this movement from curative/rejuvenating blood to blood against supernatural danger and to an `efficacious covering` of the person.

### Trumbull pp.126–129 — a new nature through new blood

Trumbull then generalizes the material as acquisition of another’s life/nature. The examples proceed through ingestion or transfer of blood/body matter, courage and strength obtained from an adversary, and related forms of life/quality appropriation.

Lovejoy’s p.86 moves in the same direction: blood, eyes, bones and selected body parts can confer sight, intelligence, strength, health or other qualities.

### Trumbull p.129 — Ashantee case

Trumbull’s printed p.129 contains the Ashantee example in which fetishmen use the hearts and blood of enemies with consecrated herbs in a rite intended to vivify or strengthen the conquerors.

Lovejoy’s explicit `(quot. Trumbull p.129)` occurs in precisely this Ashantee/Gold-Coast and transferable-quality cluster.

The cumulative pattern is therefore not merely `p.118 cited once` + `p.129 cited once`. The intervening examples also occur in the same source order. This is strong evidence for sequential reading/extraction.

## 3. The decisive transformation: Lovejoy changes the evidentiary status of Trumbull’s facts

Trumbull’s organizing theory is expansive. His book treats blood as life; the transfer, sharing or commingling of blood therefore creates transfer or inter-union of life. Blood covenant, blood alliance, sacramental blood and communion can be placed inside one large explanatory family.

Lovejoy uses the same evidence differently.

### In Trumbull

`blood/body transfer → life transfer → covenantal union / inter-communion`

### In Lovejoy 005

`blood/body transfer → several possible mechanisms`

including:

- curative efficacy;
- rejuvenating efficacy;
- prophylactic protection;
- magical covering;
- acquisition of courage/intelligence/strength;
- sympathetic or transferable efficacy;
- sometimes incorporation or alliance;
- but **not automatically sacrifice**;
- and **not automatically communion**.

Notebook p.84 already gives the limiting rule for foundation/building blood: bodily remains or blood may protect a building through their own efficacy and **need not express union through blood**. Pp.85–86 then supply Trumbull-derived cases that allow Lovejoy to populate this alternative causal class.

This makes Trumbull particularly valuable for the paper because Lovejoy does not merely criticize a theory abstractly. He **reuses its evidence after withdrawing the theory’s diagnostic force**.

A compact formulation:

> **Lovejoy keeps Trumbull’s facts and changes their explanatory job.**

## 4. Three-way control: Trumbull, Hubert–Mauss, Lovejoy

Trumbull is now a particularly good control because Hubert and Mauss also use him in the 1899 sacrifice essay.

### Trumbull

The broad tendency is covenantal/unitive: blood carries life, and shared/transferred blood can establish a union of lives. In *The Threshold Covenant*, foundation and threshold blood are likewise drawn into a covenantal theory of entry, protection and relation with the protecting deity/power.

### Hubert–Mauss

Hubert and Mauss do not simply repeat Trumbull. They explicitly distinguish blood alliance from sacrifice:

- blood alliance can create a **direct fusion** of lives;
- sacrifice requires the victim as an **intermediary** in a structured movement between profane and sacred.

In their construction-sacrifice discussion they again use Trumbull for blood on foundations and human remains incorporated into buildings, but place these cases inside their generic sacrificial mechanism, often through the creation/direction of a protecting sacred being or spirit.

### Lovejoy

Lovejoy moves farther away from the covenantal/unitive reading. The same family of facts can be explained by the direct efficacy of blood/body matter, protective charm, sympathetic transfer, incorporation, or another causal mechanism. Foundation blood does not itself prove blood-union; selected body parts do not themselves prove sacramental cannibalism.

The three-way contrast is therefore:

> **Trumbull universalizes through blood-life covenant; Hubert–Mauss reorganize the material through a generic sacrificial mechanism; Lovejoy disaggregates it by causal bearer and mechanism.**

This is not a direct influence chain. It is a controlled comparison of different reclassifications of overlapping evidence.

It does **not** establish:

- that Lovejoy read Hubert–Mauss 1899;

- that identical page sequences circulated between the researchers.

Indeed, the fact that Hubert–Mauss cite Trumbull for a different classificatory purpose remains compatible with the `generative documentary environment` model developed in Batches 55–56: overlapping books and problem nodes can produce divergent selections and classifications.

The evidentiary hierarchy can now be stated more finely:

- Lovejoy ↔ Trumbull: **level 4**, exact pages + continuous source sequence + transformed use;
- Lovejoy ↔ Hubert–Mauss: **level 3**, same books/problem nodes + proposition-level comparison, without direct-reading proof;
- Marillier as documentary mediator: strong institutional/contextual node, but Lovejoy’s receipt of a fixed packet remains unproved.

## 6. Relation to 1906: concrete source disappears while the mechanism survives

The existing 005 ↔ 1906 concordance becomes more interesting after the Trumbull collation.

Notebook 005 gives us a concrete source path:

`Trumbull pp.118–129 → blood/body transfer cases → Lovejoy reclassification as curative/protective/transferable efficacy`.

The 1906 *Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy* then prints the abstract causal category — impersonal, communicable, transferable, quasi-mechanical efficacy — and even includes the example of eating a brave enemy’s heart to absorb the relevant power/quality. Yet the published first chapter deliberately privileges linguistic evidence for `wakonda`, `manitou`, `mana`, etc.; the Trumbull-centered customs dossier is largely absent from the article’s visible citation architecture.

This supports a cautious but important developmental inference:

> **the source can disappear while the mechanism abstracted from its cases survives.**

Or, more materially:

> **005 preserves the reclassification stage that publication later hides.**

This is not proof that `Primitive Energetics` was derived from Trumbull. The point is narrower: Trumbull supplies demonstrable empirical inputs to a class of causal cases that 005 separates from communion, and the 1906 article later gives that class a general theoretical vocabulary.
