## 1. Full primary text recovered: “Religion and the Time-Process” (1902)

The complete article is available as a free JSTOR PDF:

- Arthur O. Lovejoy, **“Religion and the Time-Process,”** *The American Journal of Theology* 6 (1902), 439–472.
- JSTOR stable item: `3153919`.

This article is much more important for the Orientalist project than a bibliographic description suggests.

### 1.1 It explicitly begins as history of categories

Lovejoy opens by describing the **“changes of fashion in categories”** that the historian of philosophical and theological opinion records. His object is the historical career of a category — time/becoming/process — across religious and philosophical systems.

This is already recognizably adjacent to the later history-of-ideas operation: isolate a determinate conceptual presupposition, follow its changing combinations and consequences, and distinguish the logical motive from the named doctrine or school in which it appears.

### 1.2 India is not an ornament: Vedānta and Buddhism are the first major analytic case

After defining religious `otherworldliness`, Lovejoy says its logic is best seen by analyzing **the great religious philosophies of India, specifically Vedānta and Buddhism**. He identifies two motives for their negative evaluation of temporal process:

1. a cosmological motive: infinite/cyclical world-process appears to lack cumulative direction or finality;
2. a psychological/volitional motive, given its clearest expression in Buddhism: impermanence makes lasting satisfaction impossible.

He then treats `aniccam` / impermanence as the first of the Three Characteristics and argues that misery and lack of substantial reality follow from becoming; the Formula of Dependent Origination is presented as the Buddhist analysis explaining why change entails suffering.

### 1.3 Direct bridge back to the 1898 article and notebook 004

In a footnote at the key dependent-origination passage, Lovejoy explicitly refers readers to his own **“Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa.”** The 1902 article therefore gives us a primary, authorial statement that the 1898 technical philology was not a dead specialist exercise: Lovejoy reuses it four years later as the analytical foundation for a much larger comparative history of the religious valuation of time.

This sharply strengthens the chain:

> notebook 004 → 1898 technical philology → 1902 comparative history of a category.

### 1.4 A strong anti-diffusionist control appears inside the comparison

Lovejoy notes the obvious affinity between Aristotle’s changeless/self-sufficient deity and Vedānta, then rejects the suggestion that Aristotle therefore acquired the conception through “eastern speculation” after Alexander’s Asiatic campaigns. His reason is methodological: the Greek conception can be generated from the internal Platonic/Eleatic/Socratic dialectic already reconstructed.

He similarly says Oriental influence need not be invoked to explain Neoplatonic otherworldliness. The point is not a denial of all transmission. It is a demand that **conceptual resemblance not be converted into historical borrowing when an internal genealogy accounts for the result.**

This is strikingly consonant with notebook 004’s caution about Sāṁkhya/Buddhist priority and with later Lovejoyan suspicion of false genealogies.

### 1.5 The historical unit is a presupposition/motive, not an intact system

Across Plato, Aristotle, Hellenistic schools, Christian theology and modern idealism, Lovejoy repeatedly follows what he calls a **“perfectionist” presupposition/theory of worth**: the good as self-sufficiency, completion, changelessness and release from volitional outreach. He treats historically different systems as recombinations or consequences of a persistent logical motive.

Near the end, discussing Royce, he explicitly decomposes post-Hegelian idealism into **two incompatible logical motives** and describes the doctrine as a combination of “warring categories,” one modern and one a survival of ancient perfectionist metaphysics.

This is stronger evidence for an early history-of-ideas technique than a generic statement that Lovejoy was already an intellectual historian: the operational vocabulary is already `category`, `presupposition`, `logical motive`, `survival`, `combination`, and diachronic transmission/transformation.

### 1.6 Formulation can be later than the insight

Lovejoy also remarks that in history the coherent philosophical formulation of a new insight can be the **last rather than first stage** in its development. That is methodologically important for how we should read his own notebooks: a later published abstraction need not imply that the underlying distinctions were generated only at the date of publication.

This point is directly relevant to 005’s 1898–99 Marillier layer and 1905 reuse before the 1906 `primitive philosophy` article.

## 2. Full primary text recovered: “The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought” (1907)

The full 22-page article is freely available as a JSTOR PDF:

- Arthur O. Lovejoy, **“The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought,”** *The American Journal of Theology* 11 (1907), 228–249.
- JSTOR stable item: `3153710`.

### 2.1 Direct Harvard/Toy continuity

When establishing the relative sequence of Wisdom literature — Job, Psalms, Proverbs, Ben Sira, Ecclesiastes, Wisdom of Solomon — Lovejoy states that he accepts the sequence **“in deference to the authority of Professor Toy.”**

This is direct published evidence that Crawford H. Toy, one of Lovejoy’s documented Harvard teachers in Hebrew religion, remained an acknowledged scholarly authority inside Lovejoy’s mature 1907 historical argument.

### 2.2 Logical sequence is explicitly separated from chronological sequence

Lovejoy warns that texts of the same calendar period can embody very different stages of reflection, just as old intellectual forms can coexist with modern ones. He therefore says that for this inquiry **the logical rather than chronological sequence** is primary, while remaining constrained by the doctrinal character of the sources.

This is a highly relevant methodological statement. It does not mean chronology is irrelevant; rather, Lovejoy distinguishes:

- absolute dating;
- relative textual chronology;
- logical sequence of a developing conception;
- survival/coexistence of earlier and later phases.

That four-way distinction belongs squarely in the genealogy of his later historical method.

### 2.3 The article reconstructs a concept through indirect development

The core thesis is that Christian ethical “inwardness” has definite antecedents in Jewish Wisdom literature and emerged through a **singularly indirect and gradual** development. Lovejoy traces stages in which rationalistic/individualistic attempts to justify inherited morality create contradictions that eventually force attention toward the inner springs of conduct.

The object is therefore neither a school nor a single doctrine but the genesis of a conceptual/ethical disposition across heterogeneous texts.

### 2.4 Direct relationship to notebook 005 is now plausible and testable

Notebook 005 pp.39–44 already contains:

- `Hebrew Eschatology`;
- Job, Psalms, Ecclesiastes, Sheol;
- `nephesh` and `ruach`;
- Greek `psychē / thymos` comparison;
- a `Hist. Rel.` teaching outline;
- the now-resolved date **`Hist. Relig. — Dec. 20, 1905`**.

The 1907 paper does not simply reproduce those pages; its principal subject is Wisdom ethics rather than eschatology. But the manuscript and article share the same source-world, the same Toy-linked Hebrew historical scholarship, and a close chronology: a dated 1905 history-of-religion outline followed by a 1907 published reconstruction of Jewish ethical development.

A controlled notebook→article concordance is therefore warranted, especially for Job/Psalms/Ecclesiastes, the move from communal/customary morality to individual reflection, and Lovejoy’s use of relative/logical sequence.

## 3. A new primary-text bridge for the whole project

The 1902 and 1907 texts substantially alter the strongest version of the project argument.

The progression is no longer only:

> Orientalist training → isolated Buddhist paper → later history of ideas.

We now have a documented sequence in Lovejoy’s own published work:

> **1898 technical Buddhist philology → 1902 comparative history of the category of time, explicitly built in part on the 1898 Buddhist analysis → 1905 continued `Hist. Relig.` notebook work → 1907 historical reconstruction of Jewish ethical concepts with explicit Toy continuity.**

This gives a continuous early laboratory in which Lovejoy repeatedly:

- isolates categories/presuppositions/logical motives;
- separates resemblance from demonstrated borrowing;
- decomposes systems into historically different elements;
- distinguishes logical sequence from calendar chronology;
- allows earlier conceptual phases to survive beside later ones;
- reconstructs indirect developments rather than treating named traditions as homogeneous wholes.

The later `unit-idea` vocabulary is not yet present in these papers in its mature form, so the responsible claim is methodological continuity of operations, not terminological identity.

## 4. Implication for the Orientalist thesis

The 1902 article is especially significant because Indian thought is used in two ways simultaneously:

1. as a substantive object of historical-philosophical analysis, drawing directly on Lovejoy’s Buddhist specialist work;
2. as a comparative control through which Lovejoy distinguishes **structural affinity** from **historical diffusion**.

This makes early Orientalism part of the construction of Lovejoy’s historical method, not simply one subject he later abandoned.

At the same time, the paper’s language (`Hindu`, `Oriental`, generalizations about India) belongs to its period and requires historiographical contextualization rather than endorsement.
