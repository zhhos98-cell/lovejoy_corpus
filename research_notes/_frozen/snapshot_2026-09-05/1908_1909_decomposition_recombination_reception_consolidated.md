## Core result

The 1908–1909 material does not show Lovejoy simply moving from analytical splitting to synthesis. It reveals a more exact sequence in which **disaggregation, evidentiary insulation, warranted recombination, and global proof burden are kept distinct**.

Four primary/reception controls matter:

1. **Lovejoy, `The Thirteen Pragmatisms` (1908):** one historical label does not allow proof or refutation to migrate among logically independent contentions.
2. **Immediate 1908 reception:** contemporaries already framed pragmatism through both decomposition/differentiation and integration; the generic pair is therefore field-level, not Lovejoy-specific.
3. **Lovejoy, `Pragmatism and Realism` (1909):** after differentiation, compatible motives may be recombined so that they modify and interpret one another; historical novelty may reside in the configuration, a `new compound in intellectual chemistry`, even when the components are inherited.
4. **Lovejoy 1908 vs W. B. Smith 1909:** in an actual historical-genealogical dispute, Lovejoy decomposes a grand theory into local source/chronology/semantic links, then restores a second-stage cumulative judgment in which `antecedent probabilities` determine how much specific historical evidence a theory requires. Smith supplies the explicit counter-method: the evidence must be judged `as a unit` through cumulative configurational coherence.

The strongest bounded formulation is therefore:

> **separate before combining; prevent evidentiary leakage among locally independent claims; recombine only when the relations among the parts are demonstrated; and do not let global coherence repair a failed load-bearing historical link. Once local links survive, however, configuration itself may be historically distinctive and cumulative judgment remains legitimate.**

This is not mature `unit-ideas`. The 1908 objects are contemporary contentions and historical hypotheses, not transhistorical elemental ideas.

Project terms such as `decomposition–recomposition cycle`, `proposition insulation`, `evidentiary leakage`, `configurational novelty`, and `local-link validation` are analytical vocabulary unless explicitly anchored in Lovejoy's wording.

## I. 1908 `The Thirteen Pragmatisms`: decomposition and evidentiary insulation

Primary object:

- `The Thirteen Pragmatisms. I`, JPP&SM 5.1 (1908), pp.5–12;
- `The Thirteen Pragmatisms. II`, JPP&SM 5.2 (1908), pp.29–39.

Lovejoy begins from the instability of the collective label `pragmatism`. Even after excluding casual uses, he finds a `baker's dozen of contentions` and insists that they are logically independent in the relevant sense:

- one can accept one without accepting the rest;
- refuting one does not refute the others;
- historical use of one shared label has encouraged defenders and critics to transfer argumentative success or failure across propositions that do not logically entail one another.

The operative rule is therefore:

> **membership under one historical name does not license evidentiary transfer among independent propositions.**

This is more than lexical clarification. Lovejoy is separating:

- historical naming;
- logical content;
- argumentative consequence.

A historical label must first be decomposed before it can carry claims about unity.

## II. Classification after decomposition

The 1908 operation does not stop at multiplying meanings. Lovejoy sorts the distinct pragmatisms according to the kind of philosophical question they answer: meaning, truth, knowledge, ontology and related problem-types.

The structural point is secure even where publication-grade quotation of every numbered contention should still be checked against the primary pages:

> **question/function precedes valid grouping.**

This resonates with earlier 004/005 proof practices but should not be narrated as an invention in 1908. Oldenberg/Jacobi/Marillier already provide field-level precedents for refusing to group by generic resemblance or external form alone.

## III. Immediate reception: decomposition and integration were already a field problem in 1908

### A. C. Armstrong, `The Evolution of Pragmatism`

Armstrong, JPP&SM 5.24 (19 Nov. 1908), pp.645–650, directly cites Lovejoy and describes pragmatism as developing through **both `integration and differentiation`**.

He accepts the reality of internal distinctions but also insists on a large amount of `essential agreement` among pragmatist leaders. In his conclusion:

- integration and differentiation have both been occurring;
- their balance varies with historical stage;
- analysis and distinction have recently been gaining ground;
- movement-level identity need not disappear because logical differentiation is real.

Thus:

`logical independence / differentiation`

**does not entail**

`historical non-unity of the movement`.

### French 1908 survey

A contemporary supplement to the `Revue de métaphysique et de morale` describes the American debate literally as a **`décomposition du pragmatisme`**, places Lovejoy within that tendency, and contrasts synthetic or anti-fragmentation responses.

This is important attributionally. The project must not treat `decomposition` itself, or the generic need to combine differentiation and integration, as Lovejoy's unique 1909 innovation.

### Meyer / Boodin controls

Max Meyer's `The Exact Number of Pragmatisms` and J. E. Boodin's 1909 response belong to the same anti-fragmentation reception ecology. Their bibliographic/reception positions are secure; exact proposition-level quotation should still be controlled against primary pages if used publicly.

The reception branch has done its main conceptual work. Further Meyer/Boodin retrieval is citation hygiene unless a public draft needs exact wording.

## IV. 1909 `Pragmatism and Realism`: warranted recombination and configurational novelty

Lovejoy now makes a complementary argument.

A named doctrine is not a `logical brick` with one stable essence. `Pragmatism`, like `Christianity`, is a historical complex containing diverse motives/elements/tendencies. Arbitrarily selecting one element as `essential` lets the analyst manufacture affinities and incompatibilities at will.

His procedure is explicit:

1. make a `complete enumeration` of the important ideas or logical motives actually emphasized by the historical actors;
2. ask whether any component separately is distinctive;
3. identify which elements are compatible;
4. combine only as logic permits;
5. allow synthesized elements to **modify and interpret one another**;
6. test whether historical distinctiveness lies in a constituent or in the configuration.

The decisive formulation is Lovejoy's own:

> a doctrine can be a **`new compound in intellectual chemistry`** even when its constituent parts are not new.

The important delta is therefore narrower than generic `integration`:

> **historical novelty can reside in mutual transformation produced by conjunction.**

This becomes particularly important after the Class-A attribution corrections. If many of the diagnostic/mechanism operators in 004/005 are field-inherited, the historically interesting question shifts from ownership of ingredients to **selection, reweighting, combination and transformed configuration**.

This does not prove Lovejoy consciously interpreted his notebooks in 1909 through `intellectual chemistry`. It gives an actor-authored criterion showing that he regarded recombination as a legitimate locus of historical distinctiveness.

## V. 1908→1909: decomposition and recomposition impose different proof burdens

The pair is best treated as a controlled cycle, not as atomism followed by repentance.

### Decomposition burden

To treat propositions as independently assessable, show that acceptance/refutation of one does not logically determine the others.

### Recomposition burden

To treat several motives as one historically meaningful complex, show that:

- the elements are genuinely present in the historical material;
- the combination is logically possible;
- the conjunction produces a determinate interpretive transformation;
- distinctiveness can then be assessed at configurational level.

Thus:

> **separate before combining; combine only after relations among the parts have been demonstrated.**

This is neither pure atomism nor holistic essentialism.

## VI. The Smith controversy: decomposition in a real historical-genealogical argument

Primary symmetry is closed:

- Lovejoy, `The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus`, `The Monist` 18.4 (1908), pp.597–609;
- W. Benjamin Smith, `Professor Lovejoy on "Der Vorchristliche Jesus"`, `The Monist` 19.3 (1909), pp.409–420.

Lovejoy explicitly says Smith's revolutionary theory should receive an unprejudiced examination, then reduces the main argument to a few definite contentions. His procedure is:

1. state each contention;
2. identify its principal evidence;
3. test the genuineness and adequacy of that evidence.

This is direct primary evidence for **local decomposition of a grand genealogy**.

## VII. Local proof controls in Lovejoy's 1908 Smith critique

### Simon Magus: broad probability without specific descent

Lovejoy grants several broad assumptions as `fairly probable` and accepts as a `reasonable probability` that early Gnostic-type tendencies mixed Jewish, Greek and Persian elements around the beginning of the Christian era.

Yet that contextual probability does not prove Smith's stronger descent relation.

Thus:

> **broad syncretic plausibility can survive while a specific genealogy fails.**

### Hippolytus: adverse source evidence breaks a load-bearing chronological link

Lovejoy shows that Smith's cited source does not state the genealogical claim assigned to it and contains adverse evidence describing the Naassenes as Christians. A downstream interpretation can remain intrinsically plausible, but it cannot bear the required pre-Christian chronological function once the source link fails.

> **intrinsic plausibility cannot rescue a genealogy whose chronological middle link fails.**

### Paris Magic Papyrus: diagnostic potential without chronology remains inert

If the Jesus invocation were demonstrably pre-Christian, Lovejoy says it would be significant. Because the dating is conjectural, the datum remains suspended rather than discarded.

`potentially diagnostic datum + unsecured chronology → insufficient genealogical evidence`.

### Apollos: equally adequate local explanation defeats independent genealogical force

Lovejoy grants prima facie force, then restores context and shows that an early-Christian reading explains the passage at least as naturally. The evidence therefore cannot independently establish Smith's proposed genealogy.

### Nazoraeus / Nasaraioi: linguistic possibility is not sectarian continuity

Lovejoy keeps separate:

`possible etymology`
≠ `same sect`
≠ `historical continuity`
≠ `pre-Christian Jesus cult`.

### Resurrection vocabulary: lexical range is not historical priority

Alternative meanings can exist without proving that one sense was historically original, technical, and causally generative.

Across the critique, Lovejoy blocks **evidentiary leakage from one proposition type to another**.

## VIII. p.609: local decomposition feeds a second-stage global judgment

Lovejoy's final page is crucial because it prevents an atomistic reading.

He says the preceding analysis treated Smith's hypotheses **as if there were no general logical presumption either for or against them**. He then shifts level.

Smith's theory, Lovejoy argues, conflicts with the **`antecedent probabilities`** and therefore would require an **`overwhelming mass of specific historical evidences`**.

His historical reason is that the theory requires an unusually rapid transformation of a metaphysical/divine being into the markedly human Synoptic figure, whereas history supplies many examples of apotheosis but no comparably secure inverse process of that speed and specificity.

This gives two distinct proof-burden dimensions:

1. **proposition-sensitive burden:** chronology, semantics, source identity, descent, context each require evidence appropriate to that claim;
2. **prior-sensitive burden:** historically extraordinary propositions require a stronger cumulative body of specific evidence.

Modern Bayesian language should not be projected backward, but Lovejoy's own `antecedent probabilities` makes the prior-sensitive structure explicit.

## IX. Smith 1909: cumulative-configurational counter-method

Smith's reply is not a crude refusal of source criticism. He argues that his historical case is cumulative and must be judged **`as a unit`**.

His regime gives more weight to:

- mutual adjustment of many incomplete/diffuse facts;
- configurational coherence across the evidentiary field;
- reinterpretation of hostile or involuntary testimony;
- parsimonious unification of otherwise disconnected evidence.

The contrast is therefore:

### Lovejoy

`local-link validation`
→ preserve broad contextual probabilities where warranted
→ prevent the whole from repairing failed chronological/semantic/descent links
→ then perform a prior-sensitive cumulative adjudication.

### Smith

`cumulative-configurational genealogy`
→ individual items may be weak/diffuse
→ strength emerges through their mutual fit inside the whole reconstruction.

Both are genuine historical inference regimes.

The best project formulation is:

> **Lovejoy is not opposed to cumulative or configurational explanation; he refuses to let configuration confer evidentiary strength on constituent historical relations that fail their local tests.**

And after p.609:

> **recomposition does not cancel local proof burdens, while antecedent historical probability changes how much cumulative evidence is required.**

## X. Revised longitudinal significance

After the field-level attribution corrections, the 1908–1909 material gives a more defensible account of Lovejoy's own explicit methodological development.

### Prior field controls

Oldenberg/Jacobi/Marillier already separate generic resemblance from diagnostic relation and outward form from explanatory mechanism.

### 004/005 notebooks

Lovejoy assembles, reweights and redeploys heterogeneous proof practices across textual, semantic, causal and historical problems.

### 1906

A recurrent causal mechanism is reaggregated into a broad non-genealogical comparative category.

### 1908 pragmatism

A shared philosophical label is decomposed into logically independent contentions; evidentiary success/failure cannot migrate among them.

### 1908 reception

The field explicitly debates decomposition, differentiation, integration and movement identity.

### 1909 pragmatism

Lovejoy gives unusually explicit historical status to transformed configuration: inherited motives can create a distinctive compound through conjunction.

### 1908–1909 Smith controversy

The same period shows the method applied to historical genealogy: local links are audited separately, then the whole theory is judged cumulatively under a prior-sensitive burden of proof.

The stable longitudinal formulation is therefore:

> **controlled disaggregation followed by warranted recombination, with different proof burdens for local relation, global configuration, and historical genealogy.**

## XI. Guardrails

Do not claim:

- `decomposition` or differentiation/integration as a uniquely Lovejoyian invention;
- Armstrong, Meyer, Boodin or Smith directly caused Lovejoy's 1909 formulations without documentary evidence;
- the thirteen pragmatisms are mature `unit-ideas`;
- global configuration is illegitimate for Lovejoy;
- local-link failure always defeats every weaker contextual or comparative proposition;
- `antecedent probabilities` is identical to modern Bayesian formalism.

Lovejoy's narrower actor-authored contribution is especially strong in:

- logical independence / anti-transfer among contentions;
- complete enumeration before essentialization;
- mutual modification of compatible motives under synthesis;
- historical novelty at the level of the compound;
- local source/chronology/semantic validation before historical genealogy;
- prior-sensitive scaling of cumulative evidentiary burden.
