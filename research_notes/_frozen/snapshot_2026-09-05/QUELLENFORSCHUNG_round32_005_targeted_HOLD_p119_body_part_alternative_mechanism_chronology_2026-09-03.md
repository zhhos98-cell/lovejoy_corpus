## 2. Frazer owns the specific-organ / specific-property mechanism

Frazer's comparative cannibalism/magic material explicitly gives the proposition that human flesh/blood may be consumed to acquire qualities for which the dead person was remarkable **or which are believed to have their special seat in the particular part eaten**.

His case packet then assigns:

- liver -> valour;
- ears -> intelligence;
- forehead/forehead skin -> perseverance;
- testicles -> strength;
- heart -> courage/strength;
- other body parts -> other virtues/qualities.

This is extremely close to the first half of p.119.

Notebook 005 is already demonstrably working inside a Frazer-rich source field elsewhere. Therefore:

`SPECIFIC ORGAN -> SPECIFIC SEAT/PROPERTY -> QUALITY ACQUISITION`

is source-owned, F/A0.

The exact immediate Frazer page/edition carrier at p.119 remains unproved, but the proposition cannot carry Lovejoy originality.

---

## 3. Part-as-whole is also an established magic relation; Mauss later states it with exceptional explicitness

The second p.119 possibility says, in effect, that a selected part may work `as a representative of the whole body`, thereby giving the eater possession of the force of the eaten person.

Part/whole sympathetic efficacy belongs broadly to the nineteenth-century magic field. Frazer already supplies contagious/sympathetic relations in which body fragments preserve connection and efficacy.

More importantly for chronology, Marcel Mauss's *Esquisse d'une théorie générale de la magie* (1902–03) formulates the relation explicitly:

- `identification de la partie au tout`;
- `La partie vaut pour la chose entière`;
- teeth, saliva, sweat, nails, hair represent the whole person;
- the whole person/personality is present in each part;
- `Totum ex parte`.

This does **not** establish Mauss -> Lovejoy uptake. No direct Mauss citation has been recovered on p.119 or elsewhere sufficient to prove this physical edge.

It does establish that the apparently distinctive second mechanism cannot be protected as a pre-Maussian Lovejoy formulation unless p.119 itself is independently dated before 1902–03.

## 4. Material chronology is decisive: p.119 is an undated late insert

Round 13's material reconstruction identifies p.119 as a **small late insert** in a notebook otherwise known to be longitudinal and reused at least through the explicitly dated `Hist. Relig. — Dec. 20, 1905` layer on p.44.

The notebook therefore cannot be read as a single 1898–99 chronological codex.

At present p.119 has no independent date that places it before Mauss 1902–03.

Consequently:

`MAUSS IS LATER THAN THE PARIS CORE`

Likewise:

does not prove that Lovejoy read Mauss.

The correct status is chronology-sensitive uncertainty.
