# Batch 42 — Lovejoy 005 only: Mauss calls kin endocannibalism `communion`; Lovejoy separates transfer-of-quality from communion

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope lock: **MS38_004_001_061_005 only**.

## Research move

Batch 41 recovered Mauss’s exact redivision of Steinmetz’s `endocannibalisme` as:

> `l’anthropophagie rituelle des parents`

and his explanation that eating dead relatives allows the `vertus du mort` to pass into the survivors. A closer collation of the next electronic page produces the more important result:

> **Mauss explicitly classifies the mechanism as communion.**

His sentence is:

> `Cette pratique est donc un simple acte de communion`

This creates a precise local divergence from Lovejoy 005. In the notebook’s later anthropophagy pages, acquisition of the dead person’s qualities is not sufficient evidence for sacramental communion. Lovejoy routes selected-organ transfer primarily through sympathetic / transferable efficacy and, in his synthetic summary, treats sacramental union as comparatively exceptional.

The new historical object is therefore not merely continuity of a `transfer` idea. It is **active reclassification of the same explanatory mechanism across adjacent comparative-religion taxonomies**.

## 1. Mauss’s sequence is now recoverable proposition by proposition

In the critical section of `La religion et les origines du droit pénal d’après un livre récent`, electronic reproduction pp.30–31, Mauss argues:

1. Steinmetz’s broad `endocannibalisme` should more exactly be called `l’anthropophagie rituelle des parents`.
2. The rite rests on the original religious unity / solidarity of the family or clan.
3. Observers report that people believe that, by eating their relatives, the `vertus du mort` enter their own bodies.
4. Nothing is thereby lost from the general spirit animating the clan.
5. The meal must be made in common because part of the family spirit is being shared among relatives.
6. Related funerary transfers can work through a magical representative fragment: hair, any part of the person, or a drop of blood can represent the person.
7. Mauss then states explicitly: `Cette pratique est donc un simple acte de communion`.

This is much more precise than saying that Mauss links cannibalism, magic and clan solidarity. He assigns the transfer mechanism to a definite taxonomic box: **communion**.

## 2. Lovejoy 005 uses an almost identical transfer problem but changes the taxonomic placement

The corrected final 005 batch gives three especially useful controls.

### PDF p.108

Lovejoy asks whether human flesh or selected organs have a supernatural efficacy because they transmit strength, courage, intelligence or another quality of the victim. The clean transcription explicitly records his analytical alternative:

> this can belong to **sympathetic magic rather than sacrifice or communion**.

### PDF p.117

Lovejoy summarizes the field by treating alimentary sacrifice and expiatory / propitiatory sacrifice as common, while **sacramental union is comparatively exceptional**.

### PDF p.119

The mechanism is made anatomically specific:

- head / brain → intelligence;
- heart → courage / breath / life;
- sexual organs → sexual power.

The clean transcription classifies this as:

> **sympathetic or transferable efficacy rather than sufficient evidence of sacramental cannibalism.**

Thus the same broad causal grammar appears on both sides:

> a property associated with a dead person or body-part → ingestion / contact → acquisition by the survivor.

But the higher-order classification changes:

> **Mauss: transfer-through-ingestion = communion**
>
> **Lovejoy: transfer-through-ingestion can be sympathetic efficacy and therefore need not be communion.**

## 3. This is stronger than a generic `influence` resemblance

The earlier genealogy could have been read as conceptual continuity:

> Steinmetz / Mauss / Marillier discuss cannibalism and transferable qualities → Lovejoy also discusses them.

The new evidence gives a controlled disagreement at the level of analytical classification. Lovejoy’s notebook does not simply absorb the Paris category; it **cuts across it**.

That matters for the paper’s central claim about the workshop. The strongest story is not that Marillier or Mauss supplied Lovejoy with one doctrine. The evidence now shows a shared problem-field in which classifications were themselves contestable:

- what counts as communion?
- when does ingestion establish religious unity?
- when is the effect better described as magical transfer?
- can the same outward rite contain different causal mechanisms?

005 repeatedly answers by lowering the level of generalization and separating mechanisms that another comparative writer could keep under `communion`.

## 4. It also sharpens the road to `Primitive Energetics`

The 1906 endpoint should still be treated as abstraction, not origin. But this local disagreement gives a cleaner explanation of what has been abstracted.

In 005, transferable efficacy is becoming analytically portable **outside** the sacramental category. A body part, blood, charm, plant, stone or other carrier may work because a quality / efficacy is transferable, intrinsic or mechanically communicable without requiring a personal god or a communion relation.

The movement is therefore not simply:

> communion → generalized magic.

It is closer to:

> **strip the transfer relation out of a sacramental interpretation → compare that relation with other direct / intrinsic / transmissible efficacy cases → later abstract a cross-cutting causal family.**

This is the strongest current micro-mechanism for connecting the anthropophagy block of 005 to the later `Primitive Energetics` argument without projecting the 1906 vocabulary backward wholesale.

## 5. Paper-level use

The Steinmetz/Mauss/Marillier section can now close on an exact disagreement:

> Mauss took kin endocannibalism, transfer of the dead person’s virtues, and representative fragments of person/blood as a form of communion. Lovejoy’s 005 notebook later encounters the same transfer problem but refuses to make transfer itself diagnostic of communion: selected organs transmit specific qualities through sympathetic efficacy, while sacramental union is treated as comparatively rare.

This gives the paper a visible **reclassification event** rather than a loose genealogy of shared topics.

## 6. Strongest safe formulation

> **Mauss’s critique of Steinmetz explicitly classifies ritual eating of relatives — including the transfer of the dead person’s virtues — as `un simple acte de communion`. Lovejoy’s 005 notebook later treats an almost identical transfer-of-quality mechanism as analytically separable from communion: the acquisition of intelligence, courage/life or sexual power from selected organs can belong to sympathetic or transferable efficacy, and sacramental union is comparatively exceptional. The significance of the Paris context therefore lies not in doctrinal repetition but in a shared classificatory problem that Lovejoy redivides.**
