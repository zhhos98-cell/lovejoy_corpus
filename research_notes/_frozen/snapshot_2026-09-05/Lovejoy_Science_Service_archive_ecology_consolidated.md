# Lovejoy in the Science Service archive — consolidated custody, annual-run, preservation-gap, and retrieval model

Date: 2026-08-26
Status: canonical consolidation
Supersedes active explanatory prose in archive-side Batches 105, 107, 108, and 109. Row-level component, lead, coverage, preservation-gap, opportunity-map, split-custody, and request-tranche CSVs remain the audit layer.

## Core result

The Science Service evidence is best modeled as a split-custody documentary ecology rather than as a single correspondence series or a sequence of name-search hits.

Four Lovejoy presences are explicit in Smithsonian Institution Archives RU7091 annual correspondence folders: 1927 B87/F2, 1928 B97/F1, 1929 B105/F1, and 1940 B219/F8. These prove folder-level archival presence, not document count, direction, bilateral correspondent, subject, or material form. The 1927–29 folders belong to the director/senior-staff correspondence regime; the 1940 folder belongs to the later editorial correspondence regime. Series custody must not be converted into an inference that Edwin E. Slosson or Watson Davis was necessarily Lovejoy's counterpart.

The intervening and later chronology is no longer an undifferentiated silence. Filing-scheme reconstruction identifies exact surviving alphabetic candidate containers for every year 1930–38 and for 1942–47 and 1950–62. Those are retrieval facts, not positive Lovejoy components. A negative result requires complete physical inspection or a faithful item-level surrogate.

Repository-declared losses interrupt the ordinary annual test in several places. 1939 lacks K–Smi; 1941 lacks He–Sg, among several discontiguous missing spans; 1948 lacks A–Sm; most general correspondence for 1949 is missing. A separate 1926 Series-5 I–Z lacuna covers Lovejoy alphabetically, while overlapping Series-2 L containers survive. These gaps are evidence about preservation and filing regimes. They do not establish that a Lovejoy document once existed or was destroyed.

The Science Service documentary universe is also distributed across manifestation domains. Accession 90-105 names Arthur O. Lovejoy in a portrait/biographical-morgue folder; Accession 90-068 supplies a topical-morgue search domain but no verified Lovejoy component; Watson Davis's papers and Edwin E. Slosson's 1928 correspondence are reciprocal-fonds leads rather than established bilateral manifestations. A Lovejoy-titled component in the John Joseph Honigmann Papers is a separate derivative/reference object whose material form remains unresolved. Shared organizational provenance does not imply document identity.

## Canonical evidence states

Use the following states rather than hit/no-hit:

1. `explicit_name_presence` — the finding aid names Lovejoy in the component.
2. `exact_alphabetic_candidate_container` — a surviving folder contains Lovejoy's filing position but has not been inspected.
3. `repository_declared_missing_expected_range` — the expected alphabetical segment is missing.
4. `repository_declared_general_correspondence_gap` — the annual run is too incomplete to construct a safe Lovejoy locator.
5. `series_specific_gap_with_parallel_surviving_record_classes` — loss belongs to one filing regime while overlapping series or other same-year record classes survive.
6. `cross_accession_survival_possible_but_unverified_for_target_item` — institutional precedent makes alternate custody plausible, without an object-level match.
7. `primary_folder_inspected` — reserved for a completed item-level inspection, positive or negative.

These states are epistemic, not biographical. In particular, edited finding-aid silence must not be converted into absence, and a missing filing range must not be converted into a lost Lovejoy letter.

## Annual-run control

### Positive controls

- 1927 — RU7091 B87/F2, `Correspondence Li - Ly`, Lovejoy explicitly named.
- 1928 — B97/F1, `Correspondence Li - Ly`, explicitly named.
- 1929 — B105/F1, `Correspondence Li - Ly`, explicitly named.
- 1940 — B219/F8, `Correspondence Li - L`, explicitly named.

### Pre-1940 candidate/gap structure

For 1930–38 the exact surviving L-position folders are already recorded in the Batch107 opportunity-map CSV. They form nine finite physical tests. 1939 is blocked by the explicit missing K–Smi run.

For 1926, Series 5 reports I–Z missing, but Series 2 preserves B28/F14 (`Correspondence L, July 1925 - June 1926`) and B31/F12 (`Correspondence L, July 1926 - June 1927`). The preservation claim must therefore remain series-specific.

### Post-1940 structure

1941 is blocked in the ordinary annual run by the missing He–Sg span. The same year also has missing T, Wam–War, and Wa–Weav spans, showing a broader Series-5 discontinuity rather than a person-specific anomaly. B230/F1, `Note on missing 1941 folders`, is the highest-value preservation object and remains uninspected.

Surviving exact candidate containers exist for 1942–47. 1948 is blocked by missing A–Sm. Most 1949 general correspondence is missing. Every year 1950–62 has a surviving exact L-position candidate container. Exact box/folder locators and retrieval tranches remain in the Batch108 CSVs and should not be duplicated here.

## Preservation interpretation

The 1941 Science Service move to 1719 N Street N.W. is a contextual retrieval hypothesis only. Temporal coincidence between the move and missing correspondence spans does not establish causation. B230/F1 must be inspected before any move/loss narrative is advanced.

The 1941 losses are also not year-wide: executive, trustee, staff, invitation, radio, Daily Mail Report, and other record classes survive. Neighboring 1940 itself contains a separate missing Ro–Z span while Lovejoy's positive B219/F8 survives. This argues for a filing/preservation problem broader than the Lovejoy query.

Smithsonian's Science Service accessions demonstrate parallel office filing systems and cross-accession survival of some record types. That establishes a search architecture, not a substitute manifestation. Missing from RU7091 is not equivalent to destroyed everywhere; survival elsewhere becomes a claim only after object-level matching.

## Split-custody and reciprocal-fonds controls

- RU7091: correspondence custody, with explicit Lovejoy folder presences and annual candidate/gap map.
- Accession 90-105: biographical/portrait morgue; Box13 `Portraits, Loo-Loz` explicitly includes Lovejoy, but count, date, photographer, caption, and exact material form remain unresolved.
- Accession 90-068: topical/informational morgue candidate. Do not search backward from Lovejoy's profession and attach generic philosophy/religion topics to his correspondence. Recover correspondence topics first, then test topical morgue files.
- Watson Davis Papers: reciprocal-fonds lead, with 1940 the first controlled search year because of the RU7091 positive hit. Davis's institutional role does not identify him as counterpart without item evidence.
- Edwin E. Slosson Papers: 1928 correspondence container is a tightly date-matched reciprocal test against RU7091 B97/F1. It remains a lead until a Lovejoy item is identified.
- Honigmann Papers: `Lovejoy -- Some Eighteenth Century Evolutionists` proves a Lovejoy-titled archival component, not annotation, reading, influence, or a specific material form.

## Retrieval order

1. Inspect B230/F1 `Note on missing 1941 folders` before constructing any causal preservation narrative.
2. Inspect the four explicit Lovejoy-positive RU7091 folders, preserving item-level date, direction, signer/addressee, material form, copy status, annotations, enclosures, and immediate file neighbors.
3. Run the 1940 positive control together with the 1942–47 candidate tranche.
4. Inspect the 1930–38 L-position run and the 1926 surviving Series-2 L containers as finite tests.
5. Inspect Acc90-105 Box13 at item level and recover Lovejoy object count/form/local identifiers.
6. Use actual counterpart names from recovered items to drive reciprocal-fonds searches; do not infer Slosson/Davis from custody alone.
7. Proceed to 1950–56 and 1957–62 tranches if earlier inspection justifies the cost.

## Claim ceilings

- Repeated annual folder presence demonstrates archival recurrence, not continuous correspondence.
- Folder-level topics are not Lovejoy topics unless his item is shown to carry them.
- An exact alphabetic candidate container is a locator, not presence evidence.
- A missing range is evidence of archival incompleteness, not evidence of a missing Lovejoy document.
- Preservation gaps belong to filing regimes; overlapping series may survive.
- Shared institutional provenance does not establish duplicate or derivative identity across correspondence, portrait morgue, topical morgue, or personal papers.
- Cross-accession survival is a structural possibility until object-level collation establishes a manifestation relationship.
