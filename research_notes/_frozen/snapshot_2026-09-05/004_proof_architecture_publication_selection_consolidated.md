## Core result

Notebook 004 and Lovejoy's 1898 article are best understood through a layered proof architecture rather than through a simple `resemblance ≠ borrowing` maxim.

Three controls need to remain distinct:

1. **diagnostic specificity:** a resemblance may be real but too generic to identify the proposed philosophical system or genealogy;
2. **independent genealogical warrant:** even a strong affinity does not establish historical transmission if the same target can be explained through an internally reconstructible genealogy and no independent evidence chooses transmission;
3. **publication selection:** the notebook contains broader genealogical, taxonomic and metaphysical hypotheses than the 1898 article; print preferentially retains narrower semantic, textual and causal claims that can be locally controlled, while broad historical reconstructions are omitted, bracketed or softened.

The direct 1897 Oldenberg third edition and Jacobi 1897 controls recovered later in Batch159 materially change attribution. These operators are not Lovejoy inventions. Contemporary German Indology already articulated most of the proof disciplines visible in 004: system-characteristic versus generic traits, source chronology and genre, comparison by conceptual function, caution about negative evidence, usage over etymology, and the possibility that inherited categories can be recombined into a new coherent system.

The manuscript remains historically valuable because it exposes **Lovejoy's selection, reweighting, local application and publication pruning** of those field-level controls.

Project terms such as `diagnostic specificity`, `two-gate model`, `genealogical redundancy`, `proof-level partition`, `manuscript surplus`, and `evidentiary compression` are analytical vocabulary, not Lovejoy's own terms.

## I. Oldenberg 1890 baseline: resemblance admitted, filiation underdetermined

The 1890 second edition of Oldenberg's `Buddha` supplies an important baseline before the Jacobi/Senart controversy.

### p.100 n.1 — family resemblance

Oldenberg admits real Sāṁkhya/Buddhist `Anklänge` but says they do not exceed the familiar `Familienähnlichkeit aller philosophischen Systeme Indiens`.

The evidentiary point is not that resemblance is unreal. It is that a widely distributed trait has low power to discriminate one specific genealogy from neighboring possibilities.

### p.226 n.2 — structural parallel without direction

Oldenberg notes a striking fourfold Sāṁkhya/Yoga liberation schema parallel to the Four Truths but says the direction of borrowing cannot be established.

Thus:

`real structural parallel`

≠

`demonstrated historical direction`.

### pp.262–268 — detailed parallels remain admissible

Oldenberg compares Buddhist and Sāṁkhya material around `avidyā/aviveka`, beginningless causal chains, `saṃskāra/vāsanā`, and the possible preservation of older doctrinal configurations. The 1890 p.268 explanation of `saṅkhāra` is substantially the same passage Jacobi later attacks at third-edition p.286f.

This matters because Oldenberg's caution was never a blanket rejection of comparison. He allowed meaningful parallels while imposing a higher burden on historical filiation.

## II. Oldenberg 1897 third edition: expansion and polemical consolidation, not reversal

The true 1897 third enlarged edition is now directly controlled.

The third edition adds a dedicated Sāṁkhya/Buddhism excurs at pp.443–455 and a directly relevant note at pp.272–273. The new material intensifies source criticism and replies to the stronger contemporary genealogy claims, but it does not reverse the 1890 rule.

### pp.272–273 — inherited terminology can still be intelligibly recombined

Oldenberg accepts that Buddhism repeatedly works with inherited `Schlagworte`, yet says those inherited terms can be combined so that recognizable thoughts remain. He then directly rejects Senart's identification of `upādāna` with the five `upādānakkhandha`, using canonical passages and conceptual relation to distinguish attachment from the aggregates to which attachment relates.

This is a direct pre-Lovejoy control on one of the central 1898 semantic moves.

### pp.443–455 — source and function controls

The excurs contains, before Lovejoy's published 1898 intervention, a dense set of proof rules:

- inherited keywords/category-series can be placed in the service of new fundamental ideas;
- generic resemblance must be separated from genuinely characteristic doctrine;
- category-sequences should be compared by the question/function they answer, not merely by formal position;
- negative coincidence or non-mention has little genealogical weight;
- source chronology and literary genre constrain historical inference;
- established textual usage can defeat speculative etymology;
- `upādāna` is not identical with the five `upādānakkhandha`.

Accordingly the later project claim must be attributionally modest:

> **004 applies and reweights a contemporary proof culture; it does not originate its constituent operators.**

## III. Rhys Davids → Lovejoy 004: diagnostic specificity becomes explicit in the manuscript

Lovejoy copies Rhys Davids's criticism of an alleged early Sāṁkhya witness. Rhys Davids already uses two strong controls:

- the technical term `Prakṛti` is absent;
- the alleged `world = primordial matter` identification fails functionally because the Buddhist passage makes the world non-generative while Sāṁkhya primordial matter is generative.

The manuscript hinge is Lovejoy's explicit addendum:

`To R.D.'s remark it should be added...`

Lovejoy argues that eternity of Matter and Self is too generic for the historical task. A more system-defining Sāṁkhya marker is the **separateness of Self and World**, the `thorough-going dualism` on which release depends. He then looks for a better Buddhist witness expressing that relation.

The local manuscript operation is:

`candidate resemblance`
→ `ask what feature actually defines the target system for this question`
→ `replace a low-discrimination feature with a more discriminating relation`
→ `seek a better textual witness`.

This is a real Lovejoy intervention in the notebook, but not a priority claim for the operator itself.

## IV. Gate 1: diagnostic insufficiency

The first failure mode of genealogy is **low discriminatory power**.

A resemblance can be genuine yet unable to identify the proposed source because:

- it is widespread among neighboring systems;
- the alleged witness lacks diagnostic vocabulary;
- the shared element performs a different causal or conceptual role;
- the relevant difference-points are more system-characteristic than the apparent similarity.

The remedy is not simply `be cautious`. It is to find a more discriminating property, relation or textual witness.

Analytical shorthand:

> **specificity controls identification.**

---

## V. The 1898 article relocates diagnosticity from system identity to semantic and causal function

Lovejoy's published `The Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa` largely drops the broad Sāṁkhya/Buddhism priority problem. The operator survives in a narrower evidentiary field.

### `upādāna`

Lexical recurrence does not establish conceptual identity. The skandhas are not identical with attachment merely because `upādānakkhandha` exists.

Likewise karma and `upādāna` both bear on rebirth but perform distinct causal jobs: one helps explain the particular condition of rebirth, the other renewed existence as such.

### `upādi / upadhi`

Competing etymological genealogies remain possible. Lovejoy therefore does not let etymology decide meaning; distribution, doctrinal contrast and textual usage carry more weight.

The higher-order operation is preserved:

`ambiguous/shared evidence`
→ `insufficient discrimination`
→ `move to a test with greater functional information`.

Thus the publication transformation is not `method discarded with unused material`; it is **topic deletion + operator retention**.

---

## VI. Gate 2: genealogical redundancy / explanatory non-necessity

Lovejoy's 1902 `Religion and the Time-Process` supplies a different failure mode.

In the Aristotle/Vedānta comparison, Lovejoy concedes a strong or obvious affinity. The resemblance survives Gate 1. Yet he rejects the external Oriental-influence inference because the Greek conception can be reconstructed through an internal Greek logical development.

The point is not that contact becomes impossible. It is that the affinity alone no longer supplies sufficient reason to posit transmission.

The sequence is:

`strong affinity survives`
→ `construct competing historical genealogies`
→ `ask whether an internal genealogy independently explains the target`
→ `require separate evidence before adding transmission`.

Analytical shorthand:

> **independent genealogy controls transmission.**

This is distinct from Gate 1. A weak diagnostic is repaired by better discrimination; a genealogically redundant strong affinity requires independent historical warrant.

## VII. Proof-level partition: failed genealogy does not cancel comparison

The 1890–1902 material repeatedly keeps different propositions apart:

- comparative resemblance;
- semantic or logical function;
- system identity;
- historical filiation;
- direction of borrowing;
- configuration after uptake.

A genealogical inference can fail while other relations remain valid.

Oldenberg can retain family resemblance; Lovejoy can retain Aristotle/Vedānta affinity; borrowed terms can remain part of a distinct Buddhist `arrangement and application`.

This is the deeper architecture:

`similarity`
→ `diagnostic / functional test`
→ `competing genealogies`
→ `historical transmission warrant`
→ `configuration/function in the receiving system`.

The historical value lies in the **partition of proof burdens**, not in generalized skepticism.

## VIII. Manuscript surplus: 004 contains a much broader research space than the 1898 article

Notebook 004 includes substantial material that does not become part of the published paper:

- broad chronology and priority of Sāṁkhya and Buddhism;
- explicit system-identification tests;
- large psychological/khandha/nidāna taxonomies and diagrams;
- an original-versus-derived-elements model;
- stronger Nirvāṇa/metaphysical conclusions.

The published article narrows its object to two technical terms and related causal/semantic problems.

The key archival fact is not merely that the article is shorter. The pruning is asymmetric by proposition type.

## IX. Publication selection: local semantic/causal claims survive better than broad genealogy

### Broad Sāṁkhya genealogy

Mostly omitted. The article does not make the manuscript's broad historical priority problem its burden.

### Explicit `thorough-going dualism` diagnostic

Omitted as a published Sāṁkhya thesis, even though it is one of the clearest manuscript authorial interventions.

### Khandha/nidāna maps

Compressed from large exploratory diagrams into smaller causal propositions about the relation among dependent-origination terms, rebirth and karma.

### Original versus derived elements

The manuscript's historical anatomy is not published as a firm genealogy. Instead the article makes the narrower, better-controlled claim that terminology and perhaps ideas may be borrowed while their **`arrangement and application`** are original and characteristic.

### Nirvāṇa

Stronger global/metaphysical conclusions are softened. The article narrows the issue to technical semantics and acknowledges resistant passages, leaving the conclusion provisional.

The observed gradient is:

> **the broader the genealogical or metaphysical proposition, the more likely it is to be omitted or softened; the more locally controllable the semantic, textual or causal proposition, the more likely it is to be sharpened in print.**

This is an observed manuscript/publication asymmetry, not evidence of a conscious formal editorial rule.

## X. Why the archive matters

The 1898 publication alone can make Lovejoy look like a narrowly technical Buddhist philologist. Notebook 004 exposes the much larger research space from which the article was selected:

- competing genealogies;
- source criticism;
- system-identification criteria;
- diagrams and taxonomies;
- historical stratification hypotheses;
- unresolved metaphysical possibilities.

The manuscript therefore preserves not merely preliminary wording but **rejected, suspended and compressed proof problems**.

Compact project formulation:

> **the notebook preserves the proof problems that publication removes.**

## XI. Revised attribution after Batch159

No publication-safe claim should say that Lovejoy invented:

- system-characteristic diagnostic comparison;
- source chronology/genre control;
- usage-over-etymology discipline;
- function-sensitive comparison of category-series;
- caution about negative evidence;
- the idea that inherited elements can be recombined into a coherent new system.

These are directly visible in Oldenberg/Jacobi before or contemporaneously with 004.

Lovejoy's stronger documentary contribution is instead:

- selecting among those available controls;
- applying them locally to the 004 dossier;
- reweighting which features count as probative;
- recombining textual, causal and semantic tests;
- pruning broad research surplus into a narrower publication;
- later transporting the proof discipline into other domains.

This is **application/reweighting and publication transformation**, not operator priority.
