# Batch 26 — Sylvain Lévi returns from Asia: 1898 mission, Buddhist manuscripts, and the unresolved private-reading archive

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced

## Research move

The previous batch established the exact Paris teaching matrix around Lovejoy in 1898–99. This batch narrows the remaining French problem: **what was Sylvain Lévi actually bringing back into Paris at the moment Lovejoy reportedly began private sacred-text reading with him, and where might evidence of those meetings survive?**

The answer is now clearer, although the exact Lovejoy–Lévi texts remain unresolved. Lévi returned from his 1897–98 Asian mission with newly acquired/copied Sanskrit and Buddhist manuscripts, resumed Sanskrit teaching, and reported the mission to the Académie des Inscriptions in January 1899. The Collège de France now confirms that a Sylvain Lévi scientific archive survives in its Indian/Central Asian library. This gives a concrete French archival route beyond the Wilson transcriptions at Johns Hopkins.

## 1. Lévi’s 1898 Nepal mission produced an immediate manuscript intake

Contemporary Académie des Inscriptions reports trace the mission in real time.

In early 1898, Auguste Barth reported that Lévi had reached Kathmandu and had already found **rare and little-known manuscripts**, intending to acquire or have them copied, alongside new inscriptions.

Émile Senart then reported a particularly important acquisition:

> a manuscript of Asaṅga’s **Sūtrālaṃkāra / Mahāyāna-Sūtrālaṃkāra**, expected to provide a new basis for future study of the Mahāyāna.

The reports also stress that the Nepalese government promised continued transmission of newly found inscriptions and manuscripts after Lévi’s return.

This matters chronologically. Lovejoy’s Paris year begins just as Lévi returns from a mission that had materially enlarged his Buddhist/Sanskrit research corpus.

## 2. Lévi publicly reported the completed India/Japan mission in Paris on 27 January 1899

The *Comptes rendus de l’Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres* records that on **27 January 1899**, Sylvain Lévi personally reported on his mission in India and Japan. The full mission report occupies pp.71–92 of the 1899 volume.

Thus Lévi’s Asian field/manuscript work was not remote background during Lovejoy’s Paris year. It was being actively processed, presented and reintegrated into Paris scholarship during the same months in which Wilson places Lovejoy’s private reading visits.

## 3. Candidate manuscript field: what survives from the Sylvain Lévi collection

The Collège de France / BnF France–South Asia digital heritage projects identify multiple manuscripts in the Sylvain Lévi collection, including Buddhist materials such as:

- **Mahāyāna-Sūtrālaṃkāra** of Asaṅga;
- a fragment of **Sarvatathāgatadvādaśasāhasrikāpārājikāvinaya**;
- **Aṣṭasāhasrikā-Prajñāpāramitā** material;
- several Vajrayāna/ritual manuscripts;
- other Nepalese Sanskrit manuscripts and historical texts.

The BnF heritage history of Indian studies at EPHE also notes that Lévi had long used **Sanskrit Buddhist manuscripts in the Bibliothèque nationale** as teaching material.

These items establish the kind of textual material available in Lévi’s Paris practice. They are **candidate context only**. No source yet identifies any of these as the “sacred texts” read privately with Lovejoy.

## 4. The actual 1898–99 regular Lévi courses make the private-reading report more plausible

The EPHE historical-philological annual report now shows that Lévi’s regular 1898–99 teaching consisted of:

- **Bhagavad-Gītā** explanation;
- a continuous introduction/survey to Indian literature.

The desired Pāli-Buddhism course recalled by Wilson was therefore not among the recovered regular offerings. At the same time, Lévi had just returned with Buddhist/Sanskrit manuscripts and was actively reporting on them.

The resulting model is coherent:

> desired Pāli-Buddhist training unavailable in the formal timetable → occasional private sacred-text reading with a Sanskritist/Buddhist scholar newly returned from India/Nepal/Japan.

The model remains secondary-from-primary until Lovejoy’s letters are extracted.

## 5. A concrete Sylvain Lévi archive survives at the Collège de France

The **Bibliothèque d’études indiennes et centrasiatiques, Collège de France** explicitly states that it holds scientific archives including a **S. Lévi** archive, alongside more than 70,000 printed volumes, manuscripts, photographic collections and other research materials.

The library also states that part of the Sylvain Lévi manuscript collection and iconographic material is already digitized through **Salamandre**.

What the public web interface currently exposes well:

- manuscript and image collections;
- Collège de France course/programme and chair records;
- selected Sylvain Lévi material.

What is not yet exposed in a sufficiently granular public inventory:

- 1898–99 appointment books/diaries, if any;
- personal correspondence at the Lovejoy level;
- lists of private students/readers;
- notes on private reading sessions.

Therefore the correct next step is a **targeted archive enquiry**, not an assumption that the correspondence is absent.

## 7. The 1898–99 French Orientalist ecology now has a material dimension

The Paris context can now be described at three levels:

### Institutional curriculum

- Marillier — survivance / sacrifice;
- Vernes — Psalms / ancient Israel;
- Réville — Fourth Gospel criticism;
- Foucher — Nyāya / Manu;
- Stickney — Sāṃkhya via Garbe;
- Sylvain Lévi — Bhagavad-Gītā / Indian literature.

### Research materials

- Bibliothèque nationale Sanskrit/Buddhist manuscripts;
- Lévi’s newly acquired/copied Nepalese manuscripts;
- recently returned field notes, inscriptions and manuscript records from India/Nepal/Japan;
- Marillier’s comparative dossiers and printed/source collections.

### Lovejoy’s documented or reported practices

- active participation in Marillier;
- Psalms with Vernes;
- frequent Réville Fourth-Gospel lectures;
- intended Pāli-Buddhist study with Lévi;
- occasional private sacred-text reading with Lévi;
- extended library research.

This is a much denser and more specific account of Lovejoy’s Paris Orientalist year than “studied in Paris.”

## 8. Archival request target for the Collège de France

High-yield enquiry wording should ask whether the **Sylvain Lévi scientific archive** contains, for 1898–99:

- incoming/outgoing correspondence with **Arthur O. Lovejoy / Lovejoy**;
- appointment diaries, calendars or address books;
- notes on private students or private textual readings;
- teaching notes for Buddhist/Pāli material not represented in the formal course programme;
- correspondence relating to American students in Paris, especially Lovejoy and Joseph Trumbull Stickney;
- post-mission notes identifying manuscripts/texts used in Paris immediately after the 1897–98 mission.

## 9. Evidence limit on the “sacred texts” question

At present we can say securely:

- Lovejoy sought Pāli-Buddhist study from Lévi according to Wilson’s reconstruction from Lovejoy’s letters;
- no formal Lévi Pāli-Buddhist course appears in the recovered 1898–99 EPHE programmes;
- Lévi instead taught Bhagavad-Gītā / Indian literature;
- Lévi had just returned with significant Sanskrit/Buddhist manuscript material;
- a Sylvain Lévi scientific archive survives in Paris.

We still cannot name the private texts or language used with Lovejoy. Candidate manuscript titles must not be converted into a claim until the letters or Lévi archive supply the bridge.
