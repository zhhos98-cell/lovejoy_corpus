# Journal of the History of Ideas, Inc.: corporate governance and editorial network, 1940–2006

Date: 2026-08-31  
Status: **ACTIVE INSTITUTIONAL DOSSIER / CORPORATE CONTINUITY SECURE / ORIGINAL 1940 CHARTER + FIRST DIRECTORS OPEN**

## Core result

The institutional history of JHI cannot be reduced to:

`ACLS COMMITTEE -> JOHNS HOPKINS PRESS -> JOURNAL`.

The surviving evidence shows that **Journal of the History of Ideas, Inc.** emerged essentially at the journal's foundation and became the durable legal/publishing/governance carrier of the enterprise.

The current best architecture is:

`ACLS FIELD-BUILDING INITIATIVE`
→ `LOVEJOY / WIENER EDITORIAL PROJECT`
→ `JHI FIRST ISSUE, 1940`
+
`JOURNAL OF THE HISTORY OF IDEAS, INC. (1940)`
→ `EDITORIAL OFFICE OPERATED THROUGH CITY COLLEGE / WIENER`
→ `EDITORIAL + CORPORATE BOARDS EXPAND`
→ `INSTITUTIONAL OFFICE MIGRATIONS WITHOUT LOSS OF CORPORATE CONTINUITY`.

This is important because it changes the history from a journal attached to one university into a **mobile learned-corporate institution**.

## II. 1941 public evidence: subscriptions already run through JHI, Inc.

An April 1941 journal advertisement for JHI vol.II no.2 gives subscription payments to:

`JOURNAL OF THE HISTORY OF IDEAS, INC.`
`The City College, Convent Ave. and 139th St., New York, N.Y.`

This is only about a year after the first issue.

It demonstrates that the corporation is not merely a late legal shell. It already functions publicly as the journal's subscription/financial recipient.

This also immediately makes **City College of New York** a key institutional node in JHI history through Philip P. Wiener.

## III. City College / Wiener is the journal's durable operational base

The same operating pattern persists for decades.

### 1951

An advertisement states:

`Checks payable to JOURNAL OF THE HISTORY OF IDEAS INC.`
`CITY COLLEGE OF NEW YORK, NEW YORK 31, N.Y.`

The issue is advertised as edited by Arthur O. Lovejoy, Marjorie H. Nicolson, John H. Randall Jr. and others.

### 1962 directory

A major periodicals directory records:

- Journal founded: 1940;
- subject: Intellectual History;
- editor: **P. P. Wiener**;
- publisher: **Journal of the History of Ideas**;
- address: City College, Convent Ave. and 139th St., New York.

### 1968

JHI advertisements still instruct payment to:

`JOURNAL OF THE HISTORY OF IDEAS, INC.`
`The City College, New York, N.Y. 10031`.

Thus the operational history is substantially different from a simple Hopkins institutional story:

`HOPKINS = LOVEJOY / CLUB / EARLY PRESS AND INTELLECTUAL ORIGIN`
while
`CITY COLLEGE / WIENER = LONG-RUN EDITORIAL-BUSINESS OPERATING NODE`.

This division requires further archival reconstruction.

## IV. First editorial committee: intentionally interdisciplinary

Donald Kelley's later JHI retrospective reconstructs the first issue's editorial architecture.

Lovejoy was editor, with **Philip Wiener doing most of the editorial work as managing editor**.

The assisting committee consisted of:

- Crane Brinton;
- Gilbert Chinard;
- Morris R. Cohen;
- Francis W. Coker;
- Richard McKeon;
- Perry Miller;
- Marjorie Hope Nicolson;
- John Herman Randall Jr.;
- J. Salwyn Schapiro;
- Louis B. Wright.

Kelley classifies this first committee as:

- 2 historians;
- 3 philosophers;
- 4 literary scholars;
- 1 political theorist.

This is strong retrospective control from a later executive editor, but the original 1940 masthead/frontmatter should still be direct-locked.

The committee shows that interdisciplinarity was embedded not just in Lovejoy's programme but in **editorial governance**.

## V. The founding governance is already geographically distributed

Even before the later international board, the first committee joins scholars from different universities/disciplines rather than operating as a Hopkins departmental board.

Important first-generation nodes include:

- Hopkins: Lovejoy / Nicolson / related Club ecology;
- City College: Wiener / Cohen;
- Chicago: McKeon;
- Harvard: Perry Miller;

Thus the journal's corporate/editorial structure was trans-institutional at birth.

This should be tested against the ACLS Committee on the History of Ideas membership: overlap may explain how the original editorial committee was assembled.

## VII. Internationalization reaches governance, not just article content

The 1973 structure is especially important for the global/cross-cultural master map.

### Board of Editors

Arthur F. Wright connects JHI governance to Chinese-civilization / Committee on Chinese Thought / area-studies networks.

### Corresponding Editors

Hajime Nakamura connects to:
- Japanese Buddhist/Indian intellectual history;
- the Wiener-edited *Ways of Thinking of Eastern Peoples*;

Thus by the Wiener era the journal does more than publish non-Western subjects:

> **it incorporates scholars associated with distinct regional histories-of-ideas traditions into its governance and correspondence infrastructure.**

This supports the emerging model of mature JHI as an **interface institution**.

## VIII. Editorial succession and institutional mobility

Donald Kelley provides a highly useful later first-person editorial history.

### Wiener era

Wiener is effectively involved from 1940 and remains editor for approximately 45 years.

At some point the journal's editorial base is associated with Temple University before the 1985 transition. The exact City College→Temple date and reason remain open.

### 1985

Donald R. Kelley replaces Wiener.

The journal moves to the University of Rochester.

### 1986

Robin Ladrach joins Kelley as assistant/associate editor.

### 1991

Kelley and Ladrach move with JHI to Rutgers University.

### 2006

JHI moves to the University of Pennsylvania.

Kelley passes editorial responsibility to a team:

- Warren Breckman;
- Martin J. Burke;
- Anthony Grafton;
- Ann Moyer;

with Robin Ladrach continuing as managing editor.

The durable corporation therefore allows a striking institutional mobility:

`CITY COLLEGE`
→ `TEMPLE`
→ `ROCHESTER`
→ `RUTGERS`
→ `PENN`

while `Journal of the History of Ideas, Inc.` persists as the legal/publishing entity.

Exact transition dates/contractual publishing relations need direct archival verification.

## IX. Current continuity

Modern bibliographic/index records still identify the publisher/corporate entity as:

`Journal of the History of Ideas, Inc.`

Current nonprofit data place it in Philadelphia and preserve the same EIN.

University of Pennsylvania Press currently publishes the journal and describes JHI as a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization.

Thus the 1940 corporate body appears to have survived multiple university/editorial migrations.

This is an unusually strong institutional continuity across the entire history of the field.

## X. `Society for the History of Ideas` — HOLD / ENTITY RESOLUTION REQUIRED

One older repertory describes JHI from 1996 as:

`Society for the History of Ideas - Johns Hopkins University Press`.

This is currently **not enough evidence** to infer that:

- a separate Society for the History of Ideas owned JHI;
- the Society was a rename of JHI, Inc.;
- it was the same entity as the International Society for the History of Ideas;
- a corporate reorganization happened in 1996.

Other contemporary/current bibliographic sources continue to list `Journal of the History of Ideas, Inc.` as publisher.

Therefore:

`SOCIETY FOR THE HISTORY OF IDEAS` = **ENTITY-RESOLUTION HOLD**.

Do not build institutional genealogy on this catalog label until independent evidence is recovered.

## XI. New conceptual consequence: the journal is a mobile corporation, not merely a venue

The institutional form itself matters for the history of intellectual history.

JHI's structure permitted:

- editorial work across universities;
- interdisciplinary governance;
- international corresponding editors;
- long Wiener continuity after Lovejoy;
- later institutional migration;
- publication partnerships that could change without dissolving the scholarly corporation.

Thus the history of JHI can be treated as a history of **organizational technologies for maintaining an interdisciplinary field**.

The corporation supplied continuity while:

`EDITORS`
`UNIVERSITY BASES`
`PUBLISHERS / PLATFORM RELATIONS`
`REGIONAL NETWORKS`
`METHODS`

could change.

This may explain why `history of ideas` survives repeated methodological critiques more effectively than a method-centered genealogy would predict.

## XIII. Master-map implication

The expanded field history now has at least **four different institutional carriers**, which must remain distinct:

1. `JOHNS HOPKINS HISTORY OF IDEAS CLUB` — seminar/research community;
2. `ACLS COMMITTEE ON THE HISTORY OF IDEAS` — field-building/planning infrastructure;
3. `JOURNAL OF THE HISTORY OF IDEAS, INC.` — durable legal/editorial/publishing corporation;
4. `INTERNATIONAL SOCIETY FOR THE HISTORY OF IDEAS` — international conference/network organization.

They overlap through Lovejoy, Wiener, McKeon and others, but they are **not one institution**.

Later regional networks add additional independent infrastructures:

- Committee on Chinese Thought / AAS / ACLS;
- East-West Center;
- Zea / IPGH Committee on History of Ideas in America;
- other regional traditions still to map.

The historical problem is increasingly one of **institutional interfaces and translations among research programmes**, not one disciplinary lineage.
