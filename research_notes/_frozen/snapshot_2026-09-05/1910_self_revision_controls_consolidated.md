# Lovejoy 1909–1910 — self-revision controls: Kant predicate fission and formal-logic erratum

Consolidated: 2026-08-26
Status: **CANONICAL THEMATIC DOSSIER / ≤1910 SEARCH BRANCH CLOSED**
Supersedes active-tree Batches217–218; incorporates the terminal results of archived Batches221–222. Original versions remain in Git history.

## Core result

The early corpus contains two directly visible kinds of authorial correction in 1910 that must not be conflated:

1. **intellectual self-reclassification** — closer historical investigation forces a broad predicate to split into domain-specific claims;
2. **local erratum** — a writing/proofreading slip is repaired without changing the analytic object, evidentiary scope or historical classification.

The Kant 1909→1910 sequence is the clean positive control for the first type. Lovejoy's 1910 formal-logic note is the negative control for the second.

Project terms such as `predicate fission`, `category fission`, `resolution increase`, and `intellectual self-reclassification` are ours, not Lovejoy's.

## I. 1909 → 1910 Kant — direct before/after self-revision

Primary sources:

- Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Argument for Organic Evolution Before the Origin of Species`, *Popular Science Monthly* 75 (November 1909), especially p.513;
- Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Kant and Evolution`, Part I, *Popular Science Monthly* 77 (December 1910), 538–553.

Part II (January 1911) is excluded under the ordinary 1910 cutoff.

### 1909 — the broad classification

While reconstructing pre-Darwinian arguments from vertebrate homology, Lovejoy writes that the relevant facts had before the end of the eighteenth century made `evolutionists of Diderot, of Kant`, and nearly Buffon.

Kant therefore functions in the 1909 argument as an early-evolutionist example; the sentence does not yet separate cosmic evolution, anthropological development, biological transformism and mechanistic organic generation.

### 1910 — Lovejoy identifies his own earlier error

In `Kant and Evolution` Lovejoy attacks the retrospective legend of Kant as a wholesale evolutionary/Darwinian precursor. A footnote explicitly says that he himself, before coming `to close quarters with the subject`, had fallen into this error and points directly to *Popular Science Monthly*, November 1909, p.513.

The correction edge is therefore author-supplied rather than reconstructed by us.

### The corrective procedure

Lovejoy's 1910 procedure requires:

1. approximate chronological ordering of Kant's relevant writings;
2. restoration of disputed expressions to context;
3. control of Kant's historical meanings of terms;
4. decomposition of the umbrella label `evolution` into non-equivalent propositions before historical identity is assigned.

The resulting predicate split is:

- Kant can be a **cosmic evolutionist**;
- Kant can remain an **anthropological evolutionist**;
- neither licenses **general biological transformism**;
- mechanistic cosmic explanation does not license a mechanistic account of organic generation.

Lovejoy explicitly stresses that mechanistic physiology and transformism have no necessary logical connection, just as vitalism does not logically entail fixity of species.

The before/after structure can be represented as:

`1909 GLOBAL LABEL: Kant = evolutionist`
→ `closer source investigation`
→ `1910 predicate/category fission`
→ `cosmic evolution ≠ anthropological evolution ≠ biological transformism ≠ organic mechanism`.

### Historiographical value

This is unusually strong evidence because both the earlier overbroad claim and the later correction are published primary texts, and Lovejoy himself identifies the earlier statement as the error being repaired.

It supplies a direct published analogue to the relation-specific pressure visible in 004/005 without proving one continuously self-conscious method from 1898 to 1910.

Safe formulation:

> **the 1909→1910 Kant sequence shows Lovejoy explicitly shrinking the unit of historical classification from a whole-actor umbrella label to actor × domain × proposition.**

## II. 1910 formal logic — erratum negative control

Primary pair:

- Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Treatment of "Opposition" in Formal Logic`, *The Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* 7 (1910), 101–105;
- Arthur O. Lovejoy, `A Note from Professor A. O. Lovejoy`, same journal 7.5 (3 March 1910), 133.

The correction note is dated Columbia, Missouri, 19 February 1910. Lovejoy characterizes the problem as an `erratum`, a `slip in writing`, and something escaping `too hasty proof-reading`; he asks for a local deletion at the beginning of Rule IV and restates the rule.

This is genuine explicit self-correction, but it does not show:

- new or closer historical investigation;
- redistribution of a broad historical predicate;
- changed classification of the object under study;
- changed evidential scope or historical unit.

Classification:

> **AUTHORIAL ERRATUM / LOCAL FORMAL-RULE REPAIR / NEGATIVE CONTROL.**

The distinction prevents our own category mistake: authorial acknowledgement of error is not enough to establish intellectual self-reclassification.

## III. Threshold for counting a self-reclassification event

A correction should enter the longitudinal historical-method argument only when it alters at least one of:

- evidentially licensed unit;
- relation;
- scope;
- historical predicate/classification.

A writing slip, notation repair or typographical erratum is a different revision operation.

Thus:

> **acknowledged error is insufficient; the correction must change what the evidence licenses us to say about the historical object.**

---

## IV. ≤1910 saturation verdict

Repository search, Wilson's early bibliography and recoverable full-text searching were audited for further Lovejoy backward references/corrections using `error`, `correction`, `misconception`, `slip in writing`, `former paper`, retraction and title-specific language.

Current qualified verdict:

> **the Kant 1909→1910 sequence is the only currently controlled ≤1910 case surviving the stronger intellectual-self-reclassification threshold.**

This is a corpus/search verdict, not a metaphysical claim that no unknown letter, lecture manuscript or inaccessible print item can exist.

A later 1914 Lovejoy review of Leuba forms an author–reader–author feedback loop around the 1906 `Primitive Philosophy` paper, but it lies beyond the ordinary cutoff and its exact corrected proposition remains direct-primary dependent. It should not be used to manufacture a continuous early chain.

Generic `Lovejoy + error/correction` searching is therefore saturated. Reopen only when a genuinely new primary object appears.

## V. Relation to 004/005

The evidentiary asymmetry must be retained:

- **004/005:** working notebooks show repeated relation-specific reclassification and selector/relation discrimination in sequence;
- **1909→1910 Kant:** explicit published authorial self-correction with a directly identified before/after statement;
- **formal-logic erratum:** local repair showing that not every correction belongs to the intellectual class.

The safe longitudinal statement is:

> **004/005 show repeated pressure toward relation-specific judgment; the Kant sequence supplies one unusually explicit published self-revision in which a broad label is narrowed after closer historical control.**

Do not write that Lovejoy repeatedly published explicit intellectual self-corrections before 1910.
