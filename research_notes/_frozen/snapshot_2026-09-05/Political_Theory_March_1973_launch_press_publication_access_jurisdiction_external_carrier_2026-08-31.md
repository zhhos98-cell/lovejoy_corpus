# *Political Theory* public launch, March 1973: publication-access jurisdiction in a contemporary press carrier

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT CONTEMPORARY NEWSPAPER PAGE IMAGE + TEXT CONTROL / EXTERNAL CARRIER / QUOTE PROVENANCE (INTERVIEW vs PRESS RELEASE) OPEN**

## Core result

A contemporary newspaper carrier adds a dimension that is only implicit in the February 1973 `Editorial Statement` and strongly echoes the 1967 CSPT founding anxiety about publication jurisdiction.

*The Ukrainian Weekly*, Saturday, 10 March 1973, p.2, published a notice headed approximately `Start New Journal of Political Theory` shortly after the first issue appeared.

The full newspaper page has now been checked directly as a page image as well as parsed text.

It attributes to Robert Lamb and Walter Odajnyk a public launch rationale in which *Political Theory* was intended to cover the whole field of political philosophy, avoid identification with one geographical area or one particular political theory, and provide a broad platform. Most importantly, the report attributes to the founders a specific **publication-access problem**:

- some theorists felt their work was discriminated against by established journals and were pushed toward small or near-underground publications;
- other theorists had difficulty publishing because political-science journals printed very little theory;
- the new journal was presented as a platform broad enough to answer this problem.

This turns a general field-jurisdiction claim into a concrete communications-infrastructure problem:

`THEORY WORK EXISTS`
+
`ESTABLISHED VENUE ACCESS IS NARROW / UNEVEN`
-> `NEW PERIODICAL INFRASTRUCTURE`.

This is highly congruent with the earlier CSPT founding correspondence as reconstructed from Box 2, where publication access and the orientation of APSR formed part of the perceived disciplinary threat.

But preserve:

`1967 PUBLICATION ANXIETY`
+
`1973 LAUNCH PUBLICATION RHETORIC`
!= `PROVED DIRECT CAUSAL CONTINUITY`.

Direct correspondence/prospectus evidence is still needed to establish how the founders carried the earlier CSPT problem into the journal project.

## II. Field coverage and non-exclusive orientation are publicly advertised

The newspaper describes the founders as presenting the new journal as the first to encompass the whole field of political philosophy.

It says the publication would avoid identification with:

- one geographic area;
- one particular political theory.

This independently reproduces the February inaugural programme's broad/international and anti-exclusive architecture.

Thus by March 1973 the journal's non-exclusive identity was not only internal editorial copy; it was part of its **public launch representation**.

Safe formulation:

`FEBRUARY CORPORATE PROGRAMME`
-> `MARCH PUBLIC LAUNCH CARRIER WITH SAME BROAD/NON-EXCLUSIVE POSITION`.

Hold:

`NEWSPAPER INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED EDITORIAL PRACTICE`.

The newspaper may simply be carrying the founders' publicity language.

## III. Publication access is stated as a founding problem

The strongest new content is the explanation of why another journal was needed.

The founders are reported as saying, in substance, that:

- some theorists believed established journals discriminated against their writings;
- this forced them toward small, almost underground publications;
- others found publication difficult because political-science journals carried only one or two theory pieces a year;
- *Political Theory* would function as a broad platform.

This is not merely a philosophical disagreement about behavioralism.

It is a material/jurisdictional problem of:

`EDITORIAL CAPACITY`
+
`ACCESS TO PRESTIGIOUS CARRIERS`
+
`DISCIPLINARY REPRESENTATION`.

That makes the journal itself a solution in communications infrastructure.

Compact rule:

> **A method/field dispute can become institutionally consequential when it is also a carrier-access problem.**

This is directly relevant to the repo's larger argument about infrastructure making heterogeneous intellectual work recurrent and portable.

## IV. Strong but still non-genealogical connection to CSPT 1967

The existing CSPT Box 2 dossier reconstructs a 1967 founding environment in which Melvin Richter and others worried about publication access, especially the orientation of APSR, while founders disagreed about whether political theory should separate institutionally from political science.

The March 1973 report now shows founders of *Political Theory* publicly describing lack of publication space / discrimination in established journals as part of the need for the new periodical.

The structural sequence is therefore:

`1967 CSPT: PUBLICATION / DISCIPLINARY THREAT`
-> `1970 JOURNAL IDEA IN COLUMBIA CSPT CHAPTER`
-> `1973 JOURNAL LAUNCH: PUBLICATION-ACCESS RATIONALE`.

The middle edge (`journal idea in Columbia CSPT chapter`) is directly controlled by May 1973 p.131.

This makes a **historical sequence of co-present problems and institutional events** secure.

What remains open is causal/documentary continuity at proposition level:

- did Lamb/Odajnyk explicitly inherit Richter's APSR concern?
- was the publication-access language already in a 1970/71 prospectus?
- did CSPT leadership shape the SAGE pitch?
- did recipient-side recruitment letters carry the same rationale?

Thus:

`SEQUENCE + PROBLEM CONTINUITY`
!= `PROVED PERSON-TO-PERSON TRANSMISSION`.

## V. The press report reproduces the issue's heterogeneous architecture

The report does not stop at general programme language. It lists first-issue contents including:

- Giovanni Sartori;
- George Armstrong Kelly + Bertell Ollman comment;
- Felix Oppenheim;
- Virginia Held comment + Oppenheim rejoinder.

It then describes the journal's planned recurring areas:

- history of political thought;
- modern theory;
- conceptual analysis;
- polemic argumentation;

as well as:

- graduate-student articles;
- special issues;
- multiple-perspective reviews;
- author self-review/reassessment;
- bibliography of political-theory articles in other journals.

These details closely track the February direct-full `Editorial Statement`.

This strengthens the source-state interpretation that the March report is carrying founders' official launch programme rather than independently inventing a different description.

It also makes the first issue itself a public demonstration of dialogue format: comment/rejoinder architecture appears in the launch publicity.

---

## VI. Communications infrastructure has two layers: production and publicity

The February statement gives internal venue design.

The March press carrier shows that the same design was being communicated outward to potential readers/authors as a solution to publication constraints.

This suggests two analytically distinct infrastructures:

1. `EDITORIAL INFRASTRUCTURE` — sections, reviews, bibliographies, article selection, issue design;
2. `RECRUITMENT / PUBLICITY INFRASTRUCTURE` — telling a broader scholarly public why the venue exists and whom it is meant to serve.

Do not assume the same actor controlled both layers or that the newspaper item reached the intended scholarly audience in measurable numbers.

But the source proves the launch rationale had entered public circulation by March 1973.
