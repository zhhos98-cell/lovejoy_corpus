# Batch 134 — Lévi in Japan, 1898: French diplomatic microfilms are already online and can test the departure window

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: follow Batch133's return-route audit by identifying electronic diplomatic records capable of fixing Sylvain Lévi's Japan departure / Nagasaki–Vladivostok transition more precisely than later biography.

## Core result

The French diplomatic archive has two unusually well-fitted 1898 Tokyo-legation files whose microfilms are already attached as digital reproductions. These are not generic Japan holdings. One is **correspondence with private individuals covering September 1897–December 1898**; the other is **incoming/outgoing correspondence with Kobe and Nagasaki in 1898**. Together they bracket Lévi's Japanese phase and the exact port from which his own published Siberian return narrative begins.

The files are:

1. **697PO/B/63 — Correspondance avec les particuliers (septembre 1897-décembre 1898)**. The official description explicitly says the attached digital reproductions derive from microfilm **2MI/1255**.
2. **697PO/B/52 — Correspondance (arrivée-départ) avec Kobe (janvier-septembre 1898), Nagasaki (1898)**. The official description explicitly says the attached digital reproductions derive from microfilm **2MI/1253**.

No public text index has yet produced a positive hit for `Lévi`. These are therefore high-probability digital targets, not positive components. But B/63 is especially promising because it is correspondence with **particuliers**, not merely inter-office dispatches; B/52 is especially promising as a movement control because Lévi's own 1899 travel account is `De Nagasaki à Moscou par la Sibérie`.

## 1. Priority 1: 697PO/B/63, private correspondence

Official record:

https://archivesdiplomatiques.diplomatie.gouv.fr/ark:/14366/2kl3gwzvj0xd

The archival hierarchy is:

`Légation puis ambassade de France à Tokyo → Série B → Sous-série 1861–1918 → Correspondance avec les particuliers`.

Unit:

`697PO/B/63`

Date:

`septembre 1897-décembre 1898`.

The official description states that digital reproductions attached to the record come from microfilm `2MI/1255`.

### Why this is the first manual target

The date span begins immediately before Lévi left France in October 1897 and continues through the end of 1898. If the legation corresponded directly with him, arranged introductions, forwarded mail, handled an academic mission request, or recorded his movements as a private French scholar, this is the logical place for it.

The first pass should not read every frame. Use the viewer's thumbnails or register structure to identify chronological runs and prioritize roughly **April–September 1898**, with strongest attention to late summer.

Search visually for:

- `Lévi`, `Levi`, `Sylvain`;
- `professeur au Collège de France`;
- `mission`, `mission scientifique`, `Instruction publique`;
- `Nagasaki`, `Vladivostok`, `Russie`, `Sibérie`;
- Japanese scholarly contacts already associated with Lévi's visit, including Takakusu / Fujishima variants.

If a hit appears, preserve **frame number + date + sender/recipient + full page**, not a cropped name only.

## 2. Priority 2: 697PO/B/52, Kobe and Nagasaki correspondence

Official record:

https://archivesdiplomatiques.diplomatie.gouv.fr/ark:/14366/fjdshx85vp0g

Unit:

`697PO/B/52`

Title/date:

`Correspondance (arrivée-départ) avec Kobe (janvier-septembre 1898), Nagasaki (1898).`

The description explicitly identifies digital reproductions from microfilm `2MI/1253`.

### Why Nagasaki matters

Batch133 established that Lévi's own published travel account frames the final route as:

`Nagasaki → Vladivostok / Siberia → Moscow`.

Therefore the Nagasaki correspondence is a direct administrative control on the last Japanese phase. It could contain nothing about him; consular correspondence is not a passenger register. But if a French academic mission, traveler, introduction, baggage shipment, ticket/passport question, or passage to Vladivostok was handled by the consular network, the unit could narrow the departure date materially.

Manual priority:

1. find the start of the **Nagasaki 1898** subsection;
2. identify whether entries are chronological;
3. move first to **August–September**;
4. capture any Lévi/mission/travel reference with surrounding pages and frame numbers.

A silent B/52 would only be a bounded negative about preserved legation-consular correspondence, not evidence that Lévi did not pass through Nagasaki.

## 3. Secondary Tokyo files should be lead-driven, not fully swept yet

The same 1898 series exposes several adjacent units:

- `697PO/B/44` — outgoing correspondence with local authorities, September 1898–December 1900;
- `697PO/B/60` — correspondence with other legations, March 1898–December 1900;
- `697PO/B/32` — personnel, cabinet, accounting, protocol, cipher, 1898.

These should **not** be read wholesale at this stage. If B/63 or B/52 yields a name, dispatch number, date, Russian transit clue, or official introduction, then B/44/B/60 become reciprocal-chase targets. Without such an anchor, the expected return is much lower.

---

## 4. What a positive hit would change

At present the return chronology is:

- own travel narrative: Nagasaki → Siberia/Moscow;
- AGORHA: mission ends September 1898, return via Russia;
- EPHE: Lévi physically at Vimy on 9 October 1898;
- Wilson-cited Lovejoy Paris letter: 17 October 1898.

A diplomatic document could sharpen this from month-level to day-level:

`last Japan/Nagasaki date`

→ `transit toward Vladivostok`

→ `Vimy 9 Oct`

→ `Lovejoy 17 Oct`.

That would materially improve the paper's claim that Lovejoy arrived during Lévi's immediate institutional re-entry, rather than merely in the same academic year.

A direct letter from Lévi in B/63 would be even more valuable because it could disclose what he was doing, whom he met, or how the mission was administratively described at the time. None is presently claimed.

https://archivesdiplomatiques.diplomatie.gouv.fr/ark:/14366/2kl3gwzvj0xd

5. if there is an alphabetical name index, search `L` first;
6. send any likely Lévi frame plus one frame before/after if needed.

### B/52 second

https://archivesdiplomatiques.diplomatie.gouv.fr/ark:/14366/fjdshx85vp0g

1. identify where the Kobe section ends and Nagasaki begins;
2. prioritize Nagasaki and Aug–Sep 1898;
3. search for traveler/mission/passages rather than expecting a surname index;
4. preserve frame numbers.

The user need not transcribe French handwriting; screenshots are sufficient for a first paleographic pass.

## Evidence discipline

- Digitized-file fit is not a positive archival hit.
- `correspondance avec les particuliers` raises prior probability but does not imply completeness.
- B/52 is not a passenger manifest.
- A negative search through thumbnails/OCR would not be publication-grade absence evidence unless the whole unit is demonstrably indexed or inspected.
- The 9 October Vimy record remains the current hard terminus ante quem for Lévi's return to France.
