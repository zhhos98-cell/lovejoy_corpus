# Marillier 1900 → Söderblom 1901 → Lovejoy 1906: mana / magic / efficacy genealogy

## Status

This note extends the 005–Marillier–1906 reconstruction by adding a second student witness (Nathan Söderblom) and a near-contemporary relay of Marillier's own 1900 classification of magic.

**Revised working judgment:** the chain from Marillier's EPHE comparative-religion teaching to Lovejoy's 1906 “Primitive Energetics” is now stronger than a general influence claim. The evidence suggests a specific conceptual field in which Marillier distinguished (a) rites acting directly on nature, (b) efficacy dependent on ritual qualification / initiation, and (c) power or `mana` vested in specially empowered persons. Notebook 005 works through closely corresponding problems, while Söderblom independently carries a generalized Marillier-derived use of `mana` into print in 1901.

This still does **not** prove that Lovejoy copied a Marillier lecture formula or that the word `mana` occurred in the surviving 005 pages. The first-pass transcription has not produced `mana`, `manitou`, `wakonda`, or `orenda` as secure notebook readings.

## 1. Prehistory: Codrington → Marillier

### 1891–1892

Robert Henry Codrington's *The Melanesians* (1891) made `mana` a major comparative-religion datum. Marillier reviewed Codrington in 1892 and also referred to Melanesian `mana` in his 1892 discussion of Frazer. Later historiography therefore places `mana` inside Marillier's research environment well before Lovejoy's documented 1898–1899 attendance. **[S; primary review collation still needed]**

This matters because notebook 005 begins with Codrington in the bibliography and later develops questions of intrinsic, transferable, and quasi-mechanical efficacy even when the native term itself is not visible.

---

## 2. 1898–1899: two students in the same Marillier seminar

The official EPHE report names Nathan Söderblom among Marillier's regular students and Lovejoy among auditors who took an active part in the work. Marillier's two course problems were survivance / non-moral eschatology and human sacrifice / ritual anthropophagy, with Bantu, Guinea, North American, Indian, agrarian and fecundative material.

Söderblom's 1898 EPHE diploma, *Traces dans le Mazdéisme d'une ancienne conception sur la survivance des morts*, was directed by Marillier. Lovejoy notebook 005 contains the internal slip `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June` and reproduces the course architecture in unusual detail. **[P + MS]**

This creates a controlled two-student comparison rather than a single-witness reconstruction.

---

## 3. Marillier's 1900 `Religion`: mana is explicitly generalized

Marillier's article `Religion` appeared in *La Grande Encyclopédie*, vol. XXVIII, printed pp. 341–364 (some catalogues give 341–366).

Later close study of the article reports six occurrences of `mana`. At the first occurrence, p. 349, Marillier uses the Melanesian term for a generalized complex of natural and supernatural gifts/powers attaching to a person and giving efficacy over persons and things. **[S; direct facsimile collation pending]**

The article is digitally available in several public-domain witnesses:

- Gallica: vol. XXVIII, ark `bpt6k24663w`;
- Wikimedia Commons / Wikisource: `Grande Encyclopédie XXVIII.djvu`, 1,284 scans, 101.07 MB;
- Internet Archive source identifier exposed by Wikisource: `lagrandeencyclop28dref`.

The Wikisource scan has front matter before printed page 1; the Marillier article begins approximately around scan 353 and continues to roughly scan 376. This offset is a working locator and must be checked against the facsimile before being used as a citation.

## 4. Near-contemporary relay: Marillier's three classes of magic

The 1911 *Encyclopædia Britannica* article `Magic` explicitly attributes to **L. Marillier** a threefold classification:

1. **magic of word or act** — including mimetic rites, rain-making, disease-making and sympathetic magic; some rites act directly on nature (wind, sun), while others work through a god/spirit that may itself be coerced;
2. **magic of the human being independent of rite/formula** — efficacy requires ceremonial purity, initiation, or another qualifying condition;
3. **magic requiring a specially empowered person plus forms** — certain persons possess magical power (`mana`); their words can bring rain/sun and their presence can fertilize fields; power may attach to person or office, sometimes reinterpreted through an indwelling god.

**[NC]** The Britannica bibliography for `Magic` explicitly lists Marillier's `Religion` in *La Grande Encyclopédie* among its general authorities. This makes the classification a strong near-contemporary relay of Marillier's published position, although the 1900 facsimile still needs direct line-by-line verification.

## 5. Notebook 005 correspondence with the three-class scheme

### A. Direct action on nature — 005 PDF p. 64 (MS p. 129)

The corrected notebook records a class of magical sacrifice that does not work by constraining a god's will but **directly upon forces of nature**; rain-making is the example. The notebook also calls magical sacrifice a kind of machine. **[MS]**

This is the closest manuscript correspondence to Britannica's Marillier class (i): mimetic rain-making and rites conceived as directly affecting nature.

### B. Qualification, purity, initiation — 005 pp. 82, 112–117

The notebook separates circumcision and initiation from sacrifice. Blood, seclusion, puberty restrictions, ceremonial trials, purification, fortification and altered social status can qualify a person for a new ritual condition without being a mutilated survival of human sacrifice. **[MS]**

This sits closely beside Marillier class (ii), where efficacy depends on ceremonial purity, initiation or another special condition. The correspondence is structural, not yet lexical.

### C. Power in person / object; fertility — 005 pp. 85–90, 99–105, 119

Lovejoy repeatedly distinguishes efficacy inherent in blood, body parts, charms, plants or other things from efficacy explained by an indwelling spirit. Body parts transfer qualities; blood can be life-giving, protective or fecundating; agricultural rites can transfer life/power to seed and crops; p. 105 calls one proposed mechanism quasi-mechanical / magical rather than sacramental. **[MS]**

This corresponds to the broader Marillier field summarized under class (iii): specially concentrated power, fertility, and power not always reducible to a personal deity. Exact dependence remains unproved.

## 6. 1901 second student witness: Söderblom attributes generalized `mana` to Marillier

Söderblom's *La vie future d'après le mazdéisme à la lumière des croyances parallèles dans les autres religions* (Paris, 1901) is digitally preserved (Internet Archive identifier `laviefuturedapr00sdgoog`).

Later scholarship identifies a passage at p. 37 in which Söderblom, while discussing Melanesian material, explicitly invokes a generalized expression he attributes to **M. Marillier**, namely the `mana` of a person. **[S, pointing to P; direct IA page verification still pending]**

This is highly significant because Söderblom was a documented Marillier student in the same 1898–1899 seminar year as Lovejoy. It supplies an independent student trajectory in which Marillier's teaching/research vocabulary around `mana` becomes visible in print almost immediately after the seminar period.

---

## 7. 1900–1906: divergence from a common training environment

### Söderblom

Söderblom appears to use the Marillier-derived `mana` vocabulary inside a comparative history of religion and afterlife. His later intellectual trajectory increasingly emphasizes holiness, religious experience and comparative theology.

### Lovejoy

Lovejoy's 1906 *Monist* article states that its conclusions were reached in **1900** and were strongly influenced by his studies under Marillier. Lovejoy abstracts the evidence into an impersonal, diffused, transferable, controllable, quasi-mechanical energy and proposes `Primitive Energetics` / `manitouism` as a theoretical category.

Thus the same Marillier environment may have generated at least two distinct abstractions:

> **Marillier / mana / comparative efficacy → Söderblom: comparative religious power / afterlife**
>
> **Marillier / mechanism-sensitive comparison → Lovejoy: causal efficacy / Primitive Energetics**

This divergence is potentially more informative than a simple teacher-to-student influence model.

## 8. Mauss as a third institutional control

After Marillier's death, Mauss's official 1901–1902 EPHE report says that in his Tuesday conference on **magic among the Melanesians** he tried to maintain the traditions implanted by his teacher Marillier. **[P]**

This is an important institutional control: Melanesian magic was not a retrospective association imposed by later historians. It was explicitly understood by Marillier's immediate successor and former student as a continuation of Marillier's seminar tradition.

The 1911 Britannica article then places Marillier's classification alongside Frazer, Hubert/Mauss and Marett in the genealogy of theories of magic.

It may also help determine whether Lovejoy's pp. 14–20 taxonomy was an extension of seminar material or part of a still broader Marillier argument.

## 10. Evidence-grade conclusion

### Secure

- Lovejoy and Söderblom were both documented participants in Marillier's 1898–1899 EPHE orbit.
- Söderblom's diploma was directed by Marillier.
- 005 has `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June` and a large course-concordant sacrifice dossier.
- 005 p. 64 explicitly distinguishes direct action on nature from action mediated through gods.
- 1911 Britannica explicitly attributes a three-class theory of magic to Marillier, including direct nature-action, initiation/purity and mana-bearing persons.
- Mauss's 1901–1902 EPHE report explicitly presents his Melanesian magic seminar as maintaining traditions implanted by Marillier.
- Lovejoy's 1906 article dates its conclusions to 1900 and acknowledges strong Marillier influence.

### Strong inference

- Marillier's mechanism-sensitive magic classification forms part of the immediate conceptual background of 005.
- The line from Marillier's `mana` / magical power to Lovejoy's `Primitive Energetics` is now a high-probability intellectual genealogy.
- Söderblom is an independent control witness to the circulation of generalized Marillier-derived `mana` language around 1900–1901.

### Still unproved

- The exact wording and location of Marillier's three classes in the 1900 `Religion` facsimile.
- A direct notebook use of the word `mana` by Lovejoy.
- A date proving that 005 p. 64 or pp. 85–105 precede Lovejoy's 1900 conceptual formulation.
- Whether Lovejoy heard the mana formulation orally from Marillier or reconstructed it independently from Codrington and the same comparative literature.
