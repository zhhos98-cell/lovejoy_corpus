- remote civilizations cannot be reconstructed by assuming rationality is historically invariant;
- past ideas cannot be treated as analytically identical to present ideas while ignoring contextual relations and intentions;
- the present cannot serve as the criterion for the past;
- reason itself has a history;
- the historically relevant relation among ideas, institutions and social necessities can render some accounts conceptually absurd;

## I. Source-state correction: `Vico and Contemporary Thought` is a later collected reprint carrier

The earlier search trail could misleadingly suggest an independent 1976 book carrier.

Direct bibliographic controls now clarify the sequence.

The papers were originally presented at the January 1976 New York conference and published in two special issues of *Social Research*, vol.43 nos.3–4 (1976).

They were subsequently collected/reprinted as:

- *Vico and Contemporary Thought*, Humanities Press, 1979;
- Macmillan, 1980;
- 256 pp.;
- explicitly described in library/Google Books records as reprinted from *Social Research*.

In the collected pagination Haddock's pieces are conventionally cited:

- `Vico and the Problem of Historical Reconstruction`, Part I, 122–129;

Thus:

`1976 SOCIAL RESEARCH ORIGINAL CARRIER`

Do not call the book an independent 1976 original version merely because some bibliographies cite the conference year 1976.

Core source rule:

`CONFERENCE YEAR / COPYRIGHT YEAR != PUBLICATION-CARRIER YEAR`.

## II. 1976 `Vico and Anachronism`: full direct method text

B. A. Haddock, `Vico and Anachronism`, *Political Studies* 24.4 (1976), 483–487, DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9248.1976.tb00135.x.

The direct article opening says Vico sought a methodology enabling historians to portray remote civilizations without importing their own assumptions.

Haddock identifies the core error as assuming rationality is everywhere and always the same, so that ideas formulated in the past can be analysed as if they were ideas formulated in the present.

He explicitly says this procedure ignores:

- contextual relations restricting ranges of meaning;
- intentions distinguishing utterances/ideas;
- historically alien forms such as hieroglyphs and myths.

The anti-anachronist rule is therefore stronger than generic `context matters`:

> **The historical identity and admissible meaning of an idea depend on historically variable modes of thought, not simply on the propositional wording visible to a present analyst.**

Compact:

`PRESENT ANALYTIC INTELLIGIBILITY`
!= `PAST HISTORICAL MEANING`.

## III. Reason has a history

Haddock's direct formulation says anachronism can be avoided only if it is accepted that **reason has a history** and past ideas are evaluated through changing modes of thought.

This matters because it changes the proof burden for historical rationality.

The historian cannot infer:

`AN IDEA IS RATIONAL / IRRATIONAL BY OUR CRITERIA`
-> `THE IDEA HAD THE SAME RATIONAL STATUS FOR THE HISTORICAL AGENT`.

Historical reconstruction must recover the conceptual relations that made the idea/action intelligible within a historically specific form of life.

This is a direct same-year bridge to Haddock's Vichian reconstruction route without requiring a Skinner-derived genealogy.

---

## IV. 1976 directly criticizes retrospective canonization of Vico

A major section of the review attacks the conventional history-of-ideas picture of Vico as an isolated genius whose real significance lies in anticipating later nineteenth- and twentieth-century disciplines.

Haddock says this picture has two interpretive costs:

1. it makes Vico's linguistic/contemporary context appear unnecessary;
2. it permits later thought to become the key for decoding Vico's difficult passages.

He criticizes readings that use Vico as material for contemporary philosophical projects and argues that one still faces the historical task of explaining Vico's intention in a specific form of words.

Therefore:

`LATER INTELLECTUAL FRUITFULNESS / DURABILITY`
!= `HISTORICAL MEANING IN ORIGINAL ENGAGEMENT`.

And:

`RETROSPECTIVE CANONICAL ROLE`
!= `ORIGINAL PROBLEM ROLE`.

This is a particularly clean historical analogue to the repo's carrier/canon distinction.

---

## V. Context does not abolish originality

Haddock rejects a false choice between contextual/source reconstruction and originality.

He argues that locating the early Vico works in dissatisfaction with Descartes and identifying Bacon as furnishing philosophical vocabulary does not reduce Vico's originality; it may instead sharpen the idiosyncratic use he made of sources.

Thus:

`SOURCE / CONTEXTUAL DERIVATION`
!= `LOSS OF ORIGINALITY`.

This is structurally parallel to the repo's long-standing rule:

`BORROWED COMPONENT`
!= `BORROWED ARRANGEMENT / FUNCTION`.

But keep this as structural comparison, not Lovejoy->Haddock genealogy.

## VI. Theory delimits possibility; it does not determine history a priori

The strongest proposition in the latter half of the 1976 article concerns Pompa's interpretation of Vico.

Haddock describes the relevant philosophical theory as delimiting the **range of historical possibility**, not determining history a priori.

Historical accounts may be rejected as conceptually absurd where they assign advanced modes of rationality to primitive social arrangements in ways incompatible with the relation between ideas, institutions and social conditions.

Therefore:

`THEORY AS CONSTRAINT ON POSSIBLE INTERPRETATIONS`
!= `THEORY AS DEDUCTIVE SCRIPT FOR ACTUAL HISTORY`.

This is crucial for the UNIT×RELATION project because it separates:

- what cannot coherently be related;
- what actually occurred;
- what evidence is required to establish occurrence.

Conceptual exclusion is not positive historical proof.

---

## VII. A philosophically extractable doctrine is not automatically Vico's intended doctrine

Haddock then turns the same proof-burden discipline against Pompa.

Even if passages in *New Science* can be rendered as an analytical philosophy of history, Haddock says additional supporting evidence is needed to establish that this was actually how Vico intended his words to be understood.

He criticizes a position in which the implications of doctrines are analyzed even if Vico's own awareness/intention is treated as less important.

Thus:

`ANALYTICALLY CONSTRUABLE DOCTRINE`
!= `HISTORICALLY INTENDED DOCTRINE`.

This is not a claim that only conscious intention ever matters. It is a claim that an analyst cannot convert philosophical reconstruction into historical attribution without changing the question and proof burden.

This strongly converges with the Round-9 analytic-unit rule:

`UNIT ADEQUACY = QUESTION + CLAIM + PROOF BURDEN`.

A philosophical reconstruction and a historical reconstruction may legitimately use different units/relations, but they cannot be silently substituted.

## VIII. Carrier role matters: this is a review article performing method critique

`Vico and Anachronism` is visibly printed under `REVIEW ARTICLES` and reviews three contemporary Vico books.

Yet it carries a substantial explicit methodology of intellectual history.

Therefore:

`REVIEW CARRIER`
!= `SECONDARY / NON-METHOD FUNCTION`.

The article performs at least four tasks simultaneously:

- reviews current Vico scholarship;
- defines anti-anachronist proof burdens;
- positions Skinner, Dunn, Oakeshott/Collingwood-related historians within contemporary British method debate;
- reconstructs Vico's relation to context, sources, intention and originality.

This is another direct demonstration that method jurisdiction can live in different carrier roles.

## IX. 1979 JHI explicitly executes the method/case relation

B. A. Haddock, `Vico's “Discovery of the True Homer”: A Case-Study in Historical Reconstruction`, JHI 40.4 (1979), 583–602, direct Cardiff ORCA PDF.

The paper explicitly says its concern is to examine the True Homer as an illustration of *New Science* principles applied to a specific historical problem, and then to interpret the principles in light of their practical application.

Direct p.586 control:

- abstract `Elements` constitute a methodological canon for evaluating competing accounts;
- because their abstract statement is abbreviated, their character is best understood through implications for a specific interpretive problem;
- the apparent separation of abstract Elements from substantive historical theses is deceptive;
- the principles were themselves **distilled from efforts to sketch coherent pictures of problematic historical episodes**;
- artifact/document interpretation depends on theoretical considerations about the character of the people who produced them.

Therefore:

`METHOD -> CASE`
and
`CASE -> METHOD`
operate reciprocally.

This is stronger than `general theory applied to example`.

## X. 1976 constraint becomes 1979 case execution

The 1979 Homer analysis operationalizes the 1976 distinction between historically variable rationality and presentist judgment.

Homeric gods/heroes that appear morally/philosophically incoherent under later assumptions become evidence about the `age of heroes` once interpretation is regulated by the historically appropriate mode of thought.

The JHI case says the apparent contradictions cannot simply be repaired by attributing later philosophical wisdom to Homer.

Thus the 1976 proposition:

`ADVANCED RATIONALITY CANNOT BE PROJECTED BACK WITHOUT ANACHRONISM`

becomes a case operation:

`HOMERIC ARTIFACT + HEROIC-AGE MODE OF THOUGHT`
-> `RESTRICTED RANGE OF ADMISSIBLE MEANINGS`.

This is a traceable method/case relation, not proof that every 1979 sentence derives from the 1976 review.

## XI. Philosophy / philology mutual constraint and interpretation of classification

The 1979 article's p.590 and p.597 sections directly develop the philosophy/philology rapprochement.

Philosophy alone cannot impose abstract universal truths without historical situation; philology does not merely enumerate res gestae.

By p.597 Haddock explicitly argues that selection, classification and presentation of artifacts already invoke interpretive criteria: dictionaries, bibliographies, exhibitions and the designation of an object as a particular kind of relic all carry interpretive implications.

This gives a later execution of the 1976 concern with analytical reconstructions that disguise their own interpretive operations.

Rule:

`DESCRIPTION / CLASSIFICATION`
!= `THEORY-FREE ACCESS TO OBJECT`.
