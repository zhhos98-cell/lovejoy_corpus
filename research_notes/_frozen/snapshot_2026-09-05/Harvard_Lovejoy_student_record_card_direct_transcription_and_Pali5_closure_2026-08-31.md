## Critical crosswalk: `Sanskrit 5` = Lanman's advanced Pāli course

This is the major evidentiary closure.

The individual card calls the entry **`Sanskrit 5`**. The direct-primary Harvard 1896–97 course announcement, already visually controlled in `Harvard_1896_97_coursewording_locator_2026-08-27.md`, places course **5** under `INDO-IRANIAN LANGUAGES` and gives it as:

`Pāli (advanced course). — The Sacred Books of Buddhism. — Dhamma Pada. — Sutta Nipāta. — Majjhima Nikāya. Tu., Th., at 2.30, and a third hour at the pleasure of the instructor. Professor Lanman.`

Therefore the two primary records crosswalk directly:

`LOVEJOY INDIVIDUAL CARD: Sanskrit 5, A`

`+`

`1896–97 HARVARD COURSE ANNOUNCEMENT: Indo-Iranian 5 = Lanman, advanced Pāli, Sacred Books of Buddhism`

`=`

> **Arthur O. Lovejoy was formally enrolled in Charles Rockwell Lanman's advanced Pāli course in 1896–97 and received grade A.**

This supersedes every earlier repository sentence that treated Lovejoy's exact enrollment in Lanman's beginner/advanced Pāli course as unresolved.

The card's broad handwritten departmental label `Sanskrit` does not change the course identity: the actor-level number is `5`, while the contemporaneous institutional announcement identifies Indo-Iranian course 5 as advanced Pāli rather than Sanskrit language instruction.

## Critical crosswalk: `Semitic 13` = Toy, History of Hebrew Religion

The same direct-primary 1896–97 announcement gives Semitic course 13 as:

`History of the Hebrew Religion, with comparison of other Semitic religions. Fri., 2.30–4.30. Professor Toy.`

The card therefore establishes:

> **Lovejoy was formally enrolled in Crawford H. Toy's comparative History of Hebrew Religion course in 1896–97 and received grade A.**

This also upgrades the Toy relation from Wilson-mediated actor attribution to direct actor-level enrollment evidence.

Do not yet assign an instructor/topic to Philosophy 20c beyond what the exact 1896–97 catalogue or other direct course records establish.

## 1897 AOS Milinda-pañha communication — same-year relation

American Oriental Society, Final Circular, 10 Apr 1897, item 24, announces:

`Mr. ARTHUR O. LOVEJOY, Harvard University; Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha.`

The student card now changes the evidentiary meaning of this discovery. The circular is dated during the same academic year in which Lovejoy was formally enrolled in Lanman's **advanced Pāli / Sacred Books of Buddhism** course and receiving an A.

The secure sequence is now:

`1895–96: formal Sanskrit sequence`

`-> 1896–97: Lanman advanced Pāli 5 (A) + Toy Semitic 13 (A)`

`-> by 10 Apr 1897: AOS-announced "Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha"`

`-> 1898: published JAOS paper on upādāna and upādisesa`.

This is **direct actor-level chronology**, not merely reconstruction from an orientalist milieu.

### Causal firewall

The same-year overlap does **not** by itself prove:

- Lanman assigned the Milinda paper;
- Lanman caused or supervised the AOS communication;
- the paper was actually delivered at Baltimore;
- the 1897 Milinda communication was a draft/version of the 1898 `upādāna/upādisesa` article.

Those remain separate propositions requiring separate evidence.

## Historiographical consequence

The previous language `Lovejoy received formal Sanskrit and Buddhist-text training under Lanman; exact Pāli enrollment unresolved` is now too weak.

Publication-safe replacement:

> **Harvard's individual graduate record shows that in 1896–97 Lovejoy took course 5 under the card's `Sanskrit` heading and received an A. The contemporaneous course announcement identifies Indo-Iranian 5 as Charles Rockwell Lanman's advanced Pāli course, reading the Dhamma Pada, Sutta Nipāta, and Majjhima Nikāya as the Sacred Books of Buddhism. In April 1897 the American Oriental Society's final circular announced a Harvard paper by Lovejoy on the argument of the Milinda-pañha.**

The strong historical claim is therefore no longer `Orientalist environment -> possible Buddhist interest`. It is:

> **formal Sanskrit sequence -> formal advanced Pāli enrollment -> specialist Pāli Buddhist research communication -> published Buddhist philology.**

This remains a chronology of documented practice, not a causal origin story for Lovejoy's mature history-of-ideas method.
