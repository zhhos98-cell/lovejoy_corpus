# Before the 1969 manifesto: Skinner, Dunn, Leslie, and a pre-canonical argument ecology, 1966–1970

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT DUNN 1968 CAMBRIDGE TEXT + DIRECT LESLIE 1970 PUBLISHER CONTROL / DRAFT-STAGE INTERACTION SECURE / REVISION CAUSATION NOT PROVEN**

## Core result

The canonical chronology of contextualism should no longer begin with the publication of Quentin Skinner's `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas` in 1969.

Direct evidence now shows a denser pre-canonical sequence:

`SKINNER 1966 — The Limits of Historical Explanations`
+
`DUNN / SKINNER / LASLETT — several years of methodological discussion`
->
`DUNN APRIL 1968 — The Identity of the History of Ideas`
+
`SKINNER 4 APRIL 1968 — Political Theory: The Unimportance of the Great Texts, PSA conference, Sussex`
->
`SKINNER 1969 — revised published Meaning and Understanding`
->
`LESLIE 1970 — In Defence of Anachronism, explicitly arising from reflection on the 1968 conference paper; Skinner commented on an earlier version of Leslie's essay`.

The strongest historical correction is:

> **The methodological controversy was already an interactive scholarly ecology before the text later canonized as Skinner 1969 had stabilized in print. Publication date is not the origin date of the debate.**

Governance:

`PUBLICATION DATE != ORIGIN OF METHOD CONTROVERSY`.

`CANONICAL TEXT != FIRST EFFECTIVE PUBLIC FORM`.

`DRAFT INTERACTION != PROVEN REVISION CAUSATION`.

---

## I. Dunn 1968 is not merely a later parallel to Skinner

John Dunn, `The Identity of the History of Ideas`, *Philosophy* 43.164 (April 1968), 85–104, directly criticizes histories in which reified abstractions perform historical action: Science, Theology, Empiricism, Rationalism, monism, dualism, evolution, Great Chain of Being, etc.

His footnote 1 explicitly says `history of ideas` is being used more broadly than the individual meaning given to it by Lovejoy and his pupils.

Thus Lovejoy is already part of the explicit methodological target field in April 1968.

But Dunn's relation to Skinner is stronger than shared target selection.

Near the end of the article, footnote 33 states that the paper arose from **several years of discussion** with Peter Laslett and especially Quentin Skinner. Dunn then points readers to Skinner's 1966 `The Limits of Historical Explanations` as a partly analogous, partly contrasting view.

Therefore:

`DUNN 1968`
!= independent parallel discovery.

It belongs to an actor-acknowledged conversational environment extending back before 1968.

At the same time:

`SEVERAL YEARS OF DISCUSSION`
does not establish
`DUNN SAW SKINNER'S APRIL 1968 CONFERENCE DRAFT`.

The 1966 publication plus long discussion is sufficient to explain Dunn's references to a `Skinnerian` explanatory story.

Do not infer unpublished-text circulation without further correspondence/manuscript evidence.

---

## II. Dunn already has Austin before the published Skinner manifesto

Dunn's article directly cites J. L. Austin, *How to Do Things with Words* (1962), twice in the surviving Cambridge full-text apparatus.

One citation concerns promises/reports of intentions and the problems raised by sincerity; another directs the reader to Austin `passim` in the discussion of what it is to say/do something and what a speaker means.

This matters because the currently recovered 1968 Skinner conference draft did **not** cite Austin, whereas the 1969 published article did.

Later Skinner explicitly credited Dunn with the insight that Austin's theory of speech acts might be relevant to interpreting texts in the history of political thought.

The safe relation is therefore:

`DUNN 1968 USES AUSTIN`
-> `SKINNER LATER ACKNOWLEDGES DUNN'S AUSTIN INSIGHT`.

This is stronger than retrospective stylistic resemblance.

But still preserve:

`DUNN'S AUSTIN USE`
!= proof of the exact textual route by which Austin entered every sentence/revision of Skinner 1969.

A clause-level 1968→1969 version comparison is needed before assigning individual revisions to Dunn.

---

## III. The Cambridge method formation is relational before it is canonical

Dunn's acknowledgments and references give a small but highly consequential network:

- Peter Laslett;
- Quentin Skinner;
- John Dunn;
- philosophical resources including Austin, Danto, Taylor, Winch, Collingwood-related literature;
- active historical casework on Hobbes and Locke.

This suggests a better early unit than `Skinner the author of a manifesto`:

`U7 DISCUSSION / WORKSHOP ARENA`
+
`U8 TRACEABLE INTELLECTUAL RELATIONS`
+
`U3 INDIVIDUAL ARTICLES / AUTHORS`.

The method was being produced through interaction among these units.

In UNIT × RELATION terms:

`CANONICAL AUTHOR U3`
should not erase
`PRE-CANONICAL DISCUSSION ARENA U7`.

This is exactly analogous to the earlier Lovejoy correction:

`LOVEJOYIAN METHOD`
cannot be reconstructed only from the later manifesto while ignoring EPHE/Marillier workshop practice and notebooks.

No genetic relation between the two episodes is implied. The comparison is methodological historiography.

## IV. Skinner's 1968 conference paper is a distinct versioned source

Adrian Blau's 2026 reconstruction uses a previously unexplored 1968 conference version of the paper:

`Political Theory: The Unimportance of the Great Texts`.

It was delivered at the Political Studies Association conference at the University of Sussex on 4 April 1968 and later became `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas` (1969).

Blau's official chapter description says there are interesting and revealing differences among:

- 1968 conference version;
- 1969 published version;
- heavily revised 2002 version.

Therefore `Skinner's methodology` must itself be source-criticized as a versioned object.

Unit control:

- Skinner as actor = `U3`;
- 1968 conference paper = `U2`;
- 1969 article = another `U2`;
- 2002 revision = another `U2`;
- later canonical `Skinnerian contextualism` = `U6`.

The reflexive rule is:

> **Do not contextualize historical actors while decontextualizing the canonical methodological text used to interpret them.**

## V. Leslie 1970 proves direct reception of the pre-canonical conference form

Margaret Leslie, `In Defence of Anachronism`, *Political Studies* 18.4 (December 1970), 433–447, contains unusually explicit provenance language.

The publisher-hosted text states that her essay arises from reflections on Skinner's paper `The Unimportance of the Great Texts`, given at the 1968 Political Studies Association conference, `since published in a revised form` as `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas`.

Leslie also thanks Skinner for comments on an earlier version of her own essay.

This securely establishes:

`SKINNER 1968 CONFERENCE INTERVENTION`
-> `LESLIE REFLECTION / CRITIQUE`
-> `LESLIE DRAFT`
-> `SKINNER COMMENTS`
-> `LESLIE 1970 PUBLICATION`.

The method controversy therefore had a draft/comment/revision social life beyond published-journal exchange.

### Evidence ceiling

This does **not** establish:

`LESLIE CRITIQUE -> SKINNER 1969 REVISION`.

The evidence does not currently date Skinner's comments on Leslie's draft relative to the finalization of his 1969 article, nor show that her objections altered that article.

Keep causation HOLD.

---

## VI. The 1969 date is a publication stabilization, not a zero point

The canonical story often organizes chronology by famous publication dates:

`POCOCK 1962`
-> `DUNN 1968`
-> `SKINNER 1969`.

This is bibliographically useful but historically insufficient.

The actor-level evidence now requires:

`PUBLISHED PRECURSORS`
+
`SEVERAL YEARS OF DISCUSSION`
+
`CONFERENCE VERSION`
+
`IMMEDIATE AUDIENCE RESPONSE`
+
`DRAFT CRITIQUE / AUTHOR COMMENTS`
+
`REVISED PUBLICATION`.

A methodological innovation is therefore not necessarily produced at the moment its canonical article appears.

New useful concept:

### `PRE-CANONICAL ARGUMENT ECOLOGY`

The network of discussions, conference performances, drafts, comments, references and early criticisms through which a methodological position is being formed before later anthologies and field histories stabilize a canonical text and author.

## VII. Dunn and Skinner complicate the inventor/follower model

Dunn's April 1968 paper already contains several elements later strongly associated with contextualism:

- hostility to histories populated by reified abstractions;
- insistence on the historicity of intellectual performances;
- distinctions among what a thinker says, means, and historically does;
- direct use of Austin;
- emphasis on contexts that give definition to propositions;
- causal/historical reconstruction of past thought.

Yet Dunn explicitly acknowledges sustained discussion with Skinner, and later Skinner acknowledges Dunn's Austin contribution.

Thus the relation is reciprocal enough that the following model is preferable:

`SHARED PROBLEM ENVIRONMENT`
-> `PARTIALLY DIFFERENT FORMULATIONS`
-> `MUTUAL INTELLECTUAL INPUT`
-> later `AUTHOR-CENTERED CANONIZATION`.

Do not replace Skinner-centrism with Dunn-centrism.

The object is the relational formation itself.

---

## VIII. Relation to Wiener/JHI line

The recovered chronology now contains two overlapping but distinct pre-1969 strands:

### JHI / Wiener

1961 Wiener explicitly problematizes influence:

- direct contact;
- mediated chains;
- pupils vs disciples;
- climate of opinion.

Skinner 1969 explicitly credits Wiener for the alternative-source/source-discrimination condition.

### Cambridge relational ecology

1966 Skinner -> several years Dunn/Skinner/Laslett discussion -> Dunn and Skinner 1968 interventions -> Austin uptake -> 1969 publication.

These should not be collapsed into one direct lineage.

But together they make the rupture chronology more complex:

> **Some proof problems later associated with Skinner were already active inside JHI, while some of Skinner's own central conceptual resources were being generated through reciprocal Cambridge discussion before the canonical 1969 publication.**

Thus `Skinner 1969` is simultaneously:

- a genuine powerful intervention;
- a published stabilization of an already interactive problem field;
- a later canonized boundary marker.

## Evidence firewall

1. Dunn's `Skinnerian story` plus discussion acknowledgment does not prove he read Skinner's 1968 conference draft.
2. Dunn's Austin citations plus Skinner's later acknowledgment prove intellectual relevance, not the exact revision route into every clause of Skinner 1969.
3. Leslie's essay proves reception of the 1968 conference intervention and reciprocal comment, not causal influence on Skinner's 1969 revisions.
4. Published date is a source event, not automatically an innovation-origin date.
5. A later canonical author's name should not be used as the only unit for reconstructing collaborative method formation.
