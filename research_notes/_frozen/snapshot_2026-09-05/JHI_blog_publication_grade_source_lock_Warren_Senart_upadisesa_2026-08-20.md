## I. Warren chain — relation reassignment

### A. Warren primary

Henry Clarke Warren, `On the so-called Chain of Causation of the Buddhists`, Proceedings of the American Oriental Society, April 1893; printed in *Journal of the American Oriental Society* 16, pp. xxvii–xxx.

Independent bibliographic control: Lanman's bibliography of Warren gives JAOS XVI, pp. xxvii–xxx (April 1893). Lovejoy 1898 independently cites the same Warren passage as `JAOS. vol. xvi. p. xxvii.`

Warren's general methodological claim is explicit: `paccaya` need not express one homogeneous causal relation; the chain must be examined member by member, with Time only incidentally involved. He assigns different relations to different links: antecedent condition, particular instance/general category, cause/effect, material cause, interdependence, etc.

The local proposition that matters for Lovejoy is:

> `the relation, therefore, of Existence and Desire or Attachment is that of effect to cause, and that of Attachment to Desire is identity.`

For Blog quotation, use only the final clause if space is tight:

> `that of Attachment to Desire is identity.`

Cite Warren, JAOS 16, p. xxvii unless a facsimile page-turn proves that the sentence falls on xxviii. Lovejoy's own 1898 footnote cites p. xxvii, so p. xxvii is the safest current publication citation.

### B. Notebook 004 — direct visual control

MS38_004_001_061_004, PDF p.22, manuscript p.75.

The page is headed `Nidānas cont.` and contains a visually secure authorial intervention:

> `Warren “Chain of Causat.” regards relationship of attachment to desire as that of “identity.” But not so if we are to follow F. present text.`

The continuation then compares the four attachments with classes of desire and Buddhaghosa/Vibhaṅga material.

Safe normalization for prose quotation:

> `Warren ... regards [the] relationship of attachment to desire as that of “identity.” But not so if we are to follow the present text.`

If preserving manuscript flavor, mark supplied articles/expanded abbreviation editorially rather than silently.

**Status:** direct visual confirmation, high confidence.

### C. Lovejoy 1898 — published reuse

Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa`, *JAOS* 19 (1898), 126–136.

Printed p.126:

> `This signification is commonly regarded as indicating that the ninth link of the paticca-samuppada is virtually a repetition of the eighth, tanha; so Mr. Warren, “the relation of desire to attachment is that of identity.”`

Footnote 2: `JAOS. vol. xvi. p. xxvii.`

Lovejoy then rejects identity by distinguishing `upādāna` from `taṇhā` through textual classification and causal/psychological function.

### D. What this chain proves

Secure formulation:

> **Lovejoy appropriates Warren's relation-by-relation problem but reassigns Warren's own local identity relation after testing it against the textual classifications.**

Do **not** write `Warren taught Lovejoy relation-type discrimination`, unless explicitly qualified. The field-level operation is Warren's; the documentary Lovejoy-specific act is the visible reassignment.

## II. Senart chain — composite provenance versus configuration

### A. Senart primary

Émile Senart, `À propos de la théorie bouddhique des douze nidânas`, in *Mélanges Charles de Harlez* (Leiden: E. J. Brill, 1896), pp.281–297.

Master-corpus/source-page mapping establishes that scan page 314 = printed p.284 and scan page 315 = printed p.285.

Printed p.284 begins Senart's `upādāna` claim:

> `Upādāna, plusieurs textes le démontrent, n'est qu'une réduction pour upādāna skandhas ou, plus complètement, pañca upādānaskandhas...`

Senart then argues that the skandhas therefore occur twice in the formula.

Printed p.285 gives the crucial inference:

> `La série des skandhas figure donc en réalité deux fois dans la formule.`

and:

> `C'est se leurrer que de chercher une déduction objective dans cette série composite: des catégories scolastiques toutes faites y ont été manifestement fondues dans un ordre plus ou moins accidentel...`

This exact `ordre plus ou moins accidentel` phrase matters because 004 answers it in virtually literal English.

### B. Notebook 004 — direct visual control

MS38_004_001_061_004, PDF p.49, manuscript p.137.

The left page digests Senart's proposition, including the direct lexical echo:

> `all these scholastic categories have manifestly been placed together in an order more or less accidental.`

Lovejoy then marks his own response with an explicit boundary:

> `On Senart's account of the matter there is simply this to be said:`

He concedes several premises:

- the terms of the formula are not original to Buddhism;
- they are brought together from pre-existing sources;
- the formula contains objective and subjective elements;
- the logical interrelations are strange to the Western mind;
- the sequence is not strictly temporal;
- there are repetitions.

He then rejects Senart's stronger conclusion. Direct visual reading supports:

> `But it is entirely false to say that F. formula is illogical & unintelligible & that F. various terms have been merely put together in an order more or less accidental.`

The following line, also visually legible, reads in substance:

> `There is a discoverable logical system wh. governs the arrangement, and the repetitions are intentional...`

For publication, the first sentence can be quoted after a final diplomatic transcription pass. The second should currently be paraphrased unless the exact abbreviation/punctuation is rechecked at drafting time.

**Status:** direct visual confirmation; argumentative content high confidence; exact punctuation/abbreviation should remain diplomatic if quoted.

### C. Lovejoy 1898 — direct polemical closure

Printed p.127:

Lovejoy quotes Senart's `upādāna` reduction and says this interpretation makes the formula extremely repetitious; from that supposed repetitiousness Senart derives the formula's `derivative, composite, and practically meaningless character`.

Lovejoy's direct judgment:

> `But both premises and conclusion are, I believe, entirely erroneous.`

He then says of Senart's four cited passages that the three he could consult:

> `prove nothing remotely like the interpretation which they are intended to substantiate.`

This is the strongest publication-grade sentence for **proof-burden control**: Lovejoy is not merely giving a rival doctrine; he is testing what the cited witnesses actually prove.

Printed pp.131–132:

Lovejoy widens the point from the compound `upādānakkhandha` to the whole formula and says that treating dependent origination as `a virtually meaningless affair of shreds and patches` misses important Buddhist doctrine.

Printed p.132 gives the strongest concise configuration statement:

> `The terms used there, perhaps even the ideas, are doubtless largely borrowed ones; but the arrangement and application of them is certainly original and characteristic.`

### D. What this chain proves

This is the strongest full three-node closure:

`Senart primary inference` → `004 concession + rejection` → `1898 published polemic/configuration claim`.

Safe formulation:

> **Lovejoy concedes composite provenance while refusing to let provenance alone determine intelligibility, semantic role, or configuration.**

Do not write `Senart was simply wrong` or `Lovejoy restored coherence against naive diffusionism`. Senart's own stratification argument is sophisticated. The precise disagreement concerns what compositeness and repetition are entitled to establish.

## III. upādisesa chain — direct manuscript carryover and confidence calibration

### A. Notebook 004 — direct visual control

MS38_004_001_061_004, PDF p.66, manuscript p.173.

The visually secure core reads:

> `No one familiar with Buddhist modes of thought could suppose that anup. here meant the extinction of the skandhas, i.e. physical death. To the being who has ever been freed from desire, it is indifferent whether he dies or lives; to speak of death as the summum bonum will be the most un-Buddhistic of sentiments.`

The next sentence says that `anup.` means the moral condition of being freed from attachment.

The same manuscript page then states that in the three Sutta-Nipāta passages where `upādisesa` occurs, it does not refer to persistence of the five skandhas but to an ethical condition; positive use applies to the Anāgāmin, negative use to the Arahat before or after physical death.

**Status:** direct visual confirmation, high confidence.

### B. Lovejoy 1898 p.135 — near-verbatim carryover

Printed p.135:

> `No one familiar with Buddhist modes of thought could suppose that anupadisesa here means merely the extinction of the (present) skandhas, i. e. physical death.`

Then:

> `To the man who has once become freed from desire it is indifferent whether he lives or dies; to regard death, in itself, as the summum bonum, would be the least Buddhistic of sentiments.`

and:

> `Plainly, the word anupadisesa in this passage means that moral condition of freedom from attachment which is the goal of the true Buddhist's aspiration.`

This is the strongest direct textual continuity between 004 and the 1898 article.

### C. Lovejoy 1898 pp.134–136 — explicit calibration added in print

Printed p.134:

> `Both derivations seem to be etymologically possible; the meaning of upadi must therefore be settled rather by an examination of its use than by etymological arguments.`

Immediately after:

> `I can only contribute here a few points, relevant but not necessarily conclusive...`

Printed p.136:

> `although the oldest and probably the most numerous texts thus point to the interpretation of upadisesa suggested by Oldenberg, other passages might be cited in favor of the more usual view; so that the matter cannot be regarded as finally settled.`

This establishes an evidentiary ladder that is more explicit in publication than in the working notebook:

`etymological possibility` ≠ `semantic decision`;
`relevant evidence` ≠ `conclusive evidence`;
`preponderant textual support` ≠ `finally settled`.

### D. What this chain proves

Safe formulation:

> **The notebook-to-print relation is direct, but publication adds an explicit vocabulary of confidence and unresolved counterevidence.**

This is the best coda after Warren and Senart because it shows not a new doctrinal problem but the public calibration of how strongly a local result may be stated.
