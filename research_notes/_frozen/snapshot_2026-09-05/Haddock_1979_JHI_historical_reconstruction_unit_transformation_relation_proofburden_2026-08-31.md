# Haddock 1979 in JHI: historical reconstruction, unit transformation, and relation-specific proof burden

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT FULL TEXT / PAGE-IMAGE CONTROL / HIGH-VALUE UNIT×RELATION CASE**

## Core result

B. A. Haddock's 1979 JHI article, `Vico's "Discovery of the True Homer": A Case-Study in Historical Reconstruction`, is much more than a continuation of his Vico work. Read after Haddock 1974 (`The History of Ideas and the Study of Politics`) and his 1976 anti-anachronism/reconstruction essays, it supplies a concrete execution case for several of the repo's governing distinctions.

The article directly exhibits:

1. **methodological theory and historical reconstruction as reciprocal rather than one-way**;
2. **historical-unit transformation** from an individual Homer toward collective poetic nations / layered traditions / later compilers;
3. **relation-specific proof burden** in its explicit caution against inferring influence from textual resemblance without concrete historical evidence;
4. **artifact interpretation as inseparable from assumptions about producers, practices, categories and relations**;
5. **anti-anachronism as reconstruction of a network of historically specific assumptions, not merely recovery of an isolated proposition**;
6. **philosophy + philology as reciprocally corrective modes of historical reasoning**.

Compact result:

`HADDOCK 1974 METHOD DISCUSSION`
-> `HADDOCK 1976 ANTI-ANACHRONISM / RECONSTRUCTION`
-> `HADDOCK 1979 JHI CASE-STUDY EXECUTION`.

This is a direct same-author cross-venue pathway. It does **not** establish that the venues caused the development of Haddock's method, or that Haddock constitutes an independent rival school to Skinner.

## II. Method is tested in a case, but the method also emerges from cases

Haddock frames Vico's `true Homer` as a specific historical problem through which the principles of the *Scienza nuova* can be examined in practical application (printed p.585).

The important move comes immediately afterward. The apparent architecture of the *Scienza nuova seconda* separates abstract `Elements` from substantive historical theses, but Haddock argues this is deceptive. The principles that appear to inform the reconstruction essays were themselves **distilled from Vico's attempts to make coherent sense of problematic historical episodes** (printed p.586).

Therefore the relation is not simply:

`GENERAL THEORY`
-> `CASE APPLICATION`.

It is closer to:

`PROBLEMATIC HISTORICAL RECONSTRUCTIONS`
<-> `EXPLICIT METHODOLOGICAL CANON`.

This is useful for the current project because it prevents us from treating method texts as timeless algorithmic programs whose later empirical cases are mere implementations.

Actor-level method can be an abstraction from repeated work on recalcitrant objects.

Compact control:

`EXPLICIT METHOD`
!= `PRE-EXISTING METHOD APPLIED TO PASSIVE EVIDENCE`.

## III. Artifact interpretation requires a theory of the producer

On printed p.586 Haddock says Vico realized that interpreting documents and artifacts depended on theoretical considerations about the character of the people who produced them.

The target error is projecting eighteenth-century civility back into the origins of society and civilization.

This is not merely a warning to add more background context. It changes what relation licenses an interpretation:

`ARTIFACT`
-> `MEANING`

must pass through historically controlled propositions about:

`PRODUCER / PRACTICE / MODE OF THOUGHT / SOCIAL FORM`.

This is directly compatible with `UNIT × RELATION`: an artifact does not carry a historically adequate interpretation independently of the unit to which it is assigned and the historically warranted relations among artifact, producer and practice.

## IV. The Homer case performs a historical-unit transformation

The most important UNIT-level passage appears on printed p.589.

In Haddock's reconstruction of Vico's earlier Homeric scheme:

- poetry in the first two ages is **not the product of individuals** but of whole poetic nations;
- only in a later age do particular poets appear as collectors/assemblers of fables or corrupted histories derived from these nations;
- contradictions in the received Homeric corpus can therefore be related to distinct regions or periods of heroic Greece rather than forced into the psychology or consistency of a single timeless author.

This changes the historical object itself.

A simplified coding sequence is:

`U3 INDIVIDUAL AUTHOR (TRADITIONAL HOMER)`
-> destabilized
-> `U6 TRADITION / COLLECTIVE POETIC PEOPLE`
+
`U2 TEXTUAL / LAYERED SOURCE MATERIAL`
+
`U3 LATER COMPILER / PARTICULAR POET`.

The exact code assignment should remain proposition-specific, but the transformation is direct.

Thus contextual reconstruction here is not merely:

`SAME HOMER + MORE CONTEXT`.

It is:

`RECONSTRUCTION CHANGES WHAT "HOMER" IS AS A HISTORICAL UNIT`.

This is one of the clearest historical examples so far for the repo rule:

> **method pressure can change the unit before it changes the relation.**

It also provides a useful pre-Skinner historical object for later unit-change debates without implying a genealogy from Vico/Haddock to contemporary contextualism.

## V. Primitive culture is not treated as an imperfect present

The article opens by locating the Homer/Ossian problem inside eighteenth-century debates over `primitive poetry`, literary criticism, and the proper attitude toward artifacts of primitive culture (printed pp.583–84).

Haddock's reconstruction of Vico then makes the anti-presentist point concrete. Historians err when they interpret remote peoples using the modes of thought, rules and conventions that make utterances intelligible in the historian's own world (p.589).

This is stronger than a generic injunction to `understand context`.

The historical claim is that the categories, rationality, practices and relations organizing a remote culture can differ enough that present analytical assumptions misidentify the object.

Therefore:

`PRIMITIVE / REMOTE CULTURE`
!= `DEFICIENT VERSION OF PRESENT RATIONALITY`.

But this article should not be used to claim that Haddock rejects every developmental or stadial structure. His Vico still works with ages of gods, heroes and men. The useful distinction is between **historical differentiation of modes of thought** and **presentist flattening**, not a claim that all temporal stratification disappears.

## VI. Direct relation-specific proof burden: similarity is not influence

A footnote on printed p.584 is unusually valuable for the repo's evidence governance.

Haddock discusses Gustavo Costa's proposed Vico -> Thomas Blackwell influence based on textual comparison. He notes why such a thesis would be attractive, but then explicitly warns against substituting speculative conjecture for concrete historical evidence; he points to Blackwell's demonstrable Shaftesbury/Gravina commitments and differences from Vico.

This is actor-level methodological execution, not our retrospective rule imposed on him.

The relation distinction is:

`TEXTUAL / THEMATIC RESEMBLANCE`
!= `TRANSMISSION / INFLUENCE`.

And more precisely:

`POSSIBLE EXPLANATORY FIT`
!= `HISTORICALLY LICENSED GENEALOGICAL EDGE`.

This directly supports the repo's existing firewalls around Skinner, Mandelbaum, Wiener, Pocock, and other method-network reconstruction.

The Haddock case is especially valuable because the same article that reconstructs contextual meaning also polices causal/transmission overclaim.

## VII. Philosophy and philology form a reciprocal evidentiary relation

Printed pp.590–92 reconstruct Vico's proposed rapprochement between philosophy and philology.

Haddock characterizes the problem as follows:

- philosophy can become detached from the historical situations that give claims human form;
- philology can identify/classify artifacts without making explicit the theoretical assumptions embedded in those acts;
- neither is sufficient in isolation;
- historical reconstruction depends on a reciprocal relation between theoretical reasoning and evidence about what human choice has made.

This yields another useful non-binary architecture:

`THEORETICAL CRITICISM`
<-> `PHILOLOGICAL / ARTIFACTUAL CONTROL`.

The relation is not `theory versus evidence` but mutual constraint.

Printed pp.597–98 sharpen the point further: even selecting, arranging, identifying or classifying artifacts already invokes interpretive criteria. A dictionary, bibliography, exhibition or antiquarian reconstruction is not epistemically neutral merely because it avoids an explicit narrative thesis.

Compact rule:

`DESCRIPTION / CLASSIFICATION`
!= `THEORY-FREE OPERATION`.

This parallels the 1975 *Political Theory* editorial finding that categories are operational and overlapping rather than natural ontologies.

## VIII. Historical meaning is networked, not atomized

Printed p.598 gives one of the strongest passages for comparison with later contextualism.

Haddock argues that changing cultures involve changing modes of thought; utterances cannot be made intelligible in isolation but must be reconstructed within the network of assumptions from which they issued. Vico's ages are described as complexes of language, customs, jurisprudence, government, authority and rationality whose practices are conceptually related.

This makes the relevant explanatory unit potentially larger than an author-intention dyad.

The reconstruction can require:

`UTTERANCE`
+
`LANGUAGE`
+
`CUSTOM`
+
`JURISPRUDENCE`
+
`INSTITUTIONAL / POLITICAL FORM`
+
`NOTION OF RATIONALITY`
+
`RELATIONS AMONG THESE`.

Therefore Haddock's Vichian contextualism is particularly useful for the repo's distinction:

`EXPANDING CONTEXT AROUND SAME UNIT`
!= `RECONSTRUCTING A DIFFERENT / COMPOSITE UNIT`.

Do not infer that all these components always constitute one fixed unit. The point is that the article itself treats historical intelligibility as relationally distributed across a structured set of practices and assumptions.

## IX. The conclusion rejects natural-science templates as the wrong genealogy for Vico's method

Printed pp.601–02 are methodologically decisive.

Haddock says Vico's historical theory is misconstrued when read through inductive, deductive or hypothetico-deductive models imported from debates about natural and social science.

He instead emphasizes Vico's reconstruction of the original character that ideas, practices and institutions had for agents, using critical criteria internal or presupposed within the historical worlds under reconstruction.

He then situates Vico's intellectual background in:

- Renaissance philological traditions;
- sixteenth-century jurisprudence;
- the *querelle des anciens et modernes*.

This is itself an anti-anachronist history of historical method: Haddock resists explaining an eighteenth-century historical project through later methodological categories whose intellectual genealogy does not fit the actor's problem-space.

Compact control:

`LATER ANALYTIC MODEL FIT`
!= `HISTORICAL GENEALOGY OF METHOD`.

## X. Cross-venue trajectory, 1974–1979

The directly recoverable sequence is now:

### 1974 — *Political Theory*
`The History of Ideas and the Study of Politics`

- jurisdictional/methodological crisis in political studies;
- cross-venue aggregation of Mandelbaum, Passmore, Dunn, Pocock, Skinner, Parekh/Berki, Collingwood, Wittgenstein, Winch, Taylor;
- `history of ideas` remains a live method label immediately after the journal's Skinner symposium.

### 1976 — *Political Studies* + *Social Research*
`Vico and Anachronism` and `Vico and the Problem of Historical Reconstruction`

- anti-presentist reconstruction;
- historically changing rationality / modes of thought;
- contextual relations and intentions;
- historical reconstruction explicitly developed as a problem.

### 1979 — JHI
`Vico's "Discovery of the True Homer": A Case-Study in Historical Reconstruction`

- method tested through a concrete case;
- abstract principles shown to emerge reciprocally from reconstruction practice;
- unit transformation from individual Homer toward poetic nations / layered tradition / compiler;
- direct evidence firewall for influence claims;
- philosophy/philology reciprocal control;
- networked historical meaning.

This route strongly supports:

`FIELD / VENUE DIFFERENTIATION`
+
`METHOD RECOMBINATION`
+
`CROSS-VENUE PRACTICE`

rather than a simple story of one method replacing another.

## XI. Relation to Skinner and canonization

The 1979 Haddock article should **not** be presented as proof that Haddock invented a contextualism independently of Skinner.

The 1974 article directly engaged Skinner/Pocock/Dunn and their critics. The available evidence does not establish what was read when, how Haddock's Vico work changed through those contacts, or whether any specific methodological proposition derives from one interlocutor rather than another.

Safe claim:

> **By the 1970s, contextualist and anti-anachronist pressures could be articulated and practiced through a repertoire that combined Vico, philology, Collingwood, contemporary political-theory debates, and history-of-ideas language.**

Hold:

`HADDOCK = INDEPENDENT ALTERNATIVE TO SKINNER`.

Also hold:

`SKINNER CAUSED HADDOCK'S VICHian METHOD`.

The historical point is plural available resources and cross-venue recombination.

## XII. UNIT × RELATION matrix implications

This article deserves a high-value matrix row or proposition cluster because it contains several explicit transformations.

Suggested proposition-level coding:

### A. Homer authorship / production
- units: `U3 -> U6 + U2 + U3` depending proposition;
- relation: `IDENTITY / CLASSIFICATION / COMPOSITION`;
- evidence burden: contradictions, layered traditions, historically differentiated poetic production.

### B. Vico -> Blackwell
- units: authors/textual corpora;
- relation: `TRANSMISSION / INFLUENCE`;
- negative control: textual resemblance insufficient without concrete historical evidence.

### C. Artifact -> producer/practice
- units: artifact + agent/collective/practice;
- relation: `MEANING / REPRESENTATIVENESS / CONTEXTUAL DEPENDENCE`;
- burden: historically warranted producer-character / mode-of-thought reconstruction.

### D. Philosophy <-> philology
- units: methodological practices / evidentiary operations;
- relation: `MUTUAL CONSTRAINT / CORROBORATION`;
- burden: neither abstract reason nor artifact classification alone suffices.

### E. Method canon <-> case reconstruction
- units: abstract methodological propositions + historical case work;
- relation: `ABSTRACTION / APPLICATION / FEEDBACK`;
- burden: version- and practice-sensitive reconstruction rather than one-way method application.
