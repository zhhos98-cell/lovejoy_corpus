## 2. Lovejoy was not present at the Baltimore meeting

The Proceedings give an explicit list of the members who attended one or more sessions, total 40. Lovejoy's name is absent.

This is not merely an inference from the way his paper was handled. It is a direct attendance-list negative control.

Status:

> **Lovejoy personal attendance at the 22–24 Apr 1897 Baltimore AOS meeting: DIRECT PRIMARY NEGATIVE — he is not on the complete 40-person attendance list.**

This supersedes the earlier unresolved language about whether Lovejoy may have delivered the paper personally.

## 3. Lovejoy's paper No. 30 was read by title, not personally delivered

Near the end of the final Saturday session, the Proceedings state that, because adjournment was close, papers numbered 17, 18, 46, **30 (by Lovejoy)**, 47, 38, 48, 50, 49 and 27 were:

> `read by title, and with or without a brief statement of their contents`

The Proceedings add that these were `for the most part, papers of members not present`.

Combined with the complete attendance list, Lovejoy's case is clear:

> **Lovejoy did not personally present his 1897 AOS paper. Paper No. 30 was read by title at the meeting; the record does not permit us to determine whether his item, specifically, also received the optional brief statement of contents.**

Do not write `Lovejoy presented/delivered a paper at the 1897 AOS meeting`.

Publication-safe verbs:

- `the Society read Lovejoy's paper by title`;
- `Lovejoy's communication was read by title in his absence`;
- `the Proceedings record Lovejoy's No. 30 among papers read by title`.

## 4. Critical title shift: 10 Apr circular -> Baltimore proceedings

The AOS Final Circular dated **10 April 1897** announces Lovejoy as item 24:

`Mr. ARTHUR O. LOVEJOY, Harvard University; Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha.`

Direct circular scan:
`https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/ead/pdf/ofcpreshjb-0007-013.pdf`

The final Proceedings, however, list Lovejoy as paper No. 30 under a different topic:

`Mr. Arthur O. Lovejoy, Harvard University; On the meaning of the Buddhist technical terms ...`

The Internet Archive OCR loses the final term names after `terms`; therefore **do not silently reconstruct the exact missing words as a diplomatic transcription until the printed page image is visually checked**.

What is already direct and secure is the substantive shift:

`10 Apr 1897 — Milinda-pañha argument-summary title`

`-> 22–24 Apr 1897 Proceedings — meaning of Buddhist technical terms`

The numbering shift from 24 to 30 is not evidentiary trouble: the final meeting list had been expanded/reordered relative to the earlier circular.

## 6. Lovejoy was elected an AOS corporate member at the same meeting

At the beginning of the final Saturday session, the Proceedings state that persons `recommended by the Directors` were `duly elected` and list them under `As Corporate Members`.

The list explicitly includes:

`A. O. Lovejoy, Oakland, Cal.`

The revised membership list later in the volume gives:

`ARTHUR ONCKEN LOVEJOY (Harvard University), Oakland, Cal. 1897.`

Thus:

> **Lovejoy's election as a corporate member of the American Oriental Society occurred at the Baltimore meeting in April 1897. DIRECT PRIMARY CLOSED.**

The Proceedings establish collective recommendation by the Directors, not an individual nominator. Do not write that Lanman, Toy, or another named scholar nominated Lovejoy unless a nomination/minute record is recovered.

## 7. Historical Study of Religions Section was formally instituted at the same meeting

The same Proceedings directly record the institutional creation of the AOS **Section for the Historical Study of Religions**.

A committee chaired by Richard Gottheil recommended that:

1. the AOS establish a special Section devoted to the historical study of religions;
2. the Section have a Secretary;
3. at least one session of each Society meeting be devoted to Section papers;
4. the Section cooperate with the American Committee for Lectures on the History of Religions.

The report was adopted. The Constitution and By-Laws were then amended unanimously to create the office and the Section formally.

The Proceedings later show:

- Morris Jastrow, Jr. elected Secretary of the Section;
- a committee of **Toy, Jastrow, and Gottheil** appointed to secure candidates for Section membership;
- Toy and Lanman elected AOS Vice-Presidents.

This puts Lovejoy's corporate election and Buddhist communication inside the same meeting that formally reorganized part of AOS activity around the historical study of religions.

### Membership firewall

Corporate AOS membership is **not** the same as membership in the new Section. The 1898 volume separately lists Section members, and Lovejoy is not in that section list.

Current status:

- Lovejoy AOS corporate member, elected Apr 1897: **DIRECT PRIMARY CLOSED**;
- Lovejoy member of Historical Study of Religions Section: **DIRECT LIST NEGATIVE in 1898 / do not claim membership**;
- Lovejoy institutional proximity to the Section's Harvard organizers: **DIRECT NETWORK FACT** through Toy/Lanman and the contemporaneous meeting, not proof of formal Section affiliation.

## 9. Historiographical consequence

The 1898 Buddhist paper no longer looks like a one-off graduate afterthought or an isolated first publication.

By direct primary evidence, before publication Lovejoy had already:

- completed prior Sanskrit work;
- enrolled in Lanman's advanced Pāli course and received A;
- announced a Milinda-pañha communication;
- shifted, at least at the level of meeting documentation, to a technical-terms paper;
- entered the AOS as a corporate member;
- had that technical-terms communication formally read by title at the annual meeting.

The safer historical object is therefore an **emerging research programme / publication path**, not an isolated article.

This still does not prove a stable long-term identity as a Buddhologist, nor a causal origin of his later history-of-ideas method.
