# JHI Blog — Lovejoy 1948: primary warrant for practice-before-programme reading

Date: 2026-08-20
Status: PRIMARY SELF-DESCRIPTION / HISTORIOGRAPHICAL CONTROL

A high-value primary self-description surfaced in the deep historiography sweep.

Daniel J. Wilson's 1982 annotated bibliography summarizes the author's preface to Lovejoy's *Essays in the History of Ideas* (1948) and quotes Lovejoy's statement that the `general conceptions` visible in the collected essays **`grew out of rather than preceded most of the inquiries into special topics.`**

Wilson then identifies the three retrospective generalizations Lovejoy says emerged from those studies:
1. recurrence/influence of the same presuppositions or operative ideas across diverse provinces and periods;
2. semantic transitions, confusions, shifts, and ambiguities in terms;
3. internal tensions/waverings within individual writers caused by conflicting ideas or dispositions.

## Why this matters

This gives direct Lovejoyian warrant for a **practice-before-programme** historiographical move. We do not have to argue that a fully formed `history of ideas` method existed in notebook 004 or that Buddhism causally generated unit-ideas. Instead:

> **Lovejoy himself retrospectively said that his general historiographical conceptions grew out of special inquiries rather than preceding them. That makes the character of his early special inquiries historically relevant to the genealogy of his later programme.**

Notebook 004 can therefore be framed as one recoverable early inquiry in which we can see the kinds of operations that later historiographical self-description may or may not formalize.

## Guardrail with Windelband

This does not supersede Lovejoy's retrospective remark to Lewis Feuer that he had `picked up` the idea of historically tracing unit-ideas from Windelband's *A History of Philosophy*. The two claims can coexist:
- Windelband = retrospective named source for the idea of tracing unit-ideas;
- special inquiries = Lovejoy's own retrospective account of how broader historiographical generalizations emerged in practice.

Thus do not write `Indology caused the unit-idea method`.

Better:

> **The question is not where Lovejoy got a single method, but what kinds of historical problems his early special inquiries required him to solve before the mature programme was retrospectively articulated.**

## Blog-level use

A compact sentence after introducing notebook 004 could be:

> `The relevance of such an early specialist notebook is not merely biographical. Lovejoy later remarked that his general conceptions about the history of ideas had “grew out of rather than preceded” most of his inquiries into special topics.`
