# Pocock 1973 -> 1984 -> 2009: same-text carrier reclassification and version proof burden

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **1973 ORIGINAL DIRECT / 1984 ANTHOLOGY INCLUSION DIRECT-BIBLIOGRAPHIC + CONTEMPORARY REVIEW FRAMING / 2009 SELF-COLLECTION + EDITORIAL-POLICY CONTROL / SAME-TEXT-FAMILY CARRIER MIGRATION LOCKED**

## Core result

J. G. A. Pocock's `Verbalizing a Political Act: Toward a Politics of Speech` can now be traced across at least three distinct carrier states:

`1973 POLITICAL THEORY RESEARCH ARTICLE`
-> `1984 SHAPIRO LANGUAGE AND POLITICS READER SELECTION`
-> `2009 POCOCK POLITICAL THOUGHT AND HISTORY SELF-COLLECTED METHOD ESSAY`.

The historical text family is continuous, but its carrier role and evidentiary status are not.

Core rules:

`SAME TEXT FAMILY != SAME CARRIER ROLE`;

`REPRINT != AUTOMATICALLY IDENTICAL VERSION`;

`LATER SELF-CANONIZATION != ORIGINAL PUBLICATION FUNCTION`.

This is a useful proof-burden case because the 2009 collection's editorial policy explicitly permits light body editing without marking every change. Therefore the 2009 text cannot substitute for the 1973 original when the claim concerns exact contemporary wording or the state of the argument in 1973.

## I. 1973: specialist-journal research article

Original:

J. G. A. Pocock, `Verbalizing a Political Act: Toward a Politics of Speech`, *Political Theory* 1.1 (February 1973), 27–45, DOI 10.1177/009059177300100103.

Direct SAGE/vLex controls establish:

- research-article carrier;
- direct author note identifying an earlier CSPT presentation version in April 1971;
- opening use of Confucian and Taoist examples inside a general politics-of-speech argument.

Companion dossier:

`research_notes/Pocock_1971_CSPT_1973_PT_version_edge_ancient_China_bodylevel_role_calibration_2026-08-31.md`.

At this state the text functions as an original contribution inside the inaugural specialist political-theory journal.

## II. 1984: reclassified inside Michael Shapiro's *Language and Politics*

Bibliographic controls identify Pocock's essay as a selection in:

Michael J. Shapiro, ed., *Language and Politics* (Oxford: Basil Blackwell / New York: New York University Press, 1984), pp. 25–43.

A contemporary Basil Blackwell advertisement describes the book as a `balanced introduction to the relationships between politics and language` and lists selections from C. Wright Mills, J. G. A. Pocock, Murray Edelman, Kenneth Burke, Tracy Strong, Michel Foucault, William Connolly, Frederic Jameson, Paul de Man and Michael Shapiro.

A contemporary review of Shapiro's anthology says the book reprints nine diverse essays plus an original contribution by Shapiro. The reviewer describes the opening path through Mills, Pocock, Edelman and Burke as relatively familiar Anglo-American terrain, with the pieces showing by different analytical routes how speech acts can carry political force, before the reader moves toward structuralist/post-structuralist material.

Evidence ceiling:

- Pocock's inclusion/reprint is secure;
- the anthology's broad introductory / reader function is direct through publisher advertising;
- the precise sequential pedagogical interpretation above comes from a contemporary review, not a directly read Shapiro introduction;
- do not attribute every sentence of the reviewer's reconstruction to Shapiro.

Carrier role:

`ORIGINAL PT RESEARCH ARTICLE`
-> `SELECTED READING IN CROSS-DISCIPLINARY LANGUAGE/POLITICS READER`.

This is reclassification without demonstrated change in the article's internal argument.

The unit becomes portable as an example within a broader `language is political / speech-act political potential` problem-field.

## III. 2009: Pocock self-collects the essay as part of a method history

Cambridge University Press, *Political Thought and History: Essays on Theory and Method* (2009), directly places `Verbalizing a political act: towards a politics of speech` as chapter 3 of Part I, `Political Thought as History`.

Publisher description says the collection selects essays from a lifetime of reflection on political thought and how to study it historically, emphasizing the theory and practice of political thought as action in history.

A contemporary Notre Dame Philosophical Reviews review quotes Pocock's preface: he selected the essays because they indicate what he had taken, and still took, his method and its implications to be.

Thus the later carrier role is:

`SELF-SELECTED METHOD/HISTORIOGRAPHY CANON`.

This differs from the 1984 language/politics reader role and from the 1973 inaugural-journal research role.

Therefore:

`ONE TEXT`
can become
`POLITICAL THEORY ARTICLE`
+
`LANGUAGE/POLITICS READER SELECTION`
+
`AUTHOR'S RETROSPECTIVE METHOD-HISTORY UNIT`.

No one of these roles exhausts the historical identity of the text.

## IV. 2009 editorial policy changes the proof burden

The 2009 review gives an especially valuable version-control statement.

It contrasts Pocock's collection with Skinner's *Visions of Politics* and reports that Pocock's essays were only `lightly edited`; relatively minor changes to the bodies of essays are not individually indicated, while additions to footnotes are marked with square brackets.

This creates a hard source-use rule:

`2009 REPRINT`
!= `DIPLOMATIC COPY OF 1973 ORIGINAL`.

Therefore:

- use 1973 PT for exact contemporary wording, pagination, author note and argument state;
- use 2009 to study retrospective selection, self-canonization and later method framing;
- do not quote 2009 wording as proof that an exact clause appeared in 1973 unless checked against the original;
- do not infer that an unmarked body difference is historically insignificant merely because the editorial policy calls changes light.

Compact:

`LATER REPRINT CAN PRESERVE ARGUMENT FAMILY WHILE ALTERING VERSION-EVIDENCE STATUS`.

This is directly relevant to the project's treatment of Skinner, whose later revisions are known to be more substantial.

## V. Same text, changing classificatory unit

The strongest result is not merely `article was reprinted twice`.

The carrier sequence performs different classificatory operations:

### 1973 PT

Unit as:

`CURRENT POLITICAL-THEORY RESEARCH CONTRIBUTION`.

### 1984 Shapiro reader

Unit as:

`SELECTED LANGUAGE/POLITICS / SPEECH-ACT POLITICAL RESOURCE`.

### 2009 Pocock collection

Unit as:

`RETROSPECTIVE COMPONENT OF POCOCK'S METHOD AND ITS DEVELOPMENT`.

Thus:

`TEXTUAL CONTINUITY`
!= `CLASSIFICATORY ROLE CONTINUITY`.

And:

`CANONIZATION`
can occur by moving a text between problem-fields without requiring a new text.
