## 1. 1898 is now actor-level, not retrospective field reconstruction

MS-0873 Wilson transcription witness, 20 Oct and 1/5 Dec 1898, directly records:

- Buddhism to be treated `from a philosophical point of view` while retaining control of `the technicalities of the texts`;

- Ecclesiastes as an explicit textual object;
- Maurice Vernes's Psalms course chosen to get `en rapport` for Lovejoy's **`Wisdom work`**;
- Israel Lévi / Ecclesiastes textual material;
- Faculty of Theology library used for Wisdom-book commentary;
- fellowship constraint: principal work had to remain within what Lovejoy understood as philosophy rather than drift too far into comparative religion.

This gives the 1901–02 cluster a documented prehistory at the level of **Lovejoy's own contemporary project language**.

## 2. 1901 remains a double teaching stream, both still Wilson-mediated

### A. `The Philosophy of Buddhism`

Wilson item 243 describes four Washington University Association lectures:

1. place of Buddhism among Indian philosophies;
2–3. two essential formulas of Buddhism;
4. Buddhist ethics and its goal.

The syllabus original remains unrecovered.

The structure is compatible with the documented afterlife of notebook 004, but exact wording and internal argument remain **PRIMARY SYLLABUS HOLD**.

### B. `Robert Louis Stevenson, Moralist`

Wilson item 244 describes a separate 1901 Washington University Association syllabus and says Lovejoy treated Stevenson's moral views as representative of modern tendencies, including the opposition of:

`an otherworldly ethics`

vs

`the new temporally based ethics`.

Again, the syllabus original remains unrecovered. Do not turn Wilson's summary into a quotation from Lovejoy.

### Documentary consequence

The two 1901 syllabi should remain separate carriers. Their coexistence establishes that in the same institutional year Lovejoy was publicly organizing:

- Buddhist philosophy / formulas / ethics;
- a modern literary-moral problem through otherworldly versus temporal ethics.

It does not establish that the two were one course, one manuscript, or one argument.

### Bibliographic locator refinement

Independent controls place *University of California Magazine* vol.8 no.6 in **October 1902**:

- Harry A. Overstreet, `Oxford Student Customs`, 8.6 (Oct.1902), 292–300;
- John Galen Howard, `The Hearst Memorial Mining Building for the College of Mining`, vol.8 (Oct.1902), 285–291.

Berkeley Alumni Association's institutional history says the alumni publication became known as **`The Graduate` in 1902**, while Sacramento Public Library catalogs the 1895–1904 run under the older serial title. Therefore the retrieval request should use both title states:

> `University of California Magazine / The Graduate`, vol.8 no.6, October 1902, around p.285; Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Ecclesiastes Instructed`.

The apparent overlap at p.285 with Howard's article is not enough to reject Wilson's locator. A short poem and article opening may share a page, or bibliographic conventions may differ. Direct page inspection controls the issue.

## 4. 1902 `Religion and the Time-Process` is the first fully direct object in the cluster

Unlike the 1901 syllabi and unrecovered poem, Lovejoy's full signed article is directly available and already primary-controlled in the repo.

The article:

- analyzes `otherworldliness` through Indian religious philosophies, especially Vedānta and Buddhism;
- explicitly sends readers back to Lovejoy's 1898 Buddhist technical-terms article;
- distinguishes an otherworldly/perfectionist valuation from positive valuation of process/becoming;
- moves through India, Greek philosophy, Christianity and modern philosophy;
- rejects historical borrowing from mere affinity where an internal genealogy explains the Greek case;
- closes around modern problems of process, value and social significance.

This means the strongest documented sequence is no longer just:

`004 -> 1898 Buddhist philology -> 1902 Time-Process`.

`1898 Buddhist specialist work`

`1902 Ecclesiastes literary carrier [bibliographic primary target]`

`1902 full philosophical recombination in Religion and the Time-Process`.

The arrow here denotes **chronological/conceptual convergence worth testing**, not demonstrated manuscript genealogy.

## 7. Why this matters methodologically

This cluster supplies a concrete case of **recombination without a single draft genealogy**.

The historical unit changes with carrier:

- private research plan;
- specialist Buddhist article/notebook;
- public lecture syllabus;
- literary sonnet;
- journal philosophy article.

Therefore:

`SAME OBJECT NAME / TEXTUAL CORPUS`
!=
`SAME QUESTION`
!=
`SAME CLAIM STATE`
!=
`SAME CARRIER FORM`
!=
`SINGLE DEVELOPMENTAL LINE`.

A later publication may combine problem fields previously distributed across carriers without any one earlier carrier being its complete draft.

This is directly relevant to the repo's general distinction among object, relation, carrier and publication selection.
