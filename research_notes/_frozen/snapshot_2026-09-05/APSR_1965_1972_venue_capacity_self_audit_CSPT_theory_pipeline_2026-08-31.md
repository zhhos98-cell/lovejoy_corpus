# APSR 1965–1972: venue capacity, self-audit, CSPT crossover, and theory pipeline

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT APSR EDITORIAL SELF-AUDIT + DIRECT 1969 MASTHEAD + DIRECT WOLIN/CSPT PUBLICATION EDGE + DIRECT 1972 PIPELINE LIST / CAUSAL CEILINGS LOCKED**

## Core result

The publication-access grievance surrounding the formation of CSPT and later *Political Theory* can now be tested against APSR's own execution rather than treated only as founder perception.

The resulting picture is neither `APSR EXCLUDED POLITICAL THEORY` nor `THERE WAS NO PUBLICATION PROBLEM`.

The stronger model is:

`FORMAL ACCESS + SELECTIVE PERSONNEL INCLUSION + OCCASIONAL HIGH-PROFILE THEORY PUBLICATION`
coexisting with
`LOW ARTICLE CAPACITY / WEAK REPRESENTATION FOR POLITICAL PHILOSOPHY`.

APSR's own June 1972 editorial self-audit explicitly recognized the imbalance.

This gives a crucial distinction:

`EDITORIAL-BOARD PRESENCE`
!= `ARTICLE CAPACITY`;

`ONE SUCCESSFUL THEORY ARTICLE`
!= `SUBFIELD REPRESENTATION`;

`PRINTED SCARCITY`
!= `ZERO SUBMISSION / ZERO ACCEPTED PIPELINE`.

---

## I. Ranney-era gatekeeping was formalized rather than simply personal

Historical APSR scholarship reports that a fully established peer-review system came when Austin Ranney became managing editor in 1965.

A contemporary/near-contemporary control also reports that for manuscripts submitted between 1 August 1965 and 31 July 1968, approximately **16.8%** were ultimately published.

This is not a political-theory-specific acceptance rate.

Therefore it establishes a generally selective venue, not differential treatment of theory.

Control:

`LOW GENERAL ACCEPTANCE RATE`
!= `THEORY-SPECIFIC DISCRIMINATION`.

Source controls:

- Patterson/Ripley/Trish retrospective summarized in APSR/PS historical scholarship;
- Ranney, `Report of the Managing Editor: American Political Science Review 1969–1970`;
- contemporary discussion of APSR refereeing and acceptance rates.

## II. 1969 masthead: Richter and Mansfield were inside the APSR editorial structure

Direct Cambridge front matter for APSR volume 63 (1969) lists:

- Austin Ranney — Managing Editor;
- Fred I. Greenstein;
- **Harvey C. Mansfield**;
- Warren E. Miller;
- Walter F. Murphy;
- **Melvin Richter**;
- John E. Turner;
- Vernon Van Dyke;
- Myron Weiner.

This is a direct institutional control against any simple personnel-exclusion story.

Richter, one of CSPT's founders, and Mansfield, part of the wider political-theory coalition ecology, sat on the APSR editorial board while publication-access anxiety remained live.

Thus:

`THEORIST / CSPT-AFFILIATED PERSONNEL PRESENT ON BOARD`
!= `THEORY HAD ADEQUATE ARTICLE SPACE`.

Conversely:

`THEORY ARTICLE SCARCITY`
!= `THEORISTS WERE ABSENT FROM EDITORIAL GOVERNANCE`.

This is a particularly clean UNIT×RELATION case because board membership is an institutional relation, while publication share is a distribution/capacity relation.

## III. December 1969: APSR directly published a CSPT paper attacking methodological dominance

Sheldon S. Wolin, `Political Theory as a Vocation`, APSR 63.4 (Dec. 1969), 1062–1082.

The direct Cambridge article record states that this was a **revised version of a paper delivered in September 1968 before the Conference for the Study of Political Thought**.

The article explicitly places `method` in the context of the behavioral revolution and contrasts the `methodist` with the political theorist.

This gives a direct carrier edge:

`CSPT 1968 PAPER`
-> `REVISED APSR 1969 ARTICLE`.

That edge is important because it prevents a binary institutional narrative in which CSPT speech and APSR publication occupied mutually exclusive arenas.

Safe claim:

> APSR could carry an explicitly CSPT-originated and strongly anti-methodist political-theory intervention even during the period when CSPT members described the discipline's publication environment as threatening.

Hold:

- the fact of publication does not establish generous overall capacity;
- it does not show that other theory manuscripts faced identical odds;
- it does not refute actor perceptions of structural scarcity.

`ONE HIGH-PROFILE CROSSOVER`
!= `SUBFIELD CAPACITY`.

## IV. June 1972 APSR self-audit: `Present and Absent`

The decisive execution-level source is the APSR editorial comment `Present and Absent`, vol. 66 (June 1972), pp.585–587.

The direct Cambridge PDF and page images are controlled.

The editors begin with an explicit **feedback-loop model of journal publication**:

- scholars observe what gets through the editorial process;
- they infer editor preferences from what appears in print;
- this can affect what kinds of manuscripts are subsequently submitted;
- if good but dissimilar manuscripts are deterred, the journal itself loses access to them.

This is extremely important for the present project because it makes venue capacity endogenous:

`PRINTED CONTENT`
-> `PERCEIVED EDITORIAL PREFERENCE`
-> `SUBMISSION BEHAVIOR`
-> `FUTURE PRINTED CONTENT`.

Therefore publication scarcity can be reproduced without a simple explicit ban or doctrinal editorial policy.

Compact rule:

`OUTPUT DISTRIBUTION`
can alter
`INPUT DISTRIBUTION`.

Do not treat this as a proven quantified causal mechanism; it is the editors' contemporaneous operational diagnosis.

## V. Direct numbers for APSR Volume 65 (1971)

The 1972 editors directly audit the previous full volume:

- **46 articles** total;
- **35** included at least one table or figure;
- **19** dealt with political psychology, mass voting behavior, citizen value/attitude acquisition, or low-/middle-level political participation;
- **13** were classed as `formal theory`, operationalized by the presence of Greek letters or algebraic expressions beyond ordinary statistical-test formulas;
- **3** were classed as `political philosophy`.

The editors explicitly call political philosophy one of several areas of **weak representation**.

Other weakly represented areas include public law, public administration/organization theory, policy analysis, elite politics and international politics.

This is direct actor-level evidence for:

`POLITICAL PHILOSOPHY SHARE = LOW`
within
`A JOURNAL HEAVILY COMMITTED TO QUANTITATIVE / MASS-BEHAVIORAL / FORMAL WORK`.

A crude share calculation gives:

`3 / 46 ≈ 6.5%`

for articles the editors themselves called political philosophy.

Use that percentage only as a descriptive ratio for this one volume under their own classification.

Do not extrapolate it automatically to 1967–73 or to all `political theory`, because the editors' `formal theory` category and political-philosophy category are different and potentially overlapping with later uses of `theory`.

---

## VI. The editors themselves refuse a single causal explanation

The same editorial asks how to explain the absences and explicitly keeps several possibilities open:

1. scholars may simply have been writing less in those areas;
2. APSR may have received less than its share because strong specialist journals existed elsewhere, with examples including *Ethics*, *World Politics*, *Public Administration Review*, and law journals;
3. standards might conceivably have been higher in older established areas than in newer ones.

They say they have no sure way of knowing whether APSR's published sample accurately reflected the profession's best current intellectual work.

This is a direct contemporary warning against causal overreach.

Therefore:

`LOW PUBLISHED SHARE`
!= `EDITORIAL REJECTION BIAS PROVEN`;

`LOW PUBLISHED SHARE`
!= `LOW SUBFIELD PRODUCTIVITY PROVEN`;

`LOW PUBLISHED SHARE`
!= `SPECIALIST-JOURNAL DIVERSION PROVEN`.

The source establishes the **capacity/result problem** much more firmly than its cause.

---

## VII. The 1972 accepted-future pipeline complicates `absence`

The same `Present and Absent` editorial prints a list of articles already accepted for future publication.

Among them are several unmistakably relevant to political philosophy / history of political thought / methodology:

- Charles E. Butterworth, `Averroes: Politics and Opinion`;
- Eugene F. Miller, `Positivism, Historicism, and Political Inquiry`;
- Walter Odajnyk, `The Political Ideas of C. G. Jung`;
- Lawrence A. Scaff, `Max Weber's Politics and Political Education`.

This creates a highly useful temporal distinction:

`1971 PRINTED VOLUME = WEAK POLITICAL-PHILOSOPHY REPRESENTATION`

while

`JUNE 1972 ACCEPTED PIPELINE = MULTIPLE THEORY / HISTORY / METHOD ARTICLES ALREADY PRESENT`.

Thus a low printed share cannot be converted into a claim that the editorial office was not receiving or accepting any political-theory work.

It may reflect:

- lag between acceptance and publication;
- variation from volume to volume;
- changed submission flow;
- changed editorial choices;
- or some combination.

The current evidence does not discriminate among these mechanisms.

Crucially, because the theory-relevant articles were already **accepted** when `Present and Absent` appeared, they cannot simply be treated as effects caused by that editorial's invitation to submit more diverse work.

Control:

`EDITORIAL CALL FOR DIVERSITY`
!= `CAUSE OF ALREADY-ACCEPTED THEORY PIPELINE`.

## VIII. The APSR case transforms the 1967–73 publication-jurisdiction story

The current publication-access sequence can now be stated more precisely.

### 1967 — CSPT founding anxiety

Founder correspondence, controlled through Box 2 archival scholarship, records fear that political theory's future inside political science and access to central professional outlets were under pressure.

### 1968 -> 1969 — cross-venue permeability

Wolin delivers `Political Theory as a Vocation` to CSPT in 1968; a revised version appears in APSR in December 1969.

### 1969 — institutional overlap

Richter and Mansfield sit on APSR's editorial board under Ranney.

### 1971 output, audited in 1972

APSR publishes only three articles its editors classify as political philosophy out of 46 total and directly deplores the weak representation.

### 1972 pipeline

Several political-philosophy/history/method manuscripts are already accepted for future publication.

### 1973 — *Political Theory* launch

The new journal's public launch rhetoric describes established publication outlets as offering inadequate space for political theory and presents the new journal as a broader carrier.

The resulting model is:

`NOT EXCLUSION`
+
`NOT ADEQUATE CAPACITY`
+
`CROSS-VENUE PERMEABILITY`
+
`VISIBLE OUTPUT IMBALANCE`
+
`SUBMISSION-FEEDBACK CONCERN`
+
`NEW DEDICATED CARRIER`.

This is more historically exact than either `behavioralists expelled political theory` or `there was no publication problem because APSR sometimes published theorists`.

## IX. New conceptual distinction: carrier access versus carrier capacity

The APSR evidence demands a distinction not yet sufficiently explicit in the repo.

### `CARRIER ACCESS`

Can a kind of work enter the venue at all?

Evidence:

- Wolin 1969 clearly can;
- theory-relevant articles are in the 1972 accepted pipeline.

### `CARRIER CAPACITY`

How much recurring space does the venue supply to the kind of work, relative to competing work and the subfield's needs?

Evidence:

- 1971 political philosophy = 3/46 by APSR's own classification;
- editors themselves call the representation weak.

Therefore:

> **ACCESS != CAPACITY.**

A venue can be formally open and occasionally publish prominent work while still providing low recurrent capacity for a subfield.

This may be the cleanest formulation yet of the publication-jurisdiction problem behind CSPT / *Political Theory*.

It also explains why editor-board personnel overlap is insufficient evidence against the founders' grievance.

## XI. Evidence ceiling

### Direct primary locked

- APSR 1969 masthead with Ranney, Richter, Mansfield and other board members;
- Wolin 1969 APSR article metadata/abstract/footnote identifying September 1968 CSPT paper origin;
- APSR 1972 `Present and Absent` full three-page PDF and page images;
- direct 1971 article counts/classifications reported by APSR editors;
- direct accepted-future article list printed in 1972.

### Secondary / historical control

- Ranney became managing editor in 1965 and formalized a fully established peer-review system;
- overall 1965–68 acceptance-rate estimate around 16.8%.

### Not established

- political-theory-specific submission numbers for 1965–72;
- political-theory-specific rejection rates;
- Ranney personally suppressed theory submissions;
- Richter/Mansfield controlled theory manuscript decisions;
- the three political-philosophy articles in 1971 exhaust every article a later analyst might code as political theory;
- the 1972 pipeline resulted from the `Present and Absent` editorial;
- causal transmission from 1967 CSPT publication anxiety to 1973 journal formation beyond the separately controlled institutional sequence.
