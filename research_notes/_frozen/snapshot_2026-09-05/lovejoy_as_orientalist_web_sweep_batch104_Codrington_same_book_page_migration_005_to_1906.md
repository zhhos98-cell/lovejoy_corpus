## Core result

The book persists; the proof-bearing page does not.

Notebook 005 registers `Codrington: The Melanesians` in its opening bibliography. In the notebook's early sequence on death, future life, and origin-of-death explanations, Lovejoy returns to Codrington and cites **p.260**. The current clean transcription reads `p.26`, but a fresh visual recheck of the manuscript page shows a terminal zero; more importantly, Codrington's own printed structure independently resolves the ambiguity: p.260 is precisely the Saa `Origin of Death` passage about the old woman who changes her skin and then resumes the discarded slough, whereas Codrington later explicitly refers back to `the story of the Origin of Death noticed in the account of Saa (page 260)`.

In 1906 Lovejoy re-enters the **same 1891 book at a different locus**, *The Melanesians*, pp.118–119. Here the extracted object is not a death myth but the definition of `mana`: a nonphysical / in-a-way supernatural power or influence, manifest in physical efficacy, not fixed in one thing, conveyable, possessed and imparted by spirits, and — importantly — said by Codrington to `essentially belong to personal beings to originate it`.

The longitudinal relation is therefore:

`same bibliographic source`
→ `different internal locus`
→ `different extracted proposition`
→ `different evidentiary job`.

Notebook 005:

`Codrington 1891 → p.260 → failed rejuvenation / origin-of-death myth → comparative death-causation and future-life dossier`.

Lovejoy 1906:

`Codrington 1891 → pp.118–119 → mana / transferable efficacy → Oceanic control for the cross-regional primitive-energetics category`.

This is stronger than generic source persistence and finer-grained than Batch79's author/title-level source persistence. The best formulation is:

> **bibliographic persistence does not imply evidentiary continuity: the same book persists while Lovejoy relocates the proof-bearing page and changes the proposition extracted from it.**

Or compactly:

> **source persistence + page migration → proposition migration → new evidentiary job.**

## 2. What Lovejoy is doing with Codrington in notebook 005

The relevant notebook sequence is not yet a mana theory.

The opening pages distinguish naturalistic, ritual, social and moral determinants of future destiny; they repeatedly warn against treating all post-mortem differentiation as moral retribution. The death section then asks a related classificatory question: must death itself be conceived as natural and necessary, or is each death explained through a special cause such as sorcery, taboo violation, accident, hostile agency or mythic error?

Codrington p.260 enters this second problem.

Its evidentiary job is narrow and concrete:

- document a Melanesian **origin-of-death narrative**;
- illustrate lost bodily renewal / skin-changing;
- support a comparative packet in which mortality is historically or mythically explained rather than simply accepted as natural necessity.

Codrington is therefore being read as a source for **mythic causation of death and survival**, not yet as an authority for a generalized theory of impersonal efficacy.

This matters because it prevents a retrospective flattening of 005 into an embryonic `Primitive Energetics` notebook. The same ethnographic book is already present, but its active evidentiary affordance is different.

## 3. In 1906 Lovejoy re-navigates the same book to pp.118–119

The 1906 article reaches Codrington only after Lovejoy has constructed a high-resolution North-American packet around `wakonda`, `manitou`, and `oki` and has argued that native linguistic usage itself supports an underlying conception of impersonal vital energy.

At printed p.372 Lovejoy opens `II. Melanesians and Polynesians` and says that the same conception is known from the best authorities on the island peoples. The first authority is Codrington, now cited as:

`The Melanesians, 1891, p.118`.

The continuation on p.373 supplies the famous mana definition. The relevant propositions are:

- mana is power/influence rather than ordinary physical power;
- it manifests in force, excellence and efficacy;
- it is not fixed in one object;
- it can be conveyed;
- spirits possess and impart it;
- Codrington explicitly says that it essentially belongs to personal beings to originate it;
- it can nevertheless act through water, stone, bone and other media.

The evidentiary job has therefore changed completely. Lovejoy now uses Codrington to establish an Oceanic analogue to the North-American impersonal/transferable-energy field.

### New source-function code

`005 / p.260`
→ `MYTHIC-EVENT WITNESS`
→ proposition: **death is explained through failed renewal / historical-mythic cause**.

`1906 / pp.118–119`
→ `COMPARATIVE SEMANTIC-CAUSAL WITNESS`
→ proposition: **mana is transferable efficacy/power and can support the comparative energetics field**.

The author/title does not tell us the continuity. The **page and extracted proposition do**.

## 4. The adverse clause is important: 1906 does not simply mine Codrington for a clean impersonalism

The 1906 quotation is especially useful because Lovejoy retains Codrington's sentence that mana `essentially belongs to personal beings to originate it`.

This prevents a simplistic account in which Lovejoy merely suppresses all source material inconsistent with his impersonal-energy thesis. The Codrington packet itself is hybrid:

`transferable / non-fixed efficacy`
+
`spiritual/personal origination`.

Lovejoy nevertheless gives the transferable-efficacy axis comparative weight.

This confirms the proposition-relative source architecture established in Batches77–78: an authoritative source can be admitted for one proposition while containing adverse material for another.

The same-book longitudinal control now adds another dimension:

> **Lovejoy not only partitions propositions within a page; over time he also re-selects pages within a source.**

So the evidentiary atom can be modeled at four nested levels:

`book`
→ `page/locus`
→ `statement/proposition`
→ `assigned evidentiary function`.

A history based only on bibliography stops at the first level and therefore misses the transformation.

## 5. This refines Batch79: persistence must be measured below author/title level

Batch79 tracked source persistence and function migration from notebook 005 to 1906. Codrington now gives a direct page-level demonstration of what that migration can mean materially.

There are at least three distinct forms of apparent `source persistence`:

1. **bibliographic persistence** — the same author/book remains present;
2. **locus persistence** — the same page/passage remains active;
3. **proposition/function persistence** — the same extracted claim continues to do the same work.

Codrington currently scores:

- bibliographic persistence: **YES**;
- locus persistence: **NO** (`p.260` → `pp.118–119`);
- proposition persistence: **NO** (origin-of-death myth → mana/efficacy);
- evidentiary-function persistence: **NO** (mythic-causation example → cross-regional semantic/causal warrant).

Hence `same source` is much too coarse a description.

A better model for Lovejoy's working practice is:

> **source reuse is often source re-navigation.**

## 7. A route-control complication: Codrington's mana definition was already circulating before the 1891 book

One further control prevents us from overclaiming the mechanics of Lovejoy's 1906 return to Codrington.

Max Müller's 1878 Hibbert Lectures printed material from a Codrington letter dated 7 July 1877. The passage already contains the core later mana definition:

- nonphysical / in-a-way supernatural power;
- manifestation in physical force or excellence;
- non-fixed and conveyable character;
- spirits possess and impart it;
- mana essentially belongs to personal beings to originate;
- it may act through water, stone or bone.

This is extremely close to the 1891 book passage Lovejoy quotes.

Therefore the present evidence proves that Lovejoy **cites** *The Melanesians* pp.118–119 in 1906, but it does not by itself prove the physical/documentary route by which he recovered the wording. The definition was a pre-existing quotation object in comparative-religion literature.

→ DIRECT MANUSCRIPT.

`Lovejoy 1906 explicitly cites Codrington 1891 p.118 and quotes the mana definition`
→ DIRECT PRIMARY.

`Lovejoy necessarily reopened the 1891 volume rather than consulting a quotation relay`
→ **OPEN / ROUTE HOLD**.

The next test should collate wording, punctuation, paragraph boundaries and any omissions among:

`Codrington 1877 letter as printed by Max Müller 1878`
↔ `Codrington 1891 pp.118–119`

If Lovejoy preserves an 1891-specific textual feature absent from 1878, direct 1891 consultation becomes materially stronger. If the Lovejoy wording tracks the 1878 relay more closely, the route model must remain open even though his footnote points to the 1891 book.

## 8. Historiographic payoff: follow pages, not just books

The strongest methodological lesson from this Lovejoy-centered control is simple:

> **bibliographies record availability; page loci record use.**

For intellectual-history reconstruction, a source should therefore be represented not merely as:

`Lovejoy → Codrington`.

But as:

`Lovejoy stage/date → Codrington object → internal locus → extracted proposition → evidentiary function → verdict`.

Codrington gives us the first particularly clean longitudinal instance because both loci can now be controlled:

`005 → p.260 → origin of death`
versus
`1906 → pp.118–119 → mana / transferable efficacy`.

This opens a new audit across the other persistent authorities in 005 and 1906: ask not merely whether Fletcher, Brinton, Frazer, Callaway, etc. persist, but whether **their proof-bearing internal loci migrate**.
