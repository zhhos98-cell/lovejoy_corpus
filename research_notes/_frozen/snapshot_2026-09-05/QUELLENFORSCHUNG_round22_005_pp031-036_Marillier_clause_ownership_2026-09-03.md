## 1. p.31 / ms p.57 — rank, sex, age, occupation, and post-mortem continuity are Marillier-owned

Current canonical entry is a low-confidence inserted-leaf summary contrasting lower/higher future-life forms and earthly/social conditions with post-mortem existence.

Marillier printed p.7 explicitly states that differential afterlife destinies often depend not on good/bad terrestrial conduct but on social rank, age, sex, and occupation. His general formulation includes:

- king remains king;
- slave remains slave;
- nobles/rich may alone receive renewed existence;
- common people may perish with the body or occupy an inferior region;

Printed p.16 then makes this continuity concrete: cultivator remains cultivator, fisherman remains fisherman, slave serves the master, chief remains chief; Dahomey/Kutomen examples reproduce hierarchy and service after death.

is a **Marillier proposition and classificatory frame** before Lovejoy's notebook use.

## 2. p.32 — skill, chance, ritual qualification, and access conditions are already separated by Marillier

The canonical summary currently says that the dead may have to maintain themselves in existence and distinguishes a `struggle for survival` from later ideas in which food/gifts are supplied to them.

Marillier printed p.23 already distinguishes a class of non-moral afterlife determinants in unusually explicit terms. Access to the blessed abode may depend on the soul's `énergie et habileté`, on surviving dangers comparable to terrestrial dangers, or even on blind chance. He expressly says that no moral conception lies at the base of these myths.

Printed pp.28–31 then supplies another non-moral packet in which post-mortem access or treatment depends on ritual or bodily qualifications:

- burial according to rites;
- tattooing / pierced ears;
- membership in a secret society;
- sacrifice performed for the dead;
- proper funerary ceremony;
- gifts or rites that open the way into the other world.

### Source-ownership result

The broad contrast

`ABILITY / HAZARD / RITUAL ACCESS CONDITION != MORAL DESERT`

is **Marillier-owned inside this source packet**.

The phrase `struggle for survival` in the current p.32 editorial summary must not be promoted into a Lovejoy concept unless the image actually contains comparable wording or a heading that reorganizes Marillier's cases in that way.

Calibration: S3 for Marillier's non-moral access logic; T2 pending manuscript image; A0 for the general contrast; possible A1–A2 only if a Lovejoy heading/redivision is directly recovered.

## 6. p.36 — old editorial summary conflates at least two, probably three Marillier packets

The current canonical summary describes a `great tree` / underworld image, asks whether it implies annihilation or continued existence under better conditions, then cites `Callaway, Relig. [of] Amazulu`.

Clause-level source collation requires a sharper decomposition.

### A. Futuna `fale-mate` packet — Marillier p.8

Marillier states that married warriors who killed enemies go to Langi/Pulotu, while other dead go to the `fale-mate`; each family/clan has its own, which may be `un arbre creux, un rocher, etc.`. He then continues into second and third deaths.

Therefore the old `great tree` reconstruction should be withdrawn at source level. The relevant image is a **hollow tree or rock as a clan-specific house of the dead**, followed by successive death/degradation.

### B. AmaZulu continuation packet — Marillier pp.4–5

Marillier cites Callaway, *The Religious System of the Amazulus*, pp.8, 140, 165. The relevant argument is that the dead may live under the earth and continue the occupations of their terrestrial life; other souls live in the sky, but this is not a just/wicked partition. Future life is substantially continuation rather than moral recompense.

This securely resolves the source-level Callaway abbreviation family.

### C. New Zealand annihilation packet — Marillier p.9

Immediately after Tonga/Tahiti/Marquesas, Marillier describes New Zealand Po as divided into successive sections in which the soul loses vitality until it becomes a worm and dies.

The current clean entry says:
