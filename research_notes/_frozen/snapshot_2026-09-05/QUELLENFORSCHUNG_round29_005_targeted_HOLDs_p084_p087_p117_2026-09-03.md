## 1. p.84 — the old `foundation-sacrifice carrier unresolved` status was too coarse

Round 25 left p.84 as a plausible mechanism claim with the immediate foundation/building-sacrifice carrier unresolved. The current notebook summary says, in substance, that foundation/building blood or bodily remains need not signify `union through blood`; the victim's body/blood can instead operate through independent protective efficacy, with pieces of flesh functioning as charms.

That page-level HOLD failed to propagate two already-existing source controls in this repository: Batch 57 and Batch 143.

### A. Trumbull is a directly named notebook source and supplies the theory being restricted

Notebook p.62 explicitly lists:

`Blood-Covenant & The Threshold Covenant of H. Clay Trumbull.`

Trumbull's *The Threshold Covenant* (1896) contains a dedicated section, `Laying Foundations in Blood`. It interprets blood shed at the foundations/threshold of a new house or structure as a covenantal act that places the household/building under divine protection. Trumbull explicitly generalizes foundation blood through covenant, union, and protective deity, and treats earlier human victims / later animal substitution as part of the same history.

This makes Trumbull a very strong **theoretical carrier/problem target** for p.84 even without a p.84 page citation.

The same notebook section then gives direct exact-page Trumbull anchors:

- p.85 / ms p.173: `V. Trumbull Blood Cov't 118.`
- p.86 / ms p.175: `(quot. Trumbull p.129)`.

Batch 57 established that the intervening notes follow Trumbull's pp.118–129 sequence while changing the explanatory use of the evidence.

### B. Robertson Smith / Casalis already supply sacrificial-parts-as-charms

Robertson Smith's *Lectures on the Religion of the Semites*, discussing Casalis's Basuto material, explicitly argues that especially vital parts of sacrificial victims are unusually potent/dangerous and are used as `powerful charms`. He further warns that what humans do not eat is not therefore automatically given to the god; blood and heads can be used lustrally, medicinally, or as protective guardians.

Thus the alternative proposition `sacrificial body/blood may work by its own protective efficacy rather than as gift/union` is not available only in Lovejoy's notebook.

### C. Most decisive control: Marillier 1898 p.417 already performs the near-identical foundation decomposition

Batch 143 recovered Marillier's 1898 continuation/review work and showed that printed p.417 explicitly treats foundation blood / immured living beings as compatible with several distinct mechanisms rather than one blood-union theory. Marillier separates at least:

1. attaching a guardian/protective spirit to the monument;
2. a charm in which blood's magical power acts directly as force/life;
3. a propitiatory sacrifice intended to disarm a hostile or malevolent god.

Marillier therefore already owns, at field/proposition level, the crucial anti-unilateral move visible on notebook p.84:

`FOUNDATION BLOOD / REMAINS`
!=
`AUTOMATIC BLOOD-UNION`

and instead:

`SAME FORM -> MULTIPLE POSSIBLE CAUSAL BEARERS / MECHANISMS`.

The institutional relation is documented: Lovejoy was an active participant in Marillier's 1898–99 EPHE work. Direct reading or copying of Marillier p.417 is **not** established.

### Revised p.84 ownership

The correct terminal status is therefore no longer `foundation carrier unresolved -> possible Lovejoy mechanism seam`.

It is:

- Trumbull: strong named theoretical source/problem target for foundation-blood-as-covenant/union;
- Robertson Smith / Casalis: upstream control for sacrificial body parts as independently potent protective charms;

## 2. p.87 — exact carrier remains unproved, but the candidate field is now much narrower

Round 25 left the p.87 list

`saliva / perspiration / urine / excrement`

inside the general sympathetic/contagious-magic field with exact carrier unresolved.

The focused pass now identifies a substantially closer source hierarchy.

### A. Strack is the first candidate because the notebook itself names him

Notebook p.62 lists `Strack, Der Blut-Aberglaube` as relevant blood material, and pp.85–87 lie inside the same blood/body-efficacy run.

Hermann L. Strack's 1891 *Der Blutaberglaube bei Christen und Juden* contains a dedicated section beginning printed p.17:

`Abfälle und Abgänge des menschlichen Körpers`

(body wastes and bodily discharges).

The same Strack textual tradition explicitly collects human medicinal/magical substances including:

- `Harn` — urine;
- `Kot` — dung/excrement;
- `Schweiß` — sweat/perspiration;
- `Speichel` — saliva/spittle.

That four-member correspondence is unusually close to the notebook's English list.

The currently legible p.87 `Grant Allen/source reference` should not be used to attach the four-item list to Grant Allen. Full-text control of Allen's 1897 *The Evolution of the Idea of God* finds no occurrences of `saliva`, `perspiration`, `urine`, `excrement`, or `sweat`. The Allen reference and the bodily-discharge list may belong to different source blocks on the page.

### B. Bourke 1891 is a strong independent contemporary control

John G. Bourke, *Scatalogic Rites of All Nations* (1891), independently gathers exactly this material family:

- a dedicated section on `Human Sweat`;
- an extended section on superstitions connected with human saliva;
- repeated magical/witchcraft uses of urine and excrement;
- passages placing sweat, dung, spittle, and urine together among old medicinal/magical substances;
- a sorcery example where saliva, urine, or excrements of an enemy are obtained for magical action.

Bourke has not yet been found as a named notebook citation, so he is a field/control candidate rather than demonstrated direct uptake.

### Revised p.87 status

p.87 should now be routed as:

1. **Strack — strongest immediate-carrier candidate**, because he is explicitly present in the notebook bibliography/source run and has the exact bodily-discharge category;
2. Bourke — strong independent 1891 control showing that the four-substance cluster was already a comparative magic object;
3. Grant Allen — not supported as carrier for the four-item list by current full-text search.

Calibration:

- bodily-discharge magical efficacy proposition: F/A0;
- source field: substantially closed;
- exact p.87 carrier/page: T2/HOLD, with Strack priority candidate;
- no Lovejoy originality claim is licensed by the list itself.

Direct manuscript image remains necessary to determine whether `Strack`, Allen, or another source cue is materially attached to the list.
