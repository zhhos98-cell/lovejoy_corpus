## I. Near-neighbor 1 — population inference scope restriction, 1906 Democracy

The direct-primary 1906 `Democracy in the Twentieth Century` evidence already locked in the repo gives a different operation.

Lovejoy differentiates populations along:

- a longitudinal `stage of culture` axis;
- a cross-wise racial/national axis.

But the inferential strengths differ.

He treats institutional mismatch across sufficiently different stages of culture as carrying a stronger claim, while racial/national difference may make one institutional form inappropriate elsewhere but does not license a categorical `must`.

He also warns against overuse of race as an explanatory category.

Therefore:

`POPULATION CATEGORY RETAINED`
+
`INFERENCE FROM CATEGORY TO INSTITUTIONAL FIT NARROWED`.

This is not `REP-DEMOTE` in the strict sense.

Better code:

`REP-SCOPE RESTRICTION`.

The population distinction survives; what changes is the modal/proof strength of the proposition attributed to it.

## IV. Why the distinction matters

The original M5 category may have bundled several operations:

1. **REP-DEMOTE**
   - `X is representative/fundamental of population P`
   - counterdistribution defeats representativeness
   - author explicitly demotes the macro status.

2. **REP-SCOPE RESTRICTION**
   - population distinction retained
   - what it licenses is weakened (`may`, not `must`).

3. **CAUSAL REASSIGNMENT**
   - descriptive trait/category remains
   - hereditary/racial causal explanation replaced by training/environment/history.

4. **GENERALIZATION STRESS TEST**
   - provisional macro relation confronted with countercase
   - local premise or sequence revised without an explicit representativeness statement.

5. **TYPE / CHRONOLOGY SEPARATION**
   - e.g. 1914 primitive energetics: non-animistic type strongly asserted while pre-animistic priority remains only probable.

These should not be forced into one `demotion` code.

## V. Consequence for Primitive Energetics

The strict M5 question remains especially important for 1906 `primitive philosophy` because the 1914 Leuba self-identity control makes the type invariant relatively thin:

`IMPERSONAL POWER / POTENCY`.

A thin invariant makes cross-case recurrence easier. That raises, rather than solves, the separate burden of proving:

`FUNDAMENTAL / REPRESENTATIVE OF PRIMITIVE PHILOSOPHY`.

The Meylan historiographic clue adds a second possible boundary: case admission may depend on the historical/civilizational status assigned to source populations, not semantic similarity alone.

Thus the absence of a clean M5 demotion is itself analytically useful. Lovejoy may be very good at local claim restriction while still leaving the macro category relatively under-demoted.

This is a hypothesis, not an actor-intention claim.
