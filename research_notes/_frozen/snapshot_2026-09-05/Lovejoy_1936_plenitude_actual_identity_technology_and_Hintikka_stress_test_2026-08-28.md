# Lovejoy 1936 — principle of plenitude: actual identity technology and the Hintikka stress test

Date: 2026-08-28  
Status: **MATURE PRACTICE GATE-A CONTROL / IDENTITY TECHNOLOGY STRESS TEST**

Primary target: Arthur O. Lovejoy, *The Great Chain of Being* (1936), especially chapters on the principle of plenitude, Spinoza, Leibniz, and temporalization.

Secondary controls used in the current run include Richard Yeo on plenitude/natural theology and modern Leibniz scholarship discussing Lovejoy's attribution and Hintikka's criticism.

## Core result

The mature *Great Chain* does not in practice track the principle of plenitude by requiring one unchanged sentence or one unchanged metaphysical rationale.

The operative invariant is thinner and more structural:

> **a commitment connecting genuine possibility/potentiality with realization/fullness of actuality — paradigmatically, that no genuine potentiality of being can remain unfulfilled, or that creation should be commensurate with the range of possible being.**

Lovejoy treats historically different formulations as versions of the principle even when they differ in:

- theological rationale;
- modal strength;
- divine freedom/necessity;
- static vs temporalized realization;
- scope of possible beings;
- relation to compossibility and best-world choice.

This is a mature example of **thin-core aggregation under large doctrinal variation**.

It also exposes Gate A to genuine historical criticism: later modal scholarship, especially Hintikka's critique of Lovejoy's Leibniz reading, challenges whether the similarities are sufficient to warrant grouping the relevant Leibnizian modal doctrines under Lovejoy's plenitude principle.

Thus the unit-idea identity problem is not merely theoretical. It can change substantive history.

---

## I. The core is modal/metaphysical, not lexical

Lovejoy's plenitude principle is commonly reproduced from *Great Chain* in formulations such as:

- no genuine potentiality of being can remain unfulfilled;
- the abundance of creation must be commensurate with possibility and the productive capacity of a perfect/inexhaustible source;
- a plenum of forms exhaustively exemplifies the range of possible being.

The historical unit therefore does not depend on recurring terminology.

It is an analyst-reconstructed relation:

`possibility / potentiality`
→ `actualization / fullness`.

This is a strongly abstract invariant.

---

## II. Plato, Spinoza, and Leibniz do not instantiate one thick doctrine

The historical trajectory Lovejoy constructs contains significant variation.

### Plato / Platonic source

The relevant structure is the complete translation/realization of ideal possibility in actuality, connected to divine goodness/fecundity.

### Spinoza

Lovejoy says Spinoza expresses the principle in its most uncompromising form and gives it strict logical necessity.

Here plenitude approaches:

`logically possible / follows from divine nature`
→ `necessarily actual`.

### Leibniz

Lovejoy is aware that Leibniz tries to avoid Spinozist universal necessity through distinctions involving:

- divine choice;
- compossibility;
- the best possible world;
- possible species/worlds that need not belong to the actual chosen series.

Yet Lovejoy continues to detect plenitude/fullness tendencies in Leibniz, including no-gap/continuity commitments and modal-temporal variants.

Thus:

`same plenitude family`
coexists with
`different modal architecture`.

This is not strict proposition token identity.

---

## III. The actual aggregation criterion is better represented as thin-core + characteristic consequences

The *Great Chain* practice suggests a model more flexible than the earlier shorthand `explicit proposition + corollary profile`.

For plenitude, the unit can be represented as:

### Thin core

`genuine possibility should not remain permanently unrealized / actuality tends toward fullness of possibility`.

### Characteristic consequence-family

Depending on thinker/context:

- fullness/diversity of creation;
- no gaps;
- continuity/gradation;
- infinity or multiplicity of worlds/beings;
- necessitarian pressure;
- temporal eventual realization.

### Variable rationale

- divine goodness/fecundity;
- emanation;
- strict logical necessity;
- sufficient reason;
- best-world optimization;
- historical/evolutionary temporalization.

The identity claim therefore survives substantial changes in **why** the principle is held and in **how strongly** it is formulated.

---

## IV. This is a Gate-A decision before relation history begins

Before Lovejoy can narrate:

`Plato → later Platonism → Spinoza/Leibniz → eighteenth century → Romantic/evolutionary transformations`,

he must first classify historically different propositions as instances/versions of `plenitude`.

That is Gate A:

`historical token A, B, C...`
→ `same analytical family/unit P`.

Only afterward can he discuss:

- transmission;
- implication;
- conflict with other principles;
- temporalization;
- historical effects.

Thus much of the famous long-range narrative depends on the prior aggregation technology.

---

## V. Lovejoy himself partly controls variation

The case should not be caricatured as simple reification.

Lovejoy notices that:

- versions differ in modal/theological rationale;
- Leibniz resists the strongest Spinozist necessity;
- static plenitude can become temporalized;
- plenitude combines historically with other principles such as continuity/gradation in changing ways.

So the method is not:

`one fixed doctrine repeated verbatim`.

It is closer to:

> **recognize a stable abstract relation through historically changing versions and combinations.**

This is exactly the mature `same idea, sometimes disguised` recognition problem.

---

## VI. Hintikka criticism targets Gate A, not merely a downstream detail

Later Leibniz scholarship associated with Jaakko Hintikka criticizes Lovejoy's attribution of the principle of plenitude to Leibniz by emphasizing distinctions among:

- absolute possibility/necessity;
- hypothetical necessity;
- possible worlds;
- compossibility;
- temporal/modal structure.

Modern work still describes the issue explicitly as a challenge to the `Principle of Plenitude ascribed to Leibniz by Lovejoy`.

This is important historiographically.

The criticism is not simply:

`Lovejoy missed one influence edge`.

It asks whether Lovejoy's thin-core abstraction erased distinctions constitutive of Leibniz's modal position.

In Gate-A terms:

> **Are Lovejoy's Leibnizian tokens genuinely members of the same plenitude equivalence class, or does the abstraction level force sameness by stripping away modal differences that should count as identity-relevant?**

This is a direct substantive test of the unit-idea method.

---

## VII. The mature criterion is object-indexed, not one universal rule

The plenitude case should modify the aggregation genealogy.

### Lecture-I practical control

For some units Lovejoy can use:

`explicit proposition/principle + corollary profile`.

### Plenitude in practice

The cross-century unit is often thinner:

`abstract modal/metaphysical core + characteristic consequence-family`,

while rationale and formulation vary.

### 1941 `infinite`

The invariant can be thinner still: a shared negative conceptual core across discrepant positive meanings.

Therefore even within mature Lovejoy:

> **unit recognition has variable thickness.**

The historian chooses how much structure is identity-essential.

---

## VIII. A useful new variable: invariant thickness

Gate A should now explicitly code **thickness of invariant**.

### Thick invariant

- detailed proposition;
- substantial inferential/corollary profile;
- narrow range of tolerated variation.

### Medium invariant

- core proposition plus a loose consequence-family;
- rationale/configuration may vary.

### Thin invariant

- minimal conceptual relation or negative core;
- large variation in positive content/function allowed.

The thinner the invariant:

- the easier trans-contextual recurrence becomes to establish;
- the greater the risk of analyst-driven over-aggregation.

This does not make thin invariants invalid. It increases the burden of explaining why the omitted differences are historically non-essential for the specific inquiry.

---

## IX. Consequence for Skinner

The plenitude case gives a concrete form to the contextualist dispute.

The issue is not whether Lovejoy knows that contexts/rationales differ. He does.

The issue is:

> **Can the historian legitimately treat a thin modal core as the same historical idea when its rationale, force, modal semantics and pragmatic/doctrinal role change?**

A Skinner-style challenge can argue that some of those changing features are constitutive of what the historical actor was doing with the proposition.

Lovejoy's defense is that a thinner conceptual relation remains historically traceable and analytically fruitful.

This is the real argument, not `context vs no context`.

---

## X. Revised mature Gate-A model

For *Great Chain* practice:

`historical token`
→ `strip variable wording/rationale/context`
→ `identify invariant of chosen thickness`
→ `test characteristic consequences/relations`
→ `group as version of unit idea`
→ `trace combinations, effects, transformations`.

The most vulnerable step is:

`choose invariant thickness`.

This should become a standard field in future case audits.

---

## Current shorthand

> **The principle of plenitude shows Lovejoy's mature unit-idea practice operating through a variable-thickness invariant rather than verbatim propositional identity. He preserves a thin possibility→actuality/fullness core across markedly different Platonic, Spinozist, Leibnizian and later rationales. Hintikka's Leibniz critique demonstrates that this Gate-A choice is historically consequential: the question is whether Lovejoy's abstraction preserves a real recurrent idea or erases modal differences that should defeat identity.**
