# Batch 101 — Fletcher 1897 → Durkheim 1912 → Swain 1915 → Hardy 1966: evidence-packet inheritance with recomposition

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced
Scope: continue Batch99’s quotation-lineage test from a single Codrington excerpt to the larger North-American/Melanesian proof packet surrounding it. The key question is whether Hardy 1966 merely repeats the Durkheim–Swain Codrington truncation, or inherits and recomposes a wider evidence sequence including Alice C. Fletcher on Omaha `wakan`.

## Core result

Batch99 established a material quotation chain for Codrington:

`Codrington 1891 full hybrid mana statement`
→ `Durkheim 1912 marked truncation`
→ `Swain 1915 translated truncation`
→ `Hardy 1966 inherited truncation`.

Batch101 shows that Hardy inherits more than one quotation. He explicitly states that his Durkheim extracts are taken from J. W. Swain’s 1915 English translation. In the same lecture he reconstructs a compact comparative proof sequence from Durkheim using:

- Riggs on `wakan` as mystery / secret power / divinity;
- Alice C. Fletcher on the Omaha belief that animate and inanimate things are permeated by a `common life` and that this life is continuous;
- Durkheim’s gloss that the totem places the individual in relation with this `source of energy` and incarnates `wakan`;
- Codrington’s mana definition in the shortened Durkheim–Swain form;
- Durkheim’s commentary identifying mana with the same anonymous, diffused, impersonal force.

But Hardy does **not** simply reproduce Durkheim’s original local order. In *Elementary Forms*, the chapter moves through:

`wakan / orenda`
→ `Codrington mana quotation`
→ `Durkheim impersonality commentary`
→ `Fletcher common-life quotation`.

Hardy rearranges this into:

`wakan / Riggs`
→ `Fletcher common-life quotation`
→ `Durkheim source-of-energy gloss`
→ `Codrington shortened mana quotation`
→ `Durkheim impersonality commentary`.

Thus the packet is inherited **with recomposition**.

The strongest formulation is:

> **A transmitted evidence packet can preserve inherited quotation boundaries while changing the order in which its authorities are made to prove the proposition.**

Or:

> **documentary descent does not require sequence fidelity.**

This matters because it prevents an overly rigid version of excerpt-boundary fingerprinting. A downstream writer may inherit a textual object from an upstream authority, preserve its distinctive omission, yet rearrange surrounding sources into a new proof architecture.

---

## 1. Durkheim’s 1912 packet is already a composite proof architecture

In Book II, chapter VI of *Les formes élémentaires de la vie religieuse*, Durkheim argues that Australian totemism contains an anonymous and impersonal force, though the Australian actor does not represent it in fully abstract form. He then seeks explicit comparative controls in societies he regards as related but more conceptually generalized.

The Swain 1915 English sequence is directly searchable and gives the following progression.

### North America

Durkheim moves through Siouan `wakan`, Iroquois `orenda`, Algonquian `manitou`, and related terms. He treats `wakan` as impersonal and non-personified, quoting Riggs that the word embraces mystery, secret power and divinity. Hewitt’s `orenda` is treated as mystic potency inherent in diverse bodies and as an efficient cause of phenomena.

### Melanesia

Durkheim then says mana is the exact equivalent of Sioux `wakan` and Iroquois `orenda`. At this point he quotes Codrington in the selective form controlled in Batch97/99:

`force distinct from physical power`
→ `mana`
→ `power/influence not physical; manifests in physical force or excellence`
→ `not fixed / conveyable`
→ **ellipsis**
→ `all Melanesian religion consists in getting/using mana`.

Immediately afterward Durkheim asks whether this is not the same anonymous and diffused force found in Australian totemism and emphasizes impersonality and ubiquity.

### Omaha / Fletcher

Only after that does Durkheim return to Omaha material and introduce Fletcher:

- Omaha individual and collective totems are particular forms of `wakan`;
- Fletcher says the efficacy of the totem rests on a conception of nature/life involving a `common life` permeating animate and inanimate things and the continuity of that life;
- Durkheim then states that this common life principle is `wakan` and that the totem puts the individual into relation with this `source of energy`.

This is not a bibliography list. It is an ordered proof:

`comparative lexical field`
→ `mana cross-regional equivalence`
→ `Codrington witness`
→ `impersonality conclusion`
→ `Fletcher common-life reinforcement`.

## 2. Hardy explicitly identifies the Swain 1915 translation as his Durkheim textual base

In Lecture III of *The Divine Flame*, Hardy introduces Durkheim at length and states that the extracts he is about to give from *Elementary Forms of Religious Life* are from the **English translation by J. W. Swain (1915)**.

This is unusually valuable provenance evidence because it removes the need to infer Hardy’s immediate textual witness from verbal similarity alone.

Status:

- Hardy’s dependence on Durkheim: **DIRECT PRIMARY**;
- Hardy’s use of Swain 1915 for Durkheim quotations: **DIRECT PRIMARY**;
- any claim that Hardy never consulted Fletcher or Codrington independently: **NOT ESTABLISHED**.

The last caution matters. Hardy provides Fletcher’s bibliographic reference in a note, and he names Codrington’s *The Melanesians*. But when he presents the relevant passages, he explicitly frames Fletcher as `also quoted by Durkheim` and Codrington as something Durkheim quotes. The secure relation is therefore that Hardy’s **argumentative packet is Durkheim-mediated**, even if direct consultation of underlying authorities cannot be excluded.

---

## 3. Hardy preserves the Fletcher wording but moves it earlier

Hardy’s sequence begins with Durkheim’s broader description of totemism as an anonymous, impersonal force and its physical/moral character. He then turns to North American material.

He summarizes the Sioux field and quotes Riggs. Next, before introducing Codrington, he gives Fletcher’s Omaha passage in substantially the Swain wording:

- faith in the efficacy of the totem rests on beliefs concerning nature and life;
- animate and inanimate things are permeated by a common life;
- this life is continuous / cannot be broken.

He then immediately gives Durkheim’s own gloss that this common principle is `wakan` and that the totem is the means of relation with a `source of energy`.

Only after this does Hardy cross to Melanesia and introduce mana as Durkheim’s equivalent of `wakan` and `orenda`, followed by the Codrington quotation.

This reordering changes the rhetorical vector.

### Durkheim/Swain order

`wakan/orenda field`
→ `mana/Codrington`
→ `impersonality`
→ `Fletcher common-life confirmation`.

### Hardy order

`wakan/Riggs`
→ `Fletcher common-life continuity`
→ `source-of-energy gloss`
→ `mana/Codrington`
→ `impersonality`.

In Hardy, Fletcher becomes a **bridge authority** between the North-American lexical field and Codrington’s Melanesian mana.

This is not a trivial rearrangement. It makes the packet read more smoothly as one transregional `Power / life / energy` argument.

---

## 4. The Codrington omission survives the rearrangement

The most diagnostic result is that Hardy’s rearrangement does **not** cause the Codrington source to be reopened.

The personal-origin clause omitted by Durkheim remains absent. Hardy preserves the shortened Swain/Durkheim excerpt boundary while changing the placement of Fletcher around it.

Therefore two levels of documentary inheritance must be separated:

### Local quotation object

`Codrington excerpt boundary + ellipsis`
→ HIGH FIDELITY inheritance.

### Surrounding proof packet

`order of Fletcher / Codrington / interpretive glosses`
→ RECOMPOSED.

This produces a more refined transmission model:

> **stable local excerpt + mobile packet architecture.**

Or:

> **quotation fidelity can coexist with argumentative recomposition.**

The omission is therefore still a positive marker of descent, but the relative position of neighboring authorities is not.

## 5. Fletcher’s proposition had an independent pre-Durkheim public life

A contemporary 1897 *Popular Science Monthly* notice of Fletcher’s AAAS paper summarizes the same central content independently of Durkheim:

- the Omaha totem’s efficacy rests on belief in continuity of life;
- visible/invisible, living/dead, and part/whole are linked through this continuity;
- the totem opens communication between the person and agencies of the environment.

The Smithsonian annual report bibliographically places Fletcher’s `The Import of the Totem` at pp.577–586; Smithsonian SOVA also records a 12-page 1897 reprint in the Alice C. Fletcher paper/reprint environment.

This is an important negative control. Fletcher’s `common/continuous life` proposition was not created by Durkheim and was publicly available before 1912.

Therefore Hardy’s use cannot be coded as `Fletcher content proves Durkheim descent` merely because the same proposition appears. The stronger evidence of Durkheim mediation is instead the **combination**:

- Hardy’s explicit declaration that his Durkheim extracts use Swain 1915;
- his framing of Fletcher as `also quoted by Durkheim`;
- the Durkheim-specific `source of energy` gloss around Fletcher;
- the immediate transition from Fletcher/wakan to Durkheim’s equivalence with mana;
- preservation of the distinctive Durkheim–Swain Codrington truncation.

This is exactly the kind of conjunctive fingerprint demanded by Batch93.

## 6. Fletcher is now a second same-source laboratory beside Codrington

The project already has direct primary control of Lovejoy 1906’s use of Fletcher (Batches77–78).

Lovejoy treats Fletcher as a valuable specialist source and extracts two especially important structures:

- animate and inanimate things are permeated by a common life;
- this life is communicable / transferable.

He does not simply inherit Fletcher’s whole interpretation. Where Fletcher narrows the operative principle toward `will-power`, Lovejoy treats that as too specific because the same efficacy is assigned to objects/natural forces. Fletcher is thus source-partitioned:

`field/use structure`
→ RETAIN;

`common life / transferability`
→ RETAIN;

`will-power narrowing`
→ CORRECT.

Durkheim uses the same Fletcher source differently:

`common life + continuity`
→ identifies the common principle with `wakan`
→ totem connects individual with a `source of energy`
→ supports equivalence with wider mana/orenda field and totemic principle.

Hardy then inherits Fletcher chiefly through Durkheim’s proof environment and gives it a new destination: evidence for a trans-cultural `Power` relevant to a proposed natural theology.

The resulting three-branch source laboratory is:

### Fletcher → Lovejoy 1906

`common life / communicability`
→ **primitive energetics / transferable efficacy**.

### Fletcher → Durkheim 1912

`common life / continuity`
→ **wakan as totemic principle / source of energy / comparative mana equivalence**.

### Fletcher-as-Durkheim/Swain → Hardy 1966

`common life / wakan / energy`
→ **anthropological evidence for a Power beyond the individual and for natural theology**.

This is not evidence of Lovejoy→Durkheim or Lovejoy→Hardy transmission. It is a controlled demonstration of **same-source functional divergence and later packet inheritance**.

## 7. Codrington and Fletcher show two different kinds of source transformation inside the same packet

The combined packet now contains two distinct source operations.

### Codrington

The source contains an adverse causal clause:

`personal beings originate/impart mana`.

Durkheim’s quotation omits it. The transformed excerpt then travels through Swain to Hardy.

Operation:

**SELECTIVE EXCERPT / DIRECTIONAL CONTRADICTION REMOVED**.

### Fletcher

The quoted sentence itself is not presently shown to be truncated in the same diagnostically adverse way. Instead, Durkheim gives it a strong interpretive placement:

`common/continuous life`
→ `wakan`
→ `source of energy`
→ comparative mana/totemic principle.

Operation:

**INTERPRETIVE REASSIGNMENT / FUNCTIONAL EMBEDDING**.

Hardy inherits both in one sequence:

- Codrington’s transformed local quotation object;
- Fletcher’s Durkheimian functional placement.

Thus a packet can transmit heterogeneous transformations simultaneously.

## 8. This upgrades `citation shadow` into `evidence-packet genealogy`

Batch99 established that an omission can travel.

Batch101 adds that the surrounding evidence architecture can be reorganized while the transformed quotation remains stable.

The object of reception is therefore larger than a citation but smaller than a whole theory. It can be modeled as:

`quotation object`
+
`source adjacency`
+
`interpretive gloss`
+
`proof function`
+
`ordering`.

Each component has a different stability profile.

### Current stability estimate

- author/title attribution: HIGH;
- local Codrington excerpt boundary: HIGH;
- omitted personal-origin proposition: HIGH negative persistence;
- Durkheim impersonality gloss: HIGH;
- Fletcher wording: HIGH;
- Fletcher/Codrington relative order: LOW / mobile;
- ultimate theoretical use: LOW / heavily transformable.

This suggests a general historiographic principle:

> **Reception should be reconstructed as descent among documentary components with unequal mutation rates.**

That is a stronger and more testable claim than `ideas change as they travel`.

## 9. Relationship to the Lovejoy project

The value of this case is not that Durkheim must secretly have read Lovejoy. No direct edge is claimed.

Its value is methodological and comparative.

Lovejoy 1906, Durkheim 1912, and later Hardy can all draw from overlapping ethnographic authorities while producing different analytical objects. The archive now lets us separate:

1. **shared source ecology** — Fletcher, Codrington, North-American power terms, mana;
2. **source transformation** — what each writer retains, omits, corrects, or reassigns;
3. **packet transmission** — when a later writer inherits an already transformed source object from an intermediate text;
4. **theoretical repurposing** — primitive philosophy, sociology of religion, natural theology.

Lovejoy’s distinctiveness therefore cannot be measured by shared names alone. It lies in the particular transformation he performs on the packet: proposition-relative grading, grammar/language as independent existence-control, causal-law assimilation, and the construction of `Primitive Energetics`.

Durkheim’s distinctiveness lies elsewhere: comparative reproof of an Australian totemic principle, selective use of Codrington, and social derivation of religious force.

Hardy’s distinctiveness lies in taking the Durkheim–Swain package as a historical evidentiary mine for the reality/experience of `Power` while openly rejecting some early evolutionist assumptions.

## 10. Claims to retain / withhold

### RETAIN

- Hardy explicitly says his Durkheim quotations are from Swain 1915.
- Hardy explicitly presents Fletcher as quoted by Durkheim.
- Hardy reproduces the Fletcher common-life passage and Durkheim’s source-of-energy gloss.
- Hardy reproduces the Durkheim–Swain shortened Codrington excerpt with the personal-origin clause absent.
- Hardy reverses the local Durkheim order of Fletcher and Codrington, placing Fletcher before Codrington.
- The inherited object is therefore best described as **recomposed evidence-packet descent**, not verbatim sequence copying.
- Fletcher’s common/continuous-life proposition circulated independently before Durkheim, so wording alone is not a unique Durkheim fingerprint.

### WITHHOLD

- Do not claim Hardy never read Fletcher or Codrington directly.
- Do not claim Durkheim’s reordering/ellipsis proves conscious suppression or intent.
- Do not infer Lovejoy→Durkheim or Lovejoy→Hardy transmission from shared Fletcher/Codrington sources.
- Do not treat exact source order as necessary evidence of packet descent.

## 11. High-value next tests

1. Search 1915–1966 English-language comparative religion / sociology texts for the **joint** sequence `Fletcher common life + source of energy + Codrington shortened quotation`, rather than for either quotation alone.
2. Test whether later authors preserve the Codrington ellipsis but omit Fletcher, or preserve Fletcher but restore Codrington’s personal-origin clause; this will map packet fission.
3. Collate Fletcher 1897 facsimile pp.577–586 against Lovejoy 1906 and Durkheim 1912 sentence by sentence. The contemporary 1897 summary controls the main proposition, but facsimile-level comparison would reveal whether Durkheim’s Fletcher quotation itself is selective in a propositionally important way.
4. Search later editions/reprints of Swain to determine whether typographic ellipsis shape remains stable; this could distinguish inheritance through a particular edition family from generic reuse of Durkheim.
5. Build an authority-transformation graph where each edge records not only citation but `excerpt boundary`, `omitted proposition`, `interpretive gloss`, `source order`, and `theoretical destination`.

## Compact formulation

> **Batch99 showed that omissions travel. Batch101 shows that they can travel inside a packet whose surrounding authorities are reordered. The stable documentary unit is therefore not a fixed paragraph or sequence but a set of source fragments with unequal mutation rates: some quotation boundaries persist, some glosses persist, while adjacency and theoretical function remain mobile.**
