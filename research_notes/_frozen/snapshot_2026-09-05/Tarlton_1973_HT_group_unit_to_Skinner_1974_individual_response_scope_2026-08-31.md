# Tarlton 1973 H&T group-unit -> Skinner 1974 PT individual-response scope

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **H&T OFFICIAL ABSTRACT DIRECT / TARLTON P307-308 PAGE-SPECIFIC SECONDARY QUOTATION / SKINNER 1974 SCOPE NOTE DIRECT / CROSS-CARRIER UNIT NARROWING LOCKED**

## Core result

Charles D. Tarlton's 1973 *History and Theory* article did not merely criticize three scholars separately. It explicitly constructed **Pocock + Skinner + Dunn** as a plural methodological object.

The official *History and Theory* Volume 12 summary says:

- Pocock, Skinner and Dunn try to introduce historicity into the study of political thought;
- they share a relational conception of meaning;
- they construct cognitive contexts within which events/statements are to be fitted;
- Tarlton criticizes the structural/contextual assumptions of this approach.

A page-specific later reconstruction quoting Tarlton pp.307–308 reports that Tarlton described their recent statements as constituting a `distinctly recognizable approach` and the three scholars as a `nucleus` around which a revisionism in the study of political thought might develop.

Thus the critic-side analytic unit is securely:

`POCOCK + SKINNER + DUNN AS EMERGENT REVISIONIST / METHODOLOGICAL CLUSTER`.

Skinner's 1974 *Political Theory* response then performs an explicit unit narrowing. Note 1 acknowledges that Tarlton also addresses Dunn and Pocock but says Skinner confines his response to the sections about **his own work**.

Therefore the cross-carrier move is:

`H&T 1973 GROUP / PROGRAMME UNIT`
->
`PT 1974 INDIVIDUAL-WORK RESPONSE UNIT`.

Core rule:

`CRITIC-CONSTRUCTED SCHOOL / CLUSTER UNIT`
!= `RESPONDENT-ACCEPTED RESPONSE UNIT`.

## I. Direct H&T abstract controls the plural target

Official *History and Theory*, Volume 12 (1973), entry for:

Charles D. Tarlton, `Historicity, Meaning, and Revisionism in the Study of Political Thought`, H&T 12.3, 307–328.

The official summary directly names:

- J. G. A. Pocock;
- Quentin Skinner;
- John Dunn.

It treats them together as scholars attempting to introduce historicity into political-thought study through relational meaning/context reconstruction.

This is sufficient direct evidence that the article's target is plural and programmatic rather than Skinner-only.

Direct source ceiling:

- names and group-level characterization are direct;
- exact wording of every claim inside pp.307–328 remains unavailable in the current public carrier.

## II. Page-specific quotation shows active group construction

Samuel James's later archival/intellectual-history reconstruction quotes Tarlton pp.307–308.

Reported Tarlton language:

- the recent statements of Pocock, Skinner and Dunn constitute a `distinctly recognizable approach to the problem of intellectual history`;
- together they form `a nucleus` around which at least the style and tone of a revisionism in political-thought study seemed likely to grow.

James also reports Tarlton's characterization of shared features:

- dissatisfaction with existing political-thought historiography;
- emphasis on historicity;
- contextual reconstruction as a strategy for constraining plausible interpretation.

Evidence status:

`PAGE-SPECIFIC SECONDARY QUOTATION OF PRIMARY`.

Do not upgrade this to direct full-text inspection.

Nevertheless it resolves an important question: Tarlton was **constructing a collective methodological unit**, not merely mentioning three independent authors in one article.

## III. Tarlton's unit is historically analytical, not necessarily an already existing corporate school

Tarlton's clustering operation itself must be distinguished from institutional reality.

He identifies similarities and predicts a possible revisionist nucleus.

That establishes:

`CRITICALLY CONSTRUCTED METHOD CLUSTER`.

It does not by itself establish:

- a self-declared `Cambridge School`;
- complete methodological homogeneity;
- a formal organization;
- identical objects/proof burdens among Pocock, Dunn and Skinner;
- independent origin of their shared features;
- a causal genealogy among them.

Thus:

`ANALYST / CRITIC CLUSTERING`
!= `ACTOR SELF-IDENTIFICATION`
!= `INSTITUTIONAL SCHOOL`.

This is especially important because existing correspondence evidence directly shows both overlap and differentiation among the actors.

---

## IV. Skinner 1974 refuses the plural unit for purposes of his response

Skinner 1974 note 1 directly states that Tarlton's critique also concerns the methodological writings of Dunn and Pocock, but Skinner confines his remarks exclusively to the parts about his own work.

Therefore Skinner does not need to deny the existence of overlap in order to reject the larger unit as the object of **his response**.

The operation is:

`GROUP-TARGET CRITIQUE`
-> `INDIVIDUAL SELF-SCOPED REPLY`.

Proof burden:

- Skinner is responsible for defending his own claims;
- he does not thereby undertake to defend the entire Pocock/Dunn/Skinner cluster as Tarlton has constructed it.

Thus:

`RESPONSE SCOPE`
!= `HISTORICAL NETWORK / AFFINITY SCOPE`.

This directly supports the standing anti-leakage rule:

`A != B does not establish independence`.

Skinner can narrow the response unit while still acknowledging methodological relatedness elsewhere.

`EMERGENT REVISIONIST CLUSTER = POCOCK + SKINNER + DUNN`.

### PT 1974

Skinner response:

`SKINNER'S OWN WORK / GENERAL APPROACH`.

The controversy is historically connected, but the unit is no longer identical.

Therefore:

`CROSS-CARRIER CONTROVERSY CONTINUITY`
!= `STABLE CONTROVERSY UNIT`.

A carrier move can be accompanied by an actor's explicit **re-specification of what is answerable**.

This is a stronger relation than generic `H&T debate moved to PT`.

## VI. Comparison with Parekh–Berki 1976

Parekh–Berki's 1976 communication supplies a third unit logic:

`SKINNER AS HISTORICAL INDIVIDUAL / OEUVRE`
!= `1969 ARTICLE AS REPRESENTATIVE METHOD POSITION`.

Together:

- Tarlton constructs a **school/cluster** unit;
- Skinner narrows to **individual work/general approach**;
- Parekh–Berki defend a **single representative article** for a philosophical method question.

This gives a nested hierarchy:

`GROUP / SCHOOL`
-> `ACTOR / OWN WORK`
-> `REPRESENTATIVE ARTICLE`
-> potentially `CASE / TEXT EXAMPLE`.

No level can automatically stand in for another.

Compact rule:

> **CONTROVERSY PARTICIPANTS MAY DISAGREE NOT ONLY ABOUT ANSWERS BUT ABOUT THE CORRECT SIZE OF THE OBJECT THAT IS ANSWERABLE.**

## VII. Relation to later Cambridge-School canon

Later histories frequently call Pocock/Skinner/Dunn a Cambridge School.

Tarlton 1973 is useful precisely because it catches an early moment of **external aggregation**.

It should not be read teleologically as proof that the later school label was already settled.

Safe sequence:

`EARLY EXTERNAL METHOD CLUSTERING`
-> later `SCHOOL / CANON LABELS`.

Hold:

`TARLTON CAUSED CAMBRIDGE SCHOOL CANON`.

The later label has many carriers and actors.
