## Core decision

The JHI Blog should remain primarily a guided tour of notebook 004, with notebook 005 and later Lovejoy functioning only as bounded controls. The repository now contains enough later Lovejoy, `primitive`, Brinton, Boas, reception, and comparative-method material to build a much larger intellectual history, but that material should function chiefly as calibration. The public essay gains more by showing readers **how the notebook can and cannot be read, and how specific source problems are transformed between working notes and print**, than by extending the number of later endpoints.

v3.7 therefore promotes Quellenkritik from backstage governance to visible prose without turning source criticism into the essay's independent thesis.

## 1. What the reader must learn before interpreting notebook 004

The notebook is not a continuous authored exposition. It mixes:

- copied / paraphrased source propositions;
- translations and lexical apparatus;
- bibliographic cues;
- questions and objections;
- local authorial supplements;
- classificatory diagrams and recompositions.

Public rule:

> **Lovejoy handwriting is a carrier, not automatic proposition authorship.**

The article should therefore teach a source-critical reading sequence:

`identify source packet`
→ `return source-owned proposition to source`
→ `locate explicit interruption / connective / reassignment`
→ `state only the Lovejoy-local residual`.

This sequence is illustrated rather than named as a formal method.

## 2. 004 source criticism now carries argumentative weight

The Warren example remains useful precisely because Warren owns the larger dependent-origination relation grammar and Lovejoy's contribution is a local refusal of one identity claim. The current body now makes that asymmetry explicit: Warren already distinguishes antecedence, cause/effect, identity, interdependence, and other relation types; Lovejoy turns that existing rule back on Warren's `taṇhā = upādāna` assignment.

The Senart example becomes stronger after source recut. Senart owns the compositeness/duplication problem, while the explicit response seam (`ordre plus ou moins accidentel` -> `discoverable logical system`) survives as Lovejoy-local. Oldenberg's 1897 priority for the basic anti-Senart *upādāna ≠ upādānakkhandha* semantic objection is now visible in the body, preventing that constituent move from becoming a Lovejoy originality claim.

The p.123 `temporally (?) ... logically` locus remains especially valuable because source recovery lowers originality elsewhere but strengthens this recomposition: source-supplied constituents remain separable from the locally written relation between temporal antecedence and logical subdivision.

Two additional source-transformation cases are now visible rather than left in the apparatus:

- **Hardy -> notebook -> 1898:** Hardy supplies a two-factor rebirth architecture; Lovejoy recasts it as `fact` versus `manner`, then publishes `fact of rebirth` versus `particular condition of rebirth` and gives that allocation a historical-functional rationale.
- **Oldenberg / lexical fork -> notebook -> 1898:** Oldenberg supplies the main ethical-residue direction for *upādisesa*, while Childers/Rhys Davids and Müller/Dahlmann preserve competing word histories; Lovejoy lets etymology remain underdetermined and moves semantic adjudication toward usage while retaining resistant passages.

Add concrete corrections to prevent the notebook guide from appearing selectively source-critical:

- Rhys Davids/Warren/Childers/Oldenberg jointly own much of the dependent-origination apparatus;
- the Four Truths / nidāna parallel closes upstream to *Vinaya Texts*;
- khandha numerics and a continuity model are largely Childers-owned.

The point is not to inventory every source, but to let the reader see that source recovery changes what counts as Lovejoy evidence.

## 3. Witness criticism: notebook, AOS record, print are distinct states

Retain the directly controlled title path:

`10 Apr 1897 — Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`
→ `22–24 Apr — No. 30 handled by title; exact meeting title unresolved`
→ `June 1897 Proceedings p.389 — upādānam / upādāna-kkhandhā`
→ `1898 — upādāna / upādisesa`.

Frame it explicitly as witness criticism:

- public-object reallocation is direct;
- one unchanged manuscript under several titles is unproved;
- the June printed title must not be back-dated automatically to the April meeting;
- *Milindapañha*, *upādānakkhandha*, and *upādisesa* change documentary roles across the surviving carriers.

This strengthens the notebook→print argument without overclaiming manuscript descent or a recovered revision mechanism.

## 4. 005 requires material and source criticism together

Notebook 005 remains useful because scan pages can contain multiple physical/chronological witnesses.

Publicly usable controls:

- strong Paris 1898–99 core does not date the whole notebook;
- later reuse is directly demonstrated;
- host-page prose and inserted Marillier sheets are distinct witnesses;
- insertion proves accretion, not insertion date;
- ink/page order/proximity do not independently prove composition chronology;
- printed source collation may identify packet boundaries or names but cannot supply illegible manuscript wording.

Source-first procedure:

`identify source/seminar packet`
→ `exhaust source-owned theory/case description`
→ `locate Lovejoy ranking / reclassification / mechanism-bearer mapping / genealogy test / HOLD`.

This yields a better public claim than `Lovejoy develops a theory of primitive religion`.

## 6. Later publications are exits, not organizing sections

The current public body retains only the 1902 comparison/transmission case as a short exit. It does not retain the 1906 `primitive`, 1906 political/developmental, or *Great Chain* material.

- 1902: comparison != transmission; retained as one scale-control paragraph.
- 1906 and later canonical works: backstage controls only for the present short piece.
- Brinton and Boas: frozen backstage controls only.

The remaining 1902 paragraph must not make the Buddhist notebook a blueprint for a mature method. It is a later instance in which a similar burden of proof reappears at larger scale.

## 7. Closing historiographic gain

The strongest ending remains archival rather than programmatic:

> a notebook page is not yet a proposition; handwriting is not yet authorship; source match is not yet assent; publication is not simply a cleaned notebook.

Once those distinctions are maintained, the early Buddhist work can be historicized without either inflating Lovejoy's originality or dissolving him into his sources. The current ending therefore keeps the claim first-order: concrete disputes redistribute temporal, logical, causal, lexical, semantic, and historical burdens, and publication selects among those working relations unevenly.
