# Lovejoy, `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism` — 1933→1948 version delta

Date: 2026-08-31  
Status: **DIRECT 1933 PAGE-IMAGE CONTROL + 1948 REVISION CONTROL THROUGH MOUTON / CAUSAL-WEIGHT SHIFT SECURE / DIRECT 1948 PAGE IMAGE STILL DESIRABLE**

## Core result

The revision of `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism` between its 1933 JEGP publication and the revised/expanded 1948 version in *Essays in the History of Ideas* supplies unusually strong actor-level evidence that Lovejoy could revise not merely details but the **distribution of historical agency and causal weight** when new source evidence changed the genealogy.

The safe transformation is:

`GREAT AUTHOR AS ORIGIN`
→ `EARLIER REPORTING / MISSIONARY SOURCE INFRASTRUCTURE`
→ `TRANSLATION / CIRCULATION`
→ `CANONICAL AUTHOR AS LATER UPTAKE`.

This is a history-of-ideas version change in the direction of thicker mediation, not a claim that Lovejoy became a social historian in a later technical sense.

---

## I. 1933: Montaigne receives origin-level causal weight

Primary:

Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism`, *Journal of English and Germanic Philology* 32.1 (Jan. 1933), 1–20.

JSTOR issue/record:

- https://www.jstor.org/stable/i27703720
- article stable: `27703723`.

A modern JHIEA Chinese translation of the 1933 article has been page-image checked on 2026-08-31. On translated p.417, corresponding to the opening historical framing, Lovejoy describes the enormous European reputation of Chinese civilization and gives Montaigne the decisive initiatory role: Montaigne is said to have begun the fashion only seven years after the establishment of the first Jesuit mission at Peking in 1581.

The operative causal picture is therefore approximately:

`PEKING JESUIT MISSION (1581)`
→ `MONTAIGNE INITIATES EUROPEAN CHINA FASHION`
→ `LONG EUROPEAN REPUTATIONAL / AESTHETIC HISTORY`.

This does not mean the 1933 article ignores intermediaries elsewhere. The precise point is narrower: in this opening origin sentence, the canonical European writer carries the initiating verb.

## II. 1948: Lovejoy removes the Montaigne-origin sentence

The revised and expanded version appears in:

Arthur O. Lovejoy, *Essays in the History of Ideas* (Baltimore: Johns Hopkins Press, 1948), chapter 7, `The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism`, pp.99–135.

Current direct bibliographic controls:

- Johns Hopkins University Press current edition TOC;
- bibliographic records explicitly identify the 1948 essay as revised and expanded from 1933.

Nico Mouton, `An Apologia for Arthur Lovejoy's Long-Range Approach to the History of Ideas`, *History and Theory* 62.2 (2023), directly collates the 1933 and 1948 formulations.

His comparison shows that the 1948 revision **replaces** the 1933 statement that Montaigne `began the fashion` with an earlier and more distributed prehistory:

- reports by voyagers and missionaries who had visited China already circulated **before** the Jesuit mission in Peking;
- those reports emphasized Chinese government and administration of justice;
- by around 1590, the superiority of the Chinese political system was, in Lovejoy's revised judgment, already a commonplace;
- a missionary report was rapidly translated into the principal European languages;
- Montaigne then read that book between 1588 and 1592 and prepared a paragraph inserted in the posthumous 1595 edition of the *Essais*.

Mouton's interpretation is correct at the minimum level required here: the revision decreases Montaigne's origin-level role and increases the role of anonymous voyager/missionary reports and their circulation.

## III. What exactly changed

### 1933 weighting

`KNOWN EUROPEAN AUTHOR`
→ initiates fashion.

### 1948 weighting

`EARLY VOYAGERS / MISSIONARIES`
→ reports / descriptions
→ translation into major European languages
→ already distributed political image of China
→ Montaigne receives / reuses material
→ later European intellectual/aesthetic consequences.

The change is therefore not merely:

`Montaigne date corrected`.

It changes the **historical relation assigned to Montaigne**:

`ORIGINATOR`
→ `PROMINENT RECIPIENT / AMPLIFIER WITHIN AN ALREADY ACTIVE SOURCE NETWORK`.

And it changes the status of apparently minor source carriers:

`BACKGROUND INFORMATION`
→ `CAUSALLY LOAD-BEARING MEDIATION`.

## IV. Relation to Lovejoy's own mature methodological programme

This version delta is especially important because it supplies practice-level evidence for claims Lovejoy made programmatically about history of ideas:

- ideas and assumptions move across national, linguistic and disciplinary boundaries;
- a historian must follow antecedents and consequents beyond conventional departmental jurisdictions;
- reconstructed relations remain hypotheses requiring historical evidence;
- mature methodological generalizations, by Lovejoy's own 1948 retrospective, grew out of special inquiries rather than preceding most of them.

The Chinese-origin revision shows one concrete consequence of those commitments:

> **when the evidentiary route changes, the historian must be willing to redistribute causal agency across source carriers, intermediaries, translations, and canonical actors.**

This is stronger than treating `migration` as a metaphor for free-floating ideas.

## V. Comparison with 1898 and 1906/1914: problem continuity, not method identity

### 1898 Buddhist work

Lovejoy reconstructs relations internal to Buddhist conceptual/textual materials and keeps distinct:

- classificatory inclusion;
- temporal antecedence;
- semantic identity;
- causal sequence;
- textual/source derivation.

### 1906/1914 Primitive Energetics

Lovejoy separates:

- non-animistic content/type;
- pre-animistic historical priority;
- cross-cultural aggregation;
- representativeness;
- nominal definition vs analytical description.

### 1933→1948 China revision

Lovejoy revises:

- origin;
- mediation;
- circulation;
- causal weight;
- canonical uptake.

The defensible longitudinal proposition is:

> **Across very different objects, Lovejoy repeatedly confronts the problem of what evidence warrants moving from resemblance or presence to a stronger historical relation such as priority, derivation, source, transmission or causation.**

Do not call this one unchanged `method` or infer direct transfer from Buddhist philology to mature history of ideas.

## VI. Asian material changes historical role across Lovejoy's career

This version delta strengthens the `role conversion` model already recorded in `JHI_Asia_role_jurisdiction_matrix_1898_1955.tsv`.

### 1898 India/Buddhism

Asian material = **object whose internal categories/history must be reconstructed**.

### 1933/1948 China

Asian material = **source/antecedent entering a European genealogy through reports, translations and reception**.

### 1934/35 India

Asian material = **supplementary comparative regional corpus** inside the documentary history of primitivism.

These are not interchangeable modes of inclusion.

The proposed JHI article should therefore ask not simply whether Asia was `included` or `excluded`, but:

> **What historical role was Asian material allowed to perform inside the emerging history of ideas, and what source/mediation rules made that role possible?**

## VII. Article payoff

This finding weakens two familiar caricatures at once.

### Caricature 1

`Lovejoy tracks ideas only through canonical great thinkers.`

The 1948 revision makes anonymous voyage/missionary reports and translation infrastructure causally important before Montaigne.

### Caricature 2

`Long-range history of ideas treats influence as unexamined thematic resemblance.`

Here Lovejoy explicitly revises a source genealogy and the causal position of an actor when a different mediation route becomes evidentially available.

Neither point proves that Lovejoy always handled mediation well. The significance is that the methodology permits, and this case practices, source-sensitive redistribution of causal weight.
