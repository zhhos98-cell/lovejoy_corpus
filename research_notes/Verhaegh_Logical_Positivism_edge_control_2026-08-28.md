## Why this source matters

Sander Verhaegh's *Logical Positivism: An American History* (Oxford University Press, 2026) should be used in the Lovejoy project primarily as an **external field-control and boundary map**, not as a source for reclassifying Lovejoy into the later analytic-philosophy canon.

The working value of the book is precisely Lovejoy's likely **edge position** relative to the networks Verhaegh reconstructs.

### Governing anti-absorption rule

> **Do not ask whether Lovejoy can be assimilated to analytic philosophy. Ask where, when, and in what argumentative capacity he appears at the edges of the American logical/scientific-philosophy networks that later historiography consolidates into analytic philosophy or logical positivism.**

The edge is evidentiary.

A marginal, adversarial, intermittent, or one-way appearance may be more useful than canonical membership because it can show:

- shared argumentative venues without shared disciplinary identity;
- shared technical vocabulary without school affiliation;
- direct controversy across later historiographical boundaries;
- common institutional or pedagogical infrastructure without a single genealogy;
- points where later disciplinary narratives select some actors as ancestors and leave others outside.

This source must therefore be used to resist retrospective capture, not to intensify it.

## Coding scheme for full-text reading

When the full text is available, code every Lovejoy-relevant occurrence or near-occurrence under the following categories:

1. **central/member** — actor is explicitly presented as belonging to the reconstructed network;
2. **interlocutor** — actor appears in direct exchange, criticism, review, reply, correspondence, conference, or controversy;
3. **boundary critic** — actor shares a problem-space or technical vocabulary while opposing the network's claims or methods;
4. **infrastructural contact** — shared department, university, journal, society, course ecology, appointment chain, conference, editor, or funding/institutional setting;
5. **conceptual adjacency** — comparable analytical operation or vocabulary appears without demonstrated contact or genealogy;
6. **retrospective omission** — Lovejoy is absent from a reconstructed site where independent primary evidence shows he was present or relevant;
7. **historiographical sorting event** — a moment where later labels such as `analytic`, `scientific philosophy`, `logical positivism`, `realism`, or `pragmatism` redistribute earlier actors in ways that may obscure actual contemporaneous interaction.

These are not ranks of importance. A category-2, 3, 4, or 6 case may be more probative for the Lovejoy project than category 1.
