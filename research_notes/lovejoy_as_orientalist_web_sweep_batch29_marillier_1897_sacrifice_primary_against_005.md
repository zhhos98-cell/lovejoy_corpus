# Batch 29 — Direct Marillier primary: sacrifice, blood, magic, and the critique of a single sacramental origin

Date: 2026-08-18
Status: synced

## Research move

The high-priority source behind N. W. Thomas's 1911 citation has now been identified and its primary OCR captured. It is Léon Marillier's long two-part article:

> **`La place du totémisme dans l'évolution religieuse à propos d'un livre récent`**, *Revue de l'histoire des religions* **36 (1897), 208–253 and 321–369** (continuation follows in vol. 37, 1898).

JSTOR's volume table independently confirms the first two instalments and exact page ranges. A Gallica-derived public-domain OCR reproduction exposes the full 1897 article text and lets us inspect the passages on Robertson Smith, sacrificial communion, blood, magic and alternative origins directly.

This is a major upgrade for the 005 context. We no longer depend on Thomas's 1911 summary for the central mechanism claims.

## 1. Thomas's `RHR xxxvi. 243` citation is this article

Thomas's 1911 Britannica `Sacrifice` cites `L. Marillier (Rev. de l'hist. des religions, xxxvi. 243)` while attacking Robertson Smith's theory of totemic communion sacrifice.

The RHR 36 contents identify Marillier's article as pp.208–253. The primary text around the relevant discussion makes the argument Thomas summarizes:

- if the god is naturally already a member/relative of the clan, a rite is not needed to maintain a bond that cannot easily be broken;
- if the union is instead produced by common food/blood rites, its specifically **totemic** character becomes doubtful;
- the historical existence of sacramental communion does not prove it was the universal or original form of sacrifice.

Marillier explicitly says Robertson Smith's tightly systematic construction seems to make the mobile variety of facts submit to the system rather than allowing the theory to model itself on their complexity.

That methodological sentence is directly relevant to 005's repeated refusal to force heterogeneous rites into one evolutionary genealogy.

## 2. Marillier directly rejects a universal sacramental/totemic stage

After reconstructing Robertson Smith's position, Marillier says that not all religions passed through the proposed stage and that many Semitic rites cannot securely be given a totemic origin.

The primary passage is particularly useful because its logic is evidentiary:

- the existence of a sacrificial common meal does not by itself demonstrate totemism;
- the fact that a rite occurs among a totemic people does not establish that the rite itself is of totemic origin;
- surviving or later cult forms may combine elements of different historical origins.

This maps tightly onto 005's central operation in the sacrifice section: **same outward form ≠ same causal or historical mechanism**.

## 3. Blood and sacrificial efficacy are already separated from a simple communion theory

Marillier considers several alternative mechanisms for the blood rite.

He allows that in cases involving a sacred/divine victim, the victim may be killed to renew the god's life. More importantly, he proposes that the **blood of a divine victim may be needed to bring the god into the sacred altar-stone and into contact with worshippers**, who can then obtain the fulfilment of their wishes.

He immediately adds that eating the victim's flesh can be understood through the widespread belief that consuming a being transfers its gifts/qualities. A person eating the sacred animal privately could acquire **excessive power** dangerous to others.

Only after these beliefs are in place does Marillier say the notion of a mystical unity sealed by sacrifice and ritual meal could have developed. In his words, this would be a **point of arrival, not a point of departure**.

This is extremely close to the mechanism structure of Lovejoy 005:

- blood/body matter carries power;
- efficacy can be transferred by contact/consumption;
- sacramental communion can be a later interpretation of a mechanism that was not originally communion;
- a single theory cannot be inferred backward from the later ritual form.

## 4. Direct primary passage: sacrifice resembles magic

In the continuation of the article Marillier turns explicitly to Jevons's opposition between religion and magic.

He says the `sacrifice d'union` / totemic sacrifice that Jevons presents as the first religious act bears a **striking resemblance to practices whose magical character is undeniable**. His key example is the sprinkling of blood that calls the god into the altar-stone; he compares it to the evocation of a spirit by fumigations, mysterious words or gestures.

Marillier then rejects a sharp boundary between religious rites and magical practices. Charms and shamanic procedures can contain conciliatory elements, while religious ceremonies retain traces of close similarity to rain-making, healing and fertility magic.

This is a direct French primary counterpart to 005 PDF p.64 and p.105:

- magical sacrifice can act through a mechanism rather than through ordinary persuasion of a god;
- later ritual interpretation does not erase earlier/direct efficacy;
- `quasi-mechanical and magical rather than sacramental` is not an isolated Lovejoy phrase but sits inside a French controversy over whether sacrifice and magic are sharply separable.

## 5. Marillier's own 1897 description of magic is mechanism-sensitive

The same primary text reconstructs the magic/religion distinction in causal terms. Marillier describes magic as acting on an animating soul/spirit through charms, gestures and words, constraining rather than persuading. He compares the magician to an engineer or physician who uses appropriate means rather than petition.

But he immediately warns that no sharp line separates magical and religious rites.

This gives a useful chronology for Lovejoy's later move. Marillier in 1897 still tends to formulate magic through action on spirits/souls. Lovejoy 005, especially the fetish/charm passages and `quasi-mechanical` language, increasingly isolates cases where efficacy can be **intrinsic, transferable or direct**, and the 1906 paper then makes impersonal efficacy more fundamental than personality.

That difference should be preserved. The genealogy is not simple repetition:

> **Marillier: mechanism-sensitive critique inside an animistic/psychological framework → Lovejoy: extraction of a more impersonal causal category from the same comparative field.**

## 6. The primary article also contains the anti-evolutionary control visible in 005

Marillier rejects the claim that totemism is a necessary universal stage. He points to peoples with clan organization and supernatural animal beliefs but no secure totemism, and emphasizes that ancestor cult, agrarian cults, magic and other practices coexist with or survive independently of totemism.

He also criticizes the temptation to assign a totemic origin to a rite merely because it is found among peoples who possess totemic organization.

This has a direct 005 analogue in Lovejoy's later notes on:

- human vs animal sacrifice not being a simple evolutionary index;
- agriculture sometimes preceding animal domestication;
- rituals preserving combined/reinterpreted motives;
- first-fruit, funerary, magical, propitiatory and sacramental rites requiring separate historical classification.

## 8. Important difference from Thomas's 1911 compressed summary

Thomas's Britannica summary says Marillier regarded sacrifice as originally essentially magical, with blood releasing a magical force that bends gods to human will and the gift theory arising later with the cult of the dead.

The 1897 primary text recovered here is more complicated. It certainly:

- treats union-sacrifice as closely resembling magic;
- treats blood as an efficacious medium;
- allows transfer of supernatural qualities;
- rejects the communion/totemic form as universal origin;
- keeps several possible mechanisms in play.

But this recovery does **not yet justify reducing Marillier's whole 1897 position to a single `sacrifice was originally magical` formula**. Thomas/Warfield may be condensing the larger 1897–98 sequence, including later instalments. The remaining vol.37 continuation should therefore be checked before turning the relay into Marillier's exact final theory.
