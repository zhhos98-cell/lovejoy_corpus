# JHI 1987: *Great Chain* after fifty years — programme, performance, and comparative method reassessment

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: **DIRECT JHI ISSUE CONTROL / RICHTER SEARCHABLE ARTICLE CARRIER / OTHER CLUSTER ARTICLES PARTLY SECONDARY-CONTROLLED**

## Core result

JHI 48.2 (Apr.–Jun. 1987) contains a concentrated five-article reassessment of Arthur O. Lovejoy and *The Great Chain of Being*, pp.187–263:

1. Daniel J. Wilson, `Lovejoy's The Great Chain of Being after Fifty Years`, 187–206;
2. Gladys Gordon-Bournique, `A. O. Lovejoy and the "History of Ideas"`, 207–210;
3. Edward P. Mahoney, `Lovejoy and the Hierarchy of Being`, 211–230;
4. Francis Oakley, `Lovejoy's Unexplored Option`, 231–245;
5. Melvin Richter, `Begriffsgeschichte and the History of Ideas`, 247–263.

This cluster should be treated as a **methodological reassessment node**, not a commemorative celebration.

The most important direct result comes from Richter. In comparing German *Begriffsgeschichte* with American history of ideas, he says both should be reconstructed from:

`FOUNDERS' PROGRAMMES`
+
`ACTUAL PERFORMANCES BY FOUNDERS AND DISCIPLES`.

That distinction is almost exactly the firewall already governing this repository:

`PROGRAMME != PRACTICE`.

Richter further insists that the same word appearing across time says nothing by itself about sameness of meaning; diachronic analysis must reconstruct successive meanings and dislocations. He therefore concludes that a notion of an **unchanging unit-idea** cannot be adapted straightforwardly to strict historical semantics, and explicitly notes Skinner 1969 as making a similar case against Lovejoy's use of unit-ideas.

Thus by 1987 JHI was not merely living after the contextualist critique. It was **comparatively decomposing its own founding method**, placing Lovejoy beside conceptual history and Skinner, while asking which procedures remained usable.

## II. Richter 1987 makes `programme vs performance` explicit

Richter refuses to identify either *Begriffsgeschichte* or history of ideas simply with doctrinal manifestos.

His comparative rule is:

> methods should be reconstructed both from the programmes of their founders and from actual scholarly performances by founders and disciples.

This is crucial because many later attacks on Lovejoy take the opening analytic-chemistry metaphor or the formal definition of unit-ideas as exhaustive of what historical work in the Lovejoy/JHI ecology actually did.

Richter's rule instead requires at least two levels:

`DECLARED METHOD`
!=
`EXECUTED METHOD`.

The present repository has independently arrived at the same distinction from Lovejoy's Buddhist philology, primitive-religion work, Spitzer exchange, Mandelbaum/Pearce debates, Wiener, and the later JHI execution census.

The 1987 source therefore supplies actor-level historiographic warrant for using that distinction rather than imposing it retrospectively.

---

## III. Same word != same meaning

Richter's strongest conceptual-history objection concerns diachrony.

He argues that the continued use of the same word across periods tells the historian nothing by itself about continuity of meaning.

Historical semantics requires reconstruction of:

- what a term/concept meant at a given moment;
- what it did in a particular argumentative setting;
- its illocutionary force;
- subsequent changes and semantic dislocations.

Hence:

`LEXICAL IDENTITY ACROSS TIME`
!=
`SEMANTIC IDENTITY ACROSS TIME`.

This is directly relevant to the project's broader relation-specific proof problem.

It is another instance of:

`SURFACE SIMILARITY`
!=
`HISTORICAL CONTINUITY`.

## IV. Richter places Skinner and Lovejoy in the same comparative methodological field

Richter says a notion of an unchanging unit-idea cannot be reconciled with strict historical semantics and notes that Quentin Skinner made a similar case against Lovejoy in `Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas` (1969).

This matters because Skinner is no longer positioned simply as an external destroyer of JHI's founding method.

Inside JHI 1987:

`LOVEJOY`
+
`SKINNER`
+
`KOSELLECK / BEGRIFFSGESCHICHTE`

are made comparable methodological resources/problems.

Richter's article then asks what conceptual history can offer intellectual historians rather than announcing one school's total victory.

This is another institutional mechanism by which critique becomes incorporation.

---

## V. Richter also refuses a simple concepts-vs-ideas ontology

Richter says there is little point in stipulatively distinguishing `concept` from `idea`, since the terms can be synonyms and acquire determinate meaning only inside theories and usages.

The important distinction lies in **procedures of historical inquiry**, not in pretending that `concepts` and `ideas` are naturally different kinds of entity.

He therefore compares:

- units of analysis;
- semantic procedures;
- synchronic and diachronic reconstruction;
- language/context;
- actual research practices.

This is another useful firewall:

`NEW LABEL`
!=
`NEW OBJECT ONTOLOGY`.

Field labels may encode different methodological programmes without referring to ontologically distinct historical things.

## VI. Wilson 1987: criticism survived by modifying how unit-ideas are read

Direct full article text for Wilson is not yet locked in this pass, but multiple high-grade later controls summarize his 1987 retrospective.

Wilson surveys major criticisms of Lovejoy from the 1940s through the 1980s, including methodological and hermeneutic/deconstructionist challenges.

Later discussions report that he ultimately argues *The Great Chain of Being* withstood much of this criticism because Lovejoy's actual practice was more flexible and dynamic than the rigid definition of unit-ideas at the book's opening might suggest.

This should remain a secondary-control proposition until Wilson pp.187–206 are directly read.

If confirmed, it will reinforce Richter's programme/performance distinction from another article in the same cluster:

`RIGID PROGRAMMATIC FORMULATION`
vs
`MORE FLEXIBLE EXECUTION`.

## VII. Oakley 1987 supplies an internal revision rather than simple rejection

Francis Oakley's `Lovejoy's Unexplored Option` is explicitly framed as an alternative possibility within or adjacent to Lovejoy's own conceptual architecture.

Richter's conclusion refers appreciatively to Oakley's attempt to recover a path that Lovejoy did not fully pursue.

The key point at current evidence level is institutional:

JHI's fifty-year reassessment includes not only criticism of Lovejoy but attempts to identify **unused or revisable possibilities inside his programme**.

That differs structurally from a replacement narrative.

### 1988

Tully, *Meaning and Context*, packages Skinner + selected critics.

### 1990

Kelley, `What is Happening to the History of Ideas?`, criticizes Lovejoyian assumptions but calls new intellectual history an extension of Lovejoy's encyclopedic/eclectic vision in a `Lovejovian spirit`.

This makes 1987–90 a concentrated period of **competing canon formation**.

JHI is reassessing and modularizing Lovejoy at the very moment the Cambridge/Skinner debate is being anthologized into a portable canonical form.

## IX. Stronger model: methodological modularization

A useful new concept from this cluster is:

### `METHODOLOGICAL MODULARIZATION`

Instead of treating a named method as an indivisible package to be accepted or rejected, later practitioners separate components:

- unit of analysis;
- temporal scale;
- semantic stability;
- context reconstruction;
- source discrimination;
- transmission/influence;
- canonical scope;
- institutional programme.

They can reject some components and retain or transform others.

Thus:

`LOVEJOY METHOD`
need not be coded as
`ACCEPTED / REJECTED`.

It can be decomposed into relations and procedures with different survival histories.

The 1987 JHI cluster is one institutional moment when such modularization becomes explicit.

---

## X. Article-level payoff

The closing historiographic argument now gains a sharper chronology.

The later clean rupture memory obscures not only earlier JHI internal pluralism but a **late-1980s self-conscious reassessment programme** in which JHI itself asked how to distinguish Lovejoy's manifesto, actual historical practice, later descendants, and competing methods.

A strong sentence for eventual use:

> **By the time Skinner's methodological writings were canonically repackaged in 1988, JHI was already treating its own Lovejoyian inheritance not as an orthodoxy to defend or abandon but as a set of procedures to be historically decomposed, criticized, compared with Begriffsgeschichte, and selectively reused.**

This is much more historically specific than saying `history of ideas survived Skinner`.
