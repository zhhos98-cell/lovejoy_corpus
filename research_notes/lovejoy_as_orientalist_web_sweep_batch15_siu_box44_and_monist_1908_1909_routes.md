## 2. Public-domain primary route for the 1908 Lovejoy–1909 Smith debate is confirmed

The Online Books Page independently confirms that early *Monist* volumes are in persistent public archives, including **volume 18 (1908)** and **volume 19 (1909)**. Wikisource links each volume directly to Internet Archive.

Confirmed Internet Archive identifiers from the Wikisource links:

- 1908, vol. 18: `monistquart18hegeuoft`
- 1909, vol. 19: `monistquart19hegeuoft`

The vol. 18 Internet Archive object is directly accessible and identifies itself as volume 18, 694 scanned pages, `NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT`, with OCR/full-text and PDF download derivatives. This establishes a clean public-domain retrieval path independent of Oxford Academic’s paywalled article interface.

Oxford metadata confirms the exact article locations:

- Arthur O. Lovejoy, “The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus,” *The Monist* 18.4 (1 Oct. 1908), **597–609**, DOI `10.5840/monist190818440`.
- William Benjamin Smith, “Professor Lovejoy on ‘Der Vorchristliche Jesus.’,” *The Monist* 19.3 (1 July 1909), **409–420**, DOI `10.5840/monist190919311`.

The 1908 issue context is also now exact: Lovejoy’s article immediately follows Karl Borinski’s review extracts on *Der Vorchristliche Jesus* (587–597); the same issue contains D. T. Suzuki on early Chinese philosophy, “The Classification of Religions,” and Berthold Laufer’s “The Jonah Legend in India.” This is publication ecology/context only; no direct intellectual influence is inferred from adjacency.

## 3. What can and cannot yet be said about the 1908 argument

Daniel J. Wilson’s annotated bibliography summarizes Lovejoy’s 1908 article as an essay-review of W. Benjamin Smith’s *Der Vorchristliche Jesus*. Smith’s thesis is characterized there as primitive Christianity being the product of a large and slow syncretism, with fundamental/distinctive elements derived from Gnosticism. Wilson says Lovejoy tests Smith’s arguments and concludes that the major origin theory is not sustained by the evidence, with some evidence contradicting it.

This remains a **secondary synopsis** until the 1908 primary pages are extracted. It is useful because it specifies the methodological question to test in the primary text: how Lovejoy distinguishes resemblance, borrowing, syncretism, and sufficient genealogical evidence.

The 1909 reply is now bibliographically and publicly retrievable but has not yet been close-read. It should be analyzed as a paired debate, not as a free-standing reception item.

## 4. Why this matters for the Orientalist / history-of-religion project

The methodological sequence we are testing is becoming increasingly concrete:

- 004 / 1898: borrowed/composite Buddhist terms can still have an intelligible arrangement; resemblance does not settle historical origin.
- 1902: Lovejoy explicitly rejects inferring Eastern borrowing into Aristotle merely from conceptual affinity when an internal Greek genealogy suffices.
- 005 / 1906: heterogeneous ritual forms are decomposed by causal mechanism rather than forced into one animist/religious master theory.
- 1908–09 debate: potentially another direct test case in which a large syncretic genealogy is accepted or rejected according to the evidentiary burden for historical connection.

If the 1908 primary text confirms Wilson’s synopsis in detail, this would give us a strong cross-domain continuity in **anti-shortcut genealogy** before the mature “unit-idea” vocabulary: similarity is evidence to investigate, not proof of transmission.
