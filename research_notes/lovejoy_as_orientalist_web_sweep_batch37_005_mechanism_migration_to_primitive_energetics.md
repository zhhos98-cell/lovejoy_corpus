## Research move

Batch 36 established that notebook 005’s two largest blocks correspond closely to the two long-running research programmes Léon Marillier brought together in the 1898–99 EPHE conferences in which Lovejoy is documented as an active auditor: **survivance / justice** and **human sacrifice / ritual anthropophagy**. The next question is sharper:

> Is there one analytical operation that actually connects the early eschatology pages, the middle sacrifice pages, the blood/fetish pages, and Lovejoy’s 1906 `Primitive Energetics` paper?

The answer is now strongly **yes**.

The notebook does not simply move from one topic to another. The **object changes while the classificatory operator remains stable**:

> **What outcome is being explained? → what mechanism actually produces it? → which rival mechanisms must be excluded? → is the visible form historically composite?**

Applied successively:

- future life: **what determines post-mortem destiny?**
- taboo/social sanction: **what produces exclusion, danger or punishment?**
- sacrifice: **what makes the rite effective?**
- blood/body parts/charms: **where is efficacy located and how is it transferred?**
- cannibalism: **what is actually being acquired, appeased, transmitted or united?**

By 1906 the cross-domain answer is abstracted into a general category of **impersonal / quasi-mechanical / communicable efficacy**.

This is the strongest current bridge from a material research notebook to the later conceptual paper. It is more precise than claiming that 005 contains a draft of the 1906 article.

## 1. First object: future-life destiny — classify the determinant, not the destination

The early 005 pages are organized around a deceptively simple question: when different dead persons meet different fates, **what determines the difference?**

The notebook’s staged outline and its case analyses distinguish:

1. naturalistic continuation of earthly life/status;
2. manner of death;
3. ritual/manual qualification;
4. social/community relation;
5. taboo/interdiction and clan solidarity;
6. only finally, strictly moral guilt/desert.

The crucial criterion appears repeatedly around PDF pp.16–20: the existence of a pleasant/unpleasant or separate afterlife is not yet evidence of moral retribution. The fate becomes moral only when reward/punishment is assigned **because of desert**.

This is an operation of causal/normative discrimination:

> `different outcome` does not imply `moral judgment`; identify the determinant that actually generates the outcome.

This matches the Marillier programme directly. The EPHE reports of the early 1890s explicitly separated taboo as protection against supernatural danger from moral significance, and the 1893–94 survivance work argued that afterlife rank continued earthly rank rather than compensating moral merit. In 1898–99 the conference in which Lovejoy actively participated again set itself the task of proving that no moral element occurs at the origin of this eschatology.

## 2. Source criticism is part of the same operator

005 PDF p.19 does not merely classify a Pawnee afterlife report. Lovejoy asks whether the report itself has been altered by missionary contact and whether its date makes it unsafe evidence for an earlier indigenous conception.

This is not a separate “methodological aside.” It is required by the same operation. Before assigning a mechanism to a belief, one must establish **which historical layer of the testimony produced the statement being classified**.

Marillier’s 1894–95 EPHE report already treated biblical traditions imported by missionaries as a contamination/interpolation problem in American myth; the 1899–1900 programme again specified Christian interpolations in American eschatological myths.

Primary controls:
- https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1894_num_8_4_19409
- https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1899_num_13_9_19524

Thus in 005:

> **historical criticism determines what evidence belongs to which layer; mechanism-classification determines what that layer means.**

These are two sides of one procedure.

## 3. Second object: sacrifice — classify efficacy, not ritual form

Once 005 turns to sacrifice, the same operator is applied to a new explanandum. The question is no longer `why does this dead person go there?` but `why is this rite expected to work?`

The notebook separates outwardly similar sacrificial acts into:

- funerary provision / social continuity;
- alimentary exchange;
- contractual or propitiatory sacrifice;
- expiatory / scapegoat transfer;
- magical constraint;
- direct action upon natural forces;
- fecundative transfer of life/power;
- mystical / sacramental communion.

The visible act — killing, bloodshed, eating, burning, offering — therefore ceases to be the unit of analysis. **Mechanism is the unit.**

This repeats the logic of the future-life section:

> `same form` does not imply `same cause/function`.

The 1897–98 EPHE report had already classified human sacrifice into funerary, expiatory, anthropophagic and magical types; the 1898–99 report continued the same taxonomy and added agrarian/fecundative comparison. Lovejoy’s 005 pushes this classification further and repeatedly asks what process makes each rite efficacious.

## 4. Historical decomposition: present ritual complexes can contain several pasts

At PDF p.65 / MS p.131, Lovejoy explicitly says that several motives are often mingled and that only delicate analysis can associate the different ritual elements. Syncretism can follow a period in which several sacrifices were **distinct in character** before later combination.

The same principle recurs at PDF p.73, where a funerary practice can acquire a new rationale after the practice itself already exists, and again at PDF p.97, where an annual/communal ritual complex may reorganize older funerary, alimentary, local or borrowed elements whose histories differ.

This is a striking echo of Marillier’s 1892–93 EPHE statement that institutions, rites and legends may **survive the beliefs that gave birth to them**.

Primary control:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1892_num_6_2_19363

The methodological implication is large:

> **classification by mechanism is simultaneously historical decomposition.**

One cannot infer origin from present meaning, and one cannot infer one historical origin from one present ceremonial form.

## 5. Third object: blood and body parts — where is efficacy located?

The most important migration occurs around PDF pp.84–90, where the notebook ceases merely to classify varieties of sacrifice and begins asking **where efficacy inheres**.

### PDF p.84 / MS p.171 — protective efficacy without communion

Foundation/building sacrifice can use blood, flesh, bones or remains to protect or render a settlement safe/impregnable. Lovejoy explicitly warns that this need not express `union through blood`: the victim’s body/blood may possess **independent protective efficacy**; bits of flesh can function as charms.

### PDF p.85 — life-giving / curative blood

Blood is treated as having life-giving, curative and rejuvenating power. Lovejoy gathers Trumbull and Strack material on restoring health, youth or vitality by blood and classifies these cases primarily as **magical**, not as sacrifice proper.

### PDF p.86 / MS p.175 — properties transferred through parts

Eyes, bones, blood and other bodily parts can confer the qualities of another person: sight, intelligence, strength, health, power. Possession of the part can mean possession of the quality.

### PDF p.87 / MS p.177 — blood can be efficacious `in itself`

Lovejoy explicitly distinguishes blood credited with power in itself from blood functioning merely as a pledge or index of a spirit’s presence. Saliva, perspiration, urine and excrement may receive analogous magical values.

### PDF p.88 / MS p.179 — fetish versus charm/amulet

The distinction becomes conceptually decisive. A fetish works because a **spirit inhabits/is present in the object**. Plants, stones, amulets, charms, ju-ju/gri-gri and similar objects, by contrast, can be credited with a **natural or supernatural efficacy in themselves** for particular purposes.

This is already almost the ontology of the 1906 paper. It isolates:

> **indwelling personal agent** versus **intrinsic efficacious property/power**.

That is a much tighter genealogy than a generic move from “magic” to “mana.”

## 6. Marillier 1899–1900 supplies the immediate conceptual environment for blood efficacy

The 1899–1900 EPHE programme, the year after Lovejoy’s documented active participation, explicitly announces:

> `La puissance magique et fécondatrice du sang.`

alongside agrarian sacrifice, expulsion of evils, critical study of Frazer, amoral eschatology and Christian interpolation.

Primary control:
https://www.persee.fr/doc/ephe_0000-0002_1899_num_13_9_19524

Lovejoy is not listed as an active auditor that year, so this report is **programme continuity**, not proof of attendance. But it demonstrates that `blood as magical/fecundative power` belonged to the immediate Marillier research programme into which 005’s blood pages fit.

## 7. Marillier 1900 `Religion` makes the mechanism explicit in print

Marillier’s encyclopedia article `Religion` provides an unusually close published parallel to the operator visible in 005.

In the sacrifice section he says primitive sacrifice releases, through the victim’s blood identified with life, a force intensified by incantations and ritual gestures that acts upon gods to bend them to the sacrificer’s will. He then distinguishes this magical relation from alimentary/contractual sacrifice:

> the means of constraint is no longer `un moyen mécanique, si j'ose dire`, but a `moyen psychologique et moral`.

The god can refuse the latter. Marillier’s concise distinction is that alimentary sacrifice must be **accepted** by the divine recipient whereas magical sacrifice is **undergone** by him.

The same article later describes a primitive conception of:

> `un même mana, d'une même force magique et divine, de qualité partout identique, éparse dans les choses`

and treats blood, sacred/impure contagion, magical transfer, expiation, communion and moralization within one historical architecture.

Searchable electronic reproduction used as locator:
https://cosmovisions.com/%24Religion09.htm

Publication-grade quotation still requires collation against *La Grande Encyclopédie*, tome XXVIII, pp.341–364. The web reproduction is used here as a searchable witness/locator, not as final facsimile authority.

## 8. Fourth object: agricultural sacrifice — efficacy can act directly on natural forces

The notebook’s agricultural block makes the abstraction still more explicit.

At PDF p.64 / MS p.129 magical sacrifice is described as operating `in a sort of machine`, and Lovejoy says some sacrifices work not by constraining a god’s will but **directly upon the forces of nature**.

At PDF pp.98–101 blood can possess an independent magical significance and agricultural rites can transfer life/power to crops. At PDF p.105 the proposed sacrifice of the plant-soul to itself is classified as **quasi-mechanical / magical rather than sacramental**.

This is the conceptual pivot:

> the efficacy is neither moral reward nor persuasion of a person; it is a regular causal relation between acts, substances, powers and outcomes.

## 9. Fifth object: cannibalism — ceremonial form is analytically useless until mechanism is known

PDF pp.106–119 provide the notebook’s most systematic stress-test of the method.

Lovejoy asks whether anthropophagy is intrinsically religious and repeatedly answers by decomposition. Possible mechanisms include:

- famine / nutritional consumption;
- revenge or hatred;
- affection for relatives;
- acquisition of strength/intelligence/courage/sexual power;
- sympathetic magic;
- propitiation of a dangerous victim-spirit;
- funerary/social continuity;
- sacramental communion.

A public, repetitive, solemn or rule-bound feast is not enough to establish a religious or sacramental origin. Ceremoniality may be secondary social regulation of an older practice.

Selected organs sharpen the mechanism: brain/head → intelligence; heart → courage/life; sexual organs → sexual power. The part is treated as a **carrier of a transferable quality**, and Lovejoy classifies these cases primarily with sympathetic magic rather than sacramental union.

The synthetic endpoint of this block is especially important: alimentary and expiatory/propitiatory sacrifice are common; sacramental union is comparatively exceptional.

This directly resists making Robertson Smith’s communion model a universal master explanation.

## 10. 1906: the operator becomes an abstract causal category

Lovejoy’s `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`, *The Monist* 16.3 (1906), pp.357–382, is securely bibliographed by the journal itself:
https://academic.oup.com/monist/article/16/3/357/995254

The 1906 argument attacks anthropomorphic/animistic master theories and identifies a more fundamental category: impersonal, quasi-mechanical, diffused and communicable efficacy/power. The later label `Primitive Energetics` makes explicit what the notebook had repeatedly isolated empirically.

A useful contemporary/later bibliographic summary by Daniel J. Wilson characterizes Lovejoy’s argument as seeking well-adjusted **quasi-mechanical relations with the source of supernatural energy** rather than primarily friendly relations with supernatural beings; the publication itself remains the authority.

The genealogy is therefore not:

> 005 contains `mana` → Lovejoy writes about mana.

It is stronger:

> **005 repeatedly trains a single discrimination between personal agency and intrinsic/direct/transferable efficacy across many kinds of evidence; 1906 turns that recurring mechanism into the fundamental comparative category.**

## 12. Why this matters for the history-of-ideas claim

There is a temptation to leap from this notebook to Lovejoy’s mature `unit-ideas`. The evidence does not require that move, and it would flatten the much more interesting local process.

What 005 securely shows is an earlier **practice of decomposition**:

1. do not infer mechanism from visible form;
2. isolate rival causal/normative determinants;
3. criticize the historical provenance of testimony;
4. allow a present complex to contain layers with different histories;
5. compare only after those layers/mechanisms have been separated;
6. then abstract a recurring causal relation across domains.

This is not yet the later formal historiography of unit-ideas. It is a concrete comparative-religion research procedure from which the 1906 causal category becomes intelligible.

## 13. Revised strongest paper sentence

A defensible formulation now is:

> **Lovejoy’s 005 notebook records more than the reception of Marillier’s comparative religion. Across eschatology, taboo, sacrifice, blood, charms and anthropophagy, Lovejoy repeatedly applies the same analytical operator: he refuses to infer cause from visible form, decomposes competing determinants and historical layers, and asks where efficacy is located and how it is transmitted. The 1906 `Primitive Energetics` paper can therefore be read as the abstraction of a causal relation repeatedly isolated in this earlier empirical work: impersonal, direct, transferable and quasi-mechanical efficacy.**

Evidence strength: **high for methodological continuity; medium-high for genealogy into the 1906 abstraction; insufficient for a claim that 005 was a literal draft or that every component came from Marillier.**

## 14. Evidence limits

1. 005 is longitudinal and was reused through at least 1905; its page sequence is not a simple composition chronology.
2. Some pp.84–90 transcriptions are medium-confidence because handwriting is difficult. The manuscript image remains authority for publication-grade quotation.
3. Marillier’s 1899–1900 programme demonstrates conceptual continuity but not Lovejoy’s attendance that year.
4. The Cosmovisions reproduction of Marillier 1900 is a locator; exact quotation/page citation requires *Grande Encyclopédie* facsimile collation.
5. The 1906 paper’s exact wording should be cited from the recovered primary PDF / original *Monist* scan, not from later summaries.
6. The genealogy is one of **operations and concepts**, not proven direct copying sentence by sentence.
