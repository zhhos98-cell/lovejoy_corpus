# 1913 American religion-methods relay — Leuba, King, Durkheim, Hubert–Mauss, and Lovejoy print ecology

Date: 2026-08-26

## Core result

By 1913 the American discussion around religion, magic, impersonal power and natural-history explanation had become a concrete transatlantic **methods relay**, not merely a diffuse background.

Three publication facts are now directly controlled:

1. James H. Leuba published `Sociology and Psychology: The Conception of Religion and Magic and the Place of Psychology in Sociological Studies: A Discussion of the Views of Durkheim and of Hubert and Mauss`, *American Journal of Sociology* 19.3 (November 1913), 323–342. Its JSTOR reference apparatus directly places **Irving King 1910**, Durkheim's 1897–98 and 1909 religion essays, Hubert–Mauss on sacrifice, and Hubert–Mauss on magic inside one methods discussion.
2. The same Leuba article was translated/reprinted in French in *Revue philosophique de la France et de l'étranger* 76 (1913), 337–357 as `Sociologie et psychologie: La conception de la religion et de la magie, discussion des théories de Durkheim et d'Hubert et Mauss`. The volume index is directly available through the BnF/Wikimedia scan. This establishes an immediate **America → France return circulation** of the critique.
3. *The Philosophical Review* 22.4 (July 1913) places in the same issue Arthur O. Lovejoy's `Error and the New Realism` (410–423), a review of Leuba's *A Psychological Study of Religion*, and Irving King's review of Durkheim's *Les formes élémentaires de la vie religieuse* (431–434). This is a direct print-ecology fact, not evidence that Lovejoy read or assimilated the neighboring reviews.

The historically useful object is therefore:

> **shared comparative-religion materials and French sociological arguments were being redistributed across American psychology, sociology and philosophy at the same moment that Lovejoy's own 1906 primitive-power argument was circulating through King and Leuba.**

Do not turn publication adjacency into an intellectual edge.

## I. Leuba's 1913 reference architecture is directly controlled

The Open JSTOR record for the AJS article gives the full title, pages and references. Its notes directly cite:

- Émile Durkheim, `De la définition des phénomènes religieux`, *Année sociologique* II (1897–98), 1–28;
- Durkheim's 1909 critical treatment of classical theories of religious thought;
- Irving King, *The Development of Religion* (1910);
- Hubert and Mauss, `Essai sur la nature et la fonction du sacrifice`, *Année sociologique* II;
- Hubert and Mauss, `Esquisse d'une théorie générale de la magie`, *Année sociologique* VII;
- George M. Stratton, *The Psychology of the Religious Life*.

Thus the American King problem-field and the Paris `Année sociologique` problem-field are directly co-addressed inside one American methods article.

This does **not** establish that Leuba encountered Hubert–Mauss through Lovejoy, King or Marillier; the citations support direct textual engagement, not provenance of reading.

## II. Content ceiling for Leuba 1913

The AJS JSTOR content viewer presently exposes metadata and references but not searchable article body through the available interface. A later PsycINFO abstract and a modern historical discussion summarize Leuba's position as arguing that Durkheim's sociology cannot fully explain religion by external social facts alone; conscious group members and psychological interpretation remain necessary.

The modern discussion quotes the French translation at printed p.348 to the effect that the methodological question is whether rites can be fully understood from their external manifestation without interpreting the conscious states they express, and that complete understanding of religion/social life requires interpreting collective results in terms of consciousness.

## III. The French return circuit is historically substantive

The French translation appears in *Revue philosophique* 76, edited by Théodule Ribot, pp.337–357. The BnF-derived Wikisource index directly lists `Leuba — Sociologie et Psychologie — 337`.

This makes the circulation directional structure more precise:

`Durkheim / Hubert–Mauss French sociology`
→ `American Leuba re-analysis in AJS`
→ `French translation in Revue philosophique`.

It is therefore inaccurate to describe the episode simply as French theory arriving in America. The methodological dispute itself recirculated transatlantically.

This is especially useful beside Lovejoy's 1898–99 Paris participation because it prevents a one-way `Paris influence → American receiver` narrative at both ends of the chronology.

## IV. Philosophical Review 22.4 as print ecology

The July 1913 issue directly contains:

- W. K. Wright, `Ethical Objectivity in the Light of Social Psychology`;
- Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Error and the New Realism`, 410–423;
- Ernest L. Talbert reviewing Leuba's *A Psychological Study of Religion*;
- Irving King reviewing Durkheim's *Les formes élémentaires de la vie religieuse*, 431–434.

A searchable scan of Wright's article in this issue also invokes `manitou or mana` and cites Irving King's chapter VI alongside Marett. Thus the issue's surrounding argumentative ecology is visibly saturated with social-psychological and primitive-power vocabulary.

Safe use:

> **Lovejoy's 1913 philosophical article appeared in an issue whose adjacent review and article sections were actively processing Leuba, King, Durkheim and mana/manitou problems.**

Unsafe use:

- Lovejoy read King's Durkheim review because it followed his article;
- Lovejoy consciously participated in that review exchange;
- editorial adjacency proves conceptual transmission;
- neighboring vocabulary explains Lovejoy's philosophical argument.

## V. Contemporary external period control: Hartland 1914

Edwin Sidney Hartland's *Ritual and Belief* (1914) supplies a useful near-contemporary classification of the field. Hartland says the psychological side of the problem had recently been treated by American writers, notably James Leuba and Irving King. He describes King as giving a suggestive survey of savage rites/beliefs, emphasizing priority of rites to belief and social origins, then immediately turns to Durkheim as deriving religious ideas/practices from social origin.

This is a contemporary period witness that **Leuba/King could be grouped as an American psychological approach while Durkheim supplied the strongest recent sociological/social-origin comparator**.

Hartland does not mention Lovejoy in this local classification; do not insert him into the grouping without a direct edge.

## VI. Relation to the two-notebook JHI argument

The 1913 relay does not prove a continuous methodological genealogy from 004/005 to American social psychology. Its value is different.

The notebooks show Lovejoy working within fields where one must separate:

- visible form from operative relation;
- comparative similarity from mechanism;
- mechanism from genealogy/priority;
- source admissibility from stronger historical inference.

The 1910–14 American controversy now independently shows neighboring disciplines arguing over another set of boundaries:

- comparative attestation vs historical priority;
- analyst category vs historical/native object;
- social genesis vs psychological interpretation;
- genesis vs causal exhaustiveness.

Thus the broader American setting can be described as an **ecology of contested explanatory jurisdiction** rather than as a single Lovejoy method spreading outward.

JHI-facing compact formulation:

> **When comparative-religion objects returned to American psychology, sociology and philosophy, the argument did not concern only which theory was true. It increasingly concerned which level of explanation—ethnographic, psychological, sociological, historical—was licensed to establish which proposition.**

This is structurally adjacent to the notebook proof problem, but no direct transmission claim is required.
