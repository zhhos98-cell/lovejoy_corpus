## 2. New pre-Lovejoy Harvard control — Jastrow 1893

`Jastrow_1893_preLovejoy_Harvard_source_specialism_primary_control_2026-08-26.md` changes the institutional interpretation of Lovejoy's training.

Before Lovejoy arrived at Harvard in 1895, Morris Jastrow already described the American/Harvard history-of-religions field as a division between:

- Everett — general/comparative history of religions;
- Toy — Hebrew religion / Semitic specialization;
- Lanman — Sanskrit/Buddhist source specialization;
- broader general comparison — legitimate only after prolonged study of particular religions requiring source knowledge and specialist competence.

Lovejoy later directly traversed Everett, Toy and Lanman in 1896–97.

Safe JHI implication:

> **Lovejoy did not accidentally assemble an interdisciplinary package. He entered a Harvard ecology already organized around different evidentiary jurisdictions: general comparison and source-bound specialisms were institutionally co-present before his arrival.**

Do not infer Jastrow→Lovejoy reading or method transmission.

### Crucial counter-control

Jastrow combines this fine-grained source specialization with a progressive/developmental totalization of religious history. Therefore:

> **source discipline and civilizational staging were not methodological opposites in the field.**

This is an important pre-Lovejoy analogue for the later project finding that decomposition can coexist with, and sometimes enable, totalization.

## 5. Jastrow 1899 — `evidentiary jurisdiction` becomes actor-declared

## 6. Lovejoy 1906 Kant — same-year decomposition/totalization control

New canonical: `Lovejoy_1906_Kant_historical_rubric_discontinuity_control_2026-08-26.md`.

In `Kant's Antithesis of Dogmatism and Criticism`, Lovejoy treats `dogmatism / criticism` as an accepted rubric in histories of philosophy that silently bundles several different historical propositions:

- what Kant's predecessors actually taught/methodologically did;
- how Kant differed from them;
- what argumentative criteria were genuinely novel;
- whether a large breach of historical continuity occurred.

He calls the simple historical scheme seriously misleading, reopens the constituent doctrinal/argumentative relations, and concludes that the binary **as a historical contrast** falls to the ground.

This adds an especially important same-year comparison:

### 1906 Kant

`grand inherited historical rubric`
→ decompose bundled claims
→ local doctrinal audit
→ reject unwarranted whole-period discontinuity.

### 1906 `Primitive Philosophy`

`heterogeneous local mechanism relations`
→ compare/reweight
→ reaggregate into a cross-regional impersonal-power category.

JHI consequence:

> **In 1906 Lovejoy is simultaneously dismantling one grand classification and constructing another. Decomposition and totalization are not successive stages in his development; they are alternative operations whose legitimacy depends on the warrant for moving between local relation and aggregate scale.**

This is currently one of the strongest epilogue-level findings.

Do not claim one 1906 paper supplied the method of the other.

### New mature-primary finding

A scan-derived OCR of a 1966 Harvard printing of *The Great Chain of Being* directly confirms that Lovejoy defines the book's central object in the preface as a major conception of `Occidental philosophy` and a persistent presupposition in `Western thought`. He says the work's task is to show the relation of separately told historical parts to one `single pervasive complex of ideas` and explicitly describes the book as moving across several `distinct provinces of the history of thought`.

Current OCR search produced no straightforward hits for `Buddh*`, `Vedanta`, or `Hindu`.

Independent scholarly transcriptions consistently locate at printed p.35 the statement that Plato is the source of the **indigenous** strain of otherworldliness in Occidental philosophy/religion, distinguished from `imported Oriental varieties`. The exact p.35 primary page remains a page-proof target in MF-004.

Current safest JHI epilogue:

> **The mature book does not simply continue the early Indic trajectory on equal narrative terms. Its declared historical object is Occidental/Western, while Oriental material appears, at least in the currently controlled opening formulation, as an external/comparative boundary. The early non-Western materials are therefore better described as redistributed rather than simply abandoned.**

## Compact final thesis

> **Lovejoy's early Buddhist and comparative-religion work matters less as a hidden doctrinal source of the later history of ideas than as an unusually visible archive of historical warrant. Across philology, comparative religion and philosophy, he repeatedly confronted the problem of when evidence for resemblance, sequence, function or mechanism could legitimately be upgraded into claims about identity, genealogy or historical scale. The mature abstractions of the history of ideas were not simply produced by ignoring context; they could emerge from acts of decomposition whose success made new recombinations possible. The unresolved political question is what happened when such recombinations were projected across peoples, cultures and stages of development.**
