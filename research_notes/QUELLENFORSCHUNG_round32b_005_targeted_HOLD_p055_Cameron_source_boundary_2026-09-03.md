- the people eat men killed in battle;
- they also eat persons who die of sickness;
- corpses are macerated in running water until the flesh is nearly putrefied;
- a cannibal song then evaluates male versus female flesh.

This sequence matches the first half of notebook p.55 far too closely to remain merely a field parallel.

At source-ownership level:

`BATTLE-DEAD + SICK-DEAD + RUNNING-WATER MACERATION`
=
**CAMERON S3 / A0**.

## 2. Cameron cannot carry the notebook's gendered-selection argument as currently controlled

Cameron's printed song says, in substance:

The notebook's Round-18 direct-image delta, by contrast, secures:

Cameron's text does not do that. In Cameron, preference and taste point in the same direction.

> **Cameron must not be treated as carrier of the whole p.55 page.**

## 3. Focused second-carrier search did not recover the exact p.55 structure

Nineteenth-century and early ethnographic literature contains many reports that women or children are considered more tender or palatable than adult men, especially in Fiji/Melanesia and later African collections. Other literature makes male flesh, enemy flesh, or selected male body parts valuable because they are associated with strength, bravery or power.

But the targeted search did **not** recover a pre-/co-1899 source with the exact combined structure needed for p.55:

`FEMALE FLESH GASTRONOMICALLY BETTER`
+
`MALE FLESH NEVERTHELESS PREFERRED`
+
`PREFERENCE EXPLAINED BY MALE POWER / VALUE`.

Generic examples of female flesh being more tender cannot be used to repair or source the notebook.

Likewise Frazer's strength-acquisition material explains why male/enemy flesh may be valued for power, but it does not by itself supply the p.55 gastronomic-versus-symbolic contrast.
