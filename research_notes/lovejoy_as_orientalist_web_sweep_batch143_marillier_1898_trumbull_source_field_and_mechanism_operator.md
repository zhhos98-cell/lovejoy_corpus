## Core result

Batch 142 recovered the whole of Léon Marillier's 1898 continuation of `La place du totémisme` and identified him as the reviewer of H. Clay Trumbull's `The Threshold-Covenant` in the same RHR volume. A page-level re-collation now shows that this is more important than a generic methodological parallel.

Marillier's 1898 printed work and Lovejoy's 005 notebook occupy an overlapping **named source field** — Trumbull's `Blood Covenant` / `Threshold-Covenant`, Mary Kingsley, Strack, Dorman and related blood/foundation/fetish materials — and they repeatedly perform the same analytical operation: preserve an ethnographic fact while withdrawing the source author's right to make that fact diagnostic of one universal theory.

The strongest result, however, is **not** a copied-packet hypothesis. Where exact pages can be compared, the extraction is non-identical. Marillier cites Trumbull generally or by chapters and cites Kingsley in a different page cluster; Lovejoy independently anchors his notebook to Trumbull p.118 and p.129 and Kingsley p.511 and p.525. Thus the new evidence strengthens:

> **shared source ecology + shared causal/classificatory operator + different exact selections**

rather than:

> **Marillier handed Lovejoy a fixed extract packet**.

This is a substantially stronger historical model because it combines institutional proximity, overlapping books, contemporaneous printed method, and manuscript transformation while retaining the evidentiary boundary around direct transmission.

## 1. Marillier 1898 p.351: blood can act by immediate/direct efficacy

In the third/fourth-article continuation, printed p.351, Marillier argues against treating blood rites automatically as blood-covenant or union rites. In his Aht example a man sprinkles his own blood around a hut to protect himself from nightmares or hostile spirits. Marillier's point is that the actor is not securing the aid of protective gods; the blood rite itself is treated as having **immediate and direct efficacy**, forming a protective magical barrier.

The source apparatus is unusually important. On this page/section Marillier points readers to:

- H. C. Trumbull, `The Blood Covenant`, for the magical/curative power of blood;
- H. L. Strack, `Der Blutaberglaube`;
- Dorman, `Primitive Superstitions`;
- Mary Kingsley, `Travels in West Africa`;
- Trumbull, `The Threshold-Covenant`;
- additional blood-brotherhood and ritual-cannibalism materials.

This is not merely the same topic as 005. Notebook 005 PDF pp.84–90 is built around the same problem family: foundation blood, curative/rejuvenating blood, body-part transfer, protective efficacy, fetish versus amulet, Kingsley's West-African cases, and the distinction between spirit-mediated and intrinsic efficacy.

### Historical consequence

The source constellation around Lovejoy's mechanism distinctions was already assembled in Marillier's printed 1898 comparative work before/during the threshold of Lovejoy's Paris year.

This does **not** establish that Lovejoy read p.351 or received these references from Marillier. It establishes a much denser contemporary source environment than a thematic resemblance claim.

## 2. Lovejoy's Trumbull uptake remains independently exact

Batch 57 visually secured two manuscript citations:

- 005 PDF p.85 / MS p.173: `V. Trumbull Blood Cov't 118.`
- 005 PDF p.86 / MS p.175: `(quot. Trumbull p.129)`.

The intervening notebook sequence follows Trumbull's printed pp.118–129 closely enough to support sequential extraction: curative/life-bearing blood → rejuvenation/prophylactic covering → transfer of another person's nature/qualities → Ashantee material at p.129.

Lovejoy nevertheless changes the evidentiary function of the material. The new Marillier evidence therefore does not create Lovejoy's Trumbull link — that link was already direct and exact. What it changes is the **environmental topology** around that reading: Marillier was himself using and criticizing the same Trumbull problem field in print.

## 3. Marillier's Threshold-Covenant review, pp.405–419: facts can be right while the theory is wrong in scope

The review begins by praising Trumbull's learning and documentary accumulation, then makes two general methodological objections.

First, Marillier says Trumbull's theory did not simply arise neutrally from the evidence. The theory depends on background theological and social assumptions about primitive religion, marriage, paternal filiation and the family. Marillier converts those assumptions into explicit documentary tests.

Second, and more important for 005, he attacks the recurrent scholarly tendency to reduce the multiplicity and variety of religious phenomena to **one principle of explanation**. His examples include reduction to magic, union, fear, love, totemism, meteorological mythology, and Trumbull's blood/threshold covenant.

The review's key evidentiary rule is clear: many of the facts assembled in such theories are accurate; the error lies in assigning them a scope and generality that they do not possess. A true local explanation does not thereby become the universal origin of a whole class.

This is a contemporaneous 1898 statement of the anti-unilateral operator already reconstructed from Marillier's 1897 Jevons critique and from 005 itself.

## 4. Printed pp.411–412: Marillier explicitly reopens Trumbull's blood evidence

In his retrospective treatment of Trumbull's earlier `Blood Covenant`, Marillier notes that Trumbull had gathered valuable evidence on blood brotherhood, the sacred character of blood, ritual meals and cannibalism. But he objects that Trumbull had pushed these cases toward a broad unitive/covenantal interpretation.

Marillier then makes a move directly relevant to the 005 mechanism field: some blood rites that Trumbull treats as essentially symbolic were, for their practitioners, credited with **real and direct efficacy comparable to magical charms**.

This is an especially strong control because it is not merely an abstract warning against universalization. Marillier is reopening the **causal bearer** of the same blood evidence:

`symbolic/covenantal sign`  
versus  
`directly efficacious magical operation`.

Lovejoy's 005 pp.84–90 repeatedly performs the same kind of reopening, but with his own exact source selections.

## 5. Printed p.417: foundation blood is explicitly decomposed into different mechanisms

The closest proposition-level comparison with notebook 005 occurs in Marillier's discussion of foundation rites.

He says that putting blood on foundations or walling a living being into a structure can reflect beliefs analogous to threshold rites but nevertheless **distinct**. He then gives three possible mechanisms:

1. attaching a guardian/protective spirit to the monument;
2. a charm in which the magical power of blood acts directly as a vehicle of force and life;
3. a propitiatory sacrifice intended to disarm a god's hostility or malevolence.

This is remarkably close to 005 PDF p.84 / MS p.171. The notebook likewise refuses to infer `union through blood` from foundation blood/remains and allows the victim's body or blood to operate by independent protective efficacy.

### Strength of the concordance

This is stronger than same-topic overlap:

- same ritual family: foundation/building blood and remains;
- same rejected inference: blood use does not by itself prove union/communion;
- same positive operation: separate possible causal bearers/mechanisms.

But it still does not prove Lovejoy copied Marillier p.417. No page citation or direct textual dependence has been recovered.

The correct status is **strong contemporaneous conceptual/methodological concordance within a documented teacher-student institutional relationship**.

## 6. Printed pp.401–403: resemblance, borrowing and sacrifice are kept analytically separate

Marillier's final pages of `La place du totémisme` add two controls.

At p.401 he warns that undeniable similarities are not sufficient evidence for borrowing or conscious imitation; even an adopted ritual form need not imply that the receiving cult itself was created by the borrowing.

At p.402 he gives a deliberately plural sacrificial classification. A rite can function as:

- gift/transaction for divine favor;
- common meal or sacramental communion;
- expiatory/purificatory immolation;
- blood effusion credited with magical value or efficacy.

At p.403 he gives the final methodological judgment on Jevons: the apparent unity of the theory is artificial because evidence that speaks against the scheme has to be excluded or subordinated.

This supplies a direct 1898 primary comparator for 005's repeated procedure:

`surface resemblance`  
→ `do not infer common origin/class`  
→ `identify causal mechanism`  
→ `allow plural classes and later combinations`.

## 7. Kingsley proves that the environment is shared without being a copied packet

Batch 58 visually secured Lovejoy's Kingsley references:

- 005 PDF p.89 / MS p.181: `Kingsley, Travels of W. Afr. 511`;
- 005 PDF p.90 / MS p.183: `Miss Kingsley 525`.

Lovejoy uses p.511, where Kingsley's `suhman` works through an entered/indwelling spirit, as a boundary case for a different class: an amulet need not contain a spirit and can be credited with efficacy intrinsic to the thing. He uses p.525 while reopening the causal status of blood.

Marillier's 1898 p.351 apparatus cites the same Kingsley book, but a different page cluster, alongside Dorman and Trumbull.

This non-identity is historiographically useful:

> **same book / same problem field / different exact pages / convergent causal discrimination**.

If Marillier had handed Lovejoy a fixed extract packet, identical page selection would be one expected trace. What we instead have is more consistent with a **generative bibliographic environment** in which the same books and questions are available for different selections and different analytical recombinations.

This is an inference, not proof of how the books physically circulated.

## 8. Revised Marillier → 005 model after Batch 143

The evidence can now be stratified more tightly.

### Direct / primary-secure

- Lovejoy is officially documented as actively participating in Marillier's 1898–99 EPHE conferences.
- 005 contains explicit `Marillier — Survivance — 12 June` residue and strong programme concordance.
- Lovejoy directly cites and sequentially extracts Trumbull `Blood Covenant` pp.118–129.
- Lovejoy directly cites Kingsley pp.511 and 525.
- Marillier in 1898 prints an anti-unilateral method and works in the same Trumbull/Kingsley/Strack blood-and-foundation source field.

### Strong inference

- 005 is best understood as work produced inside a Marillier-centered comparative-religion research environment in which overlapping source objects were being critically regrouped by causal mechanism.
- The influence Lovejoy later attributed to Marillier can plausibly include not only topics or doctrines but a way of assigning different evidentiary burdens to heterogeneous reports.

### Not established

- Lovejoy read RHR 37.
- Lovejoy copied Marillier's p.351 or p.417.
- Marillier assigned Lovejoy Trumbull pp.118–129 or Kingsley pp.511/525.
- A fixed Marillier source packet circulated to Lovejoy.
- Similarity of mechanism language proves direct textual dependence.

## 9. Why this matters for the 005 → 1906 argument

The new evidence makes the developmental sequence more material without turning it into a single-source genealogy.

A cautious sequence is now:

`Marillier 1897–98: anti-single-origin comparison + named blood/foundation source field + direct/mechanical efficacy alternatives`  
`→ Lovejoy active in Marillier's EPHE conferences, 1898–99`  
`→ 005: exact extraction from Trumbull/Kingsley + repeated redivision by causal bearer`  
`→ continued notebook reuse documented in 1905`  
`→ 1906: abstraction of impersonal, transferable, quasi-mechanical efficacy as a general primitive causal category.`

The value of 005 is precisely that it preserves the middle operation that later publication hides: concrete source pages are detached from their source author's master theory and redistributed into a new causal classification.

The strongest formulation remains:

> **the source can disappear while the mechanism abstracted from its cases survives.**

But Batch 143 adds an institutional qualification: the reclassification itself was being practiced in a Paris environment where Lovejoy's teacher was already publicly arguing against the same kinds of universalizing interpretations of overlapping source material.
