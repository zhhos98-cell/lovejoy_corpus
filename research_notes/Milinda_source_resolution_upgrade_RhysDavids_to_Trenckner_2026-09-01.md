# Milinda source-resolution upgrade: Rhys Davids working interface -> Trenckner Pāli citation in print

Date: 2026-09-01  
Status: **DIRECT SOURCE-INTERFACE CROSSWALK / MAJOR PUBLICATION-RESOLUTION UPGRADE**

## 1. Research question

The previous dossiers established two things:

1. notebook `MS38_004_001_061_004` distributes Milinda-pañha material across multiple working problems;
2. the 1898 JAOS article selectively retains Milinda as local witness evidence inside the `upādāna` proof structure.

The present dossier asks a narrower source-critical question:

> **Did the source interface itself change between working notebook and publication?**

The answer is yes at a high evidentiary level. The notebook's Milinda English wording and question-numbering align with T. W. Rhys Davids's *Questions of King Milinda*, SBE 35 (1890), while the 1898 article's `Milinda Pañha` page/line references resolve directly to Vilhelm Trenckner's 1880 Pāli edition.

This should be described as a **source-resolution upgrade / citation-interface change**, not as proof that Lovejoy first encountered the Milinda only in translation or that he lacked simultaneous access to the Pāli text while taking notes.

## 2. Notebook interface: Rhys Davids SBE English

T. W. Rhys Davids, *The Questions of King Milinda*, Part I, Sacred Books of the East 35 (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1890), supplies English translations and a structured Book / Chapter / numbered-question apparatus.

Several notebook loci align closely enough to identify the immediate working interface.

### A. `ultimate point of time is not apparent`

Rhys Davids, Book II, Chapter 3, question 2, translates Nāgasena's argument with the recurrent wording:

`the ultimate point of time ... is not apparent`.

He then gives the circular causal example:

`eye + forms -> sight -> touch -> sensation -> longing -> action (Karma) -> eye once more produced`.

Notebook 004 uses the same problem, substantially the same English vocabulary, and a compressed `MP II ...` structural reference in the flux/time dossier.

This is stronger than generic thematic agreement because the distinctive English wording and question architecture coincide.

### B. `that which has not been, becomes ... dissolves away again`

Rhys Davids, Book II, Chapter 3, question 3, gives the formulation that what was not comes into being and, once begun, dissolves again. Notebook 004's adjacent flux/time notes reproduce this English problem and use the corresponding `MP II ...` style of reference.

### C. dependent-origination chain

Rhys Davids, Book II, Chapter 3, question 4, translates the sequence:

`eye/forms -> sight -> contact -> sensation -> longing -> grasping -> becoming -> birth -> old age/death...`.

Notebook MS95 records substantially this same English causal sequence as a Milinda witness and uses it to compare alternative nidāna groupings.

### D. soul / palace-window reductio

Rhys Davids's same Book II/Chapter 3 sequence gives the `living principle` inside the body and palace-window reductio. Notebook 004 separately cites Milindapañha `II.3.6` for this anti-substantialist sensory-organ argument.

Taken together, the notebook evidence is best described as:

> **Lovejoy's working Milinda notes use Rhys Davids's English translation as a visible reading/citation interface.**

This does not prove exclusivity. A student in Lanman's advanced Pāli course could consult translation and Pāli in parallel.

## 3. Published article: the references resolve to Trenckner's 1880 Pāli edition

Vilhelm Trenckner, ed., *The Milindapañho: Being Dialogues between King Milinda and the Buddhist Sage Nāgasena* (London: Williams and Norgate, 1880), is a 430-page Roman-script Pāli edition.

The 1898 Lovejoy article's Milinda references are not Rhys Davids English-page references. JSTOR's extracted footnote register preserves:

- `Milinda Pañha, p. 65. 11`;
- `MP. p. 32. 12`;
- `Milinda Pañha, p. 60`.

Direct collation against Trenckner closes all three.

### 3.1 `Milinda Pañha, p. 65. 11` — karma differentiates conditions

Trenckner p.65 asks whether the five sense-bases are produced by different karmas or one karma and answers that they arise through **different karmas**. The page then asks why human beings differ in longevity, sickness, appearance, status, wealth, birth and intelligence, and answers through differences of karma.

This exactly fits Lovejoy's published proposition that **karma determines the particular condition in which one is reborn**.

The page therefore confirms that Lovejoy's p.65 citation is to the Pāli-edition pagination, not the English SBE pagination.

### 3.2 `MP. p. 32. 12` — upādāna and renewed existence

Trenckner p.32 contains the explicit dialogue:

`Sace mahārāja saupādāno bhavissāmi paṭisandahissāmi, sace anupādāno bhavissāmi na paṭisandahissāmi`.

The proposition is direct: if there is upādāna, renewed conjunction/rebirth follows; without upādāna it does not.

This is precisely the textual burden for Lovejoy's paired published claim that **upādāna accounts for the fact of rebirth itself**.

The reference is especially diagnostic because Rhys Davids's English printed page numbers are not 32 here; `32.12` is intelligible as Trenckner page/line-style citation.

### 3.3 `Milinda Pañha, p. 60` — vedanā inside the mental sequence

Trenckner p.60 states that where mind-consciousness arises, contact, `vedanā`, perception, volition, thought and reflection arise. It then explicitly asks `kiṃlakkhaṇā vedanā` and defines the characteristic of vedanā as experiencing/undergoing; p.61 continues with pleasant/reward examples.

This directly matches the published footnote's role in Lovejoy's psychological reconstruction of the middle nidānas, where sensation is the point at which pleasure/pain determinations enter before desire and upādāna.

Thus all three published Milinda references resolve proposition-for-proposition to Trenckner's Pāli text.

## 4. The change is not merely bibliographic formatting

The contrast is now source-critical rather than cosmetic.

### Working notebook

Visible interface:

`Rhys Davids English translation`

`+ Book / Chapter / numbered-question structure`

`+ English conceptual phrasing`

`-> Milinda used as multi-problem reading dossier`.

### Published article

Visible citation interface:

`Trenckner Roman-script Pāli edition`

`+ exact page / line-level loci`

`-> Milinda used as proposition-specific textual witness`.

The combined shift is:

> **translation-mediated problem acquisition -> Pāli-edition textual warrant in publication.**

This is a source-resolution upgrade at the level of published proof.

## 5. Relation to Lovejoy's Harvard Pāli training

Harvard's individual student card directly shows Lovejoy enrolled in `Sanskrit 5`, grade A, in 1896–97. The contemporaneous course announcement identifies Course 5 as Lanman's advanced Pāli course, `The Sacred Books of Buddhism`, reading Dhamma Pada, Sutta Nipāta and Majjhima Nikāya.

The source-interface result is therefore historically coherent with an actor who was simultaneously receiving formal advanced Pāli training.

But the causal claim remains bounded:

- DIRECT: advanced Pāli enrollment, A;
- DIRECT: notebook visibly uses Rhys Davids English Milinda as a working interface;
- DIRECT: 1898 article cites loci that resolve to Trenckner's Pāli edition;
- NOT PROVED: Lanman personally required the Trenckner edition;
- NOT PROVED: Lovejoy shifted from translation to Pāli only because of Lanman;
- NOT PROVED: every notebook Milinda note was made before he consulted the Pāli text.

The important fact is coexistence and publication resolution, not a simplistic pedagogical influence story.

## 6. Publication selection now has four observable dimensions

The project can now distinguish four transformations between working dossier and print:

### A. object selection

`Milinda-pañha as announced named object`

`-> Buddhist technical terms as primary object`.

### B. witness selection

Notebook Milinda problems on flux, self, time, causal sequence, vedanā, rebirth and Nirvāṇa

`-> only locally useful Milinda functions retained explicitly in print`.

### C. proposition selection

Broad metaphysical/systemic problems

`-> narrower semantic and causal propositions`.

### D. source-resolution selection

`Rhys Davids English working interface`

`-> Trenckner Pāli page/line citation as printed warrant`.

This fourth dimension materially strengthens the concept of **publication selection**. Publication does not merely reduce quantity; it can change the evidentiary resolution at which surviving material is presented and warranted.

## 7. Historiographical consequence

The strongest safe claim is not `Lovejoy stopped using translations and began reading Pāli`.

It is:

> **The surviving notebook and published article present the Milinda at different evidentiary resolutions. In the working notebook, Lovejoy visibly uses Rhys Davids's English translation and question structure to generate and organize multiple problems. In the 1898 article, the surviving Milinda claims are cited against exact loci in Trenckner's Roman-script Pāli edition. The publication pathway therefore combines object narrowing and witness selection with a source-resolution upgrade.**

This matters for the larger history-of-humanities argument because it gives a material example of how scholarly authority is reconfigured between reading, note-taking and publication: the source that enters as translated argument can leave as primary-language textual warrant.
