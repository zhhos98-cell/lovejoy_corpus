## 2. The notebook begins by importing an explicit problem of proof

PDF pp.4–5 / ms pp.8–9 are headed:

`The Method of the Sankhya / Garbe p.150`.

The passage directly takes up Garbe 1894 on pramāṇa and the inference that unusually dense agreement in terminology, definitions, details, and examples can identify a logical apparatus as an `alien element` adopted into Sāṃkhya.

Batch160 independently reconstructs the genealogy of Garbe's rule. In 1892 Garbe had already made the Sāṃkhya/Buddhism burden of proof explicit:

- generic resemblance is weak;
- extant documents should control conjectural reconstruction;
- diagnostic details / involuntary formal peculiarities can carry stronger genealogical weight;
- connection and direction are distinct historical inferences.

Only **Garbe 1894 → Lovejoy 004** is direct documentary transmission. Garbe 1892 is the genealogy of the rule in Garbe, not yet a directly proven Lovejoy source.

This is one of the most important evidentiary controls for the blog: Lovejoy's substantive notebook opens inside a methodological problem already being argued by specialists.

## 3. The cleanest Lovejoy authorial hinge is genuinely visible

The strongest sentence-level shift remains the Rhys Davids block around PDF pp.16–17.

After digesting the Sāṃkhya/Buddhism chronology and Brahmajāla problem, Lovejoy refuses to let one early passage prove that the later Sāṃkhya system `existed as a whole`. He then explicitly marks supplementation:

`To R.D.'s remark it should be added ...`

and proposes a more discriminating diagnostic: not merely eternity of Self/Matter, but the separateness of Self and World / `thorough-going dualism` central to Sāṃkhya liberation.

The operation is observable without projecting mature `unit-idea` language backwards:

`source uptake`
→ `identify an insufficient diagnostic`
→ `restrict the scale of the claim`
→ `propose a more discriminating test`
→ `search for a better witness`.

This is exactly the kind of close-up that should carry the blog's Quellenforschung.

## 4. `upādisesa` remains the best second close-up because it shows proof partition in action

The final third of 004 distinguishes:

- textual witness;
- meaning in contextual use;
- doctrinal/causal function;
- etymology/text history/genealogy.

Lovejoy can argue strongly from Sutta-Nipāta and related usage that `upādisesa/anupādisesa` marks an ethical condition of residual attachment/freedom rather than simply persistence or disappearance of physical aggregates, while the later publication still admits contrary passages and unsettled etymology.

This is the cleanest case where a lower-level semantic/doctrinal result can remain strong while a higher-level historical/textual result stays open.

Crucial correction retained from Batch73:

> **004 is not a notebook of generalized genealogical skepticism.**

Lovejoy sometimes advances strong historical/compositional hypotheses. What persists is **proof-level separation**: an L4 historical claim has to be argued as an L4 claim rather than smuggled in from a weaker resemblance or lexical association.

# II. 004 and the 1898 publication: the relationship is stronger than a simple draft chain

## 5. The 1898 JAOS article publicly validates the inferential distinctions

`The Buddhistic Technical Terms upādāna and upādisesa`, JAOS 19 (1898), 126–136, remains the secure public endpoint of the notebook line.

The published article explicitly performs several operations visible in 004:

1. checks what Senart's cited passages actually prove before accepting the semantic equation `upādāna = upādānakkhandha`;
2. separates the causal work of karma from the causal work of upādāna;
3. allows terms, perhaps even ideas, to be historically borrowed while their `arrangement and application` remain original/characteristic;
4. says competing etymologies can both be possible, so meaning must be settled by **use** rather than etymology;
5. labels evidence `relevant but not necessarily conclusive`;
6. ends with a preferred reading while acknowledging contrary texts and saying the question cannot be considered finally settled.

This is direct published evidence for calibrated warrants, not a retrospective methodological abstraction imposed only by us.

# III. The actual controversy supports a methodological reading

## 7. The participants should be described as occupying different evidentiary positions, not as six sealed `methodologies`

Final safe map:

### Garbe

Diagnostic specificity. Generic parallels are weak; dense formal/detail-level correspondence can identify historical connection/borrowed material. Direction is separately argued.

### Rhys Davids

Chronological/documentary brake. Older doctrines or isolated thinkers may be plausible while extant Sāṃkhya textual witnesses are too late to establish the whole later system at Buddha's date.

### Senart

Compositional/structural reconstruction. Duplication and heterogeneity inside the nidāna formula can carry a stratigraphic argument for later composite formation.

### Jacobi

Systematic analogy / causal-systemic reconstruction is allowed to do more genealogical work; Sāṃkhya-Yoga categories can illuminate the historical formation of Buddhist causal structure.

### Oldenberg

Resemblance is admitted but controlled by lexical usage, local function, source chronology, and internal textual evidence; `Familienähnlichkeit` does not by itself establish direct filiation.

### Lovejoy

Recomposes the inherited dispute and repeatedly polices the **unit/scale of inference**: term, doctrine, element, arrangement, system, genealogy are not interchangeable propositions.

This is a shared philological field with different proof thresholds and weights, not a tidy taxonomy of mutually exclusive methodological schools.

---

## 8. Reader-facing abstraction should remain three questions, not the full internal ladder

The seven-level internal reconstruction remains useful for source control:

`witness/admissibility → resemblance/classification → diagnosticity → scale/unit of inference → function/configuration → genealogy/dependence → direction/chronology`.

But the blog should compress it to:

1. **What resembles what?**
2. **What exactly has that resemblance proved — a word, a doctrine, an element, an arrangement, or a whole system?**
3. **When may resemblance become a historical genealogy?**

This is sufficient to let a general intellectual-history reader exit the specialist Buddhist material quickly.

Best transition remains:

> **At close range, this looks like a dispute over Pāli terminology. At a slightly greater distance, it becomes a dispute over the permissible scale of historical inference.**

# IV. Classical / Oriental scholarship: the widening move is now unusually well supported

## 9. Max Müller's binary is usable only as retrospective disciplinary self-staging

Müller's 1901 autobiography exactly remembers his 1869 Académie election as a struggle:

`between Mommsen and myself, between classical and Oriental scholarship`, adding that `for once Oriental scholarship carried the day`.

Official French material independently confirms Müller's 1869 election to Welcker's seat and Mommsen's later election in 1895. A full 1869 candidate/ballot record proving the exact final Müller-vs-Mommsen matchup has **not** been recovered; therefore `between Mommsen and myself` remains Müller's retrospective symbolic account at that level.

The 1867 Bopp-seat competition, however, officially placed Mommsen and Müller on the same candidate list alongside Lepsius, with De Rossi and Diez added. The actual institutional field was thus more mixed than Müller's later binary.

Use Müller because the phrase makes boundary-making visible, not because it accurately maps two sealed disciplines.

---

## 10. EPHE 1869 and Harvard institutional history independently dissolve the clean binary

In 1869 the new EPHE historical-philological Bibliothèque made Max Müller's `La stratification du langage` part of its first publication number together with Georg Curtius's work on chronology in Indo-Germanic language formation. Translation personnel crossed the later labels: Louis Havet and Abel Bergaigne participated in this circulation.

Harvard supplies an even cleaner local example relevant to Lovejoy. Its official departmental history states that Sanskrit was first taught in 1872 by **James Bradstreet Greenough, a Latin grammarian**, who offered Sanskrit and comparative philology as **Latin electives**; Lanman's arrival in 1880 then created a more distinct Indo-Iranian departmental structure.

Therefore:

> **the relevant nineteenth-century story is differentiation inside a porous philological field, not a collision between two already sealed scholarly traditions.**

This is excellent contextual widening because it explains how `Oriental` scholarship could later appear external to a disciplinary history whose methods had in fact circulated through it.

---

## 11. James Turner's `Philology` in the uploaded corpus independently supports this anti-binary model

The master/file-library sweep surfaced relevant Turner passages already present in the corpus. Turner describes nineteenth-century philology as differentiating into increasingly autonomous areas while boundaries remained porous; Sanskrit, classical studies, comparative Indo-European grammar, biblical philology, history, and literature separated gradually out of a once broader learned field.

This secondary history is compatible with the primary institutional controls above and gives a high-quality interpretive framework without making Müller carry the entire disciplinary-history burden.

# V. The transregional correction survives, but `non-Western origin` needs disciplined wording

## 12. Bankim and Rajendralal Mitra remain decisive first-order controls

### Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay, 1871

His `Buddhism and the Sankhya Philosophy` responds directly to the Sāṃkhya/Buddhism problem and separates:

`similarity`
from
`identity`
from
`direction of dependence`.

The essay's structure itself postpones the question whether Buddhism borrowed from Kapila or Kapila from Buddhism after arguing for similarity.

Publication-grade verbatim quotation should still be checked against the 1871 *Calcutta Review* page images because the easiest searchable transcription derives from a later collected text.

### Rajendralal Mitra, 1883

His Patañjali preface distinguishes:

`pre-existing philosophical materials`
from
`formation of a complete system`,

and separately rejects historical genealogy from mere phonetic resemblance without evidence `worth the name`.

The bricks/architect metaphor is especially close in logical form to 004's refusal to infer a whole system from a doctrine/element.

### Evidentiary ceiling

No evidence links Bankim or Mitra directly to Lovejoy's reading. No evidence makes them the source of Garbe's protocol.

Their function is geographical/intellectual-field control:

> **the proof problem itself was transregional.**

It was not simply a European method applied to passive Indian material.

## 13. Final wording on origins

Avoid:

- `the history of ideas had a non-Western origin`;
- `India invented the history of ideas`;
- `Buddhism produced Lovejoy's method`.

Preferred:

> **the methodological prehistory of the history of ideas was not exclusively intra-Western.**

Or:

> **the history of ideas did not acquire all of its historical problems by looking inward at a Western philosophical canon.**

Strongest anti-overclaim formulation:

> **The history of ideas did not begin outside the West; but neither did its methodological prehistory unfold entirely within the West's later canonical self-description.**

This formulation now seems more durable than using `non-Western origin` unqualified.

- `Skinner was wrong about Lovejoy`;

# VII. Two newly important 2026 methodological controls

## 17. The 2026 British Academy volume on Skinner makes the proposed ending unusually timely

A genuinely useful new web result is Adrian Blau ed., *Meaning and Understanding in the History of Ideas and Beyond* (Proceedings of the British Academy, 2026), with Skinner's own rejoinder.

The volume explicitly revisits and expands the 1969 essay. Particularly relevant is Humeira Iqtidar's chapter:

`Meaning, Context, Interpretations in the Islamic Tradition: Provincialising Skinnerian Contextualism`.

Its premise is that contextual/intention-sensitive interpretive practices long predate their canonical 1970s Anglo-American methodological formulation in Islamic traditions.

This is not evidence for Lovejoy, but it changes the **current historiographical weather**: provincializing the genealogy of contextualist/intellectual-history method is an active 2026 problem.

Our article should not imitate Iqtidar's argument or claim priority over it. Instead, it can make a distinct archival intervention on the *Lovejoy side* of the famous methodological opposition:

> the canonical figure whom Skinner helped turn into a methodological foil also had an early scholarly formation outside the later Western-canon story through which the history of ideas commonly remembers itself.

This makes the Skinner opening more timely, not merely textbook-like.

# XII. Final thesis options

## Safest one-sentence thesis

> **Arthur Lovejoy's `Sankhya + Buddhism` notebook and his 1898 Buddhist article place the future architect of the history of ideas inside a transregional philological controversy over the permissible levels of historical inference — when resemblance could establish a term, a doctrine, an element, a system, or a genealogy — suggesting that the methodological prehistory of the history of ideas cannot be reconstructed solely from debates internal to a later Western philosophical canon.**

## More blog-like version

> **Before the history of ideas became a method, Lovejoy was learning how to make historical claims in an unexpected place: a nineteenth-century dispute over Buddhism and Sāṃkhya in which scholars were arguing about what resemblance was entitled to prove.**

## Strongest concise conceptual line

> **Before the history of ideas became a method, Lovejoy was apprenticed in a controversy over the grammar of historical inference.**

Use `apprenticed` metaphorically and avoid implying one master/teacher or a clean pedagogical transmission.
