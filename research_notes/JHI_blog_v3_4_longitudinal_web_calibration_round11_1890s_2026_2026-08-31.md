# Core result

The long web calibration produces **one real wording correction** and several major confidence upgrades.

It does **not** overturn the blog's architecture.

The most important correction is to the second paragraph. The current formulation says:

> `The biographical fact survived. What survived less clearly was the work inside the fact.`

That sentence was safe against Wilson/Schaffer and Lovejoy historiography alone. It becomes too broad once specialist Buddhist reception is included.

The 1898 article itself demonstrably survived as Buddhist scholarship:

- listed under `BUDDHIST BIBLIOGRAPHY` by the Pali Text Society in 1902–03;
- cited by E. J. Thomas in 1933;
- retained in Rupert Gethin's early-Buddhist scholarship;
- reconstructed and criticized by Soonil Hwang in 2006;
- still placed in a live `upādi` interpretive genealogy in a 2024 Pāli monograph;
- still held by a current Buddhist digital library.

Therefore the stronger and more accurate historiographical relation is:

`SPECIALIST BUDDHIST WORK SURVIVES`
while
`LOVEJOY HISTORIOGRAPHY ORGANIZES HIS CAREER AROUND OTHER OBJECTS / ROLES`.

The blog's thesis thus shifts slightly from:

`BIOGRAPHICAL FACT PRESENT / SCHOLARLY WORK DIM` 

toward:

> **THE SAME EARLY WORK REMAINED VISIBLE IN DIFFERENT SCHOLARLY CLASSIFICATIONS, BUT ITS ANALYTICAL CENTRALITY DIVERGED ACROSS THEM.**

This is not a retreat. It is a stronger empirical version of:

> `The formation was known but differently classified.`
