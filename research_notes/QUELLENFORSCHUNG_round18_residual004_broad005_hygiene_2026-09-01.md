## II. Notebook 005 — early future-life block revisited

### pp.3–5 — moral recompense versus continuity

The original images make the architecture of the opening argument much clearer.

p.3 directly asks `But what is meant by moral notions?` and then argues that if the rich remain rich in the next life, this is continuity, not `recompense for virtue`. The moral category requires that future reward/punishment be attached to conduct/desert, not merely to the persistence of terrestrial rank, power, or status.

p.4 extends the same distinction to religious devotion. Faithfulness in sacrifice does not automatically imply a moral idea. The relevant relation may instead be `purely quasi-mechanical`.

p.5 closes the block with a historical-lag observation: the other world is `not properly a copy of the present world of today but of yesterday`; its supposed morality is often `traditional & obsolete`.

Safe synthesis:

`AFTERLIFE DIFFERENCE != MORAL ESCHATOLOGY`

unless the difference is tied to desert/conduct rather than status continuity, ritual mechanism, or inherited social forms.

This also means that Lovejoy's use of `primitive` here is not merely a rank-label. It identifies a configuration in which several determinants of post-mortem fate remain analytically non-equivalent.

## VI. Methodological consequence of the widened pass

The newly controlled pages reinforce a recurrent operation across topics that are independent of the 1898 Buddhist technical-terms paper:

1. distinguish a visible/practical form from the mechanism that gives it meaning;
2. distinguish persistence/continuity from moral or causal significance;
3. distinguish generic resemblance from a structurally central relation;
4. propose developmental direction;
5. test direction against chronology, source conflict, or alternative pathways;
6. reverse or qualify the developmental route when the evidence requires it.

The p.104 correction is particularly useful because it shows why clean directionality matters. A first-pass summary can preserve the general topic while reversing the historical arrow. For publication-facing argument, arrows must therefore be witness-controlled, not inferred from thematic proximity.
