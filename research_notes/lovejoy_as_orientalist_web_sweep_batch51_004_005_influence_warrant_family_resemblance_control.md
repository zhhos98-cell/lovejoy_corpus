# Batch 51 — 004→005: influence warrant, ambient resemblance, and the internal evidentiary control

## Why the newly uploaded 004 sources matter for the 005 paper

The Jacobi–Oldenberg–Senart–Dahlmann controversy that Lovejoy was actively working through in notebook 004 is not merely background on Buddhism and Sāṃkhya. It is itself a late-nineteenth-century debate about **what kind of evidence licenses a claim of intellectual dependence**.

That makes 004 an unusually useful methodological control for the present reconstruction of Lovejoy, Marillier, Mauss, and notebook 005. It supplies a historically internal vocabulary for distinguishing direct transmission from resemblance.

The central point is simple: **similarity is not yet influence**. The threshold for a transmission claim rises when the relevant vocabulary or conceptual form may have been ambient, widely shared, or independently available.

## 1. Jacobi: from structural correspondence to principled influence

Hermann Jacobi, in *Über das Verhältnis der buddhistischen Philosophie zu Sāṃkhya-Yoga und die Bedeutung der Nidānas* (1898), defends the proposition that Buddhism did not merely borrow isolated Sāṃkhya terms. Because concepts such as saṃskāra recur beyond a single formula and because the Buddhist causal sequence can be structurally aligned with Sāṃkhya-Yoga, he argues for a **`principiellen`**, even **`vorbildlichen`**, influence.

Yet Jacobi also qualifies what such influence means. Buddhism is not an `Abklatsch` of a finished Sāṃkhya system. It is an `individuelle Umgestaltung`; Sāṃkhya is the ground on which Buddhism grew rather than a finished model copied item by item. He further argues that Sāṃkhya ideas were a kind of `geistiges Gemeingut` of the period and could circulate outside sharply bounded philosophical schools.

This already produces an ambiguity that matters for method: a doctrine can be historically formative even when it is not transmitted through a single text, teacher, or bounded school.

## 2. Oldenberg: `Denk-atmosphäre` and `Familienähnlichkeit` as negative controls

Oldenberg's *Buddhistische Studien* makes the evidentiary objection explicit. Replying to Jacobi's claim of a principled/model influence, he proposes that a term such as saṃskāra may simply have belonged to the general **`Denk-atmosphäre`** in which early Buddhism lived. If a concept was at home in a wider sphere of shared thought and expression, parallel speculative uses need not be derived from one another.

His alternative is a **`Familienähnlichkeit`** arising from common `Denk- und Ausdrucksweise`.

This is exactly the control needed for the 005/Mauss problem. `Mana`, `force`, `efficacy`, `communion`, `sacrifice`, `taboo`, and related vocabulary were not proprietary tokens. Lexical or thematic overlap between Lovejoy and Mauss therefore cannot carry a direct-influence claim by itself.

Oldenberg's objection gives us a period-appropriate formulation of the negative control:

> when a term belongs to a shared intellectual atmosphere, resemblance may record common availability rather than transmission.

## 3. Senart: direct derivation requires detailed/formal coincidence; historical layers must be separated

Senart's contribution in the *Mélanges Charles de Harlez* sharpens the standard further. Against Jacobi's stronger derivation, he argues that a **direct and immediate derivation** would require detailed, formal coincidences of a kind he does not think the evidence supplies.

At the same time, Senart does not retreat to complete independence. After Kern, Garbe, and Jacobi, he says it is difficult to doubt that Sāṃkhya-Yoga had some `part d'action` on Buddhism. The problem is to determine **the conditions and character** of that action. Even within early Buddhism, he insists, several periods must be distinguished: one must separate elements incorporated at the origins from appropriations completed later.

This is methodologically close to the operation Lovejoy performs in both notebooks:

- accept mixture and borrowing where warranted;
- reject a single undifferentiated derivation;
- distinguish historical strata;
- ask which specific relation produced which component.

Senart's own treatment of the nidāna formula as a potentially late amalgam of previously independent categories was directly copied, criticized, and reworked by Lovejoy in 004. Lovejoy accepts compositeness and pre-existing materials while rejecting the claim that the sequence is therefore technically unintelligible.

## 4. Dahlmann: parallel is not enough unless the compared trait is discriminating

Joseph Dahlmann's *Nirvāṇa* occupies a useful middle position. He accepts important structural correspondences between Buddhist and Sāṃkhya psychological categories and regards Jacobi as having materially strengthened the case for historical connection. But he repeatedly asks whether a proposed parallel is **discriminating**: if the compared idea belongs equally to other Indian systems, it cannot by itself decide historical derivation.

His use of `Familienähnlichkeit` is therefore not a denial of historical continuity; it is a demand that the comparison identify features specific enough to discriminate among possible sources.

## 5. Lovejoy's own 004 practice: ambient common material versus structurally unmotivated import

The important result is that Lovejoy does not simply take one side in this dispute. His working notes use both evidentiary moves.

On the one hand, he writes that the khandha formula is probably **pre-Buddhistic**, `a thing current at the period` in which Buddhism developed and which Buddhism takes for granted. Here common availability is sufficient; no uniquely identifiable donor is required.

On the other hand, when he considers the position of **avijjā** at the head of the nidāna series, he argues that Buddhism's practical interpretation of ignorance would not by itself justify the term's ontological/structural role. For that role, he says, **`There was necessary ... the influence of an outside tradition.`** He makes a similar structural argument about saṅkhāra.

Lovejoy's own warrant is therefore conditional:

- if a feature is widely current and internally intelligible, ambient transmission may suffice;
- if a feature occupies a structurally specific role not explained by the receiving system's own problem, an external historical source becomes more plausible.

This is much subtler than a generic doctrine of `influence`.

## 6. Consequence for 005: influence should be treated as an evidentiary conjunction, not a resemblance relation

The 004 controversy supplies a useful rule for the Marillier/Mauss/Lovejoy reconstruction:

**Do not infer transmission from resemblance alone. Require converging evidence that discriminates transmission from shared atmosphere.**

For the 005 paper, the strongest transmission claims do not depend on words such as mana, communion, taboo, or force. They depend on an evidentiary conjunction:

1. **temporal/institutional adjacency** — Lovejoy is in Marillier's 1898–99 research environment;
2. **demonstrable paper/source circulation** — Marillier circulates bibliography/course notes to Mauss, and teacher notes/dossiers to Söderblom;
3. **exact bibliographic nodes** — e.g. Steinmetz's *Endokannibalismus* appears in 005 while Marillier had reviewed it and the same problem was live in the seminar environment;
4. **proposition-level specificity** — not just shared topics, but the same difficult classificatory problem (e.g. what transfer through eating counts as: communion, magic, funerary solidarity, etc.);
5. **transformation rather than repetition** — Lovejoy often reclassifies rather than copies the proposition;
6. **negative controls for ambient vocabulary** — shared terms alone are explicitly insufficient.

This is why the current model should remain **Marillier-mediated adjacency**, not `Mauss influenced Lovejoy`.

The Mauss–Lovejoy comparison becomes strongest precisely where it does **not** need a direct-contact claim: Mauss classifies transfer of a dead relative's virtues through eating as communion, whereas Lovejoy treats analogous quality-transfer as potentially sympathetic/transferable efficacy and makes sacramental union exceptional. The historically demonstrated common workshop/source environment explains why the two propositions belong in the same comparison; the divergence itself prevents a crude copying model.

## 7. Paper-level payoff

This 004 control improves the 005 argument in two ways.

First, it provides an internal historical standard for the paper's own causal language. We are not imposing a modern prohibition against `influence`; Lovejoy was reading a controversy in which his contemporaries explicitly debated the difference between principled influence, common intellectual atmosphere, family resemblance, detailed formal coincidence, and layered appropriation.

Second, it makes the documentary argument stronger than the old influence model. The paper can say:

> **We treat influence not as a resemblance relation but as an evidentiary conjunction.** Shared vocabulary is controlled by the possibility of `Denk-atmosphäre` and `Familienähnlichkeit`; stronger claims require institutional proximity, demonstrable circulation, discriminating bibliographic/propositional matches, and evidence of transformation.

That formulation is especially appropriate for the 005 case because the archive supplies exactly those non-lexical controls.

## 8. Drafting rule

Use three levels of claim consistently:

- **shared problem-space / ambient convergence** — lexical or thematic similarity only;
- **mediated adjacency / probable research transmission** — institutional + paper/source circulation + discriminating problem match;
- **direct influence / direct reading** — only when a citation, correspondence, borrowing record, or other direct trace closes the chain.

Under this rule, direct Mauss→Lovejoy influence remains **not established**. Marillier-mediated adjacency is **strongly supported**. Lovejoy's later transformation of mechanism-sensitive comparative work into primitive energetics is a **strong historical inference grounded in the notebook and 1906 endpoint**, not a claim that any one teacher supplied the mature theory.
