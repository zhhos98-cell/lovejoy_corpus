# Archive-side Batch 114 — Office-of-Secretary provenance narrowing and the Stanford incoming/outgoing manifestation split

Date: 2026-08-19  
Status: synced  
Scope: continue Batch113's deterministic retrieval work. This batch does not seek another broad Lovejoy name hit. It asks two narrower archival questions: which *current Johns Hopkins filing domain* is most plausible for the 1916-17 Bertrand Russell invitation administration, and which *Stanford correspondence manifestation domain* can legitimately be searched for a possible second witness to canonical `GLA0056`, Lovejoy to Orrin L. Elliott, 15 April 1901.

## Core result

Batch114 adds **no new GLA component**. It materially narrows both retrieval problems.

At Johns Hopkins, a previously underweighted institutional record group becomes a high-value lead: **Office of the Secretary records, RG-10-001**. The official JHU collection description dates the record group from **1916 to 1993**. Its first subgroup is **P. Stewart Macaulay, 1916-1957**, and Subgroup 1, Series 1 covers **1916-1954** and consists primarily of **correspondence and memoranda**. That date and record type are almost exactly aligned with the 1916-17 Russell invitation/appointment episode reconstructed through BRACERS `121121`, `121122`, and `3597`.

This becomes `GAL0046`.

The claim is deliberately limited. RG-10-001 is a strong *provenance-shaped retrieval domain*; there is not yet a public Lovejoy, Russell, Woods, or Goodnow child record establishing that any of the BRACERS objects is physically there.

At the same time, the current JHU **Philosophy Department records, RG-04-120** can be downgraded for this particular question. The record group begins in **1922**, and its Administrative Records series begins in **1942**. It unquestionably contains later Lovejoy material, including the Lovejoy Fellowship correspondence, but its present temporal scope does not license an assumption that it is the continuous original departmental filing system for a 1916 Russell-invitation document. Later copies or retrospective references remain possible.

Stanford requires a parallel correction. The official SC0058 history says that early incoming letters and loose carbons/drafts of outgoing letters were copied into letterpress books. But exact archival citations from later research demonstrate that at least in the 1906 philosophy search, **received letters** from John Dewey and William James are cited to **Series I-A**, whereas David Starr Jordan's **outgoing letters** are cited to **Series I-AA** with box/volume/page locators. A 1901 scholarly citation also identifies a `Private Letterbook` for an outgoing Jordan letter, while another 1901 outgoing Jordan letter is cited as `Stanford Business Correspondence`.

Therefore the generic word **letterbook** is not a sufficient locator for `GLA0056`. Lovejoy -> Elliott is an incoming communication. Batch113's idea of testing a contemporaneous letterpress witness remains valid, but Batch114 removes any temptation to equate that test automatically with Series I-AA or the 1901 Private Letterbook. The exact incoming-copy book/series covering 15 April 1901 must be identified first.

## 1. JHU: four current custody domains now have different evidentiary weights

### A. MS-0003 — Frank Johnson Goodnow papers

JHU's official arrangement rule is operationally precise:

`writer -> alphabetical file -> copies of Goodnow replies attached`.

BRACERS `121121` is independently identified as **Woods -> Frank J. Goodnow**. If the object survives in MS-0003, its filing key is therefore already known: **Woods**. The correct first request is the Woods writer file, including any attached Goodnow reply.

This rule still cannot be carried across to canonical `GLA0030` / BRACERS `121122`. That object is a Lovejoy / Department of Philosophy document about the Russell proposal, but the public BRACERS note does not expose its recipient. Catalogue adjacency with a Goodnow-directed document is not recipient evidence.

Official control: `https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/11`

### B. RG-10-001 — Office of the Secretary records

The current JHU collection list gives:

- record group dates: **1916-1993**;
- Subgroup 1: **P. Stewart Macaulay, 1916-1957**;
- Subgroup 1, Series 1: **P. Stewart Macaulay, 1916-1954**;
- record type: primarily **correspondence and memoranda**.

This is the best new institutional search domain in this batch because its chronological start and document types coincide with the Russell episode.

It becomes `GAL0046`.

The first inspection should be tightly bounded to 1916-17 and search not just personal names but administrative vocabulary:

`Woods | Goodnow | Russell | Lovejoy | Philosophy | lecture | invitation | appointment | visiting | salary/terms`.

Official control: JHU collection list, `Office of the Secretary records, RG-10-001`.

### C. RG-02-001 — Office of the President records

This remains `GAL0045`, the broader institutional fallback. It spans the relevant years and is institutionally plausible for presidential invitation/appointment business. It must stay separate from MS-0003 because Goodnow's personal papers and the President's office records are different provenance domains even when the same officeholder participates in both.

Official control: `https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/834`

### D. RG-04-120 — Philosophy Department records

The current finding aid dates the record group **1922-1993** and Administrative Records **1942-1989**. It also explicitly identifies correspondence with Lovejoy in the later `Lovejoy Fellowship` file.

For the 1916 Russell episode, that evidence changes the search order. RG-04-120 should **not** be treated as the obvious direct successor to a 1916 Philosophy Department file merely because Lovejoy acted through the department. Current record-group chronology does not establish continuous custody back to 1916.

This is not an absence statement. The department existed and generated documents before 1922; later departmental records may preserve copies, retrospective histories, or transferred material. The point is narrower: the current finding aid does not support direct-filing continuity for this transaction.

Official control: `https://aspace.library.jhu.edu/repositories/3/resources/863`

The resulting current search order is:

`121121 Woods -> Goodnow: MS-0003 Woods file first`

`1916-17 institutional packet: RG-10-001 -> RG-02-001`

`121122 Lovejoy document: do not assign a current fonds until recipient/object evidence appears`

`RG-04-120: later-copy / retrospective route only unless new provenance evidence changes the chronology`.

## 2. Temporal coverage can rule out a filing assumption without proving historical absence

This batch adds a useful negative-control principle.

A record group beginning in 1922 cannot automatically be used as the **continuous current custody path** for a document created in 1916. That is a statement about the scope of the described archival record group, not about whether the office existed, whether a document was once created, or whether an older document could have been copied into later files.

The distinction is:

`office existed in 1916`

`!= current record group contains continuous files from 1916`

`!= no 1916 document ever existed`

`!= no later copy can occur in the current record group`.

This prevents institutional-name continuity from being mistaken for archival-series continuity.

## 3. Stanford: the official copying rule remains strong, but series identity is now directional

The official David Starr Jordan papers, `SC0058`, record a complex early filing system. Jordan and Orrin Leslie Elliott used adjacent offices and later shared a secretary; the nominal categories President's Office, General Letters, and University Letters became heavily intermixed. The same guide states that **incoming letters and loose carbons/drafts of outgoing letters were copied into letterpress books**.

Official control: `https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/tf3f59n6bn`

That archival-process statement justified Batch113's second-manifestation test for `GLA0056`.

Batch114 now adds direction controls from exact scholarly citations.

A reconstruction of Stanford's 1906 philosophy search cites:

- John Dewey -> Jordan, 7 Apr 1906: `SC0058, Series I-A, Box 49, file 488`;
- William James -> Jordan, 8 Feb 1906: `SC0058, Series I-A, Box 48, file 480`;
- William James -> Jordan, 19 Apr 1906: `SC0058, Series I-A, Box 49, file 489`;
- Jordan -> J. R. Angell, 30 Jan 1906: `Series I-AA, Box 16, vol. 33, p. 214`;
- further Jordan outgoing letters to Perry, James, Dewey and others: `Series I-AA`, boxes 16-17, vols. 33-35, with page locators.

Control: Christopher D. Green's archival reconstruction of the Stanford philosophy search, `https://www.yorku.ca/christo/papers/JRAngell-Stanford.htm`.

The evidence supports a directional distinction **for the cited 1906 material**:

`Series I-A -> received correspondence examples`

`Series I-AA -> outgoing Jordan letterbook examples`.

It does **not** prove that the same box/file/series arrangement applies unchanged to 1901, nor that an incoming Lovejoy-to-Elliott letterpress copy belongs to Series I-A. It simply prevents the wrong shortcut: `letterbook = Series I-AA = search here for GLA0056`.

## 4. Near-date 1901 controls show multiple Stanford manifestation labels

Elena S. Danielson's study of Alexis Babine supplies two particularly useful near-date archival controls:

- David Starr Jordan -> Alexis V. Babine, **6 Mar 1901**, cited to the `David Starr Jordan Papers, Private Letterbook`;
- Jordan -> George Clark, **14 Jun 1901**, cited to `Stanford Business Correspondence, SC0058`;
- it also cites a Jordan -> Babine **27 Sep 1900** item in `SC0058, Box 50` explicitly as a **typed copy**.

Control: Elena S. Danielson, “Alexis Babine at Stanford, 1898-1902: The Highs and Lows,” 2023.

These citations are useful because they demonstrate that contemporaneous Stanford administrative communications can survive under multiple descriptive/form labels. They do not identify a Lovejoy object.

For `GLA0056`, the retrieval question is therefore now more exact:

1. inspect the Elliott-papers witness and determine whether it is original, copy, transcription, or another derivative form;
2. ask Stanford which **incoming-letterpress / chronological copy system** covers **15 Apr 1901**;
3. inspect the early intermixed loose correspondence / materials segregated for Elliott's Stanford history;
4. only if a literal Lovejoy-to-Elliott text is found in a second physical manifestation should the project issue `GLA0057`.

A matching date or topic alone is not duplicate identity.

## Evidence rules added

> **Institutional continuity is not archival-series continuity.**

> **A record group's start date can invalidate an assumed direct filing path without proving the historical document never existed.**

> **The word “letterbook” is not a manifestation identifier; direction, series, volume and copying practice must be resolved separately.**

> **Series evidence from one year can define a directional control without being projected backward as an unchanged arrangement.**

> **A second manifestation is created only by object-level textual/form evidence, not by a documented copying policy alone.**
