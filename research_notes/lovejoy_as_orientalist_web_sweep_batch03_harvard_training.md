## Major correction to the previous working model

The earlier status “Lanman/Warren environment strongly relevant, but direct Lovejoy training unproved” is now obsolete.

Daniel J. Wilson’s biography explicitly reports, using a **Harvard University Archives list of courses taken by Lovejoy**, *Harvard University Catalogue* 1896–97 pp. 73, 75, 99, and Lovejoy’s letters of 8/10 October 1896 and October/November 1896, that Lovejoy:

- took Charles Carroll Everett’s courses on the psychological basis of religious faith and comparative religion;
- in comparative religion studied Vedic religion, Hinduism, Buddhism, Mazdaism, and Chinese religions;
- studied Hebrew religion under Crawford H. Toy;
- studied Sanskrit and some sacred Buddhist texts under Charles Rockwell Lanman.

This is the strongest biographical discovery in the Orientalist sweep so far.

Detailed dossier: `research_notes/lovejoy_harvard_orientalist_training_1895_1898.md`.

## Independent institutional corroboration

Harvard University Archives confirms Lanman as Professor of Sanskrit at Harvard, 1880–1926, founder/co-founder of the Harvard Oriental Series, with teaching and HOS papers preserved in HUG 4510.xx. The finding aid points to the Harvard History of Religions Club records and to Lanman’s 1895 work on the King of Siam’s edition of Buddhist scriptures.

Harvard’s current South Asian Studies history confirms Lanman’s leadership of Indo-Iranian studies and the Harvard Oriental Series. Henry Clarke Warren’s *Buddhism in Translations* appeared in 1896 as Harvard Oriental Series III, edited by Lanman and dedicated by Warren to Lanman as friend and teacher.

The Harvard Crimson’s 29 April 1896 outline of Lanman’s Buddhism lecture is now particularly significant because Lovejoy was an actual student of Lanman in this period. The lecture covers Buddhism’s relation to Vedism/Brahmanism, Sāṁkhya/Vedānta, Sanskrit/Pāli source languages, the Tipiṭaka, Pali Text Society, and the King of Siam’s edition. Attendance by Lovejoy at this particular public lecture is still unproved.

Independent histories of religion at Harvard confirm that Everett taught comparative religion with emphasis on eastern and southern Asian religions and a long-running psychology-of-religion course. Crawford H. Toy was Professor of Hebrew at Harvard.

## Revised strict-core chronology

A much firmer sequence now exists:

1. **1895–98 Harvard:** philosophy plus formal comparative religion / Hebrew religion / Sanskrit / Buddhist-text study.
2. **1896–97:** documented Lanman/Everett/Toy coursework; Warren’s *Buddhism in Translations* published inside Lanman’s Harvard Oriental Series network.
3. **c.1896–98:** notebook 004, “Sankhya + Buddhism,” likely belongs to this training/research phase but still needs exact dating.
4. **1898:** technical JAOS paper on `upādāna` / `upādisesa`.
5. **1901:** four-lecture syllabus, “The Philosophy of Buddhism,” at Washington University Association.
6. **1906:** comparative-religion article “The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy,” with notebook 005 now a strong preparatory-laboratory candidate.

This means the phrase **“Lovejoy as Orientalist” can be investigated as a documented formation and early scholarly practice**, not merely as a provocative reinterpretation of one isolated paper.
