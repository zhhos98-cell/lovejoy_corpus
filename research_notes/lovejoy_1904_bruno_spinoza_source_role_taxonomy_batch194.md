# Batch 194 — `The Dialectic of Bruno and Spinoza` (1904): source-role taxonomy and citation ecology

Date: 2026-08-26
Status: synced / PRIMARY-TEXT SOURCE MAP
Scope: continued reading of the full 34-page OCR JSON for Lovejoy's 1904 article, cross-checked against Daniel J. Wilson's annotated bibliography, item 22. This batch answers a control left open in Batch188: the named sources in the article do not all do the same evidentiary work. They must be separated by role before any intellectual-transmission claim is made.

## Core result

The article uses at least **five distinct source roles**:

1. **contemporary interpreters / exegetical opponents**: writers whose readings of Spinoza define the problem Lovejoy wants to solve;
2. **historical witnesses / textual sources**: authors and texts used to reconstruct the genealogy of the dialectic;
3. **structural comparanda**: systems used to clarify a logical configuration without being claimed as historical sources of Spinoza;
4. **transmission witnesses / route controls**: scholarship or evidence invoked to establish an actual line of reading, inheritance, or terminological descent;
5. **reception / afterlife nodes**: later thinkers used to show persistence or reactivation of a device after the primary historical sequence.

This taxonomy matters because a flat name-list would convert comparison into influence and bibliography into biography. Lovejoy's own 1904 procedure is more discriminating than that.

---

## 1. Contemporary interpreters: the opening problem-field, not the genealogy

Printed pp.141–144 are densely populated by modern interpreters of Spinoza. Among them:

- Sir Frederick Pollock;
- Harold H. Joachim;
- George Stuart Fullerton;
- Kuno Fischer;
- Theodor Camerer;
- John Caird;
- Edward Caird;
- Harald Höffding;
- Johann Eduard Erdmann.

Their function is principally **exegetical**. Lovejoy inventories incompatible classifications of Spinoza: primitive Hegelian, Platonic realist, Schopenhauerian/illusionist, or a thinker harboring two irreconcilable systems. The article's historical reconstruction is presented as an alternative to resolving the dispute merely by another close reading of Spinoza's wording.

These names therefore should not be entered into a `sources of Spinoza` chain. They are sources for **Lovejoy's historiographical problem situation in 1904**.

Role code: `CONTEMPORARY INTERPRETER / PROBLEM-FIELD`.

## 2. Structural comparanda: Vedānta, Schopenhauer, Deussen, Bradley

Several systems enter because they instantiate a logical relation Lovejoy wants to distinguish.

### Vedānta / Muṇḍaka / Brahman, printed p.147

Lovejoy uses Vedānta to exemplify the possibility that the **esse / “that”** of finite things is included in the Absolute while their determinate **“what” / content** is excluded. The Muṇḍaka Upanishad and Brahman are thus a **logical comparandum**.

No claim is made that Vedānta historically generated Spinoza's ontology.

### Schopenhauer and Deussen, printed p.144

Under the conditional hypothesis that Erdmann's exegesis were correct, Spinoza would belong with a school represented by Schopenhauer and Deussen, with phenomenal plurality treated through an illusion/Māyā vocabulary. This is a **counterfactual classificatory comparison**, not Lovejoy's positive transmission thesis.

### Schopenhauer again, printed p.147

Lovejoy calls Schopenhauer a form of `modern Vedāntism` and uses Will/appearance to restate the `that/what` structure.

### F. H. Bradley, printed p.148

Bradley's Absolute enters as a modern case in which apparent contradictions and the relation of determinate appearance to an Absolute can be compared with the older problem. The role is again analytical and contemporary, not genealogical evidence for Spinoza.

Role code: `STRUCTURAL COMPARANDUM / NON-GENEALOGICAL`.

## 3. Historical source stratum I: Plotinus and Proclus

Lovejoy's positive genealogy begins with Neo-Platonism. The full article repeatedly cites or quotes:

- Plotinus, *Enneads*;
- Proclus, *Institutio Theologica* / *Elements of Theology*.

The footnotes at printed pp.153–157 are especially heavy with textual controls. Lovejoy uses Plotinus to establish both sides of the central paradox:

- the One as beyond all determinations and not identical with the multiplicity it produces;
- the generated order as nevertheless remaining in or united with its source.

Proclus supplies an even more explicit formula in which producer and produced both remain connected while the produced proceeds from the producer. At p.167 Lovejoy again cites Proclus alongside Bruno and Aquinas in relation to infinity, self-subsistence, and concepts later visible in Spinoza.

These are not merely analogues. Lovejoy explicitly treats them as **earlier historical forms of the dialectic** whose later applications make Spinoza historically intelligible.

Role code: `HISTORICAL SOURCE / GENEALOGICAL ANTECEDENT`.

---

## 4. Historical source stratum II: Philo, Pseudo-Dionysius, Aquinas, John the Scot, medieval theology

The article then maps the dialectic through late antique and medieval theology. Named/textual anchors include:

- Philo, including *On the Allegories of the Sacred Laws*;
- Pseudo-Dionysius, including *De divinis nominibus*;
- Thomas Aquinas, especially the *Summa*;
- John the Scot / Eriugena;
- later at p.168: Eckhart, Occam, Bonaventura, Vincent of Beauvais, Averroes.

The role varies slightly within the group:

- Pseudo-Dionysius and Aquinas are directly cited as textual witnesses for the coexistence of negative/transcendent and affirmative/immanent predicates;
- John the Scot is used as an unusually candid witness to the semantic problem of `super-` language;
- the p.168 list, mediated through Siebeck, documents the pre-Spinozan history of `natura naturans` / `natura naturata` and the underlying antithesis.

This stratum is Lovejoy's bridge from ancient Neo-Platonic dialectic to Renaissance and early modern metaphysics.

Role code: `HISTORICAL SOURCE / MEDIEVAL TRANSMISSION FIELD`.

---

## 5. Bruno: both historical intermediary and controlled direct-influence node

Bruno occupies a special role that no other figure in the article quite shares.

Lovejoy uses *De la Causa* and *De Immenso* as primary texts to show the dialectic `writ large`: logical infinity, coincidence of opposites, immanence/transcendence, plenitude/productivity, and the attempt to derive diversity from an Absolute.

Bruno is therefore:

- an **historical intermediary** between older Neo-Platonic/medieval material and Spinoza;
- a **demonstrably similar case** used to illuminate the structure of Spinoza's system;
- and, at p.167, a **direct-influence node**, because Lovejoy explicitly says `we know that Spinoza read Bruno`.

Yet Batch193 shows that Lovejoy immediately de-prioritizes the *degree* of direct Bruno influence because the same conceptual material was broadly available through a continuous tradition.

Role code: `HISTORICAL INTERMEDIARY + DIRECT-INFLUENCE NODE + STRUCTURAL EXEMPLAR`.

## 6. Transmission witnesses: McIntyre and Siebeck

Two modern scholars play a role different from the opening expositors.

### J. Lewis McIntyre, printed p.167

McIntyre's study of Bruno is invoked because he has `drawn out the lines of connection` between Bruno and Spinoza. Lovejoy thinks McIntyre understates rather than overstates the connection. McIntyre therefore functions as a **modern transmission-control source** rather than merely an interpretive opponent.

Open control: identify the exact passages in McIntyre on Spinoza's reading of Bruno and distinguish McIntyre's evidence from Lovejoy's inference.

### Hermann Siebeck, printed p.168

Lovejoy says Siebeck has shown that `natura naturans` and `natura naturata`, and the antithesis they express, were already present in Eckhart, Occam, Bonaventura, Vincent of Beauvais, Averroes and others, with Greek originals in Proclus. The footnote points to *Archiv für Geschichte der Philosophie*, vol. III, p.370.

Siebeck therefore functions as a **terminological-genealogy witness**.

Role code: `MODERN SCHOLAR / TRANSMISSION OR GENEALOGY CONTROL`.

---

## 7. Reception / afterlife: Hamann → Goethe

A footnote at printed p.163 says Bruno's taste for `coincidentia oppositorum` descended to Goethe `in a somewhat direct line` because the doctrine became a chief hobby of Hamann, who brought it back into fashion.

This is not needed to prove Bruno → Spinoza. It shows Lovejoy briefly tracking the **later afterlife** of a conceptual device beyond the core seventeenth-century problem.

The phrasing is worth preserving because Lovejoy here again uses differentiated transmission language: `somewhat direct line`, named intermediary, later revival.

Role code: `RECEPTION / REACTIVATION / AFTERLIFE`.

## 8. Wilson's item 22 correctly captures the macro-architecture but flattens the source roles

Daniel J. Wilson's annotated bibliography, primary-source item 22, accurately summarizes the article as an attempt to clarify Spinoza through `historical sources and affinities`, tracing Neo-Platonic thought from Plotinus through the Middle Ages to Bruno and Spinoza. Wilson further notes that the article anticipates *The Great Chain of Being* in structure, examples, and moral.

That annotation is useful as a macro-control, but the full article allows a finer distinction than the bibliography can provide. `Historical sources and affinities` contains heterogeneous relations:

- direct textual antecedent;
- broad tradition;
- modern interpreter;
- logical analogue;
- counterfactual comparison;
- documented reading;
- terminological genealogy;
- later reception.

The present batch should therefore be used whenever a project claim moves from `Lovejoy names X` to `X influenced Y`.

## 9. Article-wide evidentiary matrix

| Relation type | Representative examples | What it can prove | What it cannot prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| contemporary interpretation | Pollock, Joachim, Fullerton, Fischer, Caird, Erdmann | Lovejoy's 1904 problem-field | source of Spinoza's ideas |
| structural comparandum | Vedānta/Muṇḍaka, Schopenhauer, Deussen, Bradley | shared logical configuration / classificatory type | historical transmission to Spinoza |
| ancient source | Plotinus, Proclus | earlier form of the dialectic used in genealogy | direct personal reading by every later node without further evidence |
| medieval source/transmission field | Pseudo-Dionysius, Aquinas, John Scotus, Eckhart, Occam, Bonaventura, Vincent, Averroes | persistence/reformulation of conceptual machinery | one linear chain through every named author |
| Renaissance intermediary | Bruno | historical reworking; structural model; direct relation to Spinoza acknowledged | exclusive causal source of Spinoza |
| modern transmission control | McIntyre, Siebeck | evidence/history of connection or terminology as understood in 1904 | independent primary proof unless their evidence is checked |
| afterlife | Hamann, Goethe | later reactivation/reception | evidence for Bruno → Spinoza |

## 10. Consequence for the Paris / India / Buddhism investigation

The article itself supplies the discipline we need for Lovejoy's own biography:

`named in same intellectual field`

≠ `structural affinity`

≠ `available in same institution`

≠ `read by Lovejoy`

≠ `directly transmitted to Lovejoy`

≠ `reused by Lovejoy`

≠ `exclusive causal source`.

Batch192 gives a direct 1904 Indic **reuse** by Lovejoy. Batch193 gives his own hierarchy for **direct influence vs shared tradition**. Batch194 now gives the role taxonomy needed to stop those two findings from being over-read into a Paris transmission claim.
