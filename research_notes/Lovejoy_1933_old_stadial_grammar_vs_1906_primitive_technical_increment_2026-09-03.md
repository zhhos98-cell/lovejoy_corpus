# Lovejoy — older stadial grammar vs the technical increment of `primitive`

Date: 2026-09-03  
Status: **1933 RETROSPECTIVE EIGHTEENTH-CENTURY CONTROL / 1906 DIRECT PRIMARY / PRIMITIVE-OPERATION DELTA CLOSED ANALYTICALLY**

## Core question

If the developmental/stadial grammar of human history already existed in Rousseau/Monboddo, as Lovejoy himself argued in 1933, what additional work does `primitive` perform in Lovejoy's 1906 `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`?

The answer is not simply `it marks an earlier stage`.

The strongest delta is:

> **`primitive` turns developmental time into a detachable relational predicate that can be located inside heterogeneous traditions, compared transversely across them, and then reaggregated as a collective cognitive/philosophical type.**

Analytical sequence:

`OLDER STADIAL GRAMMAR`
→ `INTERNAL STRATIFICATION`
→ `RELATION / MECHANISM DETACHMENT`
→ `TRANSVERSE CROSS-CULTURAL REAGGREGATION`
→ `COLLECTIVE REATTRIBUTION AS PRIMITIVE PHILOSOPHY`.

This is more technically specific than saying `primitive = lower stage`.

---

## I. Older stadial grammar — what is already available without the technical category `primitive`

Lovejoy's 1933 reconstruction of Rousseau/Monboddo supplies a retrospective eighteenth-century control.

The older grammar already permits:

1. **developmental human nature** — distinctively human capacities are acquired historically rather than fully given at the beginning;
2. **successive stages** — humanity moves through distinguishable phases from near animality toward developed rational/social forms;
3. **relative social/political form** — institutions and human capacities vary with stage;
4. **living-population temporalization** — contemporary peoples described as `barbarous` can be used as evidence for earlier human conditions;
5. **attack on uniform human nature** — one civilized present cannot be universalized as `man as such`;
6. **universal evolutionary history** — a history of mankind can be reconstructed across stages.

Thus the following operation is already old:

`CONTEMPORARY HUMAN DIFFERENCE`
→ `READ AS DEVELOPMENTAL DIFFERENCE`
→ `MAP TO EARLIER / LATER STAGES`.

Therefore `primitive` cannot be credited with inventing developmental stratification itself.

## II. Notebook-level transformation — time moves inside the object

The 005/notebook controls show a different operation from whole-population stadial placement.

`primitive` can attach to:

- a Greek primitive layer;
- a primitive plant-soul representation;
- an older non-moral mechanism surviving under later ethical interpretation;
- a more primitive naturalistic or magical relation inside a later religious complex.

The crucial difference is:

`WHOLE PEOPLE / WHOLE SOCIETY AT STAGE S`

becomes

`RELATION / REPRESENTATION / MECHANISM M INSIDE OBJECT O HAS AN EARLIER STRATIGRAPHIC STATUS`.

The unit of temporalization shrinks.

A later/complex tradition can contain an earlier relation without the whole tradition being assigned wholesale to the earlier stage.

This is **internal stratification**.

---

## III. 1906 first increment — `primitive` becomes a detachable causal predicate

In `The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy`, Lovejoy begins by decomposing familiar ethnographic/religious categories until he isolates a recurring causal architecture:

- impersonal;
- quasi-mechanical;
- diffused through persons/things;
- unequally localized;
- communicable/transferable;
- manipulable by contact, insulation and ritual procedure.

At this first level `primitive` attaches not to a whole society but to a **mechanism/belief family**.

This gives the category portability.

A historically complex object can instantiate the same primitive relation as another object even when their whole institutional histories differ.

The technical increment is:

`STAGE PREDICATE`
→
`RELATION / MECHANISM PREDICATE`.

---

## IV. 1906 second increment — horizontal equivalence across heterogeneous histories

Once the primitive relation is detachable, Lovejoy can compare it across:

- different rites;
- different lexical systems;
- different geographic regions;
- historically independent populations.

He does not need to claim one shared institutional ancestor.

Instead:

`A contains relation M`

`B contains relation M`

`C contains relation M`

while

`A / B / C` have different genealogies.

Then:

`M recurs independently`
→ `M forms one comparative causal type`.

This is **transverse reaggregation**.

It differs from a single ladder of peoples.

The cross-cultural equivalence is built at the level of one extracted relation, not at the level of whole traditions.

---

## V. 1906 third increment — analyst's transverse category is attributed back to actors

Lovejoy does not stop with:

> `here is a useful analytical type found in several ethnographic cases`.

He raises the scale:

- `Primitive Energetics`;
- `fundamental concept of the primitive philosophy`;
- a recoverable `logic` of the relevant beliefs;
- `savage thought`;
- `most savages` / `many or most races` at the relevant stage of culture.

Thus:

`LOCAL RELATION`
→ `ANALYTIC CROSS-CULTURAL TYPE`
→ `COLLECTIVE ACTOR-LEVEL PHILOSOPHY / COGNITIVE STYLE`.

This is **collective reattribution**.

The operation is historically consequential because independently historical populations are allowed to remain different at the level of institutional genealogy while being regrouped as the bearers of one shared fundamental causal philosophy.

## VI. What `primitive` adds beyond old stadial history

| Operation | Older stadial grammar | 1906 `primitive` increment |
|---|---|---|
| temporal placement | whole societies / humanity pass through stages | relations/mechanisms inside complex objects can carry stage status |
| living peoples | living populations can represent earlier stages | particular relations inside living/historical populations can be extracted and compared |
| internal heterogeneity | possible but not required for the basic stage model | essential: one tradition can contain multiple temporal/logical strata |
| comparison | compare societies/stages | compare detached relation-types across nonidentical societies/traditions |
| genealogy | often stage sequence of social forms | institutional genealogies can remain plural while mechanisms are equivalent |
| cross-cultural type | earlier/later social stage | recurrent causal/semantic type across independent fields |
| actor attribution | people exemplify a stage | analyst's extracted type can become `primitive philosophy` / `savage thought` |
| geometry | primarily longitudinal developmental sequence | vertical internal strata + horizontal equivalence grid |

The most important technical difference is geometric:

> **old stadial history mainly orders whole subjects longitudinally; operational `primitive` can create a two-dimensional stratigraphic grid — vertical layers inside objects and horizontal equivalences among similarly classified layers across objects.**

## VII. Why this is not a retreat from totalization

Internal stratification can look pluralizing because it breaks whole traditions into heterogeneous layers.

But in 1906 that very decomposition makes a new totalization possible.

The sequence is:

`WHOLE-OBJECT HETEROGENEITY`
→ `ISOLATE M`
→ `M BECOMES PORTABLE`
→ `FIND M ACROSS A/B/C`
→ `RECONSTRUCT ONE PRIMITIVE PHILOSOPHY`.

Therefore:

> **decomposition can be the machinery of a higher-order totalization.**

This is a major guardrail for the larger project.

More analytical granularity does not automatically mean less hierarchy or less civilizational generalization.

It can instead produce a more flexible classificatory instrument.

## VIII. Why this helps the post-Enlightenment chronology thesis

The refined thesis should distinguish three historical problems.

### A. Developmental time

Already available in older stadial/evolutionary histories.

### B. Internal stratigraphy

Temporal, logical and causal differences move **inside** texts, religions, rites, concepts and societies.

### C. Transverse equivalence

Similarly classified strata are compared **across** traditions/populations and can be reaggregated as a cross-cultural type.

`Primitive` is potentially powerful because it can mediate B + C, not merely A.

The project's strongest technical formula is therefore:

`DEVELOPMENTAL GRAMMAR`
+
`INTERNAL STRATIFICATION`
+
`TRANSVERSE REAGGREGATION`
=
`STRATIGRAPHIC COMPARATIVE CHRONOLOGY`.

This is not a Gatterer-style table in which complete peoples occupy one stable row/time slot.

It is a relational grid in which one object may contain multiple historical statuses and one status may recur across many objects.

## IX. Relation to the user's larger conceptual distinction

The result supports the distinction:

`CONTRADICTION`
!=
`LAYERING`.

A living people, canonical text or developed religion can be simultaneously:

- `late` as a whole historical object;
- `early/primitive` in one embedded relation;
- `modern/derived` in another relation;
- geographically contemporary;
- logically antecedent or subordinate on another axis.

The apparent contradiction disappears once the unit and relation are specified.

This is exactly the sort of `in-advance` layering that a one-dimensional historical table cannot represent well.

## X. Political / evidentiary firewall

Do not infer:

- that every user of `primitive` performs the same transverse operation;
- that Lovejoy invented internal stratification;
- that 1933 Rousseau/Monboddo caused the 1906 article (chronology forbids it);
- that a broad mechanism found in several cases is genuinely actor-owned simply because Lovejoy calls it `primitive philosophy`;
- that micro-level source discipline neutralizes macro-level population generalization;
- that `primitive` ceases to be hierarchical because it becomes relation-specific.

The 1906 paper itself is especially useful because it combines:

`GRANULAR SOURCE / MECHANISM ANALYSIS`
with
`BROAD COLLECTIVE REATTRIBUTION`.

This produces a stratigraphic comparative grid: vertical differentiation within objects plus horizontal equivalence across objects.**
