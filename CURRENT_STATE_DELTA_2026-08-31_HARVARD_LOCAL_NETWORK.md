## Major state A — Brinton's primitive-religion programme was physically in Boston during Lovejoy's 1896–97 Harvard year

Daniel G. Brinton's *Religions of Primitive Peoples* was the **Second Series, 1896–1897** of the American Lectures on the History of Religions. The printed announcement states that the lectures were delivered in winter **1896–97**, including Boston at the **Lowell Institute**.

A published 1898 citation identifies one exact lecture date:

> **D. G. Brinton, Lowell Institute, Boston, 9 Nov 1896.**

This falls in the same academic year in which Lovejoy's individual Harvard record directly shows:

## Major state D — Brinton now functions as a local positive developmental control for notebook 005

Brinton's Boston course is methodologically useful beyond lexical overlap.

His printed course explicitly distinguishes historical / comparative / psychologic methods and evidence classes including archaeology, language, folklore and traveller reports. He defines `primitive` chiefly as a **stage of culture rather than absolute chronological earliest time**.

His Lecture VI, `The Lines of Development of Primitive Religions`, organizes development through:

- primitive social bond;
- family / position of woman;
- jurisprudence;
- ethics;
- positive knowledge;
- arts;
- individual life.

His treatment of sacrifice also supplies explicit developmental/motivational sequences.
