## Major state 3 — Anthropology 1: exact-year institutional bracket tightened

Direct actor fact:

> **Lovejoy completed Anthropology 1 in 1897–98 and received A.**

The exact 1897–98 FAS Course 1 description still has not been directly extracted, but contemporary Harvard evidence now tightly brackets the course sequence:

- 2 Jun 1896 *Harvard Crimson*: Anthropology 1 already existed; a new Anthropology 2 was proposed for 1897–98 with special reference to physical anthropology / somatology; students intending to take 2 were advised to take 1 first.
- 28 May 1897 *Harvard Crimson*, explicitly reporting the **1897–98 Elective Pamphlet**: new courses in American Archaeology and Ethnology included `2, Somatology` and `20b, Advanced Somatology`.
- Harvard quinquennial catalogue: Frank Russell listed in Anthropology from 1897.
- Hrdlička historical control: Russell took the place vacated by Dorsey in 1897; in 1897–98, under Russell, a three-hour half-course in physical anthropology was offered.
- 26 Aug 1898 Harvard/Peabody statement: Course 1 = `General Anthropology — Somatology (Physical Anthropology); Archaeology; Ethnology; Ethnography`, Dr. Russell.

This strongly supports continuity of an established general Anthropology 1 across Lovejoy's year while specialized somatology was split into Course 2. Exact 1897–98 word-for-word Course 1 wording and exact instructor line remain a narrow catalogue target.

## Major state 4 — near-contemporary Harvard Course 1 content CLOSED

The 26 Aug 1898 Harvard/Peabody primary statement describes Course 1 as General Anthropology under Frank Russell and explicitly includes:

- Somatology / physical criteria of race;
- origin and development of **primitive arts and culture**;
- culture/environment relations;
- material/utilitarian and aesthetic arts with Museum objects;
- social life of `natural` peoples: primitive government, marriage, law / ethnic jurisprudence;
- **Religion: psychological origin and lines of development of primitive religions; fetishism; animism; shamanism; ritual**;
- mythology;
- folklore;
- linguistics and race/language classification;
- archaeology / ethnography, origins, prehistoric ages, races, classification and migrations.

A separate Course 3, `Primitive Religions`, existed in the Aug 1898 course state under Putnam and Dixon, but **Lovejoy's card records Course 1, not Course 3**. Keep Course 3 as institutional ecology only.

## Major state 5 — NEW: Brinton 1892 provides a striking primary classificatory control

Daniel G. Brinton's 1892 *Anthropology: As a Science and as a Branch of University Education in the United States* was explicitly a university-teaching scheme. It divides anthropology into:

1. **Somatology — Physical and Experimental Anthropology**;
2. **Ethnology — Historic and Analytic Anthropology**;
3. **Ethnography — Geographic and Descriptive Anthropology**;
4. **Archaeology — Prehistoric and Reconstructive Anthropology**.

The overlap with Harvard's 1898 General Anthropology Course 1 is not merely nominal. Brinton's proposed Ethnology curriculum includes government, marriage, law, technology/arts, **psychological origin of religions**, animism, fetichism, mythology, sacrifice, shamanism, doctrines of soul, Buddhism/Judaism and other special religions, linguistics and folklore. Harvard Course 1 arranges substantially the same object classes inside Ethnology.

## Major state 6 — NEW synthesis: Harvard classification architecture versus Paris 005 proof architecture

The canonical 005 dossiers establish a relation-sensitive proof ladder:

`L1 witness admissibility`

`-> L2 classificatory admissibility`

`-> L3 mechanism inference`

`-> L4 genealogy / development`.

The recurrent rule is that stronger propositions do not automatically inherit evidence sufficient for weaker ones. Differentiated afterlife does not by itself prove moral desert; human/animal victim morphology does not identify mechanism or developmental stage; ritual form does not prove sacrifice; shared material does not prove shared mechanism; a developmental genealogy requires independent chronological warrant.

This creates the strongest current Harvard-to-Paris distinction:

### Harvard Anthropology 1

**Classification architecture**:

`body/race | material culture | social institutions | primitive religion | myth/folklore | language | ethnography | archaeology`.

### Paris-core notebook 005

**Proof architecture** within overlapping object classes:

`report -> comparative class -> operative relation/mechanism -> genealogy/development`, with proposition-specific proof burdens.

Therefore the current synthesis is:

> **continuity of problem-space + reorganization of inferential jurisdiction**.

Do **not** reduce this to `Harvard taught Lovejoy the method` or `Paris taught him criticism`. The evidence is stronger for a transition from a coordinated classificatory field into increasingly explicit audits of what classifications, mechanisms and genealogies are actually warranted.

This distinction is now **STATE-BEARING** for the broader `primitive = stratification` project.
