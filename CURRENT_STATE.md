## 5. Context-differentiated logic correction

The branch now separates at least three evidentiary levels:

1. **logic as object** — Lovejoy writes directly on formal logic or logical doctrine;
2. **explicit logical method** — Lovejoy explicitly deploys logical analysis on a non-logical object;
3. **analyst-recognized logic-shaped operation** — relation-sensitive work whose actor-level attribution to logic remains unproved.

Therefore lexical recurrence of `logical` does not establish one method.

Strong longitudinal controls:

- **1907** — formal-logical scrutiny of Kant's classificatory table;
- **1908** — `The Thirteen Pragmatisms`: shared historical label does not transfer warrant among logically independent contentions;
- **1909** — `Pragmatism and Realism`: historical complex requires logical analysis + historical comparison; compatible motives may recombine; novelty may lie in a `new compound in intellectual chemistry`;
- **1910** — direct formal-logic intervention on opposition and Venn treatment;
- **1911** — `Existence and Formal Logic`: formal implication, meaning/intelligibility, evidence/verification and historical truth have distinct jurisdictions;
- **1914** — purported deductive/formal system itself can fail standards of logical adequacy;
- **1916–17** — philosophical inquiry programme emphasizes defined problems, methodological self-consciousness, cautious belief, cooperation, logical austerity and communicable/verifiable propositions;
- **1924** — `On the Discrimination of Romanticisms`: logical relations/necessity distinguished from psychological and explicitly **alogical** association inside historical intellectual compounds; decomposition need not imply that historical composition was logically generated;
- **1936** — *Great Chain* method admits logical, pseudo-logical, affective, motivational and historical mechanisms; same conclusions can arise from different motives and similar motives can produce different conclusions;
- **1941** — logical, psychological and historical/genetic relations receive distinct epistemic status; analyst-generated logical hypotheses must be returned to historical evidence.

The stronger recurring candidate is therefore not `formal logic` as a single portable method but:

> **typed relations + inferential jurisdiction**.

Operational shorthand:

> before allowing a claim to migrate, identify the relation actually at stake, identify what kind of evidence can warrant it, and require a new proof step before upgrading it to a stronger relation.

Examples:

- implication ≠ historical truth;
- affinity ≠ transmission;
- shared label ≠ identity;
- same conclusion ≠ same motive;
- logical compatibility ≠ historical composition;
- analyst-recognized implication ≠ actor-held doctrine;
- semantic resemblance ≠ genealogy.

This remains a person-level structural hypothesis. Recurrence is not transmission.

### 7.2 1930 meaning/criterion map

Lovejoy's 1930 relativity/operationism intervention is thicker than `criterion ≠ meaning`.

Current map:

1. meaning of X ≠ operation used to test X;
2. evidence/sign for X ≠ X itself;
3. different verification routes ≠ automatically different proposition identity;
4. criterion selection requires warrant independent of the desired conclusion it is then used to derive.

Compactly:

`operation`
→ `evidence / sign`
→ `warranted inference`
→ `proposition / fact`

with Lovejoy resisting collapse of those arrows into identity.

Source ceiling: exact p.620/p.628 wording is presently mediated through reliable secondary transcription pending direct page-image collation. McGilvary 1931 is an essential counter-control: the distinction may be Lovejoy's, while his claim that Einstein himself collapsed the relations was immediately disputed.

### 7.3 1938/1942 `On the Criteria and Limits of Meaning` — new direct-primary endpoint

Lovejoy's essay was published in 1942 but explicitly says **`The present essay was written in 1938.`**

This matters because Lovejoy himself now retrospectively groups the problem-spaces the repo had been connecting. He describes a family of radical-empiricist theories of meaning whose species include:

- pragmatism;
- operationalism;
- behaviorism;
- special relativity in its methodological premises;
- neo-positivism.

Thus the 1908 → 1930 → 1935 adjacency is partly actor-authored by 1938, not merely analyst-imposed.

Lovejoy deliberately uses Carnap's 1928 *Scheinprobleme* as a worked example while explicitly acknowledging that it may not precisely represent Carnap's current views. He is testing a **type of argument and conclusion**, not claiming Carnap never changed.

His central distinction:

- **criterion A:** thinkability / actual thought-content;
- **criterion B:** empirical verifiability / testability.

His decisive claim: the two criteria are **not interchangeable**. Failure under the empirical-settlement criterion does not by itself establish absence of thinkable content.

The mature partition becomes:

`verbal stipulation`
≠ `factual proposition`
≠ `thinkability / meaning`
≠ `empirical verifiability`
≠ `truth`
≠ `legitimate belief`.

This is one of the strongest direct-primary examples currently available for **typed criteria + inferential jurisdiction** outside formal logic.

### 7.4 Longitudinal meaning-criteria ladder

Current controlled sequence:

- **1908** — James/pragmatism: distinguish multiple criteria/contentions; do not transfer proof between non-equivalent formulations;
- **1930** — relativity/operationism: empirical criterion/verification/evidence does not exhaust semantic content or proposition identity;
- **31 Dec. 1935** — Baltimore: conventionalism versus the entitlement to exclude metaphysics as meaningless;
- **1938** — Lovejoy explicitly groups pragmatism, operationism, relativity methodology and neo-positivism inside one meaning-criterion problem and attacks criterion substitution in Carnap;

Strongest current hypothesis:

> **A durable Lovejoy problem concerns the legitimacy of migration between criteria: a test can settle one relation without thereby settling a semantically, ontologically or historically stronger relation.**

This is a longitudinal structural claim, partly supported by Lovejoy's own retrospective grouping in 1938. It is not yet a demonstrated causal genealogy of method.

## 8. Revised unit-idea problem

Do not replace the old caricature with a rehabilitation myth.

The unit-idea question should be partitioned into four problems:

1. **analytical individuation** — how is a component isolated?;
2. **logical relation** — what does it imply, presuppose, oppose or combine with?;
3. **historical composition/function** — why is it bundled with other components and what does it do in that configuration?;
4. **cross-context identity** — what licenses recognition as the same idea across changed labels, motives, functions and combinations?

Current evidence is increasingly strong for Lovejoy's sophistication on 1–3. The genuine Skinner-facing difficulty remains especially at 4.

Working distinction:

`identity sufficient for analytical/historical tracing`
≠ automatically
`identity independent of history`.

Relation to notebooks remains non-genealogical. 004/005 must be read against overlapping repertoires of philology/Orientalist textual criticism, comparative religion/ethnography and philosophical/logical analysis. The notebooks show Lovejoy recombining heterogeneous proof practices; relative contribution remains an attribution problem.
