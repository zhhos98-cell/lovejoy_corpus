# AOS 1896–1897 abstract procedure control

Date: 2026-09-05  
Status: **DIRECT CIRCULAR EVIDENCE / 1897 LOVEJOY ABSTRACT EXISTENCE OPEN**

## Core correction

The project's earlier procedural shorthand was too late-dated: an AOS instruction concerning **advance abstracts for long papers is directly documented in the 1896 meeting circular material**, before Lovejoy's 1897 communication. Abstract practice therefore cannot be described as appearing only in 1899/1900.

At the same time, direct image inspection of the surviving **18 Mar 1897 Preliminary Circular** and **10 Apr 1897 Final Circular** does **not** show the 1896 abstract instruction repeated. The 1897 Preliminary Circular explicitly asks intending contributors to send **their names and the titles of their papers** to Hanns Oertel at 31 York Square, New Haven. The 1897 Final Circular announces Lovejoy's title but does not establish receipt of an abstract or manuscript.

Therefore:

`AOS ABSTRACT PRACTICE DOCUMENTED BY 1896 != LOVEJOY ABSTRACT SUBMITTED IN 1897`

and

`1897 CIRCULAR OMITS ABSTRACT INSTRUCTION != ABSTRACTS IMPOSSIBLE IN 1897`.

## 1896 procedural witness

The University of Chicago recipient file for AOS meeting circulars contains indexed official 1896 circular text stating that, because a Society rule restricted the time devoted to each paper to twenty minutes, authors with long papers were requested to **prepare in advance an abstract**, whose reading would fit the time limit while giving the general outline of the thesis.

This establishes a pre-1897 AOS practice of author-prepared abstracts for long presentations.

It does not establish:

- that every paper had an abstract;
- that every abstract was submitted to the Corresponding Secretary in advance rather than simply brought/read at the meeting;
- that abstracts were retained institutionally;
- that Lovejoy's 1897 paper was classed as a long paper;
- that Lovejoy prepared or transmitted such an abstract.

## 1897 direct image control

### Preliminary Circular — 18 Mar 1897

Direct image inspection shows:

> `Members intending to offer communications are requested to send their names and the titles of their papers at once to the undersigned, 31 York Square, New Haven, Conn.`

No abstract-submission instruction appears on the inspected two-page 1897 Preliminary Circular.

### Final Circular — 10 Apr 1897

Direct image inspection shows the meeting arrangements, business order, list of papers announced to date, and expected-presence list. Lovejoy appears as No. 24 under `LIST OF PAPERS ANNOUNCED TO DATE` with `Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`. No Lovejoy abstract or general abstract-submission instruction appears in the inspected Final Circular.

Lovejoy is absent from the separate expected-presence list. The later Proceedings records his communication as read by title.

## Search consequence

The minimum secure pre-10-Apr 1897 carrier remains a **name + title notice to Oertel**. However, because AOS abstract practice is demonstrably pre-1897, the archival search should not exclude an additional abstract carrier on chronological grounds alone.

Possible 1897 abstract carriers should be searched only as **contingent objects**:

- Oertel correspondence / title-submission packet;
- Board of Editors / Directors papers;
- Lovejoy correspondence or retained drafts;
- institutional record books or meeting packets;
- distributed officer/recipient papers.

The strongest present argument against assuming such an abstract is evidentiary, not institutional: none has been recovered, the 1897 circulars do not request one explicitly, and the communication ultimately was read by title.
