## Purpose

The April 1897 American Oriental Society meeting was held at Johns Hopkins University in Baltimore. A local Hopkins institutional record can therefore matter if it preserves a contemporaneous announcement, report, attendance note, or paper title independent of the June AOS Proceedings. This note isolates one such carrier without treating a Hopkins philological society record as an AOS record.

## 1. Exact collection and volume

Johns Hopkins University Special Collections holds:

- `Johns Hopkins University Philological Association records`;
- identifier **RG-15-010**;
- extent **0.28 cubic feet**, one flat box;
- records consist of five bound volumes plus event records.

The current finding aid states that the minutes volumes include:

> **Volume 1 — May 31, 1877 to February 20, 1914.**

Volume 1 therefore directly spans April–May 1897.

The same finding aid states that the minutes record:

- meeting dates and locations;
- member attendance lists;
- speakers;
- paper titles;
- announcements.

Important-paper abstracts were included only in October–December 1877 and again after 1915; the finding aid explicitly says that this abstracting practice was discontinued in December 1877 and did not resume until 1916.

Primary locator: Johns Hopkins University Libraries Archives Public Interface, `Johns Hopkins University Philological Association records`, RG-15-010, collection overview.

## 2. Institutional proximity

The Association was a Hopkins-based philological forum founded at Gilman's behest and presided over by Basil L. Gildersleeve through 1915. Its meetings in this period were held in the original McCoy Hall, the same Hopkins institutional environment in which the 1897 AOS meeting took place.

Its papers and communications concentrated on Latin, Greek, Semitic and Sanskrit language/literature, with additional historical and archaeological material. This makes it a plausible **local professional echo** for a major Oriental/philological meeting hosted at Hopkins.

This proximity does not establish that the Philological Association discussed the AOS meeting, that Lovejoy was named, or that any AOS paper was entered in its minutes.

Project firewall:

> **LOCAL HOST-INSTITUTION RECORD != AOS CORPORATE RECORD.**

The immediate aim is **not** manuscript recovery. It is to test whether a Hopkins-local record independently fixes a meeting-day or immediate-post-meeting title/status state.

## 4. Interpretive outcomes

### If the minutes give the Milinda title

That would extend the 10 April Milinda state closer to, into, or immediately after the Baltimore meeting, depending on the minute date.

### If they give the technical-terms title

That would supply an independent pre-June witness and potentially date the technical title to the local April/May Hopkins environment.

### If they mention the AOS meeting without Lovejoy

That would establish local institutional attention but would not alter the Lovejoy title chronology.

### If the relevant 1897 meetings contain no AOS reference

That would be a bounded negative for **RG-15-010 Volume 1 only**, not proof that Hopkins produced no local AOS record.

## 5. Abstract expectation

Do not request this volume on the assumption that it preserves long abstracts for 1897. The finding aid explicitly says the Association's own practice of including abstracts had ceased in December 1877 and resumed only in 1916.

The realistic 1897 target is therefore:

`DATE / ATTENDANCE / SPEAKER / PAPER TITLE / ANNOUNCEMENT / LOCAL REPORT`

rather than a full paper or abstract.
