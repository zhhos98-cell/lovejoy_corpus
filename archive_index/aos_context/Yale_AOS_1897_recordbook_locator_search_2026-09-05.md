# Yale / AOS 1897 record-book locator search

Date: 2026-09-05  
Status: **RECORD CLASSES DIRECTLY VERIFIED / YALE LEGACY LOCATION METADATA SECURE / 1897 SHELFMARK NOT RECOVERED**

## Purpose

The original 1897 AOS By-Laws now directly verify two manuscript record classes that could discriminate the Lovejoy title change:

1. Corresponding Secretary **letter-copy book**;
2. Recording Secretary **proceedings book**.

This note records the present locator search ceiling at Yale. The problem is now a shelfmark/date-coverage problem, not a generic question whether AOS had such record classes.

## 1. Direct 1897 record-class warrant

The original *JAOS* 18 pt.2 Constitution and By-Laws, printed p.404, requires the Corresponding Secretary to:

> `keep, in a book provided for the purpose, a copy of his letters`

and the Recording Secretary to:

> `keep a record of the proceedings of the Society in a book provided for the purpose.`

For April 1897, the officer chain is documentable:

- Hanns Oertel — Acting Corresponding Secretary for the circular/title-intake stage;
- George F. Moore — Recording Secretary and later a journal-production/editorial node.

The search target is therefore an actual 1897 volume or successor catalogue entry for these books.

## 2. Yale legacy location metadata — AOS remained a distinct library location

Current Yale cataloging documentation preserves **American Oriental Society** as a distinct legacy/current location mapping:

- Voyager location: **`smlaos`**;
- OCLC holding code: **`YU4V`**.

Yale's retrospective-conversion documentation explicitly lists `American Oriental Society` as its own Sterling location rather than folding every item silently into the generic SML location.

This is important because old AOS catalogue cards or provisional records may carry location/call-number information that ordinary title/name searching does not expose cleanly.

Current AOS public guidance also states that the Society's library has merged into Yale University Library; printed materials belong to the Sterling-side collection environment while manuscripts are housed in Beinecke. That present arrangement does not itself supply nineteenth-century record-book shelfmarks.

Project firewall:

> **AOS LOCATION CODE != MANUSCRIPT RECORD-BOOK CALL NUMBER.**

## 3. YRG 37 — records-management metadata, not the corporate archive call number

Yale Manuscripts and Archives, RU 129, Accession 1993-A-082, Box 6 contains a 1978–1980 records-management survey entry:

`YRG 37 American Oriental Society`.

The survey is restricted to Office of Origin and the public finding aid does not expose the underlying inventory. No public Yale finding aid has been recovered that establishes `YRG 37` as a currently requestable AOS corporate fonds.

This nevertheless shows that Yale records-management staff treated AOS as a distinct organizational record problem in the late twentieth century.

Therefore:

`YRG 37 SURVEY ENTRY != OPEN AOS ARCHIVE LOCATOR`.

## 4. Direct survival comparator — `American Oriental Society Letter Book I`

Published scholarship independently cites early AOS correspondence by the archival designation:

`American Oriental Society Letter Book I` / `American Oriental Society Letter Book 1`, Yale University Library.

Examples include Edward Salisbury's 1842–1843 correspondence with Georg Wilhelm Freytag. Recent scholarship continues to cite the same volume designation.

This directly establishes survival of **at least one** institutional Corresponding Secretary letter book at Yale.

What public searching has **not** recovered:

- a `Letter Book II` or later numbered volume;
- a public date span for any successor letter-copy book reaching 1897;
- a call number/shelfmark beyond the citation-name `American Oriental Society Letter Book I`;
- a catalogued `Proceedings Book` / `Recording Secretary's Book` covering 1897.

Repeated exact searches for `American Oriental Society Letter Book II`, `Proceedings Book`, `minute book`, and variants have not produced a modern item-level Yale record.

This is a **public-catalog search ceiling**, not an archival-absence claim.

## 5. The 1930 AOS Library catalogue is not yet a record-book index

Elizabeth Strout, ed., *Catalogue of the Library of the American Oriental Society* (New Haven: Yale University Library, 1930), is directly available as a 308-page public-domain ebook. Modern scholarship confirms that it describes manuscripts as well as printed materials in the AOS collection.

Current web-text access has not exposed an index entry for the Society's own administrative letter/proceedings books. The catalogue should therefore remain a legacy-catalog target, but its mere existence does not establish that corporate record books were included in its bibliographical scope.

Do not write `1930 catalogue lists the AOS record books` until an actual entry is recovered.

## 6. Why ordinary Yale name-searching can fail

The present evidence points to several overlapping description layers:

`AOS legacy library location (smlaos / YU4V)`

+ `old Yale call-number/card-catalog environment`

+ `later manuscript custody in Beinecke`

+ `YRG 37 records-management classification`

+ `modern catalog/finding-aid systems`.

A nineteenth-century corporate record book can therefore survive while remaining difficult to retrieve through a modern search for the literal title `American Oriental Society proceedings book`.

This strengthens the existing strategy of reconstructing legacy catalog/custody metadata before treating repeated public no-hits as evidence of loss.
