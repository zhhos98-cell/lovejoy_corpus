## Purpose

The live 1897 publication-genesis problem is no longer whether Lovejoy had a communication announced before the Baltimore meeting. That state is secure from the 10 April AOS Final Circular. The open chronological problem is whether a carrier produced **during or immediately around 22–24 April 1897** records either the Milinda title, the later `upādāna / upādāna-kkhandhā` title, a correction, or a read-by-title notice independently of the post-meeting JAOS Proceedings.

Public web discovery now identifies three tightly dated press carriers around the meeting. None has yet yielded page-level Lovejoy text in the currently accessible search layer.

## 1. Baltimore Sun — 22 April 1897, p. 1

Search-index discovery identifies an opening-day notice in the **Baltimore Sun**, 22 April 1897, p. 1. The recoverable index/OCR snippet states in substance that the American Oriental Society would begin its annual meeting that afternoon at Johns Hopkins University.

This is the highest-value press target because:

- it is local to Baltimore;
- it is dated the first day of the meeting;
- it may derive from a local programme, university notice, officer briefing, or advance press copy rather than from the later printed Proceedings;
- a paper list or session notice on this page, or in 23–25 April follow-up coverage, could independently date a title state to the meeting window.

Current access limit: the discovery path exposes search/index material, not a reliably inspectable full page in the present web environment.

Targeted public searches using `Lovejoy`, `Milinda`, and `upadana` have not produced an indexed Lovejoy hit from this page. This is an indexing negative only.

Assessment: **TOP-TIER PAGE-IMAGE / FULL-OCR RETRIEVAL TARGET; LOVEJOY CONTENT UNCONTROLLED.**

## 2. Philadelphia Jewish Exponent — 23 April 1897, p. 9

Search-index discovery identifies a meeting-day notice in the **Philadelphia Jewish Exponent**, 23 April 1897, p. 9. The recoverable snippet says that, beginning Thursday afternoon in McCoy Hall of Johns Hopkins University, the American Oriental Society would be holding its 108th meeting; it also names President D. C. Gilman and notes his Friday luncheon invitation to members.

This confirms a second public carrier generated while the Baltimore meeting was underway or immediately adjacent to it. Because the notice includes meeting logistics beyond a generic retrospective mention, its underlying copy may have come from circulated programme/circular information.

Current access limit: the discovered newspaper page is not directly inspectable through the present web path. Targeted index searches have not recovered `Lovejoy`, `Milinda`, or `upadana` from it.

Assessment: **HIGH-VALUE MEETING-DAY PRESS CARRIER / LOVEJOY CONTENT OPEN.**

## 3. Central Presbyterian — 28 April 1897, p. 16

Search-index discovery identifies a near-immediate post-meeting notice in the **Central Presbyterian**, 28 April 1897, p. 16. The recoverable snippet states that the American Oriental Society held its one hundred and eighth meeting in Baltimore the previous week and identifies Daniel C. Gilman as president of the Society.

This is chronologically later than the Baltimore Sun and Jewish Exponent witnesses but still much closer to the meeting than a generic later retrospective source. It may preserve a syndicated or locally supplied account of meeting activity.

Current access limit: page-level inspection remains unavailable in the present retrieval path. Targeted indexed searches have not recovered a Lovejoy/title string.

## 5. Why these carriers matter for the title-change problem

The controlled documentary sequence remains:

`10 Apr Final Circular — Milinda title`

→ `22–24 Apr Baltimore meeting — Lovejoy absent; communication later reported as read by title`

→ `post-meeting Proceedings carrier — upādāna / upādāna-kkhandhā title state`.

A meeting-day press witness could discriminate among the live mechanisms after 10 April:

1. **If a 22–24 April page prints the Milinda title**, the Milinda state survived closer to or into the meeting and later Proceedings normalization/reframing becomes more plausible.
2. **If a 22–24 April page prints the technical-terms title**, the title change can be placed before or during the meeting even though Lovejoy was absent; the mechanism would then have to be remote authorial, officer/editorial, or another non-in-person carrier.
3. **If a page reports only `read by title` without giving a title**, it can independently control meeting status but not title chronology.
4. **If neither title appears after full inspection**, that is a bounded negative for that particular press carrier only.

Therefore:

> **MEETING-DAY PRESS TITLE, IF RECOVERED, WOULD DATE A PUBLIC TITLE STATE MORE TIGHTLY THAN THE POST-MEETING PROCEEDINGS CARRIER.**

## 6. Related contemporaneous comparator — Gilman correspondence, 23 March 1897

A publicly described Daniel C. Gilman letter dated **Baltimore, 23 March 1897**, written to Samuel P. Langley, discusses the forthcoming AOS session of 22–24 April and tells Langley that Gilman would refer a proposed matter to Paul Haupt and George B. Adams. The letter is useful only as an administrative comparator: it shows that business for this exact Baltimore meeting could be routed through correspondence and officer/scholar networks before the meeting.

It contains no presently recovered Lovejoy, Milinda, or Buddhist-paper evidence and must not be used as proof of Lovejoy's submission route.

Assessment: **REMOTE AGENDA-FORMATION COMPARATOR / NO LOVEJOY-SPECIFIC TRACE.**
