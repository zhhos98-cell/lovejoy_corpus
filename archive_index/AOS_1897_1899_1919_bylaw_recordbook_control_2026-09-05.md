# AOS 1897 / 1899 / 1919 by-law and record-book chronology control

Date: 2026-09-05  
Status: **DIRECT OFFICIAL TEXT / CHRONOLOGY CORRECTION / LATER-REPRINT TRAP CONTROLLED / 1919 ADOPTION STILL OPEN**

## Purpose

This note separates three institutional states that had begun to blur in the 1897 lost-paper search:

1. the manuscript-disposal rule in force around Lovejoy's 1897 communication;
2. the later 1899 addition of an author opt-out clause to By-Law V;
3. Charles R. Lanman's 1919 motion concerning the repository of the Society's manuscript books of record.

These states must not be collapsed into one timeless AOS custody rule.

## 1. By-Law V before the 1899 amendment

Official AOS meeting material immediately before the Cambridge annual meeting of 1899 quotes By-Law V in the older form:

> `All papers read before the Society, and all manuscripts deposited by authors for publication or for other purposes, shall be at the disposal of the Board of Directors.`

The same circular separately explains that manuscripts intended for publication should be sent to the editor by the stated deadline and that papers requiring rapid publication could be arranged for specially.

This is important for the 1897 Lovejoy search because it shows that the later opt-out clause was **not yet part of By-Law V immediately before the April 1899 meeting**.

Primary carrier: University of Chicago Library, Office of the President, Harper/Judson/Burton Administrations Records, Box 7 Folder 13, American Oriental Society circular material, digitized PDF `ofcpreshjb-0007-013.pdf`.

## 2. The opt-out clause was added in April 1899

The published Proceedings of the AOS annual meeting held in Cambridge, Massachusetts, 6–7 April 1899 record a specific amendment action. Professor Lanman reported for the Directors; the Directors recommended adding to By-Law V the words:

> `unless notice to the contrary is given to the Editors at the time of presentation.`

The Proceedings then state that the Society adopted this amendment by unanimous vote.

The Constitution and By-Laws printed with the same 1899 Proceedings consequently give By-Law V in the amended form: papers read and manuscripts deposited remain at the disposal of the Board of Directors **unless** contrary notice is given to the Editors at presentation.

Primary/public carriers:

- American Oriental Society, `Proceedings ... Meeting in Cambridge, Mass., 1899`, JAOS 20 (1899), pp. 361–371, JSTOR stable `592344`.
- `Constitution and By-Laws of the American Oriental Society`, JAOS 20 (1899), pp. 385–387, JSTOR stable `592346`.
- Searchable full-volume text also preserves the amendment action and printed amended By-Law V.

### Consequence for 1897

Do **not** import the 1899 opt-out clause backward into the Baltimore meeting of 1897.

The presently controlled chronology is:

`pre-Apr 1899 older V without opt-out`

→ `6–7 Apr 1899 amendment adopted`

→ `1899 printed V with opt-out`.

The exact 1897 Constitution/By-Laws pages in JAOS 18 remain a desirable diplomatic control, especially because the printed constitution is headed as incorporating April 1897 amendments connected with the new Section for the Historical Study of Religions. But the 1899 amendment action now independently dates the **opt-out clause itself** to 1899 rather than 1897.

Therefore:

`1899 OPT-OUT CLAUSE != 1897 RULE`.

## 2A. Bibliographic trap — later reprints retain the `With Amendments of April, 1897` heading

Later official printings prove that the heading `With Amendments of April, 1897` is **not** a diplomatic date-stamp for every provision printed beneath it.

A clear control is the `Constitution and By-Laws of the American Oriental Society` printed in **JAOS 28 (1907), pp. 434–436, JSTOR stable 592784**. Searchable official text retains the heading:

> `With Amendments of April, 1897.`

while By-Law V in that later printing contains the opt-out language:

> `unless notice to the contrary is given to the Editors at the time of presentation.`

The same pattern is visible in other post-1899 reprints. Since the published 1899 Proceedings directly record the recommendation and unanimous adoption of that opt-out clause in April 1899, a later constitution headed `With Amendments of April, 1897` cannot be treated as a frozen transcript of the 1897 by-laws.

Interpretive rule:

> **THE HEADING TRACKS A CONSTITUTIONAL AMENDMENT STATE; IT DOES NOT GUARANTEE THAT EVERY BY-LAW BELOW REMAINED UNCHANGED SINCE APRIL 1897.**

This is the specific bibliographic mechanism by which a later AOS reprint can silently back-project the 1899 By-Law V language if the reprint date is ignored.

Project firewall:

`LATER REPRINT HEADED 1897 != DIPLOMATIC 1897 BY-LAW TEXT`.

Operational consequence: exact 1897 claims should control to JAOS 18 (1897), pp. 403–405, or to an amendment chronology that independently proves a provision's date. Do not use the recurring `With Amendments of April, 1897` heading alone as evidence that a quoted by-law was already present in 1897.

## 3. What the 1897 custody rule can and cannot establish

The older form of By-Law V distinguishes two classes:

- papers read before the Society;
- manuscripts deposited by authors for publication or other purposes.

It assigns such material to the disposal of the Board of Directors. It does **not** establish that every announced communication was accompanied by a manuscript, that every title read in an author's absence counted as a deposited manuscript, or that every deposited object was permanently retained.

For Lovejoy in 1897, the direct circular evidence establishes the name-and-title submission route to Hanns Oertel before the 10 April Final Circular. The 18 March Preliminary Circular explicitly solicits contributor name + title separately from attendance intention. The later Proceedings report that Lovejoy was absent and that his communication was read by title. By-Law V strengthens the institutional warrant for searching Board/editorial/deposit carriers, but it does not turn that warrant into evidence that a Lovejoy manuscript entered those carriers.

Project firewall:

> **MANUSCRIPT-DISPOSAL RULE != MANUSCRIPT DEPOSIT; BOARD DISPOSAL != PERMANENT RETENTION.**

## 4. Lanman's 1919 manuscript-record-book motion

The 1919 pre-meeting notice prints two distinct kinds of proposed action:

- Franklin Edgerton's proposed amendments to the Constitution and By-Laws, including deletion from By-Law I of the Corresponding Secretary's duty to keep copies of his letters in a book;
- separate motions to be presented by Charles R. Lanman.

Lanman's first printed motion states:

> `Resolved: That the proper repository of the manuscript books of record of the Society is the Society's Library.`

The notice continues by asking officers holding such volumes to deposit them in the Library when the volumes were filled or replaced.

Primary carrier: `Notes of the Society`, JAOS 39 (1919), pre-meeting notice for the Philadelphia meeting of 23–25 April 1919.

## 5. Published-action negative control for the 1919 repository motion

The 1919 published record can now be calibrated beyond a generic web-search HOLD.

- The pre-meeting notice directly proves that the repository motion was scheduled to be presented.
- The published Proceedings explicitly record and dispose of **another** Lanman resolution concerning the presentation of communications: the Corresponding Secretary read it in Lanman's absence, and the Society voted to lay that motion on the table.
- The published Proceedings explicitly record adoption of Edgerton's formal constitutional/by-law amendments.
- The post-meeting Constitution and By-Laws identify the incorporated 1919 amendments.
- In the inspected published record, no corresponding action has been recovered for Lanman's separate `proper repository of the manuscript books of record` motion.

This is a stronger negative control than merely failing to find the motion through indexed search. It shows that the published Proceedings were capable of recording both amendment actions and at least one other Lanman motion, while the repository motion's disposition remains absent from the recovered published action record.

However, absence from the published Proceedings does not establish rejection, withdrawal, or non-adoption. An unpublished minute book, Directors' record, or other internal carrier could still record action omitted from the printed Proceedings.

Therefore the calibrated status is:

**MOTION TEXT SECURE / PUBLISHED ADOPTION NOT RECOVERED / UNPUBLISHED ACTION OPEN.**

Do not write that the repository motion was `tabled`: the motion explicitly recorded as laid on the table in the published Proceedings is the separate Lanman resolution concerning presentation procedure.

Project firewall:

> **ONE LANMAN MOTION TABLED != ALL LANMAN MOTIONS TABLED.**

## 6. Search consequences

The carrier search should now use the following chronology rather than a timeless custody model:

### 1897

- Oertel is the directly documented name-and-title recipient under the 18 March printed procedure.
- Board of Directors / editor / deposit routes remain institutionally warranted by the older By-Law V.
- A deposited Lovejoy manuscript remains unproved.
- The 1899 author opt-out clause must not be used to reconstruct Lovejoy's 1897 procedural choices.
- Later AOS constitutions carrying the `With Amendments of April, 1897` heading must be date-controlled before any by-law wording is projected backward.

### 1919 and later custody reconstruction

- Lanman's motion proves that the location of `manuscript books of record` was an explicit institutional issue in 1919.
- Published adoption remains unrecovered.
- Yale/AOS manuscript-book searches therefore remain justified as custody reconstruction, but should not cite the Lanman motion as an executed transfer order.
- Highest-value documentary closure would come from the 1919 Recording Secretary proceedings/minute book, Directors' book, or another internal record that records the fate of Lanman's repository motion.
