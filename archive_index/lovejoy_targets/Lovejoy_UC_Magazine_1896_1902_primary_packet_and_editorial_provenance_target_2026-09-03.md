## Core result

Three early Lovejoy objects currently needed by separate research branches were published in the same Berkeley alumni-periodical run and can therefore be recovered as **one targeted archival packet** rather than three unrelated searches.

### Text packet

1. Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Some Harvard Notes`, *University of California Magazine* 2 (1896), exact pages damaged/unresolved in Wilson OCR.
2. Arthur O. Lovejoy, `The Social Rôle of the French University`, *University of California Magazine* 6 (1900), 134–146.
3. Arthur O. Lovejoy, `Ecclesiastes Instructed`, *University of California Magazine* 8 (1902), Wilson p.285; probable vol.8 no.6 / October 1902 issue; serial-title transition to `The Graduate` must be allowed in retrieval.

## Why these three belong in one recovery packet

### 1896 — `Some Harvard Notes`

Wilson says the article discusses:

- Harvard's `public spirit`;
- social services;
- religious observances;
- the Graduate School;
- institutional adaptation to educational transition.

The primary has not been recovered in the repo. It is important for testing whether Wilson's later description of Lovejoy's social-service/institutional concerns is textually accurate and for separating early university-social-role language from later reconstruction.

### 1900 — `The Social Rôle of the French University`

Wilson gives vol.6, pp.134–146 and says Lovejoy judges the University of Paris both as an intellectual institution and as a `member in a complicated social organism`, criticizing student social training, responsibility, democratization/selection and the university's relation to political parties while still treating it as an important democratic agency.

This is a high-value **post-Paris primary**. Its full text is needed to compare safely with the 1898 France letters' direct observations of:

- French university structure;
- course choice and intellectual specialization;
- religion/politics;
- personal responsibility;
- the relation between institutional classification and actual scholarly practice.

Do not reconstruct the 1900 article from Wilson's abstract once the original can be obtained.

### 1902 — `Ecclesiastes Instructed`

Wilson records the sonnet and summarizes it as finding meaning in life by helping others. New MS-0873 evidence makes it a materially important carrier because Ecclesiastes was already a named object inside Lovejoy's 1898 Paris `Wisdom work`.

Independent controls narrow the likely issue to vol.8 no.6 / October 1902, around p.285, but direct page inspection remains required.

Berkeley Alumni Association's publication history says the alumni periodical became known as **`The Graduate` in 1902**. Therefore the request must include both serial-title states.

## Recommended single text request

Ask Sacramento Room to check the physical run `SPE PERIODICAL 1895 SAC` for:

- volume 2 (1896): table of contents/index + complete `Some Harvard Notes` by Arthur O. Lovejoy;
- volume 6 (1900): pp.134–146, `The Social Rôle of the French University`;
- volume 8, probably no.6 / October 1902: p.285 and surrounding pages, `Ecclesiastes Instructed`; search under both *University of California Magazine* and *The Graduate* if title changes within the bound volume.

For the 1902 item request **p.284–286 or equivalent surrounding spread**, not only a cropped poem, so page sharing/title transition/editorial context can be controlled.

## Willard Giles Parsons control

UC alumni records identify Willard Giles Parsons as an A.B. graduate of **1900**. The folder title directly associates him with the alumni magazine in 1899–1906. This supports treating him as an editorial/organizational node for retrieval, but his exact role in the 1900/1902 issues must be established from the folder or other contemporary source before assigning editor status.

## Proof architecture

Keep separate:

`PRIMARY PRINT TEXT`
!=
`EDITORIAL CORRESPONDENCE / SOLICITATION`
!=
`AUTHORIAL PROJECT GENETICS`.

A recovered magazine text can establish what Lovejoy published. A recovered Parsons/Hearst editorial document may establish why/how the venue carried it. Neither by itself proves a genetic relation among 1898 Paris research, the 1900 university essay and the 1902 sonnet.

## Cost / efficiency consequence

This route consolidates three otherwise separate primary-text gaps into one physical-periodical request. It should be prioritized over continued open-web searching for each item independently.

Editorial provenance can then be requested separately as one small archival folder rather than an open-ended Berkeley archive search.
