## Purpose

The 1901 Ross -> 1915 academic-freedom branch can no longer be advanced safely by comparing only the final Declaration to Lovejoy's earlier public statements.

Tiede's draft history shows that different final clauses have different immediate owners and that Lovejoy's own proposals were modified or omitted during committee revision.

The next archival request should therefore recover **version states**, not a generic `1915 Declaration file`.

## P5 — Ford / survey-first versus Lovejoy principles-first dispute

Repository routes:

- Richard T. Ely Papers, **Reel 52**: `Seligman -> Members of Committee on Academic Freedom, 15 Aug 1915`; `Ford -> Seligman, 6 Aug 1915`;
- Cornell AAUP 1913–18, **`Brewster Case`**: `Lovejoy -> Seligman, 21 Aug 1915`.

Purpose:

Directly verify Lovejoy's methodological objection to deriving academic-freedom/tenure standards from a survey of existing practices.

Tiede's reconstruction:

`FORD: SURVEY PRACTICE -> DISTILL ADEQUATE/FAIR PRACTICE`

versus

`LOVEJOY: EXISTING PRACTICE TOO UNREGULARIZED -> FORMULATE PRINCIPLES FIRST -> THEN SURVEY`.

Lovejoy reportedly says this is the reverse of how he would approach most other topics.

This is a high-value control against claims of one universal Lovejoy research procedure.
