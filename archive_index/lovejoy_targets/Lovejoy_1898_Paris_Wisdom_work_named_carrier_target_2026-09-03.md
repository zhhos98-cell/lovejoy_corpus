## Major target upgrade 2 — exact fellowship identity: James Walker Fellowship

A Washington University biographical record (*The Hatchet*, 1905) identifies Lovejoy as:

> `James Walker Fellow of Harvard University, in the University of Paris, 1898–1899.`

This gives a precise institutional handle for the fellowship restriction described in the 20 Oct 1898 letter.

Contemporary/later Harvard evidence shows that the **James Walker Fellowship was a graduate fellowship used for philosophy and could be a travelling/non-resident fellowship**. Examples include:

- 1895 Harvard announcement: Robert MacDougall, James Walker Fellow, `to study Philosophy`;
- William Savery: James Walker Fellow (traveling), Harvard, 1897–98, student in Berlin;
- 1909 Harvard fellowship list explicitly places the James Walker Fellowship among travelling fellowships and identifies Jacob Loewenberg as a student of Philosophy.

This does not yet recover Lovejoy's appointment record or define his individual project. It does sharply narrow the Harvard archival target.

- a live research intention;

- a working paper/project;

- a required fellowship-report object;
- a project abandoned or repartitioned before publication.
