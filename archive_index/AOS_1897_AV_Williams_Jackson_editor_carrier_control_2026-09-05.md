# A. V. Williams Jackson — 1897 AOS editor-carrier control

Date: 2026-09-05  
Status: **EDITORIAL ROLE SECURE / YALE MS 1009 IDENTIFIED / CORRESPONDENCE CARRIER DE-PRIORITIZED**

## Purpose

At the Baltimore meeting in April 1897, A. V. Williams Jackson entered the AOS editorial chain that would carry the June 1897 Proceedings. Because the Lovejoy technical-title state is securely a June publication carrier state, Jackson's papers are an obvious candidate only if they preserve editorial correspondence, proofs, or journal working papers. This note tests that possibility without converting office into authorship of the title change.

## 1. Editorial role

The original AOS meeting/journal record places Jackson and George F. Moore on the 1897–98 editorial side after Charles R. Lanman reported that he could no longer continue the editorial work. This makes Jackson institutionally relevant to the June carrier.

That role supports one narrow question:

> **Do Jackson's surviving papers preserve AOS editorial working material from spring 1897 capable of dating or explaining Lovejoy's title state?**

It does not support the proposition that Jackson personally copied, changed, normalized, or approved Lovejoy's title.

## 2. Yale MS 1009 — real collection, wrong surviving material profile for the immediate hunt

Yale Manuscripts and Archives holds:

- **A. V. Williams Jackson papers, MS 1009**;
- dates: **1889–1909**;
- extent: **0.75 linear feet**.

The current collection overview describes the contents as **notes and drafts for articles and speeches**, largely on Sanskrit and Avestan grammar/literature and the cultures of India and Iran. It also notes a program for the 1894 Joint Meetings of philological societies.

The overview does **not** describe a correspondence series, AOS editorial files, journal proofs, secretary material, or a spring-1897 administrative packet.

This makes the collection chronologically attractive but functionally weak for the Lovejoy title-transition problem.

Source control: Yale University Library, Manuscripts and Archives, `A. V. Williams Jackson papers`, MS 1009, collection overview/finding aid.

Assessment:

**REAL 1889–1909 JACKSON COLLECTION / CONTENT PROFILE = SCHOLARLY NOTES & DRAFTS / NO PUBLICLY DESCRIBED 1897 AOS EDITORIAL CORRESPONDENCE SERIES.**

## 3. Columbia-side public discovery

Public Columbia finding-aid searching exposes Jackson-related material in the broad `Columbiana Manuscripts` environment, including isolated incoming letters to Jackson from 1899 and Jackson manuscript/work items. It does not presently expose a coherent Jackson correspondence or AOS editorial series covering spring 1897.

This is not evidence that no Jackson correspondence survives at Columbia or elsewhere. It only means that current public discovery does not yield a better requestable 1897 editorial unit than the AOS institutional records already identified.

Assessment:

**DISTRIBUTED JACKSON MATERIAL EXISTS / NO COHERENT 1897 AOS EDITORIAL FILE RECOVERED.**

## 4. Priority consequence

Jackson should remain in the actor map because he was part of the June-carrier editorial chain. His surviving papers should not move ahead of institutional records merely because MS 1009 spans 1897.

Current order for discriminating the Lovejoy title transition remains:

1. AOS Recording Secretary proceedings/minute book covering April 1897;
2. AOS Corresponding Secretary letter-copy book covering March–June 1897;
3. Oertel MS 590 Box 3 Folder 56, especially undated `I-R`;
4. Lanman spring-1897 calendars/correspondence/editorial or HOS records, because he sits at the pre-/meeting editorial handoff;
5. Moore distributed/editorial correspondence or journal working records;
6. Jackson MS 1009 only if an item-level catalogue/manual inspection identifies AOS or editorial material not represented in the collection overview.

## 5. Evidence firewall

`APPOINTED EDITOR != HANDLED LOVEJOY ITEM`

`COLLECTION DATE RANGE INCLUDES 1897 != COLLECTION CONTAINS 1897 AOS ADMINISTRATION`

`NO DESCRIBED CORRESPONDENCE SERIES != NO JACKSON CORRESPONDENCE SURVIVES ANYWHERE`.

Compact judgment:

> **Jackson remains part of the institutional publication chain, but Yale MS 1009 is presently a low-yield carrier for the Lovejoy title-change mechanism because its publicly described surviving material is scholarly notes/drafts rather than AOS editorial correspondence.**
