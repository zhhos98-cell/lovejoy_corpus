# AOS 1896–1897 abstract procedure control

Date: 2026-09-05  
Status: **DIRECT CIRCULAR + ORIGINAL PROCEEDINGS EVIDENCE / 1897 LOVEJOY ABSTRACT OR BRIEF STATEMENT EXISTENCE OPEN**

## Core correction

The project's earlier procedural shorthand was too late-dated: an AOS instruction concerning **advance abstracts for long papers is directly documented in the 1896 meeting circular material**, before Lovejoy's 1897 communication. Abstract practice therefore cannot be described as appearing only in 1899/1900.

At the same time, direct image inspection of the surviving **18 Mar 1897 Preliminary Circular** and **10 Apr 1897 Final Circular** does **not** show the 1896 abstract instruction repeated. The 1897 Preliminary Circular explicitly asks intending contributors to send **their names and the titles of their papers** to Hanns Oertel at 31 York Square, New Haven. The 1897 Final Circular announces Lovejoy's title but does not establish receipt of an abstract or manuscript.

The original 1897 Proceedings add a third procedural state. Printed p. 387 enumerates No. 30 `(by Lovejoy)` among a group of papers that:

> `were read by title, and with or without a brief statement of their contents.`

The phrase is a **group-level disjunction**. It does not identify which enumerated papers received a brief statement and which were title-only.

Therefore:

`AOS ABSTRACT PRACTICE DOCUMENTED BY 1896 != LOVEJOY ABSTRACT SUBMITTED IN 1897`

`1897 CIRCULAR OMITS ABSTRACT INSTRUCTION != ABSTRACTS IMPOSSIBLE IN 1897`

and

`LOVEJOY INCLUDED IN "WITH OR WITHOUT A BRIEF STATEMENT" GROUP != LOVEJOY BRIEF STATEMENT PROVED`.

## 1896 procedural witness

The University of Chicago recipient file for AOS meeting circulars contains indexed official 1896 circular text stating that, because a Society rule restricted the time devoted to each paper to twenty minutes, authors with long papers were requested to **prepare in advance an abstract**, whose reading would fit the time limit while giving the general outline of the thesis.

This establishes a pre-1897 AOS practice of author-prepared abstracts for long presentations.

It does not establish:

- that every paper had an abstract;
- that every abstract was submitted to the Corresponding Secretary in advance rather than simply brought/read at the meeting;
- that abstracts were retained institutionally;
- that Lovejoy's 1897 paper was classed as a long paper;
- that Lovejoy prepared or transmitted such an abstract.

## 1897 direct circular control

### Preliminary Circular — 18 Mar 1897

Direct image inspection shows:

> `Members intending to offer communications are requested to send their names and the titles of their papers at once to the undersigned, 31 York Square, New Haven, Conn.`

The same circular separately solicits attendance intention. No abstract-submission instruction appears on the inspected two-page 1897 Preliminary Circular.

### Final Circular — 10 Apr 1897

Direct image inspection shows the meeting arrangements, business order, list of papers announced to date, and expected-presence list. Lovejoy appears as No. 24 under `LIST OF PAPERS ANNOUNCED TO DATE` with `Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`. No Lovejoy abstract or general abstract-submission instruction appears in the inspected Final Circular.

Lovejoy is absent from the separate expected-presence list.

## 1897 original Proceedings control — printed p. 387

The original 1897 *JAOS* volume is now directly controlled through Internet Archive identifier `pt2journalofamer18ameruoft`, University of Toronto / Robarts scan of volume 18 pt.2.

At the closing session, the Proceedings state that, because adjournment was near, a group including:

`30 (by Lovejoy)`

was handled as follows:

> `for the most part, papers of members not present,—were read by title, and with or without a brief statement of their contents.`

Three distinctions matter.

1. **Read by title does not exclude a brief content statement.** The printed sentence explicitly allows both forms of handling within the group.
2. **The sentence does not assign a brief statement to Lovejoy individually.** `with or without` leaves No. 30 unresolved.
3. **A meeting-floor brief statement is not automatically an author-prepared abstract.** It could in principle derive from an author-supplied abstract, manuscript, cover note, officer summary, or other carrier. The Proceedings do not identify its source for any particular item in this group.

Thus the correct Lovejoy-local state is:

`NO.30 READ BY TITLE = SECURE`

`NO.30 ACCOMPANIED BY BRIEF STATEMENT = OPEN`

`IF BRIEF STATEMENT EXISTED, AUTHOR-PREPARED ABSTRACT = STILL OPEN`.

## Search consequence

The minimum secure pre-10-Apr 1897 carrier remains a **name + title notice to Oertel**. However, because AOS abstract practice is demonstrably pre-1897 and the meeting Proceedings explicitly permit brief content statements alongside title-reading, the archival search should not exclude an additional content-bearing carrier.

The search object should now be phrased more broadly than simply `abstract`:

- author-prepared abstract;
- brief statement of contents;
- cover note / explanatory title notice;
- manuscript or excerpt from which an officer could make such a statement;
- secretary/editor summary;
- meeting packet or annotated program.

Possible 1897 carriers remain:

- Oertel correspondence / title-submission packet;
- Corresponding Secretary letter-copy book, directly required by the 1897 By-Laws;
- Recording Secretary proceedings/minute book, directly required by the 1897 By-Laws;
- Board of Editors / Directors papers;
- Lovejoy correspondence or retained drafts;
- institutional meeting packets;
- distributed officer/recipient papers.

The present evidentiary position is not that an abstract is unlikely because the paper was read by title. The stronger formulation is that **no individual Lovejoy content-bearing carrier has yet been recovered, while the institutional procedure leaves several such carrier types possible**.
