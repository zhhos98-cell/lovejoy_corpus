# AOS 1897 Lovejoy title workflow — Oertel / Moore responsibility control

Date: 2026-09-05  
Status: **DIRECT ROLE/PROCEDURE EVIDENCE / SEARCH CHAIN SHARPENED / NO TITLE-CHANGE AUTHOR IDENTIFIED**

## Purpose

The Lovejoy title-change problem is now bounded between a directly printed 10 April `Milinda-pañha` state and a June 1897 Proceedings carrier with the direct visual printed state `upādānam / upādāna-kkhandhā` on p.389. This note maps the AOS officers whose documented roles sit on the relevant administrative path without converting office responsibility into authorship of the title change.

Core rule:

> **DOCUMENTED RESPONSIBILITY CHAIN != PERSONALLY AUTHORED TITLE CHANGE.**

## 1. Hanns Oertel — pre-meeting title intake

The 18 March 1897 AOS Preliminary Circular directly instructs members intending to offer communications to send **their names and the titles of their papers** to:

`31 York Square, New Haven, Conn.`

The circular is signed by **Hanns Oertel, Acting Corresponding Secretary**. The 10 April Final Circular, likewise issued under Oertel's name, then prints Lovejoy's No. 24 title:

`Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha`.

This makes Oertel the documented first administrative recipient for the title-registration stage. It does not prove that Lovejoy personally wrote to Oertel, that the title arrived only after 18 March, or that Oertel received an abstract/manuscript.

Secure search objects on the Oertel side:

- initial Lovejoy name/title notice;
- cover note;
- attendance response, if any;
- remote correction or replacement title after 10 April;
- explanatory/brief content statement;
- forwarded material to the Recording Secretary or Editors.

Primary archival target remains Yale MS 590, Box 3 Folder 56 (`I-R`, especially undated material), with the separate AOS Corresponding Secretary letter-copy book as the stronger institutional record class if its 1897 volume survives.

## 2. George F. Moore — proceedings record and journal-production side

The original 1897 AOS Constitution and By-Laws directly require:

> `The Recording Secretary shall keep a record of the proceedings of the Society in a book provided for the purpose.`

George F. Moore is the Recording Secretary in the Baltimore Proceedings. The original volume also shows him performing that office in meeting procedure: the prior meeting record is read by the Recording Secretary and approved.

Moore therefore sits on the **official proceedings-record path** for the April meeting independently of any later journal editing role.

The same June 1897 journal carrier also places Moore on the editorial side. The original-volume editorial matter names Charles R. Lanman and George F. Moore in the journal's editorial apparatus; during the Baltimore meeting Lanman reported that he could no longer continue editorial work, and the Society appointed A. V. Williams Jackson together with George F. Moore as Editors for 1897–98. The volume's publication notice directs communications for the Journal to **Prof. Geo. F. Moore, Andover, Mass.**

Thus Moore is the clearest documented bridge between:

`APRIL PROCEEDINGS RECORD`

and

`JUNE PRINTED PROCEEDINGS / JOURNAL PRODUCTION`.

This materially raises the search value of Moore-related records for the title-state transition. It still does **not** identify Moore as the person who changed, normalized, copied, or approved Lovejoy's title.

## 3. Controlled workflow model

The documentary chain can now be expressed without guessing manuscript identity:

`Lovejoy / intermediary`

→ **Oertel / Acting Corresponding Secretary** — contributor name + title intake before 10 Apr

→ `10 Apr Final Circular` — Milinda title printed

→ possible remote correction / explanatory carrier / manuscript / brief statement **OPEN**

→ **Moore / Recording Secretary** — official proceedings-record responsibility at Baltimore

→ Editors / journal-production workflow, with **Moore directly present on both the recording and editorial sides**

→ `June 1897 JAOS 18 Second Half` — technical-terms title printed on **p.389** as `upādānam / upādāna-kkhandhā`.

What is missing is the transition object between the Milinda registration state and the June printed technical-title state.

## 4. What would discriminate mechanisms

### A. Oertel-side correction

A letter/title slip after 10 April saying `change`, `alter`, `correct`, `instead`, `title`, or giving a second title would support a remote authorial/intermediary correction before or around the meeting.

### B. Moore proceedings-book state

If the 1897 Recording Secretary proceedings book gives the technical title, that would date the state much closer to the April meeting and sharply reduce the probability that it was created only in later journal production.

If it gives the Milinda title while the June print gives the technical title, the title transformation would move decisively into the post-meeting editorial/publication interval.

If it records only `Lovejoy` / No.30 / read by title without a title, it would control meeting handling but leave the title chronology open.

### C. Editorial working state

A proof, copy text, editorial correspondence, printer copy, or journal submission note carrying the technical title would place the state in the publication workflow even if the meeting title remained unknown.

### D. Brief content statement

The June Proceedings say the relevant closing-session group was read by title `with or without a brief statement of their contents`. A Lovejoy-specific content statement, if recovered, could explain how an officer/editor arrived at a more technical descriptive title without proving that Lovejoy himself supplied that exact wording.

## 5. George F. Moore public archival audit

Current public finding-aid discovery does **not** expose a coherent George F. Moore personal/AOS administrative archive covering 1897.

### Harvard Divinity School Faculty Writings File — bMS 13001/Moore, G. F.

The current HDS description identifies this as a **Faculty Writings File**, not a personal papers collection. Its Moore contents are mainly published writings, offprints, memorial/biographical material and related vertical-file items. The listed chronology includes AOS-related published work in 1888 and then later writings, but no 1897 AOS secretary/editing file is exposed at item level.

Assessment: **DO NOT PROMOTE bMS 13001 AS THE 1897 ADMINISTRATIVE CARRIER.** It remains useful for Moore bibliography/printed-output control only unless unlisted material is identified.

### Houghton `Autograph File, M`

The collection explicitly includes George Foot Moore among fragmentary letters/manuscripts gathered singly or in small groups. It is not a coherent Moore office file and public description does not identify a 1897 Lovejoy/AOS item.

Assessment: **FRAGMENTARY DISTRIBUTED-CARRIER TARGET ONLY.**

### Andover context

Moore taught at Andover Theological Seminary from 1883 to 1901, so `Andover, Mass.` in the June journal notice is institutionally consistent with his 1897 location. Current public searching has not surfaced a Moore personal correspondence series at Andover that can be named as the missing AOS editorial file.

Assessment: **LOCATION SECURE / COHERENT 1897 MOORE PAPERS NOT YET IDENTIFIED.**

## 6. Search-priority consequence

Because a coherent Moore personal administrative archive has not emerged publicly, Moore's newly sharpened role does **not** justify another generic `George Foot Moore papers` sweep as the first move. It instead raises the priority of institutional/distributed carriers tied to his functions:

1. **AOS Recording Secretary proceedings/minute book covering April 1897**;
2. **AOS Corresponding Secretary letter-copy book covering March–June 1897**;
3. Oertel MS 590 Box 3 Folder 56 for the title-intake/correction side;
4. journal/editorial working records, proofs, printer copy, or correspondence addressed to Moore at Andover;
5. distributed incoming correspondence in papers of AOS officers/editors/authors, searched for Moore + Lovejoy/title language;
6. fragmentary Moore material only when an item-level date/name hit warrants it.

The next archival question should therefore be stated as:

> **Can the 1897 AOS proceedings book or correspondence copy-book preserve a title state earlier than the June printed Proceedings, and does it agree with the 10 April Milinda title or the June technical title?**

That question can discriminate the title-change mechanism. A generic search for Moore biography cannot.
