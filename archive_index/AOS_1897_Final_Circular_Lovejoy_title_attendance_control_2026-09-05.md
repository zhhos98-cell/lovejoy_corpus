Date: 2026-09-05

## 18 March Preliminary Circular — the response architecture is explicit

Direct image inspection of the American Oriental Society Preliminary Circular dated **18 March 1897** shows that the Society deliberately solicited two different kinds of response.

For communications:

> `Members intending to offer communications are requested to send their names and the titles of their papers at once to the undersigned, 31 York Square, New Haven, Conn.`

For attendance:

> `Members intending to be present are also asked to send in their names, even if they do not mean to read papers.`

The circular then states that **the titles of papers** and **the names of those who signify their intention of being present** would be announced in the Final Circular. A continuation page states that the Final Circular would be issued on **10 April** and urges members to respond fully and promptly. Both pages are signed by Hanns Oertel, Acting Corresponding Secretary.

This means that the later differentiation visible in the Final Circular is not merely an inference from two lists. It follows the Society's own designed administrative architecture:

`COMMUNICATION / TITLE RESPONSE CHANNEL != ATTENDANCE-INTENTION RESPONSE CHANNEL`.

The printed 1897 Preliminary Circular itself requests **name + title** from intending contributors. It contains no inspected instruction requiring an abstract or manuscript at this stage. That is a statement about this printed request only: AOS abstract practice for long papers is independently documented by 1896, so an additional abstract or manuscript carrier remains possible but unproved.

## 10 April Final Circular — Lovejoy occupies only the title-registration side of the visible record

Direct image inspection of the Final Circular dated **10 April 1897** shows the two response classes announced on 18 March instantiated in the same document:

1. Lovejoy is present in the `LIST OF PAPERS ANNOUNCED TO DATE` as item 24:

> `Mr. ARTHUR O. LOVEJOY, Harvard University; Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha.`

2. Later in the same circular, under the statement

> `From the answers to circulars it is hoped that the following members and friends of the Society will be present:`

Lovejoy is **not** among the thirty-six named persons.

The circular closes with Hanns Oertel as Acting Corresponding Secretary and is dated New Haven, 10 April 1897.

## Evidentiary consequence

By 10 April, the Society had enough information from or concerning Lovejoy to register and print his paper title, while the visible attendance-response channel contained no positive signal for his presence.

The precise formulation should therefore be:

> **Lovejoy's paper/title was represented in the communication channel by 10 April; no positive attendance-intention response for him is represented in the Final Circular.**

This does not prove that Lovejoy personally transmitted the title rather than an intermediary doing so, that he formally declined attendance, that he had already decided not to travel to Baltimore, or that no later attendance message existed. The presence list is positively worded and must not be converted into a refusal list.

The controlled pre-meeting sequence is now:

`18 Mar — AOS separately solicits name + title and attendance intention`

→ `10 Apr — Lovejoy Milinda title printed / no positive Lovejoy attendance signal in the promised presence list`.

The 18 March circular provides the administrative semantics for reading the 10 April document; the two witnesses should be used together.

## Relation to the April meeting and June Proceedings carrier

The June 1897 Proceedings independently report that Lovejoy's No. 30 was among papers read by title at the Baltimore meeting. Read together with both circulars, the documentary sequence is now:

`18 Mar — dual response architecture explicitly solicited`

→ `10 Apr — Milinda title registered / no positive presence response represented`

→ `22–24 Apr meeting — Lovejoy absent / No.30 handled by title; Lovejoy-specific brief-statement status OPEN`

→ `June 1897 Proceedings carrier — technical-terms title state: upādāna / upādāna-kkhandhā`.

This is a pre-meeting administrative configuration consistent with a remote communication. It is stronger than merely learning after the meeting that Lovejoy was absent because title registration and attendance intention were explicitly separate response classes before the Final Circular was compiled.

## Publication-genesis consequence

The Milinda title is a concrete printed administrative state twelve days before the Baltimore meeting. Because Lovejoy was later absent, any change from this printed title to the technical-terms title printed in the June Proceedings carrier cannot be explained by an in-person oral retitling by Lovejoy at the meeting.

The live pathways after 10 April are therefore narrower:

1. **remote authorial correction/revision** sent after the Final Circular was compiled;
2. **officer/editorial reframing** based on a title notice, abstract, manuscript, brief statement, or other supplied material;
3. **post-meeting Proceedings normalization** of what the communication was understood to concern;
4. another intermediate documentary carrier not yet recovered.

None of these pathways is presently established. The circular pair changes the mechanism model, not the manuscript-identity burden.

The 18 March date also defines the Society's last printed solicitation before the 10 April registration state. It does **not** prove that Lovejoy's title arrived only after 18 March; the title could have been known earlier. Use the interval only as an administrative solicitation-to-registration sequence, not as a secure submission-date bracket.
