# AOS 1897 Final Circular — Lovejoy title registration / attendance-response control

Date: 2026-09-05  
Status: **DIRECT FINAL-CIRCULAR EVIDENCE / SAME-DOCUMENT ADMINISTRATIVE DIFFERENTIATION**

## Core result

Direct image inspection of the American Oriental Society Final Circular dated **10 April 1897** shows two administratively distinct lists in the same document:

1. Lovejoy is present in the `LIST OF PAPERS ANNOUNCED TO DATE` as item 24:

> `Mr. ARTHUR O. LOVEJOY, Harvard University; Critical summary of the argument of the Milinda-pañha.`

2. Later in the same circular, under the statement

> `From the answers to circulars it is hoped that the following members and friends of the Society will be present:`

Lovejoy is **not** among the thirty-six named persons.

The circular closes with Hanns Oertel as Acting Corresponding Secretary and is dated New Haven, 10 April 1897.

## Evidentiary consequence

By 10 April, the Society had enough information from or concerning Lovejoy to register and print his paper title, while the same administrative object did not place him among respondents whose presence was positively expected.

This establishes a useful separation of channels:

`PAPER/TITLE REGISTRATION != POSITIVE ATTENDANCE RESPONSE`.

It does not establish that Lovejoy formally declined attendance, that he had already decided not to travel to Baltimore, or that no later attendance message existed. The list is positively worded: it records those whose replies supported an expectation of presence. Absence from it should therefore be described as **no positive attendance response represented in the Final Circular**, not as a documented refusal.

## Relation to the later Proceedings

The post-meeting Proceedings independently report that Lovejoy was absent and that his communication was read by title. Read together with the 10 April Final Circular, the documentary sequence is now:

`by 10 Apr — Milinda title registered / Lovejoy not in positive-presence response list`

→ `22–24 Apr meeting — Lovejoy absent / communication read by title`

→ `post-meeting Proceedings carrier — technical-terms title state: upādāna / upādāna-kkhandhā`

The Final Circular therefore makes visible a pre-meeting administrative configuration consistent with a remote communication. It is stronger than merely learning after the meeting that Lovejoy was absent, because title registration and attendance expectation are differentiated inside one pre-meeting document.

## Publication-genesis consequence

The Milinda title is a concrete printed administrative state twelve days before the Baltimore meeting. Because Lovejoy was later absent, any change from this printed title to the technical-terms title reported in the post-meeting Proceedings cannot be explained by an in-person oral retitling by Lovejoy at the meeting.

The live pathways after 10 April are therefore narrower:

1. **remote authorial correction/revision** sent after the Final Circular was compiled;
2. **officer/editorial reframing** based on a title notice, abstract, manuscript, or other supplied material;
3. **post-meeting Proceedings normalization** of what the communication was understood to concern;
4. another intermediate documentary carrier not yet recovered.

None of these pathways is presently established. The same-document title/attendance differentiation changes the mechanism model, not the manuscript-identity burden.

## Abstract / manuscript firewall

This witness does not show an abstract or manuscript. Earlier project control establishes that AOS abstract practice for long papers existed by 1896, while the inspected 1897 Preliminary Circular explicitly requests names and titles and does not itself prove abstract submission by Lovejoy.

Therefore:

`TITLE REGISTERED != ABSTRACT RECEIVED`

`TITLE REGISTERED != MANUSCRIPT DEPOSIT`

`NOT IN POSITIVE ATTENDANCE LIST != FORMAL DECLINATION`

`REMOTE COMMUNICATION CONFIGURATION != EXCLUSIVE REMOTE CARRIER PROVED`.

## Search consequence

The highest-value missing object is no longer merely “a programme proving Lovejoy had a paper.” That state is already secure. The useful missing carrier must distinguish **who changed the title, when, and through what remote/editorial channel** after the 10 April printed state.

Priority terms for surviving correspondence / letter books / editorial records therefore include not only `Lovejoy`, `Milinda`, and `upādāna`, but also language of `change`, `alter`, `correct`, `title`, `communication`, `read by title`, `abstract`, `manuscript`, and `proof` in the period 10 April through preparation of the Proceedings.

Compact control:

> **10 APR TITLE REGISTRATION + NO POSITIVE PRESENCE RESPONSE != MANUSCRIPT ABSENCE; IT DOES NARROW THE TITLE-CHANGE MECHANISM TO REMOTE / OFFICER / EDITORIAL PATHWAYS.**
