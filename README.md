### Harvard and Paris: direct Orientalist/comparative-religion formation

Daniel J. Wilson, using a **Harvard University Archives list of courses taken by Lovejoy**, the *Harvard University Catalogue* 1896–97, and Lovejoy’s 1896 letters, records that Lovejoy studied **Sanskrit and sacred Buddhist texts with Charles R. Lanman**, comparative religion with **Charles C. Everett** (Vedic religion, Hinduism, Buddhism, Mazdaism, Chinese religions), and Hebrew religion with **Crawford H. Toy**. Harvard Library has digitized the relevant 1896–97 course-announcement series. The remaining linguistic question is narrower: whether Lovejoy had separately designated formal Pāli instruction and how independently he read Pāli texts.

Wilson’s account of Paris, based on Lovejoy’s letters of 17 October, 1 December, and 5 December 1898, says Lovejoy studied Psalms with Maurice Vernes, heard Jean Réville, **hoped to take Pāli Buddhism with Sylvain Lévi**, and when that course was not offered **occasionally went to Lévi’s home to read sacred texts with him**. The EPHE evidence independently adds Lovejoy’s active participation in Marillier’s comparative-religion conferences. Johns Hopkins holds **MS-0873, Daniel J. Wilson transcriptions of Arthur O. Lovejoy letters**, the highest-value next archive target for these claims.

### New primary-text bridge: 1898 Buddhist philology → 1902 history of categories

The full primary text of Lovejoy’s **“Religion and the Time-Process”** (*American Journal of Theology* 6, 1902, 439–472) has now been recovered. It is a major bridge. Lovejoy opens with the history of changing **categories**, makes **Vedānta and Buddhism the first analytic case**, and uses Buddhist impermanence plus dependent origination to explain the religious rejection of temporal process. At the key passage he explicitly refers readers back to his own 1898 `upādāna/upādisesa` paper. The sequence is therefore authorially documented: **004 → 1898 Buddhist technical philology → 1902 comparative history of a category**.

The article also already exhibits operations later central to Lovejoy’s history of ideas: tracing a persistent `perfectionist` presupposition/logical motive across heterogeneous systems, decomposing modern doctrines into competing inherited categories, and refusing to infer eastern influence on Aristotle or Neoplatonism merely from resemblance where an internal Greek genealogy accounts for the same conception. See batch 11.

### Indian philosophy did not disappear after the 1898 paper

The strict Indic/Buddhist publication record is small but the teaching/review record is longer. Wilson records a 1901 four-lecture syllabus, **“The Philosophy of Buddhism.”** A 1907 review shows Lovejoy still evaluating specialist Indian-philosophy work: he reviewed Paul Deussen’s *Outline of the Vedānta System of Philosophy, According to Shankara* in *Journal of Philosophy, Psychology and Scientific Methods* 4.1 (1907), 23–24. Wilson says Lovejoy called the translation a useful convenience for teachers of Hindu philosophy and urged study of **Rāmānuja’s Vedānta** rather than still more concentration on Śaṅkara. The public-domain journal volume has been located; the primary two-page review still needs extraction.

### 1907 primary text: Toy continuity and logical versus chronological sequence

The full primary text of **“The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought”** (*American Journal of Theology* 11, 1907, 228–249) has also been recovered. Lovejoy explicitly accepts the relative sequence of Wisdom literature **on Professor Toy’s authority**, giving direct published continuity with his Harvard Hebrew-religion training. More importantly for method, he distinguishes the **logical sequence** of a conception from absolute/relative textual chronology and allows earlier intellectual stages to survive beside later ones. He reconstructs ethical `inwardness` as an indirect, gradual development through heterogeneous Wisdom texts.

This sits very close chronologically to 005’s Hebrew eschatology / `nephesh-ruach` / `psychē` material and its dated `Hist. Relig. — Dec. 20, 1905` outline. A controlled 005→1907 concordance is now a priority.

### The history-of-religion strand continues through at least 1908

The early religion programme now includes, among other items:

- 1902, **“Religion and the Time-Process”**;
- 1906, **“The Fundamental Concept of the Primitive Philosophy”**;
- 1907, **“The Entangling Alliance of Religion and History”**;
- 1907, **“The Origins of Ethical Inwardness in Jewish Thought”**;
- 1908, **“Pragmatism and Theology”**;
- 1908, **“Religious Transition and Ethical Awakening in America”**;
- 1908, **“The Theory of a Pre-Christian Cult of Jesus.”**

The current project is testing, rather than presupposing, the relation of these early papers to the mature history-of-ideas method. The 1902 and 1907 primary texts now provide strong evidence for continuity of **operations**: category/presupposition analysis, logical motives, survival of earlier forms, distinction of logical from chronological sequence, caution about false borrowing, and reconstruction of indirect development. The later `unit-idea` vocabulary should not be projected backward as already fully formed.

### Mature Asian/global strands

The 1933 **“The Chinese Origin of a Romanticism”** is best treated, on current source analysis, as a history of cross-cultural transmission and European representations of China rather than direct Sinological philology: its source architecture is overwhelmingly European-language (Jesuit reports/translations, Mendoza, Kircher, Leibniz, Voltaire, Temple, Le Comte, Attiret, Chambers, garden/aesthetic literature). The 1935 *Primitivism and Related Ideas in Antiquity* shows a different mature mode: the Preface explicitly thanks **W. F. Albright** and **P.-E. Dumont** for supplementary essays on Ancient Western Asia and Indian literature, making the division of area/language expertise visible in the book’s architecture.

### Current provisional career shape

1. **1895–1901: direct Orientalist/comparative-religion formation and practice** — Lanman/Everett/Toy; Sanskrit and Buddhist texts; notebook 004; 1898 JAOS Buddhist philology; Lévi private reading; Marillier EPHE work; 1901 Buddhism teaching.
2. **1898–1908: sustained comparative/historical religion programme** — 005 begins in Paris but is demonstrably reused by 1905; the 1902 article explicitly reuses the 1898 Buddhist analysis; the 1906 `primitive philosophy` paper, 1907 Vedānta review and 1907–08 Jewish/Christian/religion essays show transformation rather than abrupt abandonment.
3. **1930s: mediated/distributed global history of ideas** — Chinese transmission through European representations; Indian and ancient Western-Asian work increasingly organized through specialist collaboration.

The working thesis is that Orientalist and comparative-religion training was an early laboratory for operations later redistributed into history of ideas: decomposition of systems, semantic discrimination, historical layering, logical versus chronological sequencing, classification by causal mechanism, caution about borrowing/syncretism, and reconstruction of intelligible arrangements. The chronology and degree of direct continuity are being tested source by source.
